**[Global return-on-investment of COVID-19 vaccines in the first year of the vaccination programme: A model-based economic evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1004772)**

*PLOS Medicine, 2026*

DOI: [10.1371/journal.pmed.1004772](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1004772)

**Question:** Did the $79.4 billion spent developing and delivering COVID-19 vaccines in 2020–21 pay off globally in the first year of use?

**Answer:** Returns per dollar were 59.7 (cost-effectiveness, discounted) to 809 (cost-benefit), all far above 1. The size depends on valuation method and one transmission model's 14–20 million deaths averted.

**Three takeaways**

1. Productivity ($564 billion) plus healthcare ($222 billion) savings alone were about 10 times total costs, with no price on a life.
2. The 809 figure implies benefits of $64.3 trillion, 82.7% of global GDP; the authors caution that COVID-19 mortality risk was too large for VSL's marginal-risk logic to hold.
3. Everything rests on one counterfactual model, whose deaths averted sit above other global estimates; the only other global economic analysis put lives saved at 4.1 million.
