## Top Papers

### 1. TCR-mimic bispecific nanobody-based T cell engager targeting intracellular tumor antigens for cancer immunotherapy

Ding Z, Sun S, Yang X, Huang X, Hou X, Xie S, et al.

*Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy*

[10.1038/s41392-026-02745-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-026-02745-x)

HLA-A2/WT1 or HLA-A2/GPC3 pMHC-I × CD3ε TCR-mimic VHH–VHH bispecific (TCRm Bi-NbTE).

This proof-of-concept builds a fully nanobody (VHH–VHH) engager in which one arm is a TCR-mimic VHH that recognizes a tumor peptide–MHC class I complex, allowing the engager to redirect T cells against otherwise-inaccessible intracellular antigens (WT1, GPC3) in an HLA-restricted manner, while the other arm engages CD3ε. Replacing aggregation-prone scFvs with paired single-domain antibodies yields a modular, stable, scalable backbone that drove antigen-restricted T cell activation, selective lysis of pMHC-I⁺ targets while sparing antigen-negative cells, and tumor suppression in both CDX and PDX models without notable toxicity. The work simultaneously addresses scFv instability and the extracellular-only target restriction that has limited the engager field.

DICE score: 4 — A first-in-class all-VHH TCR-mimic format that extends T-cell-engager target space to intracellular antigens while solving the scFv stability problem, a differentiated and broadly generalizable architecture.

Guangxi Key Laboratory of Nanobody Research, Guangxi Medical University.

### 2. Bispecific antibody engineered extracellular vesicles redirect T cells to prevent postoperative epidural fibrosis

Ye S, Xu Q, Xu Y, Lu H, Liu Z, Guo J, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-74883-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74883-3)

FAP × CD3 bispecific antibody-decorated extracellular vesicles (BsAb EVs).

Rather than delivering a soluble engager, the authors decorate extracellular vesicles with a FAP × CD3 bispecific antibody, converting EVs into a multivalent T-cell-redirecting vehicle that recruits endogenous T cells to eliminate pathogenic FAP⁺ fibroblasts in situ. In a laminectomy model the BsAb EVs selectively depleted the transcriptionally distinct FAP⁺ stromal subset, reduced fibrotic collagen, and prevented epidural fibrosis without detectable systemic toxicity. The study reframes engager delivery as a surface-display problem and extends T-cell redirection beyond oncology to pathogenic stroma.

DICE score: 4 — A genuinely novel engager-display modality (EV-anchored bispecific) that offers multivalency and localized delivery, broadening T-cell redirection into fibrotic and tissue-remodeling disease.

University of Pennsylvania / Tongji Hospital, HUST / Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences.

### 3. Physics-Informed Artificial Intelligence Design of Picomolar Nanobodies Enables Deep Tumor Penetration and High-Contrast Imaging

Shi N, Ren C, Zhang L, Wang L, Yang X, Li X, et al.

*Research*

[10.34133/research.1325](https://doi.org/10.34133/research.1325)

Anti-CEA affinity-matured VHH fused to an anti-HSA VHH (half-life-extended bispecific nanobody).

The authors couple AlphaFold 3 structural priors with molecular dynamics and variable-dielectric MM/GBSA energy decomposition to pinpoint interfacial residues that are structurally permissible but thermodynamically suboptimal, then resolve them through electrostatic, desolvation, and van der Waals refinement in a focused library. The pipeline reached a 99% binding-positivity rate and ~44 pM affinity (~306-fold over parent) without sacrificing thermal stability (>63 °C); fusing the matured domain to an anti-serum-albumin VHH produced a bispecific with deep intratumoral penetration and >168 h retention in vivo. It is a transferable physics-informed alternative to stochastic affinity-maturation screening directly applicable to engager binding arms.

DICE score: 4 — A generalizable AI-plus-physics affinity-maturation framework delivering picomolar binders with retained stability and in vivo validation, addressing a core bottleneck in engineering engager targeting domains.

Central South University / Academy of Military Medical Sciences.

### 4. Teclistamab-based induction treatment in transplant-eligible, newly diagnosed multiple myeloma: a phase 2 trial

Raab MS, Weinhold N, Kortüm KM, Krönke J, Fenk R, Weisel K, et al.

*Nature Medicine*

[10.1038/s41591-026-04471-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04471-x)

BCMA × CD3 bispecific engager (teclistamab) in frontline combination regimens.

The MajesTEC-5 (GMMG-HD10/DSMM-XX) phase 2 study evaluates teclistamab-based induction, including combination with daratumumab, in transplant-eligible newly diagnosed multiple myeloma, moving an approved BCMA × CD3 engager from the relapsed/refractory setting into frontline therapy. The pooled analysis across three cohorts reports deep early responses, providing the clinical rationale for engager-anchored induction. No new engineering, but important translational data shaping where engagers sit in the treatment sequence.

DICE score: 3 — Important clinical data extending an approved BCMA × CD3 engager to frontline transplant-eligible myeloma, with potential to reshape induction paradigms.

Clinical-stage; GMMG/DSMM consortia, Heidelberg/Würzburg.

### 5. Optimizing nanobody-based molecules targeting mesothelin: Exploring format, valency, size, and half-life for next-generation cancer theranostics

Boisgard R, MacFarland D, Goubard A, Castellano R, Chames P, Kerfelec B

*Journal of Controlled Release*

[10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.115129](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2026.115129)

Anti-mesothelin VHH building blocks assembled into format/valency/size/half-life variants.

This work systematically dissects how valency, overall size, and half-life-extension strategy trade off against tumor uptake, penetration, and clearance for anti-mesothelin nanobody constructs intended for theranostics. By mapping the design space rather than optimizing a single molecule, it provides a practical engineering reference for selecting nanobody targeting moieties — directly relevant to choosing tumor-binding arms and pharmacokinetic tuning for mesothelin-directed engagers.

DICE score: 3 — A useful methodological map of nanobody format/valency/half-life trade-offs that informs engager arm selection against a clinically important solid-tumor antigen.

Aix-Marseille University / CRCM, Marseille.

### 6. Efficient inference of non-polyreactive antibody variants dependent on local fine-tuning

Saunders M, Comyn S, Medjo B, Anderson Z, LaCombe C, Ripley J, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2692763](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2692763)

Machine-learning developability engineering — prediction and redesign of non-polyreactive antibody variants.

Polyreactivity — broad, low-affinity off-target binding — drives rapid clearance and is a recurrent liability for engager binding domains. The authors show that locally fine-tuned models efficiently infer non-polyreactive variants, improving on assay-variable experimental screening and on general-purpose models that lack specificity for this property. The approach offers a transferable in silico filter for de-risking binder candidates early in engager design.

DICE score: 3 — Targets a key developability liability for engager binders with a transferable, data-efficient ML method, addressing a practical bottleneck in lead optimization.

### 7. Discovery and optimization of a pH-responsive ultra-long-acting VHH-based growth hormone mimetic

Jin H, Sun G, Shen H, Hao Q, Chen L, Che C, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2691350](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2691350)

pH-responsive albumin-binding VHH engineered for ultra-long half-life.

Although framed as a growth-hormone mimetic, the engineering payload is a pH-dependent VHH–albumin interaction tuned so that binding is maintained in circulation but released in the endosome, extending serum half-life well beyond conventional albumin hitchhiking. The pH-switchable half-life-extension principle is directly portable to engager scaffolds, where balancing persistence against clearance is a central design lever.

DICE score: 3 — A transferable pH-responsive VHH half-life-extension mechanism with clear applicability to tuning the pharmacokinetics of nanobody-based engagers.

### 8. Impact of ASO conjugation and receptor binding affinity on intracellular transport of mono- and bispecific TfR- and CD98-Brainshuttle variants

Sela T, Wirth T, Sommer A, Larraillet V, Butzer J, Lohmann S, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2691351](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2691351)

Mono- and bispecific TfR- and CD98-targeted Brainshuttle antibodies; effect of affinity and payload conjugation on transcytosis.

The study quantifies how receptor-binding affinity and antisense-oligonucleotide conjugation govern intracellular routing and blood–brain-barrier transcytosis of bispecific shuttle antibodies. The affinity/recycling trade-offs and conjugation effects it characterizes are foundational for designing CNS-penetrant engagers and conjugated multispecifics, an area where biophysical rules remain poorly defined.

DICE score: 3 — Mechanistic, quantitative insight into how affinity and conjugation shape bispecific transcytosis, informing the design of CNS-directed multispecific and engager molecules.

Roche / Genentech.

### 9. Multi-dimensional orchestration of binders for improved CAR-T immunotherapy

Zhu C, Jiang Z, Jing R, Guo W, Lu Y

*Cancer Letters*

[10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218699](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218699)

Review of antigen-binding domain (binder) design principles for cell-based immunotherapy.

This synthesis argues that binders are active design elements — shaping antigen selectivity, signaling thresholds, and exhaustion — rather than passive recognition modules, and organizes the levers (affinity, epitope, valency, geometry) that tune them. Though framed for CAR-T, the binder-engineering principles transfer directly to the tumor-targeting arms of engagers and multispecifics.

DICE score: 2 — A useful conceptual organization of binder-design principles relevant across CAR and engager modalities, but a review without new experimental data.

### 10. Semi-automated Ribosome Display for High-Throughput DARPin Binder Selection

Häfliger A, Briand-Schumacher S, Furler S, Dreier B, Plückthun A

*New Biotechnology*

[10.1016/j.nbt.2026.06.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbt.2026.06.007)

Semi-automated ribosome display workflow for non-Ig (DARPin) scaffold binder discovery.

The authors streamline DARPin selection by semi-automating ribosome display, increasing throughput and reproducibility of binder generation against diverse targets. As DARPins and other non-immunoglobulin scaffolds are increasingly used as compact, stable building blocks for multispecific constructs, faster binder discovery feeds the front end of engager assembly.

DICE score: 2 — An incremental throughput/automation improvement for non-Ig scaffold binder discovery, useful but methodologically routine.

Plückthun lab, University of Zurich.

### 11. Programming T cells for intercellular genome editing

Wasko KM, Maker M, Ngo W, Chen K, Ma E, Pattali R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.06.21.729417](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.21.729417)

Engineered T cells as cell-type-targeted intercellular delivery vehicles for genome-editing molecules.

The Doudna group exploits intrinsic immune-cell targeting properties to deliver editors from engineered T cells to defined recipient cells, addressing the specificity and efficiency limits of conventional editor delivery. While not an engager itself, the work is an adjacent cell-engineering/delivery platform whose targeting logic could inform engager-armed cell therapies.

DICE score: 3 — A novel cell-based intercellular delivery concept with potential relevance to engager-secreting or engager-arming cell-therapy strategies.

Doudna lab, UC Berkeley / IGI.

### 12. Human CD8-iTreg are potent GVHD suppressors and tumoricidal effectors by release of Granzyme-K⁺ Supramolecular Attack Particles

Larson JH, Compeer EB, Dougherty PR, Smith K, Zaiken MC, Margaritaki O, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.06.18.731665](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.18.731665)

Mechanistic study of cytolytic supramolecular attack particle (SMAP) release by induced CD8⁺ regulatory T cells.

Induced human CD8⁺ Tregs are shown to combine immunosuppression with cytotoxicity via release of Granzyme-K⁺ supramolecular attack particles, characterizing a distinct effector-delivery mechanism at the immune synapse. The synapse and SMAP biology is field-adjacent to how engagers organize cytolytic delivery and could inform readouts of engager-driven killing.

DICE score: 2 — Field-relevant synapse/effector-delivery mechanism, but not engager engineering and at preprint stage.

Dustin / Blazar groups, Oxford / University of Minnesota.

## Weekly Themes

VHH/nanobody building blocks dominated this week's engineering signal: a TCR-mimic VHH–VHH engager reaching intracellular antigens, a systematic mesothelin-nanobody format study, an AI-designed picomolar anti-CEA nanobody, and a pH-responsive long-acting VHH all point to single-domain scaffolds displacing aggregation-prone scFvs for stability, modularity, and tunable pharmacokinetics. A second thread is the maturation of computational design — physics-informed affinity maturation and ML-based developability (anti-polyreactivity) filters are moving from concept to validated pipelines for engager binding arms. Format innovation also pushed beyond soluble proteins, with bispecific-decorated extracellular vesicles introducing a multivalent, locally-delivered T-cell-redirection modality and extending engager logic from cancer into pathogenic stroma. On the clinical side, momentum continued for BCMA × CD3 engagers moving frontline in myeloma. Engager-specific preprint activity was thin this week, with the most relevant new preprints sitting in adjacent cell-engineering and immune-synapse biology rather than core engager architecture.
