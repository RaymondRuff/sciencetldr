## Top Papers

### 1. The Trispecific Antibody IBI3019 Integrates CDH17-Enhanced EGFR Antagonism with Optimized CD16A Engagement to Elicit Potent Antitumor Activity with Minimal Toxicity

Liu J, Ma J, Lin S, Dai W, Lu J, Wu M, et al.

*Cancer research*

[10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-25-5580](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-25-5580)

CDH17 × EGFR × CD16A trispecific NK engager — high-affinity CDH17 Fab as tumor anchor, EGFR-blocking arm, anti-CD16A VHH, with inter-paratope spacing as the tuned variable.

Two separable engineering results here, and the second is the one worth stealing. First, the "anchor and capture" mechanism: a high-affinity CDH17 arm holds the molecule resident on the tumor surface so the EGFR arm can rebind unoccupied or newly synthesized receptor, producing deeper and more sustained pathway suppression than cetuximab or amivantamab while shifting the exposure away from skin — an avidity-based answer to the on-target/off-tumor problem that does not require affinity de-tuning of the toxic arm. Second, and more generalizable, is the format screen. The authors found NK activation to be governed by the physical distance between the tumor-antigen-binding Fab and the anti-CD16A VHH, and attribute the dependence to CD45 exclusion: longer spacers open the synaptic cleft enough to admit the bulky phosphatase, which quenches signaling. The widely used Morrison-type architecture, in which the effector-binding domain hangs off the C-terminus, consistently underperformed shorter-spacing designs. That is a concrete, mechanistically grounded architectural rule for a decision most groups currently make by convention rather than by measurement, and it applies to CD3-based engagers as much as to CD16A. The final molecule showed superior in vivo efficacy and no observable skin toxicity in cynomolgus monkeys.

DICE score: 5 — Establishes effector-arm-to-TAA spacing as a first-order design variable via CD45 exclusion, backed by an extensive format screen, and packages it with a distinct avidity-based mechanism for widening the therapeutic window of a toxic target arm.

Innovent Biologics (Suzhou/Shanghai) with Shenzhen People's Hospital; IBI3019 nominated for clinical development.

### 2. Generation of a highly versatile TCR bispecific scaffold optimized for targeting tumor-specific peptide-HLA antigens

Hofmann M, Unverdorben F, Pszolla MG, Aschmoneit N, Hutt M, Manz T, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2722707](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2722707)

TCER — TCR-based T-cell-engaging bispecific scaffold for peptide-HLA targets, engineered jointly for affinity, half-life, tolerability, and stability.

This is the formal platform disclosure for the scaffold behind Immatics' clinical pHLA engagers, and it is the closest thing the field has to a systematic answer for the hardest target class in the space. pHLA antigens are low-copy and promiscuous, which is precisely why lineage antigens like CD19 and BCMA have absorbed most of the clinical progress; the engineering problem is that a TCR-derived binder has to be made stable and manufacturable enough to survive as a biologic while retaining the discrimination that makes it worth using. The paper describes a scaffold optimized simultaneously across efficacy, tolerability, plasma half-life, and stability rather than sequentially, and frames the result as a versatile class rather than a one-off construct — the claim that separates a platform paper from a molecule paper. For anyone weighing scFv-based versus TCR-based approaches to solid tumor antigens, this is the reference description of what the TCR route costs and what it buys, from a group with clinical-stage molecules built on it.

DICE score: 4 — Full disclosure of a differentiated, clinically deployed engager scaffold purpose-built for the pHLA target class, where the technical barrier has been the field's main obstacle to solid tumor engagers.

Clinical-stage platform; Immatics Biotechnologies GmbH, Tübingen.

### 3. Antibody framework engineering enables augmented cytokine receptor signaling capacities of bispecific single domain antibody-based IL-21 mimetics

Lipinski B, Unmuth L, Tran THH, Harwood J, Arras P, Becker S, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2726042](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2726042)

IL-21R (CD360) × IL-2Rγ (CD132) bispecific VHH-Fc cytokine mimetic; framework mutations engineering VHH:VHH interactions plus VHH-in-IgG reformatting.

The finding that matters beyond cytokine mimetics is that agonism was tuned almost entirely by geometry, not by valency or affinity. Camelid VHHs against the two IL-21 receptor subunits, combinatorially reformatted into monovalent bispecifics, initially gave attenuated STAT3 signaling relative to native IL-21. Rather than re-mature the paratopes, the authors introduced framework mutations that engineer VHH:VHH contacts within the sdAb-Fc fusion, rigidifying the relative orientation of the two binding domains — forced dimerization in place of a flexible linker. Combining that rigidification with a format change, in which the VHHs replace the VH and VL of a conventional IgG, restored agonism comparable to the wild-type cytokine, with paratope valencies unchanged throughout. That is a clean demonstration that the spatial arrangement of two paratopes against a heterodimeric receptor is an independently engineerable axis, and the same lever applies directly to engagers, where the geometry of the CD3 or CD16 arm relative to the tumor arm sets synapse quality (see also the IBI3019 spacing result above). The framework-mutation approach is attractive because it acts on the scaffold rather than the CDRs, leaving specificity untouched.

DICE score: 4 — Shows that receptor agonism can be dialed from attenuated to native-cytokine-equivalent purely by rigidifying inter-paratope geometry through framework engineering, a scaffold-level lever that transfers cleanly to multispecific engager design.

Zielonka group, TU Darmstadt / Merck Healthcare KGaA NBE Technologies, with EMD Serono.

### 4. Intratumoral injection of EpCAM BITE, IL-12, and GM-CSF mRNA-LNPs blocks the growth of local treated and distant untreated tumors

Golubovskaya V, Hu L, Zhang S, Sienkiewicz J, Zhou H, Berahovich R, et al.

*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*

[10.1073/pnas.2616059123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2616059123)

EpCAM × CD3 BiTE delivered as intratumoral mRNA-LNP, co-formulated with IL-12 and GM-CSF mRNA; two-tumor NSG model with human T cells.

The delivery format is the point. Encoding the engager as intratumoral mRNA-LNP rather than dosing protein systemically confines the highest concentration to the tumor, which is the standard rationale; what is new here is the abscopal result from adding IL-12 and GM-CSF mRNA to the same injection. Treating one flank tumor increased T cell numbers and killing-associated transcriptional programs around both the injected and the uninjected contralateral tumor, and drove substantial upregulation of HLA class II and associated genes in both — the authors' reading is that the cytokine pair converts tumor cells into antigen-presenting cells, converting a locally delivered redirecting molecule into a systemic immunization event. For engager developers, this reframes the payload question: an mRNA-encoded engager is not merely a PK trick but a slot in a co-formulated cassette where the accessory cytokines can be chosen to change what the engaged T cells do afterward. Caveats are the usual ones for this model class — an NSG mouse with adoptively transferred human T cells has no endogenous myeloid or B compartment, so the mechanistic claim about antigen presentation needs an immunocompetent follow-up.

DICE score: 4 — A locally delivered mRNA-LNP engager plus cytokine cassette produces control of distant untreated tumors, positioning nucleic-acid delivery as a route to systemic immunity rather than only a local exposure trick.

Promab Biotechnologies / IntraAb with Walter Bodmer, University of Oxford.

### 5. Artificial intelligence-assisted design of MAGE-A4 × CD16 T-cell receptor-like natural killer cell engagers

Liu Y, Peng H, Fan X, Liu X, Jiao G, Li J, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2717991](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2717991)

MAGE-A4/HLA-A*02:01 × CD16a TCR-like NK cell engager — yeast-display-derived TCR-like Fab fused to an anti-CD16a VHH in IgG format, with N297A Fc silencing.

A useful template for two reasons. The discovery route pairs yeast-display screening with computational structural prioritization to pull a TCR-like binder against the MAGE-A4 GVY230-239/HLA-A*02:01 complex, which is the class of target where empirical panning alone tends to yield cross-reactive junk; the AI step is used as a triage filter rather than a generator, which is currently the more reliable place to put it. The format decision is the second contribution: instead of the reflexive CD3 arm, the binder was built into an NK engager by appending a CD16a VHH to an IgG. The N297A control is what makes the paper interpretable — silencing the native Fc knocked out reporter activation by the parental IgG while the VHH-containing NKCE retained CD16a activity, cleanly separating engineered CD16a engagement from incidental Fc-mediated signaling, a distinction that a lot of NKCE papers leave ambiguous. Killing was target- and CD16a-dependent against MAGE-A4/HLA-A*02:01-positive lines. It is in vitro proof of concept only; the authors are explicit that cytokine profiling, safety, and in vivo work remain.

DICE score: 3 — A well-controlled construction of a pHLA-directed NK engager with a clean experimental separation of VHH-mediated from Fc-mediated CD16a signaling, though efficacy evidence stops at in vitro cytotoxicity.

University of Macau with Canton Biologics.

### 6. Teclistamab and Daratumumab Immune-Based Doublet Therapy for Relapsed/Refractory Multiple Myeloma: the TRIMM-2 Study

Rodriguez-Otero P, Morillo D, D'Souza A, Reece D, van de Donk N, Chari A, et al.

*Blood advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020648](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020648)

BCMA × CD3 IgG-like bispecific (teclistamab) plus anti-CD38 mAb (daratumumab), subcutaneous; phase 1b, n = 61 at weight-based RP2D.

The efficacy numbers are strong for a triple-class-exposed population with a median of five prior lines — 68.9% ORR, 44.3% complete response or better, median PFS 26.3 months, median duration of response not reached at 12 months' follow-up. The result the field should actually absorb is the dosing one. A cohort exploring fixed-dose teclistamab at 100–300 mg was terminated early after a safety signal for fatal infections, and all patients were moved to weight-based dosing; grade 3/4 TEAEs reached 93.4% overall with seven deaths from treatment-emergent events. Fixed dosing is the direction of travel for most engagers on convenience grounds, and this is a concrete counterexample where the flat-dose exposure in lighter patients appears to have driven infection risk past an acceptable threshold. For anyone doing exposure–response work on a CD3 engager combined with a depleting antibody, the takeaway is that the cumulative immunosuppression of the combination narrows the window in which flat dosing is safe, and that early immunoglobulin replacement belongs in the protocol rather than in the management guidance.

DICE score: 3 — Practice-relevant efficacy for a fully immune-based doublet, with a specific and transferable safety lesson about fixed versus weight-based dosing of a CD3 engager combined with anti-CD38.

Clinical-stage (NCT04108195); Janssen / Johnson & Johnson with Clínica Universidad de Navarra and international sites.

### 7. A Phase 1 Study of PF-07062119, an Anti-GUCY2C/Anti-CD3 Bispecific Fc Antibody, in Patients With Advanced Gastrointestinal Cancers

Fakih M, Doi T, Tolcher A, Hong D, Tie J, Messersmith W, et al.

*Cancer research communications*

[10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0101](https://doi.org/10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0101)

GUCY2C × CD3ε bispecific with Fc, subcutaneous dosing (45–3700 µg), monotherapy and combined with sasanlimab or bevacizumab; n = 79.

A negative result reported carefully enough to be useful. Priming worked as intended — a 400 µg priming dose with premedication reduced CRS incidence and lifted the recommended expansion dose from an 800 µg MTD to 2100 µg — and PK was dose-proportional, so exposure was not the limitation. The overall response rate was 2.5% across all cohorts, and adding a PD-1 agent or bevacizumab neither added meaningful toxicity nor rescued efficacy. Dose-limiting toxicities at the unprimed MTD were grade 3 CRS, colitis, and diarrhea, and diarrhea remained the dominant clinically significant toxicity — consistent with GUCY2C's normal expression on intestinal epithelium, which is the structural problem the molecule was always going to have. This is now a reasonably well-powered data point that CD3 engagers against GI epithelial antigens with meaningful normal-tissue expression run into a window problem that dose optimization alone does not solve, and it strengthens the case for masking, conditional activation, or avidity-gated designs on this target class rather than another round of schedule tuning.

DICE score: 3 — Substantive first-in-human dataset showing that priming solved the CRS problem while on-target gut toxicity and minimal activity persisted, an informative bound on unmasked CD3 engagers against epithelial antigens.

Clinical-stage (NCT04171141); Pfizer with City of Hope, MD Anderson, MSKCC, and international sites.

### 8. Bicistronic CD19/CD22 CAR T-Cell Therapy in Pediatric B-Cell Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia: A Nonrandomized Clinical Trial

Wan X, Tang Y, Cai J, Song L, Wang T, Li W, et al.

*JAMA oncology*

[10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3460](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3460)

Bicistronic lentiviral CD19 CAR + CD22 CAR (two separate receptors, one vector), fresh infusion after 5–7 days of culture; phase 2, n = 261.

The dual-targeting question that matters for engager design is whether adding a second tumor-binding specificity actually suppresses antigen-negative escape in humans, and this is the largest clean read on it: 99.2% MRD-negative complete remission and 61.7% event-free survival at 36 months in relapsed/refractory pediatric B-ALL, including isolated CNS (60.0% 24-month EFS) and testicular (80.0%) relapse. Two architectural details are worth noting. The construct is bicistronic — two independent CARs rather than a tandem dual-scFv receptor — which sidesteps the linker-geometry and steric problems that plague tandem designs, at the cost of controlling stoichiometry between the two receptors. And the product was infused fresh after only 5–7 days of culture, keeping cells at an earlier differentiation state. Grade 3–4 CRS at 49.4% is high. For multispecific engagers, the transferable lesson is that dual antigen coverage delivered as two separate binding units, rather than one fused bispecific paratope, is a legitimate architecture when escape is the failure mode.

DICE score: 3 — Large, long-follow-up human evidence that dual CD19/CD22 targeting delivers durable remissions, with a bicistronic-versus-tandem architecture choice directly relevant to how multispecifics should be assembled against antigen escape.

Shanghai Children's Medical Center, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, with St Jude Children's Research Hospital (ChiCTR2000032211).

### 9. Engineering TPT1-mediated apoptosis resistance for improved mammalian cell biomanufacturing

Bravo-Venegas J, Betts Z, Kalsi D, Pybus LP, Orellana CA, Altamirano C, et al.

*Metabolic engineering*

[10.1016/j.ymben.2026.102543](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymben.2026.102543)

Not a molecule — a CHO host-cell engineering cassette (TPT1 overexpression) benchmarked against BCL-2, CFLAR, and BAK/BAX double knockout, tested on monoclonal and bispecific antibody production lines.

Manufacturability is where multispecific programs quietly die, and this addresses the upstream half of it. The authors first established that viability loss in late-stage fed-batch is dominated by the intrinsic apoptotic pathway — caspase-9, -7, and -3 cleavage with minimal caspase-8 activation — and then engineered against that axis specifically rather than reaching for BCL-2 by default. TPT1, a multifunctional stress-response protein, outperformed both BCL-2 and CFLAR on overall process performance, held viability under chemical apoptosis induction, pro-apoptotic BAK overexpression, and caspase-3 activation, and matched or exceeded a BAK/BAX double knockout on viability while beating it on growth and titre. Critically for this audience, the benefit transferred to industrially relevant CHO DG44 platforms expressing both monoclonal and bispecific antibodies, improving culture longevity and volumetric titre without changing cell-specific productivity or the N-glycan profile — meaning the gain is extra culture time, not altered product quality, which is the version of a titre improvement that survives comparability. Single-cassette, and combining it with the BAK/BAX knockout gave no further gain.

DICE score: 3 — A single-cassette host-cell engineering solution that raises bispecific titres through extended culture lifespan without perturbing product quality attributes, validated on an industrially relevant CHO DG44 platform.

University of Manchester with FUJIFILM Biotechnologies and Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile.

### 10. BCMA versus GPRC5D bispecific antibodies in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma: A systematic review and meta-analysis guiding treatment selection

Liang Q, Liu S, Li C, Liang Z, Zhu L, Wang W, et al.

*British journal of haematology*

[10.1111/bjh.70824](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjh.70824)

Target-class comparison of BCMA × CD3 versus GPRC5D × CD3 bispecifics; 30 studies, 3218 patients, with meta-regression on study-level moderators.

At the recommended phase 2 dose the two target classes were essentially interchangeable on efficacy — 65% ORR for BCMA-directed versus 70% for GPRC5D-directed, with similar rates of ≥VGPR and complete response — which is the useful negative finding for anyone reasoning about target selection from published response rates alone. Any-grade CRS was more frequent with GPRC5D engagers (74% vs 57%), while grade ≥3 CRS, neurotoxicity, infections, and haematologic AEs were broadly comparable. The meta-regression is the more actionable part: prior penta-drug exposure and higher median lines of therapy were negative moderators of response across both classes, and prior BCMA-directed therapy further degraded efficacy specifically in BCMA-targeted cohorts. That last point quantifies the antigen- and effector-compartment history effect that sequencing decisions turn on, and it argues that cross-trial comparisons of engager response rates are dominated by treatment history rather than by target biology.

DICE score: 2 — Careful synthesis rather than new engineering, but it establishes that BCMA and GPRC5D engagers are efficacy-equivalent at RP2D and quantifies treatment history as the dominant driver of apparent response differences.

Sun Yat-Sen University Cancer Center.

### 11. Developability-by-design for bispecific antibodies in discovery: combining computational prediction and early screening for candidate selection

Al Meslamani AZ, Jarab AS, Al Marshoodi KA

*Expert opinion on biological therapy*

[10.1080/14712598.2026.2727673](https://doi.org/10.1080/14712598.2026.2727673)

Review — sequence-, structure-, and ML-based developability prediction for bispecific formats, plus material-efficient assays for assembly, stability, and PK risk.

A structured narrative review arguing that developability belongs inside discovery rather than downstream of it, and proposing a four-tier design–predict–screen–redesign loop anchored to the target product profile. The framing that earns it a slot is its taxonomy of bispecific liabilities into architecture-related, inherited (carried in from the parental binders), and emergent (arising only on assembly) — a distinction that explains why parental-mAb developability data routinely fails to predict bispecific behavior. The conclusion is appropriately deflationary: no single computational score or assay generalizes across formats, computational predictions should be treated as hypothesis generators, and candidate nomination requires verified molecular identity, correctly assembled yield, and orthogonal testing under conditions matched to the intended formulation and route. The authors identify the field's real bottleneck as data rather than method — format-diverse datasets with assay-resolved labels and identity-aware external validation.

DICE score: 2 — No new data, but a coherent framework for where developability prediction currently fails on multispecific formats and an honest account of why the available ML tooling does not yet transfer across architectures.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. De novo designed single-domain antibodies protect against lethal cobra venom neurotoxicity in vivo

Overath MD, Lundquist EVS, Björnsson KH, Kerwin S, Jacobsen CP, Moiniche MvB, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.01.748349](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.748349)

De novo designed VHHs against α-cobratoxin; head-to-head comparison of Germinal, RFantibody, and BoltzGen across three VHH frameworks.

The benchmark is the value here. Three current generative models with VHH-design capability were run on identical inputs and scored by AlphaFold3 interface confidence (ipTM) and RMSD self-consistency; only Germinal produced designs clearing the in silico bar for synthesis. A scaled Germinal campaign across three frameworks gave 42/46 soluble expressers and 4 binders from two of three frameworks — a hit rate that is honest about where de novo antibody design currently sits, and the framework-dependence is a practical result nobody publishes. Two leads reached 4.1 nM and 10.8 nM, were monomeric with low polyreactivity, and fully protected mice against α-cobratoxin challenge, with one retaining 56% survival against whole venom. That is de novo design landing on a clinically validated scaffold with in vivo efficacy, which is the version that matters for anyone contemplating designed binders as engager arms.

DICE score: 4 — First rigorous head-to-head of the current de novo VHH design models on a common target, with designed single-domain binders achieving nanomolar affinity, clean developability, and full in vivo protection.

Jenkins group, Technical University of Denmark, with University of Northern Colorado.

### 2. Rational Control of Basal CAR Expression Improves Discrimination in Inducible T Cell Circuits

Hoces D, Ng J, Perez J, Hernandez-Lopez RA

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.28.747722](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.28.747722)

SynNotch-CAR circuits with C-terminal tags (fluorescent proteins, degrons, endocytosis signals, ER retention motifs) added as a post-translational layer to suppress basal CAR surface expression.

Leakiness, not induced output, turns out to set the discrimination ceiling of inducible T cell circuits. The authors quantified basal CAR expression across synNotch-CAR designs and built a coupled ODE model showing discrimination depends jointly on basal output, CAR potency, and effector-to-target ratio — then attacked basal expression post-translationally rather than by further tuning the promoter. Fluorescent protein and degron tags both cut basal surface CAR while preserving induced expression and improved antigen-density and combinatorial discrimination in vitro; in xenografts the fluorescent-protein tags improved sparing of off-target antigen-positive cells while retaining activity against high-antigen tumors, whereas degron constructs underperformed in vivo, exposing a trade-off between basal suppression and induced CAR persistence. Antigen-density discrimination is the same problem engagers face when a target is expressed on normal tissue, and layered transcriptional-plus-post-translational control is a transferable framing.

DICE score: 3 — Identifies basal output as the governing parameter for inducible circuit fidelity and demonstrates a simple post-translational layer that improves antigen-density discrimination in vivo, with an honest account of where it fails.

Hernandez-Lopez lab, Stanford University.

### 3. Antibodies with Engineered Fc Domains Having Absolute Binding Selectivity to either FcγRIIa or FcγRI Delineate the Respective Effector Phenotypes by Human Monocytes and Macrophages

George K, Ko S, Irani S, Delidakis G, Lee C-H, Menon P, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.31.748147](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.31.748147)

Fc2KG — an aglycosylated human IgG1 Fc binding FcγRIIa/b at near-physiological affinity with no detectable binding to any other FcγR; paired with the FcγRI-exclusive Fc5 variant, plus a crystal structure.

Aglycosylated Fc with absolute receptor selectivity is a hard engineering target, and the crystallographic analysis showing how the mutations compensate for the missing N297 glycan is the durable contribution. Deployed as reagents, the variants separate effector phenotypes that wild-type IgG1 confounds: with CD16+ primary human monocytes, selective FcγRII engagement accounts for much of the ADCP normally attributed to IgG1, and Fc2KG-formatted antibodies drove high GM-CSF; in M1-like monocyte-derived macrophages, wild-type, Fc2KG, and Fc5 formats of trastuzumab were equally proficient at trogocytotic killing of HER2+ SK-BR-3 cells, but only FcγRI engagement produced proinflammatory cytokine secretion. That last dissociation — equal killing, divergent cytokine output, depending on which receptor is engaged — is directly relevant to Fc design on IgG-like engagers, where the usual choice is a binary between silenced and wild-type Fc.

DICE score: 3 — A structurally characterized aglycosylated Fc with absolute FcγRIIa/b selectivity that decouples phagocytic killing from cytokine induction, giving Fc engineering on multispecifics a finer-grained set of options than silence-or-not.

Georgiou lab, University of Texas at Austin.

### 4. Multivalent Anti-ACE2 Nanobodies Confer Broad Pan-Sarbecovirus Protection

Bakasis AD, Patejak R, Fridy PC, Jenkins J, Aldis M, Baharani VA, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.01.748598](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.748598)

Anti-human-ACE2 VHHs from competition-based yeast surface display, formatted as dimeric VHHs and bivalent/tetravalent VHH-Fc fusions.

Two transferable engineering points. First, the discovery pipeline uses competition-based yeast display to select for binders that block entry rather than merely bind — screening on the functional property directly, which is the same problem engager campaigns face when panning for a CD3 or CD16 arm with a target signaling profile. Second, the valency series is a clean dose-response on avidity: dimeric VHHs and bivalent and tetravalent Fc fusions showed markedly increased antiviral activity over monomers, reaching low-nanomolar to picomolar potency across SARS-CoV-2 variants and diverse sarbecoviruses, with favorable PK and prophylactic protection in mouse models of both SARS-CoV-2 and SARS-CoV. Targeting the host receptor rather than the viral spike is the escape-resistance argument, and the constructs did not perturb ACE2 enzymatic function or surface expression — a specificity result that matters when the target is a host protein with a day job.

DICE score: 3 — A well-executed VHH valency and format study on a host-receptor target, with function-first selection and a clean demonstration that engagement can be made potent without disrupting the target's native activity.

Hatziioannou, Bieniasz, and Rout labs, The Rockefeller University.

### 5. De novo design of CR2 binder as vaccine scaffold

Liu Y-T, Yin Y-N, Xu H-Q, Du W-T, Xie C, Wu P-H, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.28.747755](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.28.747755)

Deep-learning-designed high-affinity CR2 (complement receptor 2) binder, trimerized and fused to antigen as a single-chain vaccine scaffold; cryo-EM structure included.

The design target is a co-receptor rather than an antigen: CR2 lowers the B cell activation threshold when engaged by C3d-decorated antigen, but native C3d has low affinity, poor stability, and difficult manufacturing — a good example of replacing an awkward natural ligand with a designed binder that keeps the biology and drops the liabilities. Cryo-EM confirmed the design matched the computational model, and the trimeric binder fused to antigen elicited humoral responses comparable to nanoparticle vaccines while retaining single-chain protein production. For engager work the relevant read-across is the strategy: designed binders against costimulatory or coreceptor targets, validated structurally, as modular arms that lower an activation threshold rather than provide the primary specificity.

DICE score: 2 — A competent de novo binder against an immune coreceptor with structural confirmation and matched-to-nanoparticle immunogenicity, though the contribution is a vaccine scaffold rather than a new design method.

Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center.

## Weekly Themes

Geometry was the week's throughline, and it showed up independently in two papers that have nothing else in common. Innovent's IBI3019 found that NK activation depends on the physical spacing between the CD16A nanobody and the tumor-antigen Fab, attributing the effect to CD45 exclusion from the synapse and finding that the widely adopted Morrison-type architecture consistently underperforms shorter-spacing designs; the Darmstadt/Merck IL-21 mimetic paper found that agonism could be raised from attenuated to native-cytokine-equivalent purely by introducing framework mutations that rigidify the relative orientation of two VHHs, with paratope valencies untouched. Both say the same thing from opposite directions: the spatial arrangement of paratopes is an engineerable variable that is currently set by convention in most programs, and it is worth screening rather than assuming. Alongside that, the week's clinical data drew an unusually sharp line around what dose optimization can and cannot fix. Pfizer's GUCY2C × CD3 phase 1 showed that priming solved CRS cleanly — MTD moved from 800 µg to a 2100 µg expansion dose — while on-target gut toxicity and a 2.5% response rate persisted, and combination with PD-1 blockade or bevacizumab changed neither; TRIMM-2 reported strong efficacy for teclistamab plus daratumumab but terminated its fixed-dose cohort after fatal infections, reverting to weight-based dosing. Together they argue that schedule engineering has largely exhausted its returns on unmasked engagers against antigens with real normal-tissue expression, and that the next moves have to be architectural — masking, conditional activation, avidity gating. On the computational side, the cobra venom preprint is the most informative artifact of the week for anyone tracking de novo design maturity: a genuine head-to-head of Germinal, RFantibody, and BoltzGen on identical inputs, in which only one model cleared the in silico bar, hit rates were framework-dependent, and the successful designs nonetheless reached nanomolar affinity with clean developability and full in vivo protection. Paired with the Macau group's use of computational prioritization as a triage filter rather than a generator, the honest read is that generative methods are now reliably useful at narrowing experimental campaigns and still unreliable at replacing them.
