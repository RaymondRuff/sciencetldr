### 1. Genome-scale perturb-seq in primary human CD4 T cells maps context-specific regulators of T cell programs and human immune traits

Zhu R, Dann E, Yan J, Reyes Retana J, Goto R, Guitche RC, et al.

*Cell*

[10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.002)

Not a molecule — a CRISPRi perturbation of every expressed gene across 22 million primary human CD4 T cells from four donors, at rest and after stimulation, read out by a probe-based perturb-seq platform.

The engager field has spent a decade optimizing the molecule and treating the T cell as a fixed reagent, and this is the paper that makes the other half of the synapse legible at the same resolution. The headline technical achievement is scale in the right cells: genome-wide perturbation in *primary* human CD4 T cells rather than Jurkats or mouse cells, with a probe-based readout that makes 22 million cells economically tractable. The result that matters most for anyone designing a redirecting molecule is the context dependence. Active regulators and the gene programs they control changed dramatically between resting and stimulated states — meaning the regulatory network a CD3 arm engages is not one network but several, selected by the activation state of whatever T cell the molecule happens to find. That reframes a recurring clinical observation (heterogeneous response to the same engager at the same dose) as a property of the effector compartment rather than of exposure. The authors also model T cell states seen in population-scale atlases from perturbation signatures, nominating regulators of polarization and of age-related phenotypes, and connect context-specific pathways to autoimmune risk loci — directly relevant now that BCMA×CD3 and CD19-directed molecules are moving into autoimmune indications. As a resource it is a map of which intracellular nodes a future armed or costimulatory engager would want to move, and in which cell state moving them will actually do something.

DICE score: 5 — The first genome-scale perturb-seq in primary human CD4 T cells, establishing that the regulatory networks engagers recruit are strongly stimulation-context-dependent, and delivering a foundational resource linking T cell programs to human immune traits.

Marson lab (Gladstone-UCSF Institute of Genomic Immunology) with Satpathy and Pritchard labs, Stanford.

### 2. A Drug-Gated, Modular STAb-T Immunotherapy With External Control

Luengo-Arias S, Domínguez-Alonso C, Zagorac I, Tapia-Galisteo A, García-Veros E, Rubio-Pérez L, et al.

*Advanced science (Weinheim, Baden-Wurttemberg, Germany)*

[10.1002/advs.77236](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77236)

Split CD19 × CD3 engager: two separately secreted, individually inactive antibody modules that heterodimerize into a functional bispecific extracellularly via a rapalog-controlled FKBP–FRB* switch, expressed from a 2A bicistronic construct in STAb-T cells.

The clever move here is *where* the control point sits. Existing switchable engager designs gate transcription, gate the cell, or mask a paratope; this one splits the molecule itself, so the two halves can be secreted continuously and the drug decides whether they ever become an engager. That puts the assembly step outside the cell, in the tumor microenvironment, where the therapeutic interface actually forms — and it makes the OFF state a property of stoichiometry rather than of promoter leakiness, which is why the reported OFF-state behavior is stringent rather than merely low. Drug administration quantitatively regulated T cell activation and cytotoxicity in vitro, and systemic rapalog induced on-demand antitumor activity in xenografts without treatment-related toxicity, with the control demonstrated as reversible rather than one-way. Extension to an EGFR-targeting engager establishes that the switch is orthogonal to the targeting arm, which is the claim that makes this a platform rather than a construct. The unresolved questions are the usual ones for chemically induced dimerization in vivo — rapalog pharmacokinetics setting the temporal resolution of control, and whether extracellular assembly efficiency at physiological secreted concentrations holds up outside a xenograft — but as an answer to the therapeutic-window problem this is architecturally distinct from affinity de-tuning and worth watching.

DICE score: 4 — A genuinely novel engager control architecture that moves the gate from expression to extracellular assembly, demonstrated reversibly in vivo and shown to be modular across two antigen specificities.

Álvarez-Vallina group, CNIO / Hospital 12 de Octubre / Hospital del Mar Research Institute Barcelona.

### 3. A Generalization of the Ternary Binding Model to Membrane-Confined Systems With Finite Copy Number

Bellout H, Li A, Bottino D, Piatkov K

*CPT: pharmacometrics & systems pharmacology*

[10.1002/psp4.70323](https://doi.org/10.1002/psp4.70323)

Reformulation of the Douglass ternary binding model for BiTE trimer formation, replacing bulk solution volume with a coarse-grained reactive contact volume set by synapse geometry and microvillus topology, plus a stochastic chemical-master-equation extension.

This is a modelling paper that quietly invalidates a design intuition the field has been running on. The standard ternary binding model assumes a well-mixed 3D solution, and under that assumption the system sits in the affinity-limited regime where antigen density is *mathematically inert* — the model predicts the same dose–response no matter how much target the tumor cell expresses. That is plainly at odds with the experimental record, and the authors show why: the productive trimer does not form in bulk, it forms in a nanoscale membrane contact with finite receptor copy numbers. Substituting a confinement-derived contact volume shifts effective antigen concentration by six orders of magnitude and restores antigen density as a governing variable. They then propose a replacement metric — absolute formation dose, the concentration required to produce a fixed number of ternary complexes sufficient for activation — instead of the conventional per-line normalized EC50, which erases exactly the density dependence confinement rescues. Using blinatumomab against NALM-6 versus HAL-01, the framework predicts a dose separation that the bulk formulation is structurally incapable of seeing. For anyone tuning a CD3 arm against a tumor arm and reasoning about target-density thresholds from cell-line EC50s, this argues the standard analysis has been answering a different question than the one being asked, and it keeps the original algebra so the correction is adoptable rather than a rewrite.

DICE score: 4 — Identifies a structural failure of the ternary binding model as universally applied to T cell engagers, supplies a confinement-corrected replacement that restores antigen density as a design variable, and proposes a concrete metric to replace normalized EC50.

Takeda Development Center Americas (quantitative systems pharmacology).

### 4. Tisa-cel and axi-cel CAR structure influences the development of resistance to CD19-CAR-T therapy

Krawczyk M, Fidyt K, Fernandez-Fuentes N, Winiarski T, Pepek M, Bousquets-Muñoz P, et al.

*Leukemia*

[10.1038/s41375-026-03081-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41375-026-03081-3)

Same FMC63 binder, two receptor architectures — CD8α hinge/TM with 4-1BB versus CD28 hinge/TM with CD28 — compared head to head for the antigen-loss escape they select for.

Antigen loss is usually discussed as a property of the tumor and the target, and this paper shows it is substantially a property of the *receptor architecture bolted onto an identical paratope*. Repeated exposure to 4-1BB-based CD19 CAR-T cells drove FMC63-epitope loss and total CD19 protein loss; the CD28-based counterparts, targeting the same epitope with the same scFv, did not. The escape mechanism is genetically specific — aberrant splicing, loss of heterozygosity, and frameshift/missense mutations concentrated in the exons encoding the FMC63 epitope — and mathematical modelling attributes the difference to incomplete clearance of CD19-low cells by the 4-1BB construct, which then permits resistant clones to expand. For engager work the transferable claim is uncomfortable and useful: escape is selected by the *efficiency and kinetics of killing at low antigen density*, not only by binding specificity, so two molecules aimed at the same epitope can have materially different resistance profiles depending on how hard they push the effector. The paper's second finding is an immediately actionable diagnostic caveat — commonly used anti-CD19 clinical antibodies read epitopes distinct from FMC63 and will score escape variants as CD19-positive. Any engager program tracking antigen loss with a non-paratope-matched detection reagent is measuring the wrong thing.

DICE score: 4 — Establishes receptor architecture, not just the binding domain, as a determinant of antigen-loss escape with the same paratope, and exposes a detection artifact that likely affects how antigen loss is being reported across the field.

Winiarska lab, Mossakowski Medical Research Institute / Medical University of Warsaw, with Josep Carreras Leukaemia Research Institute and CHOP.

### 5. DyAb: sequence-based antibody design and property prediction in a low-data regime

Lin JY, Hofmann JL, Leaver-Fay A, Liang WC, Vasilaki S, Lee E, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2717460](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2717460)

Pairwise affinity-difference model on a pretrained protein language model backbone, used both as a ranker over mutation combinations and as the fitness function inside a genetic algorithm; validated on anti-EGFR, anti-IL-6, and an internal target.

The design choice worth stealing is the reframing of the prediction target. Absolute binding affinity is hard to learn from a hundred measurements; *differences* between pairs are much easier, because the pairwise construction multiplies the effective training set and cancels much of the assay-to-assay offset that poisons small campaign datasets. DyAb reaches Spearman ρ up to 0.85 on affinity prediction across three antigens from as few as 100 training points, and in generative mode produces variants with high binding rates including designs better than the lead by more than ten-fold. This lands in exactly the regime last week's AIntibody benchmark identified as the tractable one — local optimization of an arm you already have, with real measurements in the loop — rather than the de novo generation that benchmark found unreliable. For multispecific programs the relevance is direct and narrow: co-tuning a tumor arm against a CD3 arm means many small affinity adjustments evaluated against a hundred-odd data points per iteration, and a method that ranks candidates well in that regime is worth more than a model that needs thousands of examples. The caveat is that ranking within a mutational neighborhood is a much easier problem than cross-scaffold generalization, and nothing here claims otherwise.

DICE score: 4 — A well-motivated pairwise-difference formulation that makes antibody affinity optimization work at the data scale real discovery campaigns actually operate at, with ten-fold improvements over lead in prospective design.

Prescient Design and Antibody Engineering, Genentech.

### 6. A synthetic NKG2A engager enables long-term persistence of HLA-deficient allogeneic engineered Tregs

Guo T, Epstein KR, Hoover ME, Zammit NW, Yam JY, Hussell S, et al.

*EMBO molecular medicine*

[10.1038/s44321-026-00507-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44321-026-00507-4)

Cell-surface-displayed synthetic NKG2A engager selectively inhibiting NKG2A⁺ NK cells while avoiding NKG2C⁺ activation; delivered on HLA-ablated engineered Tregs via a clinically compatible dual-AAV construct.

Here the engager format is used defensively, and the engineering problem is receptor discrimination rather than affinity. The natural ligand HLA-E is the obvious way to protect HLA-null allogeneic cells from NK rejection, but it engages the activating NKG2C receptor as well as the inhibitory NKG2A, so it protects against one NK subset while recruiting another — a specificity failure, not a potency failure. The synthetic engager reported here separates those two receptors, and the functional payoff is large: HLA-deficient engineered Tregs carrying it resisted NK-mediated killing better than previously reported inhibitory strategies and persisted up to 12 weeks in humanized mice, while unprotected cells were rapidly rejected. Packaging it into a dual-AAV construct without disturbing Treg identity or suppressive function is the part that makes it a candidate rather than a demonstration. The generalizable lesson for engager designers is about paralog discrimination in a receptor family where the activating and inhibitory members share a ligand — the same problem shows up when selecting a costimulatory arm or when a tumor antigen has a close normal-tissue homolog, and the solution here is a designed binder that reads the difference the natural ligand cannot.

DICE score: 4 — Solves the NKG2A/NKG2C discrimination problem that has limited HLA-E-based immune evasion, with 12-week persistence in humanized models and a clinically packageable dual-AAV format.

GentiBio, Cambridge MA; preclinical, allogeneic Treg program.

### 7. A binding-to-release strategy for targeted anticancer drug delivery

Wen Z, Xu M, Yan Z, Kong Z, Wang Y, Liu P, et al.

*Nature*

[10.1038/s41586-026-10971-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10971-0)

PhoPEx (phosphorus(V)–phenol exchange), a SuFEx-inspired chemistry that positions an electrophile for cleavage by a nucleophilic residue inside the target's binding pocket, releasing payload on binding rather than on internalization; demonstrated on FAP, PD-L1, and an mRNA-display-derived FAP peptide.

Roughly 10% of surface targets internalize well enough to support conventional payload release, which is why the ADC and small-molecule–drug-conjugate fields keep returning to the same short list of antigens. This decouples release from endocytosis entirely: the binding event itself triggers cleavage, using a nucleophilic residue already present in the binding pocket as the catalyst. The numbers are the argument — a FAP binding-to-release conjugate delivered 5.9-fold higher intratumoral MMAE exposure than its internalization-dependent counterpart, matching an internalizing ADC while achieving tumour-to-blood ratios 14.7-fold and 3.6-fold better than the ITR small-molecule conjugate and ITR ADC respectively, and tumour-to-liver ratios better by a factor of ~55 against both. Improved biodistribution raised the maximum tolerated dose and produced near-complete regressions. The relevance to engager work is indirect but real in two directions. First, it widens the addressable target space to non-internalizing surface antigens — the same category engagers occupy by necessity, since an engager wants its target to stay on the membrane. Second, it is a plausible route to arming an engager scaffold with a payload released at the tumor surface rather than requiring uptake, which no current engager format does. The chemistry requires a suitably positioned nucleophile in the pocket, so target scope is a genuine open question.

DICE score: 4 — A mechanistically novel release paradigm that removes internalization as a prerequisite for payload delivery, with large, well-quantified biodistribution and therapeutic-index gains and demonstrated generality across two targets and two binder modalities.

Zhibo Liu group, College of Chemistry and Molecular Engineering, Peking University.

### 8. CD19 CAR T cell therapy for treatment-refractory seropositive rheumatoid arthritis: a phase 1 trial

Albach FN, Rehm MC, Hütter-Krönke ML, Le TH, Giezen JM, Torgutalp M, et al.

*Nature medicine*

[10.1038/s41591-026-04603-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04603-3)

Mivocabtagene autoleucel, an autologous fully human CD19 CAR T product, single infusion after lymphodepletion in six ACPA-positive treatment-refractory RA patients; phase 1 portion of the phase 1/2 COMPARE trial.

The autoimmune reset thesis keeps being tested in diseases where B cell depletion already works reasonably well; RA is the harder and more informative case, because ACPA-positive disease has a long-lived autoreactive plasma cell compartment and patients here had failed everything. All six patients developed CRS, but it stayed grade 1–2, no ICANS occurred, and the single dose-limiting toxicity (grade 3 transaminase elevation) resolved — a safety profile in the range where the intervention is arguable for a non-malignant indication. Efficacy was consistent rather than spectacular: CD19⁺ B cell depletion across blood and tissue, continuous decline of autoantibodies with seroconversion in four of six for anti-MCV and five of six for rheumatoid factor IgM, and improvement in disease activity in all patients (median 34% DAS28-CRP reduction) despite complete withdrawal of DMARDs. The engager-relevant read is the benchmark it sets. CD19- and BCMA-directed T cell engagers are being positioned as the off-the-shelf alternative to exactly this procedure, and they will be judged against depth of tissue depletion and durability of seroconversion, not against peripheral B cell counts. Six patients with 36–52 weeks of follow-up is a small dataset, and how much of the benefit requires the lymphodepletion that engagers do not deliver remains the open question.

DICE score: 4 — Extends CAR T cell autoimmune reset to refractory seropositive RA with tolerable safety and tissue-level depletion plus serological conversion, setting the efficacy bar that CD19- and BCMA-directed engagers entering autoimmunity will be measured against.

Krönke group, Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin; NCT registered as the COMPARE phase 1/2 trial.

### 9. Balancing speed and separation: A high-throughput native mass spectrometry platform for screening complex bispecific antibody variants

Spitler K, Stepurska K, Liu YD, Legg K, Hayes S, Lippold S, et al.

*Talanta*

[10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130523](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130523)

Semi-automated 96-well SEC–native MS platform with liquid-handler sample prep, comparing high-throughput SEC-MS against online buffer exchange MS for detection of light-chain mispairing and misassembled species in 2+1, 1+1+1, and conventional bispecific formats.

Chain mispairing is the tax every multispecific program pays, and the reason it is usually detected late is that native SEC-MS — the assay that actually resolves it — is slow, manual, and operator-variable enough that nobody runs it on a whole clone panel. This paper attacks the throughput rather than the chemistry, and the result is a four-fold compression of end-to-end timeline, from multiple days to 24 hours per 96-sample batch, with automated prep matching manual reproducibility and no meaningful intraplate positional or concentration effects. The methodological finding worth internalizing is that the two fast chromatography strategies are not interchangeable: HT-SEC-MS tracks benchmark SEC-MS closely enough for quantitative use, while online buffer exchange is faster but suited to triage — rapidly eliminating clones with high mispair burden rather than quantifying the ones that survive. That is a sensible two-tier assay design, and it maps onto how format screening actually proceeds. For a group evaluating many arm pairings and linker geometries, moving misassembly detection from a late characterization step to a screening-stage readout changes which formats get discarded and when, and the paper reports it on exactly the higher-order architectures (2+1, 1+1+1) where mispairing combinatorics get worst.

DICE score: 3 — Turns native-state misassembly analysis from a slow characterization assay into a screening-throughput readout for higher-order multispecifics, with a well-characterized quantitative-versus-triage distinction between the two fast methods.

Analytical Development, Genentech/Roche.

### 10. Efficient brain delivery and sustained retention of antibodies via dual targeting of blood-brain barrier and intra-brain proteins

Noguchi Y, Hironiwa N, Sato K, Yamazaki M, Kawashima M, Nagaya N, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2724587](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2724587)

Bispecific antibodies pairing a BBB-transport arm (transferrin receptor or IGF-1R) with an intra-brain retention arm against MOG, CADM3, or CSPG5; applied to delivery of neprilysin as a payload.

Transferrin receptor shuttles solved brain entry and left brain *residence* unsolved — shuttled antibodies cross and then clear, so exposure is a spike rather than a plateau. The design here treats those as two separable engineering problems assigned to two arms: one arm gets the molecule across, the other anchors it to an abundant parenchymal protein once inside. The pharmacokinetics support the premise strongly — the anti-MOG/TfR bispecific reached higher Cmax and maintained higher brain concentrations for four months than either monospecific parent, with 3D imaging and immunohistochemistry showing distribution throughout the whole brain parenchyma rather than perivascular trapping. Extending it to deliver neprilysin, and showing enhanced pharmacodynamic effect and not merely enhanced exposure, is what elevates this above a biodistribution result. The transferable principle for multispecific design is that a second arm can be spent on residence time rather than on a second signal, and that the anchoring target's job is abundance and tissue restriction rather than function. CNS engagers are a small field for good reasons, but the same logic applies anywhere a compartment is easier to enter than to stay in, and a four-month retention window is a different pharmacological object than anything current engager formats produce.

DICE score: 3 — A clean two-arm decomposition of brain delivery into transport and retention with striking four-month exposure data and a demonstrated payload application, though confined to CNS targets and murine pharmacokinetics.

Research Division, Chugai Pharmaceutical Co., Yokohama.

### 11. Epcoritamab with rituximab and lenalidomide as first-line treatment for follicular lymphoma (EPCORE NHL-2): an open-label, multicentre, phase 1/2b trial

Falchi L, Vermaat JSP, Brody JD, Leslie L, Offner F, Christensen JH, et al.

*The Lancet. Haematology*

[10.1016/S2352-3026(26)00198-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-3026(26)00198-5)

CD3 × CD20 bispecific given subcutaneously with two-step-up priming (0.16 mg, then 0.8 mg, then 48 mg) on top of rituximab-lenalidomide, in 41 previously untreated grade 1–3A follicular lymphoma patients across 20 centres.

This is the front-line move, and it puts an engager into a setting where the tolerance for toxicity is far lower than in relapsed disease and where the competing regimen is already chemotherapy-free and effective. Three-year activity and safety data from arm 6 is a longer window than most engager combination reports offer, which is what makes it worth reading rather than another response-rate announcement. Two design points carry over to engineering. First, the two-step-up subcutaneous priming schedule is now doing real work — it is the mechanism by which a full 48 mg dose becomes administrable in an outpatient front-line population, and it is a dosing-architecture answer to a problem that molecular engineering (CD3 affinity de-tuning, masking) is also trying to solve; the two approaches are substitutes competing for the same margin. Second, epcoritamab is being layered on rituximab, so the CD20 arm is competing for target with a co-administered anti-CD20 antibody — the same competition question glofitamab plus G/R-CHOP raised last week, now answered again in a treatment-naive population. Programs whose engager shares an antigen with their own backbone antibody now have two independent datasets suggesting the competition is survivable at clinical doses.

DICE score: 3 — Mature front-line combination data for a subcutaneous CD20 × CD3 engager in a chemotherapy-free regimen, with a step-up dosing schedule and target-competition result that both generalize beyond this molecule.

Genmab/AbbVie-sponsored; NCT04663347, arm 6.

### 12. Efficacy of the Bispecific Anti-BAFF/IL-21 Antibody CZ105 Bis2 in Models of Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

Feng X, Long D, Yin H, Li L, Wang Z, Zhao J, et al.

*Arthritis & rheumatology (Hoboken, N.J.)*

[10.1002/art.70310](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.70310)

Bispecific built by fusing the variable domains of a humanized anti-IL-21 antibody (h18B10) to belimumab, giving simultaneous BAFF and IL-21 blockade in a single molecule.

The construction is the least novel thing about this paper and the rationale is the most interesting. BAFF blockade with belimumab is approved and leaves 30–40% of SLE patients under-responding; IL-21 blockade alone has repeatedly underperformed in trials. The hypothesis is that neither pathway is individually sufficient because both feed the same B cell effector program from different directions, and that a single molecule blocking both should be superadditive rather than merely additive. The preclinical package supports it about as far as preclinical work can: binding and neutralization equal or better than each parent, stronger suppression of in vivo B cell effector function than either selective inhibitor, reduced antigen-specific T-dependent B cell responses in the KLH model, and reduced anti-dsDNA titres, proteinuria, and renal inflammatory infiltration in both pristane-induced and humanized chronic-GVHD lupus models. What makes it worth a slot in an engager digest is the target-selection logic rather than the format, which is a conventional variable-domain fusion. Cytokine-axis bispecifics and CD3 engagers are now converging on the same autoimmune indications from opposite mechanisms — pathway blockade versus cell depletion — and the strategic question for anyone building a BCMA×CD3 or CD19×CD3 molecule for autoimmunity is whether depletion is worth its cytokine release burden when a non-depleting bispecific reaches comparable serological endpoints in the same models.

DICE score: 3 — A clearly reasoned dual-pathway bispecific addressing a documented gap in approved SLE therapy, with consistent efficacy across two lupus models and both parent-molecule comparators, though the format itself is conventional and the data entirely preclinical.

Institute of Dermatology, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College, Nanjing.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Benchmarking antibody-antigen co-folding on human monomeric antigens

Park M, Nett R, Petersen B, Sivasubramanian A

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.20.745880](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.745880)

HuMonoAg-Bench: 412 experimentally determined antibody complexes with human monomeric antigens, 134 released after a uniform 30 September 2021 training cutoff, used to evaluate ten co-folding protocols.

Arriving a week after AIntibody set the standard for prospective evaluation of AI antibody design, this does the equivalent job for structure prediction, and it is similarly disciplined about what counts as evidence — a uniform training cutoff, a post-cutoff holdout, and a check that performance does not depend on having a close pre-cutoff homolog. The finding is a genuine but bounded advance: the most recent methods produce medium-or-better top-ranked models (DockQ ≥ 0.49) for roughly half of post-cutoff Fv complexes with no templates and no experimental restraints, and the gains trace primarily to better CDRH3 modelling rather than to anything about the antigen or the other five loops. Three results are directly actionable for engager work. Supplying true epitope residues as an idealized constraint lifts older methods by 20–30 percentage points, so epitope information is worth more than a model upgrade — an argument for spending experimental effort on epitope mapping before spending it on prediction. Failures are dominated by sampling the wrong binding mode rather than misranking, and more seeds shift the bottleneck toward ranking, which changes how compute should be allocated. And ensembling multiple methods adds only modest coverage over the single best, so the popular consensus strategy is not buying much. Half of complexes still unsolved, with no single structural property explaining which ones.

DICE score: 4 — A carefully constructed, time-split benchmark that quantifies genuine recent progress in antibody-antigen co-folding while localizing the remaining failure to CDRH3 modelling and binding-mode sampling, from a group with no model of its own to promote.

Adimab, LLC.

### 2. All-by-All Cytokine Receptor Pairing Network Unlocks Coding of Non-Natural T Cell States

Tao P, Tsui KCY, Zhao Y, Jiang H, Good Z, Garcia KC

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.19.745857](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.19.745857)

Double-orthogonal receptor platform enforcing every pairing across the ~36 JAK-associated human cytokine receptors — more than 1,200 combinations against the ~30–40 that exist in nature — with STAT-level and phenotypic readout.

Evolution sampled about 3% of the available cytokine receptor pairing space, and this asks what is in the other 97%. The answer is that the unexplored region contains T cell states that are not interpolations of the parental receptors: a synthetic IL-21R>IL-2Rβ pairing drove a cytotoxic Tc17-like state that neither parent predicts, and — the detail that makes this a design principle rather than a catalogue — its reciprocal orientation did not, so which receptor occupies which position encodes signaling specificity. A second result separates quantity from identity: natural IL-9R/γc produced a Tc1 fate despite STAT activation similar to pairings that did not, meaning the *combinatorics* of STAT activation, not its magnitude, sets fate. Recombining IL-31R, a receptor T cells do not normally express, with STAT-biased partners generated several states with superior antitumor efficacy. For engager designers this is a map of destinations rather than a deliverable — a soluble bispecific cannot install a synthetic receptor pair in a recruited T cell. But it defines what an ideal recruited-effector phenotype could be, and it makes the case that the costimulatory and cytokine-mimetic arms now appearing on trispecifics are aiming at a target space far larger and more structured than the handful of natural cytokine axes they currently borrow from.

DICE score: 4 — Exhaustively maps a combinatorial space evolution left almost entirely unexplored, establishes pairing orientation and STAT combinatorics rather than magnitude as the code for T cell fate, and produces non-natural states with superior antitumor activity.

Garcia lab, Stanford University.

### 3. A Mammalian High-Throughput Screen for AI-Designed Peptide-Guided Protein Degraders

Zhao L, Mattix A, Pal A, Chen T, Vincoff S, Hong L, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.24.746873](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.24.746873)

Doxycycline-inducible ubiquibodies fusing protein-language-model-generated peptide guides to the CHIPΔTPR E3 ligase domain, screened as pooled libraries in human reporter cell lines by FACS and guide sequencing.

The methodological point generalizes well beyond degraders: this is generative binder design closed against *functional* selection in human cells, rather than against display in yeast or phage and a downstream hope that the domain behaves in mammalian context. That is the failure mode the Bonn minibinder paper documented last week — binders that work as soluble reagents and fail when moved into the format you need — and screening in the deployment context from the start is the structurally correct answer to it. The validation set is unusually demanding: β-catenin (with Wnt signalling as an orthogonal readout), GFAP in U251 glioblastoma, the EWS::FLI1 fusion oncoprotein with EWSAT1 suppression and apoptosis, and endogenously tagged GATA2 under native genomic regulation. Hitting an intracellular fusion oncoprotein and an endogenously regulated transcription factor is meaningfully harder than hitting an overexpressed reporter. For engager programs the direct relevance is limited — the payload is intracellular and the guides are peptides, not paratopes — but the platform shape is the one worth copying: language-model generation, pooled mammalian library, functional FACS selection, sequence recovery. Applied to surface-antigen binders in a bispecific context, the same loop would test format compatibility and binding in a single experiment.

DICE score: 3 — Couples generative peptide design to pooled functional selection in human cells across four demanding targets, establishing a screening architecture that tests binders in their deployment context rather than in a display surrogate.

Chatterjee lab, University of Pennsylvania, with CHOP and Wisconsin.

### 4. CellCage™ Technology Enables High-throughput Isolation of Defined Cell Combinations to Resolve the Determinants of CAR-T mediated Cytotoxicity

Yau RG, Deshmukh S, Sacca M, Gupta R, Yang Y, Mohseni M, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.21.746043](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746043)

Spatially controlled photopolymerization forming individual enclosures around defined effector:target combinations, with longitudinal time-lapse imaging across thousands of tracked enclosures; anti-CD19 CAR-T on NALM6 and AIC100 anti-ICAM-1 CAR-T on HeLa.

Bulk cytotoxicity assays report a population average and hide the distribution, which matters because the distribution is where the biology is. Isolating defined effector:target combinations and watching them resolves what an EC50 cannot: individual CD8⁺ effectors vary substantially in potency, and serial killing capacity, contact dynamics, and intrinsic motility each independently correlate with that potency — three separable properties, not one. Two population-level findings have direct engager implications. Cytotoxic efficacy scaled with effector number in a way that was not simply additive, and CD4⁺ helper cells augmented CD8⁺ killing dose-dependently, giving direct single-cell evidence for cooperativity during clearance. In the solid-tumor model the cooperativity requirement was absolute — single AIC100 effectors could not kill at all, and only multi-effector enclosures showed robust cytotoxicity scaling with both effector abundance and CD4:CD8 ratio. Engagers recruit polyclonal T cells at whatever local effector:target ratio the tumor permits, so a killing mechanism that requires cooperation between multiple effectors and a specific CD4:CD8 composition is a much harder pharmacological target than one-cell-one-kill, and it argues that effector density at the tumor, not just molecule concentration, is the variable to engineer against in solid tumors.

DICE score: 3 — Resolves CAR-T killing into separable single-cell determinants and demonstrates a hard cooperativity requirement in a solid-tumor model, with direct implications for how effector density governs redirected killing, though it is a platform paper from the instrument vendor.

Cellanome, with Weill Cornell (Mason and Jin labs).

### 5. Cytoskeletal engineering through Formin-like 1 overexpression enhances T cell infiltration and antitumor potency in solid tumors

Chung JW, Olivas-Corral J, Wood AM, Solis H, Sigler AL, Ning E, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.20.744715](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.744715)

FMNL1 overexpression in TILs and CAR-T cells as a cytoskeletal engineering strategy for migration through dense stroma; melanoma and lung carcinoma models.

Solid-tumor failure is usually attributed to antigen heterogeneity, exhaustion, or the immunosuppressive microenvironment, and comparatively little effort goes to the purely mechanical problem — collagen-dense matrix and abnormal vasculature physically excluding effectors. This targets that directly, overexpressing a formin the same group previously showed to be required for T cell extravasation and migration through restrictive environments. FMNL1 overexpression increased migration through restrictive transwell pores without impairing reactivation or cytotoxic function, and in melanoma and lung carcinoma models chosen for poor baseline effector infiltration, FMNL1-overexpressing TILs and CAR-T cells accumulated at tumor sites at significantly higher levels than controls, translating into slowed tumor growth and prolonged survival across multiple melanoma models. The engager relevance is inverted from most cell-therapy work and worth stating plainly: an engager cannot install a transgene in the T cells it recruits, so if effector exclusion is substantially mechanical then engagers are structurally disadvantaged in stroma-dense tumors in a way no amount of affinity tuning addresses. That argues either for engager–cell-therapy combinations where the engineered cells carry the migration module, or for stromal-remodeling combinations. It also identifies migration capacity as a variable that pharmacological approaches have essentially no handle on.

DICE score: 3 — Establishes cytoskeletal engineering as a distinct and effective lever for solid-tumor T cell infiltration with clean in vivo efficacy, and by construction highlights a mechanical barrier that soluble engager formats cannot address.

Jacobelli lab, University of Colorado School of Medicine.

## Weekly Themes

Last week the field got a hard recalibration of what computational design can deliver; this week it got two useful corrections about what the *models* underneath everyday design decisions have been quietly getting wrong. The Takeda ternary binding paper is the sharper of the two. The standard Douglass model, applied as written, places T cell engagers in an affinity-limited regime where antigen density is mathematically inert — the model literally cannot distinguish a high-density from a low-density target line — because it assumes trimer formation in well-mixed bulk solution rather than in a nanoscale membrane contact with finite receptor copy numbers. Correcting for confinement moves effective antigen concentration by six orders of magnitude and restores target density as a governing variable, and the proposed replacement metric, absolute formation dose, exposes a density-driven dose separation between NALM-6 and HAL-01 that per-line normalized EC50 is structurally incapable of showing. Anyone who has reasoned about target-density thresholds from cell-line potency curves should read it as a warning about the analysis, not just the molecule. Adimab's co-folding benchmark performs the same service for structure prediction with a clean time-split holdout: real progress, roughly half of post-cutoff complexes solved to medium accuracy without restraints, gains traceable specifically to CDRH3 modelling — and three findings that redirect effort, namely that true epitope constraints buy more (20–30 points) than a model upgrade, that failures are sampling failures rather than ranking failures until you add seeds, and that method ensembling adds almost nothing. DyAb is the constructive companion, showing that reframing the prediction target from absolute affinity to pairwise differences makes affinity optimization work at the hundred-datapoint scale real campaigns operate at, with better-than-ten-fold improvements over lead — squarely inside the local-optimization regime AIntibody identified as tractable, and making no claim beyond it.

The second thread is architecture as a determinant of resistance, which is a more uncomfortable idea than target selection as a determinant of resistance. The Warsaw group's head-to-head comparison holds the FMC63 paratope fixed and varies only hinge, transmembrane, and costimulatory domains, and finds that the 4-1BB construct selects for CD19 epitope loss while the CD28 construct does not — with modelling attributing the difference to incomplete clearance of antigen-low cells rather than to anything about binding. Same binder, same epitope, different escape landscape. Their second finding is a field-wide measurement problem: the standard clinical anti-CD19 antibodies read epitopes other than FMC63 and will call escape variants antigen-positive. Running alongside this, several groups spent the week attacking exposure and control rather than affinity. The Madrid STAb-T platform splits a CD19 × CD3 engager into two individually inert secreted modules that assemble only in the presence of a rapalog, putting the gate on extracellular assembly rather than on transcription and making the OFF state a matter of stoichiometry; GentiBio's synthetic NKG2A engager solves a paralog-discrimination problem HLA-E cannot, protecting HLA-null allogeneic Tregs for twelve weeks; Chugai spends a second arm on brain *retention* rather than a second signal and gets four months of parenchymal exposure; and the Peking University binding-to-release chemistry decouples payload release from internalization entirely, which widens the addressable target space to precisely the non-internalizing surface antigens engagers already live on.

Underneath, the week's infrastructure was unusually strong and pointed in a consistent direction: measure the effector, not just the molecule. Marson's genome-scale perturb-seq across 22 million primary human CD4 T cells is the largest single contribution, and its central finding — that active regulators and the programs they control change dramatically between resting and stimulated states — reframes heterogeneous engager response as a property of the effector compartment rather than of exposure. Garcia's all-by-all cytokine receptor pairing network shows that evolution sampled roughly 3% of the available JAK-STAT combinatorial space and that the remainder contains emergent T cell states, with pairing orientation and STAT combinatorics rather than magnitude doing the encoding — a map of destinations for the costimulatory and cytokine-mimetic arms now appearing on trispecifics. Cellanome's single-enclosure assay resolves killing into separable per-cell determinants and finds a hard cooperativity requirement in the solid-tumor model, where single effectors simply cannot kill. And the FMNL1 preprint names the barrier that none of the soluble formats can touch: if exclusion from stroma-dense tumors is substantially mechanical, engagers are structurally disadvantaged there in a way no affinity tuning fixes, which is an argument for combinations rather than for another round of format iteration. Genentech's four-fold compression of native-MS misassembly analysis, finally, is the least glamorous item here and possibly the most immediately useful — it moves chain-mispairing detection from late characterization into screening, which is where higher-order multispecific programs actually need it.
