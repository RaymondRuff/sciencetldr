## Top Papers

### 1. Engineered TYK2 dependence enables myeloid-selective IL-10 receptor agonism

Canaria DA, Nguyen TTT, Huang H, Xu H, Yue L, Fu Y, et al.

*Nature*

[10.1038/s41586-026-11176-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11176-1)

IL-10Rα × IL-10Rβ tetravalent nanobody receptor agonist.

The engineering idea here is one engager designers should steal: cell-type selectivity achieved not by antigen choice but by receptor-crosslinking geometry. IL-10 suppresses myeloid cells but also stimulates T cells, which has sunk it as a drug; the authors found that myeloid and T cells differ in which JAK-family kinase pair they depend on for IL-10 signalling, then built a tetravalent nanobody agonist whose architecture makes signalling strictly TYK2-dependent and steeply sensitive to IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ surface abundance. Cryo-EM shows the designed molecule cross-links the receptor in a geometry unlike native IL-10, and that non-native geometry is what converts a shared receptor into a cell-type-restricted one. For multispecifics this is a receptor-density-gated selectivity mechanism built purely from valency and geometry — the same lever available to a CD3 arm facing the therapeutic-window problem. Caveats: a cytokine receptor agonist is a softer target than a cytotoxic synapse, and the selectivity depends on a kinase-pairing asymmetry that may not have a counterpart in TCR signalling.

DICE score: 5 — Demonstrates that valency and cross-linking geometry alone can impose cell-type selectivity on a broadly expressed receptor, with cryo-EM establishing the mechanism rather than inferring it.

### 2. Orthogonal Fc engineering enables multiplexed delivery of mRNA-encoded bispecific antibodies that constrain viral escape

Wec AZ, Cho A, Pecetta S, Hu X, Bender MF, Laciak AR, et al.

*Science Translational Medicine*

[10.1126/scitranslmed.aeh1292](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aeh1292)

Multiple anti-SARS-CoV-2 bispecific antibodies with orthogonal Fc homodimerization interfaces, delivered as LNP-formulated mRNA.

Chain pairing has always been the tax on multispecific manufacturing, and this paper reframes it as an enabling constraint. Structure-guided computational design produced Fc variants that homodimerize selectively, so several distinct mRNA-encoded bispecifics can be co-delivered in one LNP and assemble in vivo with cognate heavy-chain pairing instead of scrambling into a combinatorial mess. A triple bispecific combination expressed in human FcRn mice gave broad serum neutralization across historical and contemporary variants including the heavily mutated BA.3.2.2 lineage, with titres above 1:10,000 and extended half-life. The transferable content for engager programs is the orthogonal Fc interface set itself, which would let a single encoded dose carry several engager specificities against a heterogeneous tumor without a separate manufacturing run per molecule. Caveats: antiviral bispecifics are a permissive setting — no CD3 arm, no synapse, no CRS risk — and mRNA-encoded engagers face an exposure-control problem that a neutralizing antibody does not.

DICE score: 5 — An orthogonal Fc pairing solution that makes multiplexed in vivo assembly of several distinct bispecifics tractable, validated as a delivered combination rather than as a biophysics result.

### 3. Combating CD24 to overcome CD19 CAR-T resistance via induction of innate and adaptive immunity in B-cell malignancies

Yin Q, Gao J, Huang X, Hu A, Xiong R, Craig JW, et al.

*Leukemia*

[10.1038/s41375-026-03159-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41375-026-03159-y)

CD24 × CD3 bispecific antibody, plus a CD19 CAR-T engineered to secrete a CD24 BiTE.

Antigen loss after CD19 CAR-T is the field's most reliable failure mode, and this paper attacks it with a new target rather than a new format. CD24 is highly expressed on aggressive B-cell malignancies; a CD24 × CD3 bispecific and CXCR3-armed CD24 CAR-T both cleared lymphoma and leukemia models, including models resistant to CD19 CAR-T through antigen loss and apoptosis defects. The engineering payoff is the combination construct — a CD19 CAR-T that secretes a CD24 BiTE, covering two antigens from one cell product and addressing heterogeneity without a dual-CAR architecture. Notably the CD24-directed T cells also reprogram macrophages toward an M1-like phenotype and boost phagocytic clearance, so the mechanism is not purely T-cell cytotoxicity. Caveats: CD24 is expressed on normal B cells and some epithelium, the on-target off-tumor question is not resolved here, and secreted-engager CAR designs carry a known exposure-control problem.

DICE score: 4 — Establishes CD24 as a salvage target for CD19-resistant disease and delivers it in both engager and BiTE-secreting-CAR formats, with a macrophage-repolarizing mechanism beyond direct lysis.

### 4. Bispecific NK-Cell Engager Targeting TP53 Mutation Through Integration of a TCR-mimic Antibody

Wu H, Zhao Z, Zhang L, Xiong X, Yang M, Ge W, et al.

*MedComm*

[10.1002/mco2.71058](https://doi.org/10.1002/mco2.71058)

p53 neoantigen/HLA-A*02:01 × NK-activating receptor bispecific NK cell engager built on a TCR-mimic antibody.

Most NK engagers target lineage antigens and inherit the on-target off-tumor problem that comes with them; this one targets a mutation. The authors built NK cell engagers on TCR-mimic antibodies against a p53 neoantigen presented by HLA-A*02:01, giving tumor-specific rather than tumor-associated targeting. The discovery engineering is the real contribution: a hydrogel-based microwell single-cell chip coupled to T2-cell de-selection pulled the functional TCRm clone C33-Ab out of roughly 500,000 hybridoma cells in eight weeks, which is a throughput and timeline that makes pMHC-directed arms practical rather than heroic. Pairing a TCRm paratope with NK effector engagement also sidesteps the CRS profile of CD3 engagement while keeping MHC-restricted specificity. Caveats: pMHC targets have notoriously low surface copy number, HLA-A*02:01 restriction limits the addressable population, and cross-reactivity against the self-peptide repertoire is the risk that this class lives or dies on.

DICE score: 4 — Couples neoantigen-specific TCR-mimic recognition to NK engagement and contributes a single-cell screening workflow that compresses TCRm discovery to weeks.

### 5. Distinct epitope engagement confers differential activity of linvoseltamab vs teclistamab across BCMA mutations

Zhou Y, Sineshchekova O, Lee K, Doshi A, Brown K, Franklin M, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020381](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020381)

BCMA × CD3 bispecific antibodies, epitope comparison across clinically observed BCMA escape mutations.

This is the paper that turns "pick a different epitope" from advice into data. BCMA mutations found in patients relapsing after teclistamab — R27P, S30del, P34del, plus the germline variant P33S — were expressed on cell lines and challenged with linvoseltamab, which retained binding, NFAT reporter and primary T-cell activation, and cytotoxicity against the mutated BCMA that teclistamab loses. Acquired resistance to an engager is therefore epitope-specific rather than target-specific, and a second molecule against the same antigen can be an active salvage option. For design, it argues for mapping escape mutations before committing to a binding arm, and for building epitope-diverse arm panels against any antigen likely to see sequential engager exposure. Caveats: engineered cell lines rather than patient cells, a small mutation set, and no clinical confirmation that linvoseltamab actually rescues teclistamab-refractory patients.

DICE score: 4 — Shows clinically observed engager resistance mutations are epitope-restricted and that a differently docked arm against the same antigen retains full activity, which is directly actionable for arm selection.

Clinical-stage. Regeneron Pharmaceuticals.

### 6. Ponatinib-Blinatumomab or Imatinib-Chemotherapy for Adult Ph-Positive ALL

Chiaretti S, Piciocchi A, Skert C, Leoncin M, Borlenghi E, Cattaneo C, et al.

*The New England Journal of Medicine*

[10.1056/NEJMoa2606208](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2606208)

CD19 × CD3 tandem scFv engager (blinatumomab) plus ponatinib, randomized frontline, 236 patients.

Randomized evidence that a T cell engager can displace chemotherapy in frontline treatment, which is a different claim from anything in the relapsed/refractory literature the field usually cites. Adults with Ph-positive ALL were randomized 2:1 to ponatinib plus blinatumomab versus imatinib plus chemotherapy: 24-month event-free survival was 86% versus 67%, survival with no measurable residual disease 68% versus 42%, and complete hematologic response at end of induction 94% in the intervention arm. The relevance to engineering is strategic rather than architectural — blinatumomab is a first-generation tandem scFv with a notoriously short half-life and continuous-infusion burden, and it still wins a frontline randomized comparison, which sets the efficacy bar that better-engineered CD19 formats now have to clear on convenience and tolerability rather than on response rate. Caveats: no novel engineering, an open-label design with crossover permitted for MRD persistence, and the TKI partner differs between arms so the engager's contribution is not cleanly separable.

DICE score: 4 — Randomized frontline evidence that an engager-plus-TKI regimen beats chemotherapy on event-free survival and MRD, which resets the comparator for every next-generation CD19 engager.

Clinical-stage. GIMEMA ALL2820 investigators.

### 7. Computational Design of a TCR-like Human V Domain Antibody That Specifically Recognizes a WT1-Derived Peptide Presented by Class I Major Histocompatibility Complex

Oh TS, Kim SW, Ahn S, Suh S, Lee J, Bae Y, et al.

*Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal*

[10.34133/csbj.0236](https://doi.org/10.34133/csbj.0236)

Single-domain human VH TCR-mimic binder against WT1 RMFPNAPYL/HLA-A*02:01, fused to albumin domain III in monovalent and bivalent formats.

Single-domain pMHC binders are the building block multispecific designers want and rarely get, because specificity against a 9-mer displayed in a conserved groove is hard to engineer into one domain. Starting from the crystal structure of a Fab bound to RMF/HLA-A*02, the authors grafted the recognition onto a soluble trastuzumab-derived VH scaffold, producing αRMF_VH, which contacts all nine peptide residues and showed no binding to a control pMHC bearing a different peptide. Fusion to human serum albumin domain III gave monovalent and bivalent constructs with good biophysical behaviour, the monovalent binding at 81 nM, and the same domain functioned as a CAR binding domain on T cells. Peptide-spanning contact plus a clean control-peptide result is the right specificity evidence for this class, and a well-behaved VH module drops straight into a tandem or VHH-style engager. Caveats: 81 nM is modest, specificity was tested against one control pMHC rather than a repertoire-scale panel, and no engager format was built.

DICE score: 3 — Structure-based transplantation of pMHC recognition onto a soluble single VH scaffold, with half-life-extended constructs and CAR function demonstrated, though specificity testing stays narrow.

### 8. Fc-Dependent Targeting of the GAS6-MERTK Axis With Monoclonal and Bispecific Antibodies Overcomes Drug Resistance Across EGFR Mutations in Exons 19, 20, and 21

Giri S, Selvadurai BR, Gupta N, Simoni-Nieves A, Genna A, Lindzen M, et al.

*Advanced Science*

[10.1002/advs.78290](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.78290)

EGFR × MERTK bispecific antibody (Bis-EM), Fc-competent, degradation-inducing.

A bispecific designed to kill a bypass pathway rather than to recruit a T cell, and the mechanism is worth noting because it is one engager programs rarely exploit. Re-analysis of clinical data flagged GAS6 and MERTK upregulation in patients progressing on osimertinib; MERTK ablation re-sensitized cells, implying EGFR–MERTK crosstalk inside caveolae. The resulting Bis-EM drove degradation of both receptors and supported immune-mediated killing, and combined with TKIs suppressed growth of resistant spheroids across exon 19, 20 and 21 EGFR mutations. The engineering point is dual-receptor co-degradation as the effector mechanism — bivalent engagement of two receptors in the same membrane microdomain produces clearance that neither parental antibody achieves, a geometry lesson that applies to any cis-targeting multispecific. Caveats: effects depend on an intact Fc so the contribution of degradation versus ADCC is not fully separated, and the work is spheroid and xenograft stage.

DICE score: 3 — Targets a clinically grounded resistance axis with a bispecific whose mechanism is co-degradation of two receptors in cis, though the Fc dependence leaves mechanism attribution incomplete.

### 9. CD20 bispecific T-cell engager depletes B-cell progenitors preventing EBV-associated lymphomagenesis in humanized mice

Böni M, Schuhmachers P, von Boxberg S, Poon A, Thevenon E, Mennonna D, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2025019375](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2025019375)

CD3 × CD20 bispecific T-cell engager, humanized mouse EBV model.

The finding is about depth of depletion, and it has a clean mechanistic explanation. In humanized mice, a CD3 × CD20 engager outperformed rituximab at preventing systemic EBV spread and tumor formation, and the difference tracks to deep clearance of human precursor B cells in the bone marrow — a compartment rituximab spares. Progenitor B cells turned out to be permissive to EBV infection in vitro and correlated with viral persistence during B-cell depletion in vivo, identifying them as a reservoir from which EBV-associated lymphoproliferative disease can relapse. For engager design this is a concrete statement of what redirected cytotoxicity buys over Fc-mediated clearance: access to a low-antigen-density, effector-poor niche. Caveats: humanized mice reconstitute human myeloid and NK compartments poorly, which structurally disadvantages rituximab's Fc mechanisms, so the margin is likely overstated.

DICE score: 3 — Identifies progenitor B cells as an EBV reservoir and shows engager-mediated killing reaches a bone marrow compartment that antibody-dependent clearance does not.

### 10. MRD negativity by PBMCs and ctDNA confirms deep and durable responses after epcoritamab monotherapy in R/R FL

Altıntaş I, Morehouse C, Favaro E, Rana A, Karavitis J, Szafer-Glusman E, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2025017565](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2025017565)

CD3 × CD20 bispecific antibody (epcoritamab) monotherapy, MRD analysis from EPCORE NHL-1.

Engager trials have lacked an early readout that predicts durability, and this correlative analysis supplies a candidate. In relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma, epcoritamab drove rapid conversion to MRD negativity by clonoSEQ, with most evaluable patients MRD-negative by cycle 3 day 1 on either PBMC or ctDNA analyte. MRD negativity at that landmark predicted prolonged progression-free survival independently of radiographic response, and performance was comparable between the two analytes and held across difficult-to-treat disease features. The practical value for engineering programs is a 6-week pharmacodynamic endpoint usable for dose and schedule optimization, which matters because engager exposure–response curves are often non-monotonic and slow radiographic endpoints make them hard to map. Caveats: correlative analysis of a single-arm trial, MRD-evaluable patients are a selected subset, and the landmark has not been prospectively qualified.

DICE score: 3 — Establishes an early blood-based MRD landmark that predicts durable benefit independently of imaging, giving engager dose-finding a fast pharmacodynamic endpoint.

Clinical-stage. Genmab and AbbVie, EPCORE NHL-1.

### 11. Computational design and translational development of TCR-mimic scFv antibodies for peptide-HLA neoantigen targeting

Niazi SK, Magoola M, Mariam Z

*npj Drug Discovery*

[10.1038/s44386-026-00084-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44386-026-00084-z)

Review of TCR-mimic scFv design against peptide-HLA complexes, spanning CD3 bispecific, CAR and Fc formats.

A review rather than new data, but a well-structured one for anyone building pMHC-directed arms this year, and it pairs usefully with the two TCR-mimic primary papers above. Its organizing move is to separate three questions the field routinely conflates: which mutations are predicted to be presented, which presented complexes are genuinely targetable given surface density and cross-reactivity risk, and how immunogenic the therapeutic molecule will itself be. It then walks computational strategies across target identification, AI structure prediction, de novo design, affinity and specificity engineering, half-life extension and deimmunization, holding the distinction throughout between the scFv as recognition module and the effector architecture — CD3 bispecific, CAR binding domain, or Fc format — it must eventually sit in. That module-versus-architecture framing is the part worth carrying into design reviews. No new experiments.

DICE score: 2 — Useful synthesis that cleanly separates presentation, targetability and immunogenicity for pMHC-directed arms, but contributes no new data.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. T cell engagers convert acute tumor killing into endogenous vaccination

Lecuit MM, Corre B, Garcia Z, Lemaitre F, Vaysse A, Alonso R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.06.756695](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.756695)

Clinical-grade human T cell engagers in an immunocompetent mouse model, intravital imaging plus single-cell transcriptomics.

The long-remission-after-drug-clearance observation has circulated as an anecdote for years; this is a mechanism for it. Intravital two-photon imaging shows a perforin-dependent cytotoxic wave peaking within 24 hours and then subsiding despite continued massive T cell accumulation — so the killing window is short and largely decoupled from T cell numbers. Single-cell transcriptomics and adoptive transfer show memory CD8 T cells transiting a progenitor-like state before becoming recall-competent effectors, and tumor lysis cross-priming genuinely tumor-specific CD8 T cells via XCR1+ cDC1, a step required for durable control and protection on rechallenge. If engager benefit depends on a cDC1 handoff, dosing schedules and combination partners should be designed to protect that cascade rather than to maximize peak cytotoxicity.

DICE score: 4 — Mechanistic account of how engager killing seeds TCR-specific adaptive memory through a cDC1-dependent cross-priming step, with direct implications for schedule design.

Bousso lab, Institut Pasteur.

### 2. Programming Targeted Immune Cell Migration Using Orthogonal Chemokine-Receptor Pairs

Lee J, Srinivasan K, Llinas del Torrent C, Ghose D, Shah DH, Oliva Hernandez L, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.12.758398](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758398)

Structure-guided orthogonal chemokine–receptor pairs with minimal native crosstalk, deployed in engineered T cells.

Trafficking is the unsolved half of the solid tumor problem, and this is a clean protein-design attack on it. Structure-guided design produced chemokine–receptor pairs orthogonal to the native system; T cells carrying the ortho-receptor selectively infiltrated tissues producing the ortho-chemokine in vitro and in vivo. Engineering cells to both sense and secrete the ortho-chemokine then generated collective swarming, with pioneer cells recruiting followers along a self-organized positive-feedback gradient. The engager-relevant reading is that an ortho-chemokine delivered as a tumor-targeted fusion arm could become a recruitment module paired with a cytotoxic arm, giving spatial control that CD3 engagement alone does not provide.

DICE score: 4 — Builds a genuinely orthogonal chemokine signalling channel and uses it to program autonomous spatial targeting and collective recruitment in engineered T cells.

Lim lab (UCSF) with Manglik and Volkman labs.

### 3. Fast receptor-antigen dissociation kinetics promote CAR-T cell tumor infiltration, functional fitness, and antitumor efficacy in solid tumors

Mao R, Shi H, Hussein MS, Karia B, Koomson O, Sadek R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.10.756030](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.10.756030)

EGFR-targeting CARs built from cetuximab and its humanized derivative H1 — same epitope, matched k_on, ~18-fold different dwell time.

A well-controlled test of the affinity-versus-kinetics question, and the control is what makes it worth reading: two binders against the same EGFR epitope with comparable k_on and markedly different k_off, so dwell time varies while epitope and association rate do not. In vitro cytotoxicity was comparable, but under low-dose conditions against large established xenografts the fast-off H1 CAR-T cells showed superior expansion, persistence, tumor infiltration and tumor control, retaining surface CAR and a central-memory-like phenotype, while the slow-off cetuximab CAR-T cells downregulated CAR, upregulated PD-1 and recovered poorly. The argument that CAR binders are kinetically over-stabilized transfers directly to engager arm selection, where dwell time sets serial-killing capacity and the same over-stabilization logic likely applies.

DICE score: 3 — Isolates off-rate from affinity and epitope with a matched binder pair and shows dwell time, not affinity, governs infiltration and persistence in solid tumors.

### 4. Affinity maturation of a single-domain antibody via rational mutagenesis informed by artificial intelligence

Tetteroo SL, Tawiah C, Dekkers G, Pronker MF

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.13.758789](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758789)

Synthetic-library single-domain antibody against the IL-12 p40 subunit, matured by structure-prediction-guided point mutagenesis.

Synthetic libraries give speed and antigen flexibility but no in vivo affinity maturation, leaving low-affinity leads — exactly the gap this tests whether structure prediction can close. The selected sdAb 2F3 was predicted to bind IL-12 p40 at an epitope competing with IL-12Rβ1, the same site targeted by briakinumab and ustekinumab. Eleven point mutations were designed from the predicted complex; three improved affinity and combining them gave an eightfold gain over the lead. The honest scale is modest — eightfold, 3 of 11 designs successful — but the workflow is cheap, needs no library re-screening, and the reported hit rate is the useful number for anyone budgeting a maturation campaign on an engager arm.

DICE score: 3 — Concrete, honestly reported hit rate for prediction-guided rational maturation of a single-domain binder, a directly reusable workflow for engager arm optimization.

Sanquin Blood Supply Foundation, Amsterdam.

### 5. Computational design of protein conformational landscapes for metamorphic behavior

Khramushin A, Elia M, Schellhaas C, Borsatto A, Schmidt J, Chabaan MY, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.13.758820](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758820)

De novo designed metamorphic proteins accessing two alternative conformations with tunable populations.

Generative design has largely produced rigid single-state proteins, which is a poor match for engagers, whose behaviour depends on conformational states — masked prodrug formats, hinge flexibility, allosteric switching. This work tries two sequence-design strategies for proteins that populate two substantially different conformations: traversing sequence space between the target states, and unrestricted redesign from the structural features of each state. Combined with current structure prediction, the designs were characterized and their conformational equilibrium shifted toward either state by introducing state-defining mutations. Designing a population ratio rather than a structure is the capability worth tracking: a protease- or pH-switchable engager that changes conformation rather than merely shedding a mask would be a cleaner conditional-activation mechanism.

DICE score: 3 — Extends de novo design from static structures to controllable two-state conformational equilibria, an early but relevant capability for conditionally activated engager formats.

Correia lab, EPFL.

## Weekly Themes

Geometry did the work this week. The two highest-scoring papers both achieve selectivity or multiplexing through molecular architecture rather than through binding specificity: the Nature IL-10R agonist uses a tetravalent nanobody cross-linking geometry to make signalling kinase-dependent and receptor-density-gated, turning a shared receptor into a myeloid-restricted one, while the orthogonal Fc work makes chain pairing itself the engineered property so several bispecifics can be co-encoded and assemble correctly in vivo. Both are cases where the arms were not the innovation — the way they are held together was. A second thread concerns the binding interface at finer resolution than affinity: the linvoseltamab–teclistamab comparison shows that clinically observed engager resistance is epitope-specific rather than target-specific, and the matched-pair CAR kinetics preprint shows that dwell time, not affinity, governs infiltration and persistence. Together they argue for specifying engager arms by epitope and off-rate rather than by target and K_D, and for mapping escape mutations before committing a binder. Peptide-HLA targeting had an unusually dense week, with a TCR-mimic NK engager against a p53 neoantigen, a computationally designed single-domain WT1 binder, and a review that usefully separates presentation from targetability from immunogenicity — the practical bottleneck is shifting from making pMHC binders to proving their specificity at repertoire scale, which none of the three fully does. Finally, two results reframe what an engager is for: the Pasteur preprint shows the therapeutic benefit may run through a cDC1 cross-priming handoff rather than through sustained direct killing, which would make schedule design a question of protecting a cascade instead of maximizing peak cytotoxicity, and the frontline Ph-positive ALL randomized data confirm that a first-generation tandem scFv can displace chemotherapy outright — raising the bar that better-engineered CD19 formats must now clear on tolerability and convenience rather than on response rate.
