## Top Papers

### 1. Discovery and engineering of bispecific TCR-mimic antibodies targeting peptide-HLA complex

Watkins-Yoon J, Lu W-C, Petrunak EM, Tubo NJ, Halmos S, McConaughy W, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2731282](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2731282)

WT1 peptide-HLA × CD3 TCR-mimic bispecific; end-to-end discovery platform built on pHLA immunogens, fully human transgenic mice, single B-cell screening, and whole-proteome off-target counter-screening.

The interesting claim here is not the molecule but the pipeline, and specifically the part of it that most pHLA programs treat as an afterthought. TCR-mimic antibodies open intracellular antigens to antibody-format targeting, but the reason so few have reached the clinic is that a paratope reading a nine-residue peptide sitting in an HLA groove is exceptionally prone to cross-reacting with unrelated peptides presented on the same allele — the failure mode that killed the MAGE-A3 TCR programs. This platform puts whole-proteome off-target screening (X-Scan) inside the discovery loop rather than downstream of it, combined with high-quality pHLA immunogens and fully human transgenic mice so that binders arrive humanized and developable. Applied to WT1, it produced binders the authors report as outperforming published competitor TCRm antibodies, then reformatted them into CD3 bispecifics with potent redirected killing. For anyone weighing the pHLA target class, this is the first description that treats specificity screening as a design constraint applied during selection instead of a safety report written afterward, which is the change that would actually move the hit rate.

DICE score: 5 — An integrated TCRm discovery platform that folds proteome-wide specificity counter-screening into binder selection, addressing the single failure mode that has kept the pHLA target class out of reach for engagers.

Alloy Therapeutics (Waltham) with the ECLIPSE program at MD Anderson and Immunitrack ApS.

### 2. TCR-JANUS engager proteins enable bispecific targeting to overcome tumor heterogeneity in TCR-T cell therapy

Cheng C, Liang T, Yao P, Wang W, Chen H, Li X, et al.

*Science advances*

[10.1126/sciadv.aed9825](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed9825)

Trop2 × TRBV bispecific engager — the effector arm binds the variable region of a transgenic TCR β-chain rather than CD3, paired with KRAS-G12V-specific TCR-T cells.

Swapping the CD3 arm for one that binds a specific TCR Vβ segment is a genuinely different architecture, and it buys two things at once. Because the engager only engages T cells carrying the transgenic TCR, the redirected killing is restricted to the infused product rather than every T cell in the patient, and because it binds outside the CD3 signaling module, the engaged TCR-T cell retains its native pHLA specificity — so a single cell can recognize KRAS-G12V through its TCR and Trop2 through the engager simultaneously. In heterogeneous tumor models that dual arsenal cleared antigenically divergent populations that either modality alone missed, which is the relapse mechanism TCR-T therapy actually fails on. The most engineering-relevant result is the chronic-stimulation comparison: TCR-JANUS-stimulated cells showed better preserved function and less exhaustion than CD3-engaged controls, suggesting that engaging the TCR through its variable region delivers a qualitatively milder signal than the supraphysiological clustering a CD3 binder imposes. That is a lever on signal strength that does not require affinity de-tuning, and it should generalize to any adoptive product with a defined receptor to target.

DICE score: 5 — Replaces the CD3 arm with a TCR-Vβ-directed arm, producing an engager that is intrinsically restricted to an engineered cell product, preserves the cell's native specificity for dual-antigen recognition, and causes measurably less exhaustion than CD3 engagement.

Zhejiang University School of Medicine with the Institute of Microbiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

### 3. Sequence and structural determinants of efficacious de novo chimaeric antigen receptors

Chow A, Chu H, Li R, Nalbant BN, Dozic AV, Kida LC, et al.

*Nature biomedical engineering*

[10.1038/s41551-026-01790-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-026-01790-9)

1,758 generatively designed de novo protein binders against BCMA, CD19 and CD22, screened as CAR binding domains through protein-binding, T-cell activation and in vivo killing assays.

This is the largest published reality check on AI-designed binders deployed as functional immunotherapy domains rather than characterized as isolated proteins, and the failure taxonomy is the deliverable. Across nearly 1,800 designs the authors isolate three recurring liabilities: tonic signalling, engagement of occluded epitopes that never becomes productive in a cell-cell context, and off-target activity — none of which show up in the biochemical assays that generative design pipelines currently optimize against. The scale matters because it converts anecdote into base rates: a binder that looks excellent by affinity and structure prediction can still be useless as a receptor arm for reasons that are geometric and contextual rather than thermodynamic. They then develop computational and experimental heuristics to fix these, notably screening sequence variants around a single parental structure to keep on-target activation while shedding liabilities, which is closer to affinity maturation than to de novo generation. The read-across to engagers is direct and slightly uncomfortable: a CD3 or CD16 arm sits in the same synaptic geometry, and tonic signalling and epitope occlusion are exactly the properties that separate a functional engager arm from a good binder.

DICE score: 4 — Establishes empirical failure modes and base rates for generatively designed binders used as functional immune-receptor arms, at a scale that turns the gap between in silico binder quality and cellular function into something you can design around.

Lareau lab, Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center.

### 4. A bispecific CD3×CD19 antibody for systemic lupus erythematosus: a phase 1 trial

Xu J, Mei C, Guan X, Du R, Wu B, Wu D, et al.

*Nature medicine*

[10.1038/s41591-026-04572-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04572-7)

A-319, a CD3 × CD19 T cell engager; 12 patients with active SLE, intravenous arm, 52 weeks of follow-up.

The efficacy numbers are striking — 80% reaching Lupus Low Disease Activity State and 60% reaching DORIS remission at 12 months — but the result that changes the engineering calculus is the safety and the dosing. A-319 was given at 0.3–1.2 µg/kg after a 0.05 µg/kg priming week, with no treatment-related serious adverse events, no grade ≥3 CRS or neurotoxicity, and minimal haematologic toxicity. That is roughly three orders of magnitude below oncology CD19 engager exposures, and it works because the target B-cell burden in autoimmunity is small and the goal is depletion rather than tumour lysis. The exploratory single-cell data is what makes the case: serial sequencing showed multi-lineage suppression of interferon signatures across B, T and myeloid compartments and near-complete B-cell repertoire reconstitution — broad immune reprogramming mechanistically comparable to CD19 CAR T in SLE, without lymphodepletion, manufacturing, or apheresis. For engager designers this reframes the design target for autoimmune indications: the molecule does not need maximal potency, it needs a therapeutic window wide enough that priming alone controls CRS, and the optimization axes shift toward tolerability, subcutaneous convenience and depth of tissue depletion rather than raw cytotoxic efficiency.

DICE score: 4 — First controlled evidence that an off-the-shelf CD19 engager reproduces CAR-T-like immune reset in SLE at microgram-per-kilogram doses with benign CRS, establishing a distinct and far more forgiving design target for engagers outside oncology.

Clinical-stage; iTabMed Ltd with Union Hospital Wuhan, Stanford and CHOP. NCT06400537.

### 5. Combination Of Bispecific Antibodies Enable Targeted TNFRSF Agonism and Effective Antitumor Activity

Diby L, Malinge P, Moine V, Nouveau L, Chatel L, Masternak K, et al.

*Molecular therapy : the journal of the American Society of Gene Therapy*

[10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.09.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.09.008)

Two 4-1BB × HER2 bispecifics sharing a 4-1BB epitope but binding non-overlapping HER2 epitopes, dosed as a pair; tested alongside a CD3 T cell engager.

TNFRSF agonism has always been a clustering problem dressed up as an affinity problem, and the fix proposed here is combinatorial rather than molecular. A single tumour-anchored 4-1BB bispecific can only crosslink receptors to the degree its geometry and the target density allow; pairing two bispecifics that share the 4-1BB epitope but grip different HER2 epitopes builds a larger two-dimensional lattice on the tumour surface, and the authors show this markedly enhances 4-1BB clustering and downstream signalling in a way that scales with HER2 expression level. The HER2 dependence is the safety argument — signalling is confined to where target density is high — and the syngeneic h4-1BB/h4-1BBL transgenic comparison against urelumab makes it concrete, with efficacy retained and peripheral inflammation reduced. The result most relevant to engager programs is the triple: adding the bispecific pair to a CD3 T cell engager potentiated antitumour activity and increased intratumoural T-cell infiltration, which is the costimulation-plus-signal-1 combination that trispecific CD3 × CD28 formats have struggled to deliver in one molecule. Splitting costimulation across two paired bispecifics instead of loading it into a single trispecific is a credible alternative architecture, at the cost of a two-product development path.

DICE score: 4 — Turns TNFRSF agonism into a designed multi-epitope clustering problem solved with a bispecific pair, and shows the pair supplies costimulation to a separate CD3 engager with less systemic inflammation than an agonist mAb.

Light Chain Bioscience – Novimmune SA (Geneva), with the Cragg group at Southampton.

### 6. A logic-gated synthetic circuit coupled with microenvironmental reprogramming overcomes immune exclusion in antigen-poor chondrosarcoma

Suh J, Yook G, Bae J, Jeong S, Kim S, Kim JH, et al.

*Trends in biotechnology*

[10.1016/j.tibtech.2026.08.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tibtech.2026.08.008)

Tumour-encoded surface T cell engager under a Boolean AND-gate of chondrosarcoma-selective synthetic promoters, combined with MIF blockade.

The premise is that when a tumour has no clean surface antigen, you can stop searching for one and instead make the tumour cell manufacture the engager itself, restricting expression transcriptionally rather than by binding specificity. The authors screened a 57,715-element massively parallel reporter assay to find promoters active in chondrosarcoma and inactive elsewhere, then wired two into an AND gate so the surface T cell engager is expressed only where both fire — genetic logic substituting for the antigen-density discrimination that a conventional engager has to achieve through affinity and avidity tuning. The second half is more conventional but necessary: single-cell sequencing in PBMC-reconstituted xenografts showed the circuit initiates T-cell activation but that MIF blockade is required to prevent exhaustion, shifting the compartment toward proliferative T cells and M1 macrophages. Local administration produced regression of distant untreated tumours. The obvious caveats are delivery — this requires getting the circuit into tumour cells efficiently — and a humanized xenograft model with no endogenous myeloid compartment carrying a claim about macrophage polarization. But as an orthogonal answer to the antigen-poor tumour problem, transcriptional targeting of an encoded engager is worth tracking.

DICE score: 4 — Replaces antigen-based discrimination with a promoter-level AND gate driving a tumour-encoded surface T cell engager, opening a route into antigen-poor indications that binder engineering alone cannot reach.

Seoul National University and Ewha Womans University, with Ming-Ru Wu at Dana-Farber.

### 7. αTIGIT-guided IL-15 mimetics drive potent and safe antitumor immunity by restoring tumor-infiltrating T cells

Liu X, Li N, Chen Y, Xie Z, Huang T, Li S, et al.

*Cellular & molecular immunology*

[10.1038/s41423-026-01467-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41423-026-01467-y)

IL-15Rβ × γc tandem VHH agonist, extended to a tri-antibody αTIGIT × αIL-15Rβ × γc format; alpaca phage display with AlphaFold3-assisted structural screening.

Two design lessons transfer straight to engager work. The first is format: the authors built antibody-based IL-15 mimetics that heterodimerize IL-15Rβ and γc, and found that in vivo activity depended on the tandem arrangement — a reminder, consistent with several recent results, that agonism against a heterodimeric receptor is set by the geometry imposed on the two chains and not only by the affinities of the two arms. Their route there is notable too: immunized alpaca phage display feeding AlphaFold3-assisted structural triage and a comparative screen across multiple formats, which is a pragmatic template for format selection when the structural basis of signalling is unknown. The second lesson is the targeting layer. Appending a high-affinity anti-TIGIT arm to guide the agonist onto tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes produced a striking efficacy gain with no detectable systemic toxicity even at high doses, expanding intratumoural stem-like T cells rather than driving peripheral expansion. That is the same problem an engager's tumour arm solves — localize a powerful signal — solved by targeting the effector cell rather than the tumour, and it is a directly transplantable idea for engagers that want to bias which T cells they recruit.

DICE score: 3 — A clean demonstration that cytokine-mimetic agonism is geometry-driven, plus a TIL-directed targeting arm that localizes the signal to intratumoural stem-like T cells without systemic toxicity.

Yang-Xin Fu group, Changping Laboratory and Tsinghua University.

### 8. Preclinical development of ABL206, a novel bispecific antibody-drug conjugate targeting B7-H3 and ROR1

Kwon JA, Lee S, Kim J, Yoo BM, Park HJ, Ryu I, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2731283](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2731283)

B7-H3 × ROR1 bispecific ADC in a 2+2 format, site-specifically conjugated with the exatecan-based linker-payload tavatecan via GlycoConnect, DAR 4.

Not an immune engager, but the strongest available data this week on what dual targeting buys you when the second arm is used for selectivity rather than for recruiting an effector. ABL206 pairs two antigens frequently co-expressed in solid tumours in a symmetric 2+2 architecture, and the comparison that matters is against exact-matched monospecific ADCs at equimolar dose, where the bispecific gave superior regression — evidence that the benefit comes from co-engagement and internalization rather than from simply doubling binding sites. The breadth is unusual for a preclinical package: 38 patient-derived xenograft models across nine tumour types, consistently outperforming the clinical-stage benchmarks ifinatamab deruxtecan and zilovertamab vedotin, and inducing regression in tumours regrowing after I-DXd. Developability held up, with good plasma stability, clean PK in rodents and non-human primates, and an HNSTD of 60 mg/kg in cynomolgus monkeys. For engager developers the transferable point is the antigen-pair logic — choosing a second target for co-expression-based tumour selectivity rather than for signalling — and the demonstration that site-specific glycan conjugation keeps a 2+2 bispecific homogeneous and well behaved.

DICE score: 3 — Rigorous equimolar matched-control and 38-model PDX evidence that a co-expression-selected antigen pair in a 2+2 bispecific outperforms both parent monospecifics and two clinical benchmarks.

ABL Bio Inc. (Seoul); entering Phase 1, NCT07612176.

### 9. The FAP × CD3 bispecific antibody OMTX305 induces T cell-mediated antitumor effects in patient-derived ex vivo models of solid tumors

Thiel J, Schlegel JA, Liang C, Mürdter TE, Kneer A, Dreher S, et al.

*Science advances*

[10.1126/sciadv.aee2124](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee2124)

FAP × CD3 trivalent Fab-eIg T cell engager; precision-cut tumour slices from lung and ovarian cancer co-cultured with autologous PBMCs.

Targeting cancer-associated fibroblasts rather than tumour cells inverts the usual engager logic, and this paper supplies the mechanistic account of why it can still kill tumour. Eliminating FAP-expressing fibroblasts induced interferon responses and extracellular matrix remodelling, and the resulting bystander killing of adjacent tumour cells is the payoff — a way to attack tumours whose own surface antigens are absent or shared with normal tissue, with the stromal target providing the selectivity. The trivalent Fab-eIg architecture is a reasonable answer to FAP's modest expression differential, using avidity on the target arm to widen the window. The second contribution is methodological and arguably the more useful one: precision-cut tumour slices with autologous PBMCs retain the stromal architecture and immune composition that engager pharmacology depends on and that spheroid or monolayer assays destroy, and combining spatial biology with cytokine and transcriptomic readouts let the authors associate immune phenotype and PD-L1 pattern with differential response. That is a preclinical platform engager programs could adopt directly for candidate ranking.

DICE score: 3 — Stroma-directed engagement producing bystander tumour killing, validated in an architecture-preserving patient-derived slice platform that is itself a better candidate-ranking assay than the standard preclinical stack.

Kontermann group, University of Stuttgart, with Robert Bosch Hospital and Oncomatryx Biopharma.

### 10. Phase 1/1b open-label, first-in-human study of HER2×CD3×CD28 trispecific T-cell engager (SAR443216) in participants with R/R HER2-expressing solid tumors

Dumbrava EE, Oh D-Y, Ryu M-H, Calvo E, Garralda E, Chung W-P, et al.

*Journal for immunotherapy of cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2025-014309](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2025-014309)

HER2 × CD3 × CD28 trispecific engager, 40 participants, 18–900 µg with 2-week and 3-week intraparticipant lead-in dosing.

A negative result worth reading closely, because it is the cleanest clinical test so far of whether built-in CD28 costimulation rescues engager activity in solid tumours. It did not: no objective responses across 40 participants, disease control around 35% in both lead-in schedules, MTD not reached at 900 µg. The pharmacodynamics make the failure informative rather than merely disappointing — proinflammatory cytokines rose and peripheral T-cell activation markers went up, confirming target engagement and signalling, but there was no consistent change in T-cell abundance or activation among tumour-infiltrating cells. Signal 1 plus signal 2 was delivered to circulating T cells and did not translate into intratumoural activity, which points the problem at trafficking and the microenvironment rather than at the signalling architecture. Tolerability was acceptable — half the participants had CRS, but none severe, and no pulmonary or cardiac toxicity — so dose was not obviously the limiting variable either. Read against paper 5 in this digest, the contrast is instructive: costimulation supplied separately by a tumour-anchored clustering agent worked preclinically where costimulation hard-wired into the engager did not work clinically.

DICE score: 3 — A well-characterized first-in-human failure of integrated CD3 × CD28 costimulation in HER2+ solid tumours, with pharmacodynamics locating the barrier at tumour infiltration rather than at T-cell activation.

Clinical-stage; Sanofi. NCT05013554.

### 11. Cancer-driver-agnostic targeting of amplified surface proteins identifies MPZL1 for selective CAR-T cell therapy

Jiménez-Vázquez S, Berthel A, Patsis C, Mitstorfer M, Grimm M, Butthof L, et al.

*Nature communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-77465-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77465-5)

MPZL1 (myelin protein zero-like 1) as a surface target; new monoclonal antibody against the extracellular domain, deployed as a CAR.

The target-discovery heuristic is the exportable part. Rather than starting from lineage markers or oncogenic drivers, the authors screen for genes that are amplified in tumours and encode cell-surface proteins — a category that selects simultaneously for high surface copy number, tumour restriction, and prevalence, which is precisely the combination an engager needs and lineage antigens in solid tumours generally fail to provide. MPZL1 falls out of that screen amplified in up to 75% of patients in certain solid tumour types, abundant on the tumour surface and largely absent from healthy tissue. They raise an antibody against the extracellular domain and validate it as a CAR across xenograft, autochthonous mouse and patient-derived explant models. The binder work itself is conventional and the paper is a CAR paper, but the framework generalizes directly: amplification-driven surface antigens are an underexploited source of engager targets, and the therapeutic window arises from copy-number difference rather than from binary expression, which is the regime where avidity tuning and affinity de-tuning actually have room to work.

DICE score: 3 — A generalizable, driver-agnostic route to surface targets with genuine therapeutic windows in solid tumours, plus a validated new antigen that engager programs can pick up directly.

Tschaharganeh and Breinig groups, DKFZ and University Hospital Heidelberg.

### 12. HDX-MS-Guided computational modeling for rapid and accurate antibody-antigen interface mapping

McMahon W, Zhang H, Li X, Oganesyan V, Payne S, Mehzabeen N, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2722455](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2722455)

Workflow integrating peptide-level and ETD hydrogen-deuterium exchange MS with HADDOCK, AlphaFold2 and Protenix docking; Fab and VHH binders against homodimeric and heterodimeric antigen ectodomains.

Unglamorous and immediately useful. Structure-guided engineering of a multispecific stalls on the same bottleneck every time — you need to know where each arm binds, crystallography and cryo-EM are slow, and unconstrained co-folding predictions on antibody-antigen complexes are still not reliable enough to design against. This workflow shows that peptide-level HDX-MS restraints are usually sufficient to steer docking and structural modelling to the right interface, with targeted ETD-HDX-MS available as a higher-resolution fallback when specific residues matter. The validation is the reason to take it seriously: models were compared against in-house X-ray structures obtained subsequently, and agreed on binding orientation and interface placement. The systems span Fab and VHH binders and both homodimeric and heterodimeric antigen ectodomains, which is the architectural range a multispecific campaign actually encounters. Coming from a group that runs this on real discovery programs, it is a credible answer to "how do I get epitope and paratope maps fast enough to iterate" — the practical prerequisite for the geometry- and spacing-driven design decisions that keep appearing in this field.

DICE score: 3 — A validated, throughput-compatible route to epitope and paratope maps across Fab and VHH formats, benchmarked against subsequently determined crystal structures, that unblocks structure-guided iteration on multispecifics.

AstraZeneca Biologics Engineering and Analytical Sciences, Gaithersburg.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Specificity-driven protein binder design with Odin-Multi

Brasas V, Christensen CR, Björnsson KH, Moller VE, Scapolo B, Benard-Valle M, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.08.749745](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.08.749745)

Multi-complex binder design framework applying attractive objectives to on-targets and repulsive objectives to off-targets simultaneously; benchmarked on class B1 GPCRs, three-finger toxins and peptide-MHC.

The framing is the contribution: current de novo design tools optimize one interaction at a time and leave cross-reactivity and specificity to be discovered downstream in screening, which is expensive and late. Odin-Multi optimizes a shared binder sequence against several complexes at once, so cross-reactivity (when you want one binder to cover a family) and counter-selection (when you need to discriminate near-identical surfaces) become explicit design objectives. The gains are large where the targets are related but distinguishable — 83.5–96.8% of jointly optimized GPCR designs cleared the dual-target confidence threshold versus 6.8–36.3% from single-target campaigns — and more modest but directionally right on the hardest case, a pMHC pair differing by a single peptide residue, where counter-selection roughly doubled the fraction meeting both target confidence and a 2.5× target-to-off-target ratio. That pMHC benchmark is the one engager designers should read, since it is the same discrimination problem as paper 1 in this digest attacked experimentally, approached computationally.

DICE score: 4 — Makes specificity and cross-reactivity first-class optimization objectives in de novo binder design, with the pMHC counter-selection benchmark directly relevant to the hardest target class for engagers.

Jenkins group, Technical University of Denmark, Center for Translational Protein Design.

### 2. Generative Language Modeling for Antibody CDR Grafting and Alignment-driven De Novo Design

Gonzalez Hernandez F, Turnbull OM, Sultana M, Roldan-Martin L, Kumar RJ, Diethe T, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.06.749721](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.06.749721)

GenCDR — LLaMa-based models (IgGenCDR, p-IgGenCDR, NanoGenCDR) that condition on all frameworks as a prompt and generate all CDRs jointly as the response, enabling reward-based alignment.

The architectural insight is that CDR design is an infilling problem and autoregressive models get it wrong by construction: generating left-to-right means the model never sees the full framework context while writing a CDR, and framework and CDR likelihoods get conflated so there is no clean target for a reward signal. Treating frameworks as a conditioning prompt and all CDRs as a variable-length response fixes both, and the payoff is that binding affinity, expression and developability rewards can be composed to steer generation over multiple alignment rounds. The headline result is the nanobody model: over four rounds of alignment against co-folding and developability objectives, NanoGenCDR reached in silico interface metrics competitive with a structure-conditioned diffusion pipeline at roughly half the sampling budget and with more natural, more developable sequences — without any explicit antigen encoding. For VHH-based engager arms, where developability and humanness liabilities routinely kill otherwise good binders, a sequence-only model that can be steered on multiple objectives at once is worth watching, though everything here is still in silico.

DICE score: 4 — Reframes CDR generation as prompt-response infilling to create a clean reward-attribution interface, with a nanobody model matching structure-conditioned diffusion at half the sampling budget on developability-aware objectives.

AstraZeneca with the Deane group, University of Oxford.

### 3. Deep Learning-based Modeling Enhances Efficacy of Natural Ligand CAR Binders Targeting CD70

Kang AS, Dalal R, Li M, Ojha AA, Walunj S, Cornell CE, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.06.749651](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.06.749651)

CD27 N88A — a deep-learning plus physics-based redesign of the CD27:CD70 interface used as a natural-ligand CAR binding domain, across AML, myeloma and renal cell carcinoma models.

Natural-ligand binding domains are attractive for the same reasons in engagers as in CARs — no scFv aggregation or immunogenicity liability, guaranteed specificity — but they come with a hidden constraint: you cannot affinity-mature them the way you would an antibody without abandoning the native interface. This work asks whether the ligand sequence can be engineered at all, redesigning residues at the CD27:CD70 interface computationally and finding a single substitution that improves CAR T efficacy across three disease models. The mechanistic follow-up is the interesting part: molecular dynamics attributes the gain to increased conformational freedom of the binder rather than to higher affinity, which is a different optimization axis than the one antibody engineers habitually reach for, and one that plausibly matters more in the constrained geometry of an immune synapse. If the finding holds, it says natural-ligand arms are a tunable design space rather than a take-it-or-leave-it component — relevant to any engager considering a receptor ligand in place of a binder.

DICE score: 3 — Demonstrates that natural-ligand binding domains can be computationally matured, with a mechanism running through binder conformational freedom rather than affinity.

Wiita and Eyquem labs, UCSF, with Kortemme and Fletcher.

### 4. DLL3 expression predicts response and long-term outcome in small cell lung cancer patients treated with tarlatamab

Zhang B, Hong L, Wang K, Stewart CA, Shah K, Wang W-L, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.03.749238](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749238)

DLL3 immunohistochemistry as a predictive biomarker for the DLL3 × CD3 engager tarlatamab; 138 patients profiled, 54 treated, with longitudinal ctDNA and CyTOF.

The number engager designers should take away is that median DLL3 positivity was 70–75% of cells, not near-universal, and that this heterogeneity is what separates responders from non-responders: median time on treatment was 10.1 months above a 75% threshold versus 2.9 months below it, and median DLL3 was 80% in partial responders versus 40% in progressors. Tarlatamab is the field's flagship solid-tumour engager and the target was chosen precisely because DLL3 looked uniformly expressed in SCLC; finding that antigen-positive fraction, not merely antigen presence, sets the ceiling on benefit is a direct argument that target-density discrimination and avidity design deserve the attention they get. Longitudinal profiling adds a resistance mechanism — persistent ctDNA positivity and enrichment of NEUROD1-subtype cells in circulating tumour cells after progression — consistent with subtype switching away from the targeted lineage rather than loss of the engager's activity. A retrospective single-centre cohort, so treat the thresholds as hypothesis-generating.

DICE score: 3 — Quantifies how sharply antigen-positive fraction gates benefit from the field's leading solid-tumour engager, and points to lineage subtype switching rather than engager failure as the resistance route.

Byers lab, MD Anderson Cancer Center.

## Weekly Themes

The week's most interesting papers converged on the same conclusion from three directions: the CD3 arm, treated as a fixed component for most of this field's history, is now the variable being engineered. TCR-JANUS replaced it outright with a binder against a transgenic TCR's Vβ segment, buying product-restricted redirection, preserved native pHLA specificity for simultaneous dual-antigen recognition, and — the result likeliest to generalize — measurably less exhaustion under chronic stimulation than CD3 engagement produces. Novimmune's paired 4-1BB × HER2 bispecifics attacked the same signalling-quality problem from the costimulation side, building receptor clustering through multi-epitope lattice formation rather than through a single molecule's geometry, and then supplying that costimulation to a separate CD3 engager. Sanofi's SAR443216 shows why this matters: hard-wiring CD3 and CD28 into one trispecific produced textbook peripheral T-cell activation and zero objective responses across 40 patients, with the pharmacodynamics placing the barrier at tumour infiltration rather than at signal delivery. Signal 1 and signal 2 in one molecule is not the bottleneck; getting activated cells into the tumour is.

On the design-tools side, the honest assessment sharpened considerably. The MSKCC de novo CAR paper screened 1,758 generatively designed binders through functional assays and came back with a failure taxonomy — tonic signalling, occluded epitopes, off-target activity — none of which the biochemical metrics these pipelines optimize can see, and the fix that worked was sequence-variant screening around parental structures, which is affinity maturation by another name. Odin-Multi and GenCDR both respond to exactly this gap by moving properties that used to be screening outcomes into the objective function: counter-selection against near-identical off-targets in one case, composable developability and affinity rewards in the other. That is the right correction, and both remain in silico. Meanwhile Alloy's TCRm platform made the same move experimentally, putting whole-proteome specificity screening inside binder selection rather than downstream of it — and the pMHC target class is where computational counter-selection and experimental counter-screening are now visibly racing at the same problem.

Finally, two results quietly redraw where engagers should be aimed. The A-319 lupus trial achieved CAR-T-like immune reset at 0.3–1.2 µg/kg with no grade ≥3 CRS, which means autoimmunity is not a lower-stakes version of the oncology design problem but a different one, optimizing for window width and convenience rather than cytotoxic ceiling. And the MD Anderson tarlatamab biomarker cohort found that DLL3 positivity runs around 70–75% of cells, not ubiquitous, with time on treatment tripling above a 75% threshold — a reminder that even the field's best-validated solid-tumour antigen delivers its benefit as a density gradient. Both point the same way as the Heidelberg amplification-screen framework and the Stuttgart stroma-targeting work: the therapeutic window in solid tumours is going to come from copy-number and avidity engineering against imperfect targets, not from finding a clean one.
