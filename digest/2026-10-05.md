## Top Papers

### 1. GPCR antagonism via rewiring of receptor trafficking and degradation

Rhee K, Shue L, Adachi A, Sun P, Osei-Owusu J, Zhou D, et al.

*Nature*

[10.1038/s41586-026-11088-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11088-0)

GPCR (BILF1, RXFP1, CCR6) × TfR1 bispecific antibody degraders — "GTACs".

The bispecific-degrader idea has been circulating for several years, but the demonstrations have mostly been on targets a conventional antibody could already block, which makes the degradation modality look like an elaboration rather than an enabling step. This is the first set where the modality buys something a blocker cannot. GPCRs are seven-pass receptors with minimal extracellular surface and, critically, many signal constitutively — so an antagonist that must occupy an orthosteric site is both hard to raise and intrinsically capped in what it can achieve. Removing the receptor from the surface sidesteps both problems, and the potency numbers show it: one to two orders of magnitude better than conventional antibody antagonists on the same targets, with complete inhibition including constitutive signalling. The three targets are deliberately hard — BILF1 is viral, RXFP1 and CCR6 have both resisted biologics. The part engager programs should read closely is the design section: the authors establish that degrader performance is context-dependent and work out, by protein engineering plus multicolour live-cell imaging, which geometry and trafficking pathway actually produce degradation rather than recycling. That is the same question governing whether a bispecific engager forms a productive synapse, and it is answered here with mechanism rather than empirical format screening. Caveat: TfR1 as the effector arm carries its own biodistribution and safety baggage, and nothing here is in vivo at therapeutic scale yet.

DICE score: 5 — Establishes induced endocytosis as a general bispecific modality against a target class that resists blockade entirely, with a mechanistic design framework for degrader geometry rather than a single construct.

Kruse lab (Harvard Medical School) and Xin Zhou lab (Dana-Farber Cancer Institute).

### 2. Functional evaluation of TCR-pMHC pairs at scale allows in silico TCR reactivity prediction

Messemaker M, Kwee BPY, Moravec Ž, de Paauw S, Urbanus J, Álvarez-Salmoral D, et al.

*Immunity*

[10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.007)

TCR rapid assembly platform (T-RAP) for uniform TCR library generation; AlphaFold3 structural prediction of TCR–peptide–MHC reactivity.

Two findings, and the uncomfortable one comes first: when thousands of previously published TCR–pMHC pairs are rebuilt under standardized conditions and tested for signalling or multimer binding, only about half reproduce the reported reactivity. That is a statement about the substrate every TCR-specificity model in the field has been trained and benchmarked on, and it means the published performance ceilings were partly a ceiling on label quality rather than on method. The second finding is constructive and follows directly: once you restrict evaluation to pairs that actually validate, AlphaFold3 structural predictions identify reactive pairs well, with no task-specific training at all. The same approach then shortlists TCRs against patient-specific neoantigens. For anyone building TCR-mimic engagers or ImmTAC-like molecules this reorders the problem — the specificity-prediction tooling is better than the literature suggested, the bottleneck was validation data, and a general structure predictor is now a credible first-pass filter for whether a paratope reads a given pMHC. That is exactly the step that currently costs a TCR-mimic program a year of off-target proteome screening. The honest limit is that this predicts reactivity, not the fine cross-reactivity landscape that actually harms patients, and the functional readout remains a signalling proxy.

DICE score: 5 — Simultaneously invalidates roughly half the field's benchmark data and shows that AlphaFold3, untrained for the task, predicts TCR-pMHC reactivity well on what remains, resetting both the tooling and the evaluation standard.

Schumacher lab, Netherlands Cancer Institute / Oncode Institute.

### 3. Forimtamig, a GPRC5DxCD3 bispecific antibody with a novel 2:1 format, in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma

Hasselbalch AC, Harrison SJ, Manier S, Yoon SS, Hulin C, Volzone F, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020498](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020498)

GPRC5D × CD3 bispecific in a 2:1 configuration (two tumor-binding Fabs to one CD3 arm), subcutaneous, 171 patients.

This is the most consequential format-versus-clinical-reality dataset of the week, and the conclusion the authors reach in their own last sentence is the one to internalize: GPRC5D has a narrow therapeutic window for T-cell-engaging bispecifics. The 2:1 geometry is the interesting engineering choice — doubling tumor-arm valency against a single CD3 arm is the standard way to buy avidity-driven selectivity and to lower the CD3 occupancy needed for killing — and on efficacy it delivers, with 59.6% ORR across all cohorts in a population that was 60.2% triple-class refractory. But the toxicity does not behave like a molecule with a widened window: CRS in 75.4%, DLTs in 11.7%, eleven grade 5 events, and dose escalation terminated at 7.2 mg because toxicity accumulated rather than because an MTD was reached. The off-tumor events are on-target, against GPRC5D in normal tissue, which no amount of tumor-arm valency addresses. The finding that genuinely transfers is about scheduling, not architecture: at the same optimized target doses (0.75/1.5 mg), the condensed step-up schedule gave 71.7% ORR versus 47.1% for the weekly schedule — a 25-point swing from dosing cadence alone, at matched exposure. That is a larger effect than most format changes produce, and it is the second paper this week pointing at schedule as the underused lever. Early soluble BCMA reduction and MRD negativity tracking with PFS gives a usable pharmacodynamic readout.

DICE score: 4 — A large clinical dataset showing that a 2:1 valency format delivers potent activity but does not widen the window against an antigen with normal-tissue expression, while demonstrating a 25-point response swing from step-up schedule at matched dose.

Clinical-stage. Roche, with Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre, Lille, Salamanca and Humanitas.

### 4. Scaling laws for massively parallel microfluidics enable rapid construction of ten-million-scale natively paired antibody libraries

Chen H, Lin Y, Chen Z, Zhang D, Ding J, Zhang R, et al.

*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*

[10.1073/pnas.2612455123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2612455123)

Hierarchically parallelized droplet microfluidics (100- and 360-generator chips) for natively paired heavy/light-chain antibody library construction at 10⁷-cell scale.

Discovery depth is the unglamorous constraint on every multispecific program: you need a rare binder with the right epitope and the right developability, you want native heavy/light pairing so the hit is a real antibody rather than a chimera from a scrambled library, and droplet microfluidics gives you native pairing at throughputs that cap out well below the repertoire. The contribution here is that the parallelization problem is solved analytically rather than empirically. The authors derive an exact hydrodynamic model for ladder-geometry networks and show that maximum flow deviation scales quadratically — not linearly — with the number of parallel units, validate it by CFD, extend it to hierarchical networks, and turn the result into a tool for inverse channel design. That last step is what makes this a platform paper rather than a chip paper: anyone can now design a network to a specified uniformity. The delivered numbers are strong — up to 53.8-fold higher droplet generation with droplet-size CV ≤13.8% and occupancy λ ≤0.02, natively paired libraries from 1 million primary human B cells at 14-fold lower reagent cost, and 10 million cells encapsulated in 40 minutes. For engager discovery this is roughly an order of magnitude more repertoire per campaign at lower cost, which changes what counts as a feasible target: rare-epitope and cross-reactive-arm campaigns that were previously uneconomical become routine. It is infrastructure, so there are no binders reported here, and the demonstration is library construction rather than validated hits.

DICE score: 4 — Converts droplet-microfluidic parallelization from empirical chip design into a solved scaling problem, delivering roughly an order of magnitude more natively paired repertoire per campaign at 14-fold lower reagent cost.

Xia lab, Xiamen University (National Institute of Diagnostics and Vaccine Development).

### 5. Multiobjective VH discovery through integrated high-throughput screening and AlphaFold3-guided structural prioritization

Overath MD, Thumtecho S, Rivera-de-Torre E, Benard-Valle M, Wolff DS, Grahadi R, et al.

*Science Advances*

[10.1126/sciadv.aef5325](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aef5325)

Polyspecific VH (heavy-chain-only variable domains) against long-chain α-neurotoxins; yeast display plus deep sequencing plus AlphaFold3 complex-prediction triage.

The nominal subject is snakebite antivenom, and that will cause engager people to skip it, which would be a mistake — the problem being solved is the one every multispecific program hits. You need a single domain that binds several related antigens at a conserved epitope, you can generate thousands of candidates cheaply, and you have no way to tell which hits bind the conserved surface versus an idiotypic one without running the full specificity panel on each. The contribution is using AlphaFold3 complex predictions as a high-precision structural filter on display output: predict the VH–antigen complex for each hit, keep those whose predicted epitope is the conserved one, discard the rest before any wet specificity screening. That triage then feeds computational optimization of affinity and soluble expression simultaneously, with experimental confirmation that optimized variants keep their breadth — the multiobjective part, and the part most AI-design papers skip by optimizing affinity alone and discovering the expression problem later. Translated to engagers: a route to tumor arms that cover antigen variants or related family members at one conserved epitope, with developability co-optimized, reducing rather than adding to the specificity-screening burden. The caveat is that the structural triage is only as good as AF3 on the antigen class, and neurotoxins are small, rigid and well-represented in the PDB.

DICE score: 4 — A transferable recipe for using structure prediction as a precision filter on display campaigns, with affinity and solubility co-optimized and breadth experimentally retained, addressing the specificity-screening bottleneck in polyspecific binder discovery.

Laustsen and Jenkins groups, Technical University of Denmark (Center for Translational Protein Design), with Chalmers and MIT.

### 6. Targeted extracellular degradation of LRP8 promotes ferroptosis in cancer cells

Zhao F, Inague A, Peters-Clarke TM, Chen Y, Ganjave SD, Zhang Y, et al.

*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*

[10.1073/pnas.2616933123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2616933123)

LRP8 × cytokine receptor bispecific degrader (KineTAC format) routing LRP8 to the lysosome.

Read alongside the GTAC paper above, this is the week's second independent demonstration that bispecific-mediated surface-protein removal reaches targets blockade does not — and it is the more interesting of the two mechanistically, because the therapeutic effect is not loss of the receptor's signalling but loss of a metabolic supply line. LRP8 is the selenium uptake receptor. Degrading it depletes selenoproteins, GPX4 among them, which lowers the cellular threshold for lipid peroxidation and sensitizes the tumor cell to ferroptosis. There is no way to achieve that by occupying a binding site: you have to physically remove the receptor and keep it removed. The KineTAC architecture — coupling the target to cytokine receptor internalization pathways rather than a conventional recycling route — is the engineering detail worth stealing, because the degradation machinery becomes a cytokine arm that can be swapped as a module. For engager designers this also quietly expands target selection: a surface protein need not be a lineage marker or a signalling node to be worth engaging, it can be a nutrient transporter whose removal creates a synthetic lethality. What is not established is therapeutic index, and the selenium axis is not tumor-exclusive.

DICE score: 4 — A bispecific degrader whose mechanism is nutrient-uptake denial rather than signal blockade, establishing receptor-mediated selenium uptake as a druggable ferroptosis node and a modular cytokine-receptor route to extracellular degradation.

Wells lab, UCSF, with Olzmann lab (UC Berkeley).

### 7. Induced pluripotent stem cell-derived natural killer cell therapy induces durable responses in glioblastoma and overcomes resistance via a B7-H3-targeted tri-specific killer engager

Ning JF, Davis ZB, Zeb Q, Hinderlie P, Tuininga K, Wang L, et al.

*Neuro-Oncology*

[10.1093/neuonc/noag154](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/noag154)

B7-H3 × CD16 × IL-15 tri-specific killer engager (TriKE) combined with FT538 iPSC-derived NK cells (hnCD16, membrane-bound IL-15/IL-15Rα, CD38 knockout), across 13 patient-derived glioblastoma stem-like cell lines.

The useful thing here is not the NK product but the way the engager is deployed, and the sequence in which the two were developed. FT538 alone stratifies 13 patient-derived GSC lines into sensitive (38%), moderately sensitive (38%) and resistant (23%), with complete regression from a single intratumoral dose in sensitive xenografts, 35-day intracranial persistence and no neurotoxicity. The resistant quarter is then addressed not by re-engineering the cell but by surface proteomics on the resistant lines, which nominates B7-H3, followed by a B7-H3 TriKE that restores sensitivity — and restores it with adaptive NKG2C⁺ NK cells too, so the effect is not specific to the engineered product. That is a template worth copying: use the cell product to define the resistant fraction, profile it, and build the engager against what the profiling names, rather than picking a target up front and discovering the non-responders later. The TriKE format itself is doing two jobs at once, bridging B7-H3 to CD16 while delivering IL-15 to sustain the NK cell, which is the main architectural argument for trispecifics over bispecifics in NK engagement — and the demonstration that it works on NK cells that already carry membrane-bound IL-15 suggests the bridging rather than the cytokine arm is carrying the effect in this combination. Preclinical, orthotopic xenografts in immunodeficient mice, so the suppressive glioblastoma microenvironment is not modelled.

DICE score: 4 — Pairs a clinical-grade NK product with a trispecific engager built specifically against the resistance mechanism the product's own failures revealed, with the rescue reproducing in non-engineered adaptive NK cells.

Clinical-stage NK product (FT538). Miller and Felices labs, University of Minnesota, with Fate Therapeutics.

### 8. uPAR-targeting T cell engager exerts senolytic effects in mice and non-human primates with serum aminotransferase activity as a safety monitor

Deng J, Zeng X, Guo J, Li L, Zou S, Liu N, et al.

*Aging Cell*

[10.1111/acel.70742](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.70742)

GFD-CD3: the growth-factor-like domain of urokinase (uPA) used as the uPAR-targeting arm in place of an scFv, fused to an anti-CD3 arm.

Two things make this worth more than its senolytics framing. First, the targeting arm is not an antibody fragment at all — it is the natural ligand's growth-factor-like domain, borrowed wholesale from uPA. That is a format choice engager programs under-use: a natural ligand domain comes with evolved affinity and specificity for the receptor, is small, and avoids the immunogenicity and developability liabilities of a humanized scFv, at the cost of losing the ability to tune affinity independently of the receptor interaction. Seeing it carried through to non-human primate work is the useful part. Second, the paper is explicit about choosing an engager over the CAR-T version of the same idea for a reason that is about pharmacology rather than convenience: senolytic uPAR CAR-T causes dose-dependent toxicity, dose adjustment rescues it in mice, and the thing that does not translate is the unpredictability of CAR expansion across heterogeneous patients. An engager is dose-titratable, so the toxicity is controllable in a way an autonomously expanding cell product is not. The dose-finding work then locates the toxicity precisely — high-dose GFD-CD3 causes hepatotoxicity via T cell attack on hepatic endothelial cells — and converts it into a monitoring strategy, with transaminase-guided low dosing clearing senescent cells and improving age-related pathology in aged mice and NHPs. Naming the off-tumor tissue and supplying the biomarker that tracks it is more than most engager papers do. Caveats: uPAR is expressed on more than senescent cells, and the long-term consequences of chronic senolytic engager dosing are unaddressed.

DICE score: 4 — Demonstrates a natural-ligand-domain targeting arm in place of an scFv through to non-human primates, with the dose-titratability argument for engagers over CAR-T made concretely and the dose-limiting tissue converted into a usable safety biomarker.

### 9. Comparative clinical outcomes and single-cell profiling of CD19 CAR-T versus blinatumomab for relapsed/refractory B-ALL

Wang K, Yu L, Liang X, Song F, Jiang S, Han S, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-015196](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-015196)

CD19 × CD3 tandem scFv (blinatumomab) versus CD19 CAR-T, 106 patients, with post-infusion single-cell profiling of the T cells each modality elicits.

The engager-versus-CAR comparison is usually made with cross-trial hand-waving, so a 106-patient retrospective with matched single-cell readouts is worth more than its study design suggests. The clinical result is a split decision: day-28 CR/CRi favours CAR-T decisively (96.6% versus 77.8%), overall and leukemia-free survival are comparable across the whole cohort, but CAR-T wins on leukemia-free survival specifically in the high-burden (MRD >20%) and relapsed subgroups — at the cost of substantially more severe toxicity (grade ≥3 CRS 42.6% versus 13.3%). The mechanistic half is what makes this a protein-engineering paper rather than a clinical one. Single-cell profiling of patient-derived T cells after infusion, plus a head-to-head in vitro comparison, points to blinatumomab-elicited T cells carrying a higher exhaustion signature and poorer recovery after tumor engagement, while CAR-T cells sustain effector function. That is an argument about what continuous engager-mediated CD3 crosslinking does to the effector cell, and it lands in the same place as the treatment-free-interval literature: the engager's problem at high tumor burden may be effector durability rather than potency or valency. If so, the engineering response is scheduling and signal-strength tuning — lower-affinity CD3 arms, intermittent exposure — rather than more avidity. Caveats are real: retrospective, single-centre, nine samples for scRNA-seq, and the exhaustion comparison is correlative.

DICE score: 4 — The cleanest side-by-side yet of an engager against a CAR against the same antigen, with single-cell evidence that the engager's limitation at high tumor burden is effector-cell exhaustion rather than insufficient killing.

Clinical-stage comparator study. Bone Marrow Transplantation Center, Zhejiang University School of Medicine.

### 10. Bispecific antibodies that engage pIgR enrich in mucosa in cynomolgus monkeys

Marks K, Shah N, Pham L, Chen J, White I, Burke K, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2743413](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2743413)

pIgR (polymeric Ig receptor) × payload bispecific IgG transcytosis shuttles, with cross-species expression mapping in human, cynomolgus monkey and mouse tissue.

Transcytosis-shuttle bispecifics have had a decade of brain-delivery attention and almost none aimed at mucosa, which is odd given how many engager indications sit behind a mucosal barrier. The useful contribution is twofold and both halves are unglamorous. First, the species problem is addressed head-on rather than assumed away: RNA and protein expression of pIgR is mapped across human, cynomolgus and mouse tissues, and the finding that cynomolgus expression resembles human while mouse does not is exactly the kind of result that determines whether a preclinical package means anything. A lot of shuttle programs have been validated in mice against receptors whose expression pattern does not transfer. Second, the functional readout in cynomolgus monkeys is a 5–10-fold enrichment in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid over normal IgG, accompanied by rapid serum depletion. That pairing is the honest version of the result: the molecule goes where it is supposed to, and it leaves circulation fast, which for a CD3-engaging payload could be read either as a PK liability or as a feature that limits systemic T cell activation. Nobody has built a mucosa-restricted engager on this chassis yet and the half-life arithmetic would need work, but the receptor is now characterized well enough across species to try.

DICE score: 3 — Opens mucosal compartments to bispecific shuttle delivery with the cross-species expression work that makes a preclinical model defensible, and quantifies the enrichment-versus-clearance tradeoff in a relevant primate.

J&J Innovative Medicine.

### 11. ABL209 (NEOK002): Designing an EGFR × MUC1 bispecific TOP1i ADC with promising antitumor activity and enhanced therapeutic window

Lee B, Park HJ, Yoo BM, Kim H, Seo A, Hong Y, et al.

*Molecular Cancer Therapeutics*

[10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-26-0088](https://doi.org/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-26-0088)

EGFR × MUC1 heterodimeric 1+1 bispecific ADC, DAR 4, tavatecan (TOP1 inhibitor) payload.

The target pair is chosen to cancel two specific liabilities against each other, and that logic transfers directly to engager arm selection. EGFR is a good driver but monospecific targeting is dose-limited by skin toxicity, because keratinocytes express it. MUC1 is tumor-associated and aberrantly glycosylated but heterogeneously expressed, and its extracellular domain is shed — so a monospecific MUC1 agent is both patchy and competed by soluble antigen. Requiring both antigens in a 1+1 geometry means avidity-driven selectivity for the double-positive cell: enhanced binding and internalization relative to either monospecific ADC, and — the number that matters — no inhibition of human epidermal keratinocyte proliferation, unlike cetuximab-based comparators. That is the therapeutic-window claim actually being measured in the tissue where it matters, which is more than most dual-targeting papers do. For engager programs the read-across is the shedding argument specifically: pairing a shed antigen with a co-expressed non-shed one recovers tumor selectivity neither arm has alone, and the 1+1 heterodimer keeps the avidity requirement strict enough that single-positive tissue is spared. It remains an ADC rather than a T cell engager, so the CD3-arm cytokine problem is absent here, and the therapeutic window is preclinical.

DICE score: 3 — A well-constructed demonstration that pairing a toxicity-limited driver with a shed, heterogeneous tumor antigen in a strict 1+1 geometry recovers selectivity neither arm provides, with the keratinocyte sparing actually measured.

ABL Bio / NEOK.

### 12. A phase I study of PF-07062119, an anti-GUCY2C/anti-CD3 bispecific Fc antibody, in patients with advanced gastrointestinal cancers

Fakih M, Doi T, Tolcher A, Hong D, Tie J, Messersmith W, et al.

*Cancer Research Communications*

[10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0101](https://doi.org/10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0101)

GUCY2C × CD3ε bispecific Fc antibody, subcutaneous, 79 patients, monotherapy and with sasanlimab or bevacizumab.

A 2.5% overall response rate across all cohorts is the headline, and the temptation is to file this as another solid-tumor engager failure and move on. The dosing data are worth more than the efficacy data. A 400 µg priming dose with premedication measurably reduced CRS incidence and allowed escalation from an 800 µg MTD without priming to a 2,100 µg recommended expansion dose — a better than two-fold window gain from scheduling alone, with dose-proportional exposure and no additional toxicity from either combination. That is a clean quantification of how much step-up dosing buys in a subcutaneous CD3 engager, useful because the figure is usually buried in protocol amendments. The informative toxicity is the one that was not CRS: diarrhea and colitis were the clinically significant and dose-limiting events, which is on-target on-tissue activity against GUCY2C in normal intestinal epithelium. That is the real lesson. GUCY2C was selected because it is universally expressed in colorectal cancer, but its normal expression sits in the gut, and a CD3 arm does not care which compartment it is in. No amount of priming fixes a target whose normal tissue is accessible and essential — this needed a conditional format or a second antigen requirement from the start, and the response rate reflects a dose ceiling set by the gut rather than by the engager.

DICE score: 3 — Useful clinical quantification of what priming-dose scheduling buys in a subcutaneous CD3 engager, and a clear negative lesson that on-tissue GUCY2C expression, not CRS, sets the ceiling for an unconditional format against this target.

Clinical-stage. Pfizer, with City of Hope, MD Anderson and Memorial Sloan Kettering.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. MIMOSA: Guiding de novo antibody design with natural interaction fingerprints

Abanades B, Roncoli A, Bhagawati M, Kessel P, Imhof-Jung S, Doerr D, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.24.753155](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.24.753155)

Inference-time constraint framework for diffusion-based antibody and nanobody design, applied without retraining to RFAntibody and BoltzGen.

The failure mode in de novo antibody design is not that hit rates are low, it is that they are zero on most targets — the generator has to discover a productive interface from scratch, and for many antigens it never does. MIMOSA attacks that directly and cheaply: if the target has a structurally characterized cognate binder, constrain generation to reproduce that interface's geometry and residue chemistry, and let the model build the rest of the paratope around it. The results are the kind that settle an argument. On KEAP1, uPA and IL-8 — three targets where unconstrained methods produce zero or near-zero binders — this yields experimentally validated binders at 20–26% hit rates, with sub-micromolar to single-digit nanomolar affinities. Two details make it more than a trick. It is model-agnostic and requires no retraining, demonstrated on two architecturally distinct generators, so it is a wrapper rather than a new model to maintain. And it works for both contiguous motifs that fit in one CDR and hotspots dispersed across the paratope, which is the harder case. The scope limit is explicit and worth stating: you need a cognate binder structure, so this converts a solved-structure target into a designable one and does nothing for a genuinely novel epitope.

DICE score: 4 — Turns any target with a structurally characterized cognate binder into a tractable de novo design problem at 20–26% validated hit rates where unconstrained generation yields nothing, as a retraining-free wrapper on existing models.

Prescient Design (Genentech), with Utrecht and Roche.

### 2. De novo design of flexible protein interactions with GuideFlip

Yi K, Chen Q, Zhang D, Tian P, Wagstaff JL, McLaughlin SH, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.27.754145](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.27.754145)

Guided discrete flow matching that co-designs binder structure and sequence; applied to intrinsically disordered targets and to conformation-selective nanobody design.

Current binder design needs a target structure, which rules out the large fraction of interesting epitopes that have no structure until something binds them. GuideFlip's move is to stop separating structure generation from sequence design: binder residues are assigned progressively while the complex is re-predicted at each step, so the interface that is emerging influences what gets designed next. Two consequences are reported, and the second is the one engager people should care about. First, on disordered targets it works — de novo binders to the α-synuclein C-terminus and the disordered RBX1 N-terminus at 13.5% and 41.7% hit rates, with epitopes confirmed by NMR and mutagenesis, plus a released database of candidates against 177 human disordered proteins. Second, applying the same flexibility logic to the binder side produced a nanobody that binds agonist-bound β1-adrenergic receptor in the active state but not the inactive state, at a 75% hit rate, confirmed by cryo-EM. Conformation-selective single-domain binders designed to order is a capability the engager field does not currently have and would use immediately — for conformational tumor epitopes, and for CD3 or costimulatory arms that should engage only one receptor state. It also reduces the hydrophobic bias that plagues direct AlphaFold optimization, which matters for developability.

DICE score: 4 — Co-designing structure and sequence makes flexible and disordered targets designable, and delivers state-selective nanobody design at a 75% hit rate with cryo-EM confirmation — a capability with direct application to conformational epitopes and state-restricted effector arms.

Scheres lab, MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, with Tate lab.

### 3. pH-sensitive binder design with Proton-PottsMPNN

Jacobsen CP, Deweid L, Birnbaum F, Gormsen LW, Fryer T, Jagd U, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.30.755438](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.755438)

Sequence-design model representing protonated and deprotonated histidine, aspartate and glutamate as distinct tokens; 8,407-member de novo PD-L1 binder library, yeast display, screened across pH.

Conditional binders are the field's main answer to therapeutic index, and pH is the most generally available condition — acidic tumor microenvironment, acidified endosome, plasma at 7.4. Getting pH dependence has nonetheless meant combinatorial histidine scanning and screening, which produces molecules but no transferable rules. The reason is identified crisply here: existing design models have no representation of protonation state, so they cannot shape the residues around a titratable site to favour one state, leaving pH sensitivity as something you screen for rather than specify. Making protonation state an explicit sequence token fixes that, and the model then redesigns the microenvironment around a titratable residue to energetically favour the chosen state. The validation is unusually honest — 8,407 designs synthesized and displayed with no structure-prediction filtering applied, recovering 237 and 288 unique pH-dependent designs by two sorting methods, with transition pH spanning 4.0 to 5.8. The mechanistic check is the part that makes this a model rather than a library: recovered designs showed stronger model preference for their designed protonation state than non-recovered designs from the same backbone, and that preference was strongest for the most acidic switches. Switches centred on aspartate and glutamate as well as histidine is a genuinely new design vocabulary. For engagers this is the cleanest available route to a tumor-microenvironment-conditional arm that does not require a protease.

DICE score: 4 — Makes pH dependence a specifiable design objective rather than a screening outcome, with a large unfiltered library validating the mechanism and extending conditional-binding chemistry beyond histidine.

Jacobsen, Jenkins and Ovchinnikov groups, Technical University of Denmark and Harvard.

### 4. MHC restriction is structurally encoded by the T cell receptor germline

Wang N, Borowska MT, Jude KM, Chen X, Wang C, Xiang X, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.24.754241](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.24.754241)

Germline-like TCRs with CDR3 peptide specificity removed, plus a molecular clamping strategy to trap ultra-low-affinity TCR–pMHC complexes for cryo-EM.

A foundational question answered with a piece of method engineering worth noting on its own. To ask whether the TCR germline encodes MHC recognition independent of peptide, the authors built TCRs with CDR3 specificity eliminated and then had to image complexes too weak to survive conventional structural work — hence a molecular clamp to trap them for cryo-EM. That clamping trick is reusable for anyone trying to get structures of low-affinity TCR-mimic or engager complexes, which is most of the hard cases. The result: germline-like TCR/pMHC docking modes are identical to wild-type TCRs with intact CDR3 loops, across different peptides and MHC alleles, and human repertoire analysis shows enrichment of specific V genes and Vα–Vβ pairing patterns associated with HLA-A*02 contexts. For TCR-mimic engager design this supplies a structural prior that has been missing: the docking geometry is germline-determined, so the peptide-reading problem and the MHC-reading problem are separable, and a repertoire-level signature exists for which germline segments prefer which allele. Read next to the Immunity T-RAP paper above, the two together say that a sequence-based framework for TCR specificity prediction is now plausible from different directions.

DICE score: 4 — Direct structural evidence that MHC restriction is germline-encoded, separating peptide recognition from MHC docking and supplying a sequence-level prior for computational TCR and TCR-mimic specificity prediction, plus a reusable clamping method for low-affinity complexes.

Garcia lab, Stanford / HHMI.

### 5. Engineered CTLA-4 targeting chimeras for targeted protein degradation and immune synapse reprogramming

Ren P, Fang SY, Zhao S, Cao H, Tang K, Shang X, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.29.755403](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.755403)

cTAC4.0: CD80 extracellular domain fused to an anti-PD-L1 fragment via a human IgG4 heavy chain, exploiting CTLA-4 trans-endocytosis to degrade tumor PD-L1 into T cells.

The third degrader architecture in this week's digest and the only one that works across a cell–cell boundary, which is why it belongs in an engager digest rather than a degrader one. cTAC4.0 hijacks CD80–CTLA-4 trans-endocytosis to capture PD-L1 off the tumor cell and route it into the T cell for lysosomal degradation — both membrane-bound and soluble PD-L1 — while the same interaction converts PD-L1-mediated suppression into CD28-driven costimulation, with the direction depending on the relative availability of CTLA-4 versus CD28. That dual action in a single molecule is the interesting design claim, and it is a synapse-local one: the activity is concentrated where two cells are already in contact. The direct relevance is stated in the paper — cTAC4.0 enhances the cytotoxicity of both bispecific T cell engagers and TCR-engineered T cells in vitro, and suppresses tumor growth in vivo in a glioblastoma xenograft when combined with engineered T cells. For engager programs that is a combination partner designed to act at the synapse the engager creates, rather than a systemic checkpoint antibody. What is not shown is whether the CTLA-4 dependence survives in a tumor where CTLA-4 expression on the relevant T cells is low, and a glioblastoma xenograft is a permissive model.

DICE score: 3 — A trans-cellular degrader that depletes tumor PD-L1 into the T cell while converting suppression to costimulation at the synapse, with demonstrated enhancement of bispecific engager and TCR-T cytotoxicity, though the CTLA-4 dependence and model choice leave the generality open.

Xiaoyu Zhou lab, The Wistar Institute, with Weiner lab and Sidi Chen lab (Yale).

## Weekly Themes

Degradation displaced masking as the week's dominant architecture, and it arrived from three independent directions in seven days. The Kruse/Zhou GTACs couple GPCRs to TfR1 and get one to two orders of magnitude more potency than any antibody antagonist on targets that signal constitutively and cannot be blocked at all; the Wells lab KineTAC routes LRP8 to the lysosome and produces its effect by starving the cell of selenium rather than by silencing a pathway; and the Wistar cTAC preprint does it across a cell–cell boundary, pulling PD-L1 off a tumor cell into a T cell while flipping the same interaction from suppression to CD28 costimulation. The common lesson for engager designers is not "build degraders" but something more specific about target selection: once removal rather than occupancy is the mechanism, a surface protein qualifies if its absence hurts the tumor, which admits nutrient transporters, constitutively signalling receptors and shed ligands that no blocking paratope can address. The GTAC paper is also the only one of the three that treats degrader geometry as an engineering variable to be solved rather than screened, and that analysis — which trafficking route a given bispecific geometry commits the target to — is the same question that decides whether an engager synapse is productive. The second and more uncomfortable thread is a convergence, from three different directions, on the conclusion that valency and avidity are not the levers the field has been treating them as. Forimtamig puts a 2:1 GPRC5D×CD3 configuration through 171 patients and gets 59.6% ORR alongside 75.4% CRS, eleven grade 5 events, and escalation halted on accumulating toxicity rather than at an MTD — with the authors themselves concluding GPRC5D has a narrow window for T-cell engagers. The doubled tumor arm did not buy the selectivity the geometry promises, because the off-tumor expression is real and a CD3 arm does not discriminate by compartment. Pfizer's GUCY2C engager reaches the same wall from the other side: the dose ceiling is set by colitis, not CRS, which is on-target activity in normal gut. And the Zhejiang 106-patient comparison finds blinatumomab matching CAR-T on overall survival but losing decisively at high tumor burden, with single-cell profiling pointing at exhaustion in the engager-elicited T cells rather than insufficient killing. What did move the numbers was scheduling: forimtamig's condensed step-up schedule gave 71.7% ORR against 47.1% weekly at matched target dose, a 25-point swing from cadence alone, and PF-07062119's priming dose bought a better-than-two-fold escalation window. Two independent clinical datasets saying schedule outperforms format is the week's most actionable finding, and it points the engineering response at signal strength and exposure kinetics — lower-affinity CD3 arms, intermittent dosing — rather than at more arms. Third, computational binder design had an unusually consequential week, and notably in the direction of constraint rather than scale. MIMOSA takes two existing generators, adds no training, and converts three targets from zero-hit to 20–26% validated hits purely by constraining generation to a known cognate interface; GuideFlip abandons the structure-then-sequence split and gets binders against intrinsically disordered targets plus a state-selective β1AR nanobody at a 75% hit rate with cryo-EM confirmation; Proton-PottsMPNN makes protonation state an explicit token and so makes pH dependence specifiable instead of screenable, validated on 8,407 unfiltered designs. Three papers, three different constraints, all reporting the same thing: the generative models were never the bottleneck, the specification was. Conformation-selective single-domain binders and designed pH switches are both capabilities the engager field has wanted for years and can now order. Alongside them, the Xiamen microfluidics work solves the discovery-depth problem analytically — deriving that flow deviation across parallel droplet generators scales quadratically rather than linearly, then turning it into inverse channel design — and delivers natively paired libraries from 10 million B cells in 40 minutes at 14-fold lower reagent cost, which makes rare-epitope and cross-reactive-arm campaigns economical for the first time. On specificity prediction, the Schumacher T-RAP paper is the week's most disruptive single result and deserves attention well beyond the TCR field: rebuilding thousands of published TCR–pMHC pairs under standardized conditions, only about half reproduce, meaning every TCR-specificity benchmark has been scored against substantially corrupted labels — and once you restrict to what validates, untrained AlphaFold3 predicts reactivity well. Read with the Garcia lab's structural demonstration that MHC docking geometry is germline-encoded and separable from peptide reading, the TCR-mimic engager field has both a cleaner prior and a reason to distrust its existing evaluation. Elsewhere worth noting: the Minnesota glioblastoma work is a template as much as a result, using the NK product's own resistant fraction to nominate the engager target by surface proteomics and then showing the B7-H3 TriKE rescue reproduces in non-engineered adaptive NK cells; the uPAR senolytic engager carries a natural ligand domain rather than an scFv as its targeting arm all the way to non-human primates, and converts its dose-limiting hepatotoxicity into a transaminase biomarker, which is more honesty about the off-tumor tissue than most engager papers manage; J&J's pIgR shuttles open mucosal compartments to bispecific delivery with the cross-species expression work most shuttle programs skip; ABL209 shows that pairing a toxicity-limited driver with a shed, heterogeneous antigen in a strict 1+1 geometry recovers selectivity neither arm has, with keratinocyte sparing measured rather than asserted. Three further items did not make the ranking but are worth a look: the MIRA phase 2 readout for sonelokimab (10.1016/j.jaad.2026.09.099) quietly de-risks the albumin-binding trivalent VHH chassis that much of the engager field's half-life-extension planning depends on, across 234 randomized patients against an IgG comparator; DeepMind's function-preserving protein watermarking (10.1038/s41586-026-10965-y) is worth knowing about before it becomes a filing expectation, though whether a watermark survives humanization and affinity maturation is exactly the untested question; and ROR1 upregulation after CD19-CAR relapse in mantle cell lymphoma (10.1038/s41375-026-03146-3) nominates a sequenced-therapy target for ROR1-directed bispecifics, which is the kind of resistance-driven target rationale the Minnesota paper argues for in a different disease.
