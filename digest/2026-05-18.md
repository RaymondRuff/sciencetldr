## Top Papers

### 1. Deep peptide recognition profiling decodes TCR specificity and enables disease-associated antigen discovery

Wang N, Yeh H, Lai B, Perera J, Jude KM, Risch I, et al.

*Nature Biotechnology*

[10.1038/s41587-026-03128-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03128-x)

TCR–pHLA recognition mapping; high-throughput yeast display coupled to fine-tuned protein language models.

The authors generate per-TCR "peptide recognition profiles" by screening individual TCRs against millions of peptides via yeast display, then fine-tune protein language models on these profiles to predict T-cell activation more accurately than AlphaFold3 and tFold-TCR. They show generalization tracks functional (profile) distance rather than sequence similarity, introduce a model-intrinsic uncertainty metric, and validate novel candidate autoantigens for HLA-B*27:05-restricted TCRs. The scalable recognition-profile dataset and pLM framework directly inform engager target selection and TCR-mimetic binding-domain engineering.

DICE score: 4 — A scalable experimental-plus-AI platform that meaningfully advances TCR specificity prediction and antigen discovery, with clear utility for engager target and binder design.

Garcia/Khan groups (Stanford/HHMI, Chicago Biohub).

### 2. A bispecific anti-fluorescein × anti-CD3 T-cell engager in combination with fluoresceinated adaptors enables lysis of AML cells

Volta LG, Gobbi C, Koch C, Harrer N, Hofstetter M, Maurer M, et al.

*Molecular Cancer Therapeutics*

[10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-25-0985](https://doi.org/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-25-0985)

FITC × CD3ε scFv-based bispecific "universal/adaptor" T-cell engager redirected via fluoresceinated antibody adaptors.

AdFITC-TCE is an scFv-based anti-CD3 × anti-FITC construct that, instead of binding tumor antigen directly, recognizes fluorescein on tumor-bound adaptor molecules, porting the switchable adaptor-CAR concept into a soluble engager format. Multiplexed targeting via fluoresceinated anti-CD33 and anti-CD117 adaptors drives efficient AML lysis in vitro and suppresses AML growth in NSG mice with efficacy comparable to AdFITC-CAR T cells. The architecture decouples specificity from the engager itself, enabling on/off control and antigen multiplexing with any fluoresceinated binder.

DICE score: 3 — A useful, demonstrated modular adaptor-engager platform with in vivo proof of concept, though conceptually adjacent to established adaptor-CAR switching.

### 3. Reprogramming CAR with cytokine signaling increases the efficacy of CAR-T cell therapy in solid tumour treatment and confers sustained immune memory

Sun R, Liu S, Yang X, Che C, Zhang Z, Zhang C, et al.

*Cancer Immunology Research*

[10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-1490](https://doi.org/10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-1490)

CAR intracellular-domain engineering; compact IL-2/IL-15 receptor-derived activation motifs embedded in the CAR signaling chain.

Rather than appending bulky cytokine-receptor fragments, the authors reconstruct the CAR intracellular domain with compact IL-2/IL-15-derived motifs to enable antigen-dependent cytokine coactivation while preserving the parental CAR architecture. Systematic screening identified the lead construct S71, which rewires signaling and tumor-induced metabolism, improves mitochondrial fitness, and yields superior persistence, memory, and antitumor activity across solid and hematologic targets. The compact modular-motif strategy is portable design logic relevant to signaling-armed engager and synthetic-receptor formats.

DICE score: 3 — A clean, generalizable receptor-engineering strategy with strong functional gains in solid tumor models.

### 4. PD-1 signaling and PD-1 blockade-mediated tumor control are established at microvillar T cell contacts

Jenkins E, Fellermeyer M, Heraghty DF, Sharma S, Mitra T, Kotowski M, et al.

*Science Immunology*

[10.1126/sciimmunol.adz4983](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciimmunol.adz4983)

Mechanistic synapse biology plus anti-PD-1 antibody Fc/format engineering.

PD-1 and TCR signals are integrated at nanoscale microvillar close contacts, where PD-1 selectively limits the duration of TCR signaling via local SHP2 recruitment and by suppressing contact formation. Critically, Fc-receptor engagement traps a blocking anti-PD-1 antibody at close contacts and induces unwanted agonistic inhibitory signaling; re-engineering the antibody to prevent PD-1 trapping eliminates agonism and improves blockade efficacy. This provides a directly actionable antibody-engineering principle and a spatial framework relevant to engager synapse design.

DICE score: 3 — Mechanistic insight with a concrete antibody-engineering lever that improves checkpoint blockade and informs engager synapse optimization.

### 5. Clinical outcomes and spatial transcriptomic profiles of CD19/20 CAR-T therapy in relapsed or refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin's lymphoma

Wang L, Fang C, Zheng Y, Xu Y, Huang W, Zhao W, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-015114](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-015114)

Bispecific CD19/CD20 dual-targeting CAR; phase I/II clinical data with spatial single-cell transcriptomics.

An expanded phase I/II study of bispecific CD19/20 CAR-T in 32 R/R B-NHL patients reports a 74% best ORR (58% CR) with persistence exceeding 500 days in long-term responders and manageable CRS/ICANS. Spatially resolved single-cell profiling resolves two response-associated tumor architectures — a B-cell-dominant apoptosis-competent phenotype and a fibroblast/macrophage-enriched chemokine-rich permissive niche. The dual-antigen design plus spatial biomarker framework is directly transferable to bispecific engager response stratification and antigen-escape mitigation.

DICE score: 3 — Solid dual-targeting clinical data with a spatially informed response framework relevant to engager antigen-escape strategies.

NCT04723914.

### 6. Anti-CD3/CD20 bispecific antibodies as salvage therapy after CAR-T failure in relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Yuan N, Chen C, Xu F, Yang H, Wang Y, Liu P, et al.

*Annals of Hematology*

[10.1007/s00277-026-07025-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-026-07025-w)

CD3 × CD20 bispecific T-cell engagers; pooled clinical efficacy/safety after CAR-T failure.

Across 15 studies and 1,169 patients, CD3×CD20 bispecifics achieved a 45% ORR and 30% CR as salvage after anti-CD19 CAR-T failure, with efficacy strongly dependent on relapse interval (late relapse ORR 71% vs early 26%). Prior CAR-T exposure reduced bispecific efficacy versus CAR-naïve patients, and subcutaneous/combination regimens outperformed IV monotherapy, with epcoritamab showing the highest ORR. The quantitative sequencing and route/format effects are practically important for positioning engagers in the post-CAR-T landscape.

DICE score: 3 — Important pooled clinical data quantifying engager efficacy and sequencing effects after CAR-T failure.

### 7. Whole-protein screening and multi-modal profiling of antigen-specific CD4 T cells at single-cell resolution

Zhang R, Qi J, McKasson M, Choi J, Gutierrez V, Brennan C, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-72396-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72396-7)

Engineered class II single-chain trimer libraries for high-throughput antigen-specific CD4 T-cell capture and multi-omic profiling.

A large-scale MHC class II single-chain-trimer library platform detects antigen-specific CD4 T cells while simultaneously reading out specificity, paired TCRα/β, MHC restriction, and whole transcriptomes at single-cell resolution. Applied to the full SARS-CoV-2 spike RBD and HPV-16 E6/E7, it recovered thousands of antigen-specific CD4 cells and therapeutically promising HPV-specific TCRs. The single-chain-trimer engineering and multiplexed readout are valuable for CD4-directed target discovery and next-generation TCR/engager design.

DICE score: 3 — A robust antigen-discovery and TCR-mapping platform with clear engager target-selection utility.

### 8. PRECISE-seq reveals disease-relevant TCR repertoires with phenotypic plasticity

Liu S, Liang G, Yang Y, Shi Y, Dong L, Zhao Y, et al.

*Journal of Experimental Medicine*

[10.1084/jem.20251779](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20251779)

Contact-dependent proximity-labeling multi-omics linking TCR functional avidity to T-cell phenotype.

PRECISE-seq couples contact-dependent proximity labeling with multi-omic single-cell analysis to screen disease-relevant repertoires and quantitatively link TCR avidity to phenotype. It accurately retrieves CMV-specific clonotypes, shows high-potency cells skew toward exhaustion, and identifies a distinct PD-1-blockade-reversible Ly49+ regulatory CD8 state shaping tumor T-cell fate. The avidity-resolved phenotyping is methodologically useful for selecting and engineering high-quality binding domains and predicting engager-driven T-cell fate.

DICE score: 3 — A useful avidity-resolved repertoire method with mechanistic insight relevant to engager-driven T-cell dysfunction.

### 9. Bispecific antibodies for cancer therapy: evolution of structural formats and co-targeting strategies from wet-lab to AI-driven in silico modeling

Fan Z

*Cancer Letters*

[10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218578](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2026.218578)

Review of bispecific antibody formats and co-targeting strategies, including AI-assisted design.

This review traces bispecific generation from chemical conjugation and cell fusion to protein engineering, tallies the 16 approved bispecifics plus one fusion protein as of early 2026, and frames the transition toward AI-powered target identification, structure prediction, and format design. It is a useful current landscape and format-taxonomy reference but offers no new experimental engineering.

DICE score: 2 — A timely but non-novel landscape review of bispecific formats and emerging AI-assisted design.

### 10. A novel approach to targeting DLL3 with tarlatamab in a patient with medullary thyroid carcinoma after progression on selpercatinib

Roberts TJ, Meador CB, Fuld AD, Lasko A, Frigault M, Hangar KM, et al.

*Thyroid*

[10.1177/10507256261448706](https://doi.org/10.1177/10507256261448706)

DLL3 × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (tarlatamab); single-patient clinical case with modified step-up dosing.

A RET-altered MTC patient progressing on selpercatinib received tarlatamab with a three-step step-up regimen and modified CRS/ICANS monitoring, achieving an ongoing biochemical and radiographic complete response without grade ≥3 treatment-related events. The report extends a marketed DLL3 BiTE to a new indication and demonstrates a tolerability-management protocol, but provides no engineering novelty and is limited to one patient.

DICE score: 2 — Encouraging single-case off-indication engager data with a refined toxicity-management protocol, but anecdotal and not novel engineering.

## Weekly Themes

This was a thin week for genuinely novel engager architectures, with the center of gravity shifting toward the upstream and downstream of engager design rather than new molecular formats themselves. The strongest signal is computational/experimental specificity mapping: a Nature Biotechnology platform pairing high-throughput yeast display with protein language models to decode TCR–peptide recognition and surface autoantigens, reinforcing a field-wide move toward AI-assisted target and binder selection (echoed in this week's bispecific-format review). A second theme is modular receptor engineering and switchable targeting — a compact cytokine-motif CAR redesign and a fluorescein-adaptor "universal" T-cell engager — both decoupling tunable function or specificity from the core receptor/engager scaffold. Finally, clinical and mechanistic translational work dominated the remainder: dual-antigen CD19/20 CAR-T with spatial-transcriptomic response stratification, a meta-analysis quantifying CD3×CD20 bispecific salvage efficacy and optimal sequencing after CAR-T failure, and synapse-scale biology showing that anti-PD-1 Fc/format engineering can convert an agonistic liability into improved blockade — a reminder that contact-geometry-aware antibody engineering remains an underexploited lever for next-generation engagers.
