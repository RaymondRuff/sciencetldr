## Top Papers

### 1. JMB2403, a potential best-in-class PD-1-dependent IL2Rβγ-activating tri-specific antibody for safe and potent immunotherapy

Gu C, Wang D, Wang Y, Jia F, Zhou F, Chang S, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2709956](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2709956)

PD-1 × IL2/15Rβ × common γ chain trispecific: two weak-agonist alpaca nanobodies grafted onto an anti-PD-1 IgG.

The engineering move here is to abandon cytokine attenuation entirely. Instead of mutating IL-2 to weaken CD25 binding — the strategy that has repeatedly failed to widen the therapeutic index — the authors screened a naive alpaca library for *weakly agonistic* nanobodies against IL2/15Rβ and the common γ chain, then tethered them to a potent anti-PD-1 IgG so that receptor dimerization only happens where PD-1 is already engaged. The result is a molecule with no CD25 arm at all: pSTAT5 rises in NK cells and in activated PD-1⁺ CD8 T cells but stays near baseline in Tregs and in resting CD8s, and cynomolgus dosing produced proliferation without vascular leak or pulmonary edema. It is the same cis-targeting logic that the CD3 field uses for tumor-conditional T-cell activation, transplanted onto cytokine receptor subunits and built entirely from nanobody agonists rather than an engineered cytokine.

DICE score: 4 — A genuinely differentiated trispecific architecture that replaces cytokine mutagenesis with nanobody-based receptor-subunit agonism, with primate tolerability data supporting a real therapeutic-index gain.

Shanghai Jeyou Pharmaceutical with Shenyang Pharmaceutical University; IND-enabling stage.

### 2. Distinct epitope engagement confers differential activity of linvoseltamab versus teclistamab across BCMA mutations

Zhou Y, Sineshchekova O, Lee K, Doshi A, Brown K, Franklin MC, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020381](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020381)

BCMA × CD3 IgG-based engagers compared head-to-head against patient-derived BCMA escape mutations (R27P, S30del, P34del, P33S).

This is the cleanest demonstration to date that on the same tumor antigen, *which epitope you picked* determines whether your molecule survives clinical relapse. Linvoseltamab retained binding, Jurkat-NFAT reporter activation, primary T-cell activation, and cytotoxicity against BCMA R27P and S30del — the mutations that arose in teclistamab-treated patients and cripple teclistamab — while both agents lost activity against P34del and both retained it against the germline P33S variant. For binder design this converts epitope selection from an affinity-driven choice into a resistance-liability choice, and argues for deliberately mapping mutational escape space across candidate paratopes before lead selection; for the clinic it supplies a mechanistic rationale for sequencing BCMA engagers rather than writing off the target after first failure.

DICE score: 4 — Establishes epitope non-overlap on a shared antigen as a designable resistance-mitigation axis, with immediate consequences for both binder selection and clinical sequencing of the two approved BCMA engagers.

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals; both agents clinical-stage/approved.

### 3. 1F10, a λ light chain amyloid-specific monoclonal antibody for targeted therapy of AL amyloidosis

Fu J, Hughes MS, Kalantarov GF, Ma H, Li S, Liu G, et al.

*Blood*

[10.1182/blood.2026033437](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2026033437)

λ-restricted conformational-epitope IgG that binds AL amyloid fibrils but not soluble native λ or κ light chains.

Birtamimab and anselamimab both missed their phase 3 primary endpoints, and the authors' reading of that failure is a binder-affinity problem against λ fibrils specifically — the subtype in ~80% of patients. 1F10 is the corrective: a subtype-restricted antibody selected for high-affinity engagement of the misfolded λ conformer with no cross-reactivity to the soluble native light chain, which is the hard specificity problem in this target class since the pathogenic and physiological species share primary sequence. It outperformed both clinical antibodies in antibody-dependent phagocytosis of λ AL amyloid, stained patient biopsies specifically, and accelerated fibril resolution in an amyloidoma model. The generalizable lesson for engager work is that conformation-restricted paratopes can discriminate a pathological fold from an abundant soluble self-protein cleanly enough to be therapeutic.

DICE score: 3 — A well-executed conformational-specificity solution to a target class where two clinical antibodies have already failed on binder quality, with a clear affinity-versus-selectivity design story.

Columbia University Irving Medical Center (Lentzsch lab).

### 4. ACE2-fused nanobody targeting a cryptic RBD epitope broadly neutralizes SARS-like viruses

Zeng W, Zhang G, Ma H, Xiong L, Xu M, Wang S, et al.

*Journal of Virology*

[10.1128/jvi.00608-26](https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.00608-26)

VHH × receptor-ectodomain bispecific fusion (aSR29-ACE2-Fc) built on a cryptic, sterically shielded RBD epitope.

Two engineering results worth separating. First, alternating immunization with SARS1 and SARS2 RBDs drove the alpaca response onto a conserved epitope at the inner base of the RBD that conventional IgGs cannot reach for steric reasons — a reproducible recipe for pushing a repertoire toward cryptic, escape-resistant sites that only a single domain can access. Second, fusing that VHH to the ACE2 ectodomain and an Fc produced a bispecific that blocks entry by two orthogonal mechanisms (paratope occupancy plus receptor-decoy steric blockade) and gained 33-fold in potency over the parent nanobody with improved escape resilience. The "small domain reaches the buried epitope, second arm supplies orthogonal blockade" chassis is directly portable to tumor targets where the conserved epitope is sterically occluded.

DICE score: 3 — A transferable immunization-plus-format strategy for reaching cryptic conserved epitopes, with a clean demonstration that orthogonal blockade mechanisms multiply rather than add.

University of Science and Technology of China.

### 5. Determining the molecular and physiological actions of subtype-selective nanobodies of GABA-A receptors

Gonzalez-Prada JE, Liu S, Shang C, Chernoff CS, Bright DP, Mortensen M, et al.

*Science Advances*

[10.1126/sciadv.aeg3548](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeg3548)

Nanobody panel against α2/α3-containing GABA-A receptors, resolved into silent binders, positive allosteric modulators, and inhibitors by cryo-EM.

Small molecules against GABA-A subtypes have been stuck in a familiar trade-off — selectivity bought at the cost of efficacy — and this work breaks it with single-domain binders instead. The useful part for engager engineers is the structural dissection: cryo-EM structures for representative nanobodies explain why chemically similar binders at overlapping surfaces come out silent, potentiating, or inhibitory, which is the same paratope-geometry-determines-signaling-outcome problem that governs agonistic versus neutral CD3, 4-1BB, and cytokine-receptor arms. A panel where binding mode was mapped to functional class, then validated in brain slices and in vivo, is exactly the dataset that agonist-arm design has generally lacked.

DICE score: 3 — A rigorous structure-to-function map of how nanobody binding geometry on a multi-subunit receptor dictates silent, agonistic, or inhibitory outcomes, directly informative for designing signaling arms of multispecifics.

UCL / MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology with VIB-VUB (Steyaert lab nanobodies).

### 6. An in vitro quantitative systems pharmacology platform for characterizing CD3-bispecific antibody-mediated T-cell activation and tumor cell cytotoxicity

Yuan X, Thalhauser C, Afzal N, Kemper K, An G, Li T

*The AAPS Journal*

[10.1208/s12248-026-01259-2](https://doi.org/10.1208/s12248-026-01259-2)

Mechanistic three-module QSP model (trimer formation → T-cell activation/differentiation → killing) fit to 5T4 × CD3 DuoBody data.

Cross-lab potency comparison of CD3 bispecifics is currently near-meaningless because protocol heterogeneity swamps molecular differences, and this framework is a credible fix: a single joint fit across 14 solid tumor lines spanning 5T4 densities of 9,447–61,686 molecules/cell, two CD3 affinities (683 nM versus 16 nM K_D), and E:T ratios from 1:1 to 8:1. The headline parameter is the one worth remembering — half-maximal T-cell activation required only ~2.1–4.6 trimers per T cell. That number reframes the CD3-affinity-detuning debate quantitatively: if a handful of productive trimers suffices, low-affinity CD3 arms are not sacrificing activation so much as shifting where trimers form, and the model gives a way to compare candidates on mechanistic parameters rather than raw EC50s.

DICE score: 3 — A reusable mechanistic framework that makes in vitro CD3-bispecific potency comparable across assays, and puts a hard number on the trimer stoichiometry needed for T-cell activation.

University of Iowa College of Pharmacy.

### 7. Synchronized latency reversal and immune clearance by a multifunctional fusion protein enables HIV-1 reservoir reduction

Luo F, Cao Y, Liu N, Wang X, Hu W, Yan Y, et al.

*Molecular Therapy*

[10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.07.048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.07.048)

hyperIL-15 × soluble CD4-Fc trifunctional fusion: cytokine agonist arm, receptor-decoy targeting arm, Fc effector module.

The architecture collapses a three-step therapeutic sequence into one molecule, and the coupling is the point. The sCD4 arm binds HIV-1 Env, so cytokine delivery and target marking are spatially coincident: hyperIL-15 signaling reverses latency in CD4 T cells from people living with HIV while the same molecule flags reactivated cells for Env-specific NK killing, with the authors reporting off-target cytotoxicity is avoided precisely because effector recruitment requires Env engagement. Replication-competent HIV-1 DNA fell 93.8% in patient PBMCs and reservoir shrank in lymphoid organs of ART-treated humanized mice. Using a natural receptor ectodomain rather than an antibody arm as the targeting moiety, and pairing it in cis with a cytokine, is a design pattern the tumor engager field has underused.

DICE score: 3 — A neatly coupled cytokine-plus-targeting-plus-effector single-chain design in which the targeting arm is a receptor decoy, with strong ex vivo and humanized-mouse reservoir data.

Wuhan University / Chinese Academy of Sciences consortium.

### 8. B cell CD19 is transferred between immune cells in mice and humans

Ochs J, Schweineberg P, Thode J, Blenkle A, Husseini L, Klein M, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-75534-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75534-3)

Trogocytic transfer of CD19 — the field's canonical engager and CAR target — from B cells onto T cells and myeloid cells.

The premise underneath every CD19-directed engager is that CD19 is a lineage-exclusive B-cell marker; this paper shows it is not. In B–T co-cultures CD19 moves onto T cells by trogocytosis, and in a B-cell-driven EAE model CD19⁺ T cells expand with enhanced activation and encephalitogenic features. In patients with chronic CNS demyelination, CD19⁺ T cells carry a pro-inflammatory phenotype and are co-depleted by inebilizumab — meaning an approved anti-CD19 antibody is already hitting a non-B-cell compartment nobody designed for. Myeloid cells likewise acquire CD19 and functional IgM after phagocytosing apoptotic B cells. For engager design this is a target-integrity problem with two faces: unintended on-target killing of antigen-acquiring effectors, and a plausible route to fratricide when the transferred antigen lands on the very T cells the engager recruits.

DICE score: 3 — A mechanistic result that undermines the lineage-exclusivity assumption for the field's most-used target antigen, with direct implications for on-target off-tumor liability and effector fratricide in CD19 engagers.

University Medical Center Göttingen with Charité Berlin.

### 9. In silico clinical trials of BiTE expression by oncolytic viruses reveal the impact of patient heterogeneity on dosage protocol

Jenner AL, Araujo RP, Levi NL, Ungerechts G, Engeland CE, Heidbuechel JPW

*PLOS Computational Biology*

[10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014533](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014533)

Virally encoded BiTE (MV-BiTE) delivery modeled as an ODE system across a virtual patient population.

Encoding an engager in an oncolytic vector solves the tumor-localization problem and creates a new one — dose is no longer something you administer, it is something the tumor manufactures — and this work asks what that does across a heterogeneous population rather than in a single mouse. Virtual patients were parameterized from Phase II tumor-volume variability using T-VEC as the surrogate vector. The two dominant sources of response heterogeneity were effector T-cell killing rate and BiTE pharmacokinetics, not viral replication, and, notably, patients classified as non-responders on the Phase II schedule were predicted to respond to more frequent administration at lower dose. That is a schedule-design hypothesis derived entirely in silico, and it points the same direction as the step-up dosing lessons from systemically delivered engagers.

DICE score: 3 — A useful methodological contribution that identifies which parameters actually drive response heterogeneity for vector-delivered engagers and generates a concrete, testable dosing hypothesis.

Queensland University of Technology with Heidelberg / Witten-Herdecke.

### 10. An antibody-PROTAC conjugate targets BRD4/c-Myc/PD-L1 to enhance immunotherapy efficacy in triple-negative breast cancer

Gu Y, Li C, Zhao Y, Xu T, Zhu Y, Wang D, et al.

*Cell Reports Medicine*

[10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102948](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102948)

Anti-TROP2 IgG conjugated to a BRD4-degrading PROTAC via a hypoxia-cleavable linker (ASA).

The interesting engineering is the two-gate specificity: TROP2-dependent endocytosis controls where the conjugate enters, and a hypoxia-triggered linker controls where the degrader is released, so payload activity requires both antigen and microenvironment. Functionally the conjugate does something an ADC does not — sustained BRD4 degradation suppresses c-Myc and PD-L1 simultaneously, so the molecule is cytotoxic and checkpoint-modulating from a single warhead, and it produced stronger long-term immunological memory than sacituzumab govitecan with further amplification by anti-PD-L1. For multispecific designers the transferable idea is conditional payload unmasking driven by a microenvironmental variable rather than by protease cleavage, which is the mechanism most masked-CD3 designs currently rely on.

DICE score: 3 — A differentiated antibody-conjugate modality with dual antigen-and-hypoxia gating and an immunomodulatory rather than purely cytotoxic payload mechanism.

Nanjing Medical University / Southeast University.

### 11. A bifunctional artificial antibody for universal antimicrobial therapy

Tang H, Zhao X, Sun M, Huang Z, Zhu Y, Qin Z, et al.

*Advanced Materials*

[10.1002/adma.74355](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.74355)

Gold-nanoparticle "Fab-mimetic" bispecific (AuriFAB) recognizing FtsZ in Gram-positives and lipopolysaccharide in Gram-negatives.

This replaces the immunoglobulin scaffold outright: dithiocarbamate ligation onto a gold core, with chemical epitope screening standing in for library selection, yielding a bispecific recognition surface with picomolar affinity and nanogram-per-milliliter potency across all multidrug-resistant ESKAPE pathogens. The properties being bought are the ones protein scaffolds struggle with — colloidal stability under harsh conditions and over a year in solution — alongside favorable PK and metabolizable clearance. It is not an engager and the two arms target structurally unrelated ligand classes rather than an effector, but as a demonstration that a fully synthetic chassis can carry a bispecific paratope with real affinity and developability it is worth tracking against protein-based multispecific formats.

DICE score: 3 — A credible non-protein bispecific chassis with genuine affinity and stability data, relevant as a comparator for the stability and manufacturability constraints that shape protein multispecific design.

Southern University of Science and Technology (Jiang lab).

### 12. Exposure-response analyses to inform the optimal epcoritamab monotherapy dosing regimen in relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma

Sinnollareddy M, Sanghavi K, Gibiansky L, Li T, Putnins M, Mohamed MF, et al.

*Clinical Pharmacokinetics*

[10.1007/s40262-026-01661-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-026-01661-1)

CD3 × CD20 subcutaneous engager; exposure-response modeling of the two-step step-up regimen (0.16/0.8/48 mg) across EPCORE NHL-1 and NHL-3.

Regulatory-grade dose justification rather than new engineering, but it closes the loop on a design question the field keeps relitigating: how much of the CRS mitigation in an approved engager comes from the step-up schedule versus the molecule, and whether the full dose is doing work at the efficacy end. The analysis anchors the 48 mg full dose and the QW→Q2W→Q4W taper against response rates already reported at 82.0% ORR / 62.5% CR in NHL-1 and 95.2% / 76.2% in NHL-3. Read alongside the AAPS J trimer-stoichiometry result above, it reinforces that for high-density B-cell targets the binding arms are not the limiting variable — exposure shaping is.

DICE score: 2 — Careful but routine exposure-response work supporting an approved regimen; useful as a reference point for step-up schedule design rather than a conceptual advance.

AbbVie / Genmab; clinical (EPCORE NHL-1, NHL-3).

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Catch-bond engineering surpasses high-affinity maturation for T cell receptor therapies against solid tumors

Zhao X, Huang T, Wang R, Zhang D, Wu S, Wang Y, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.26.740839](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.26.740839)

Force-responsive catch-bond-engineered TCRs benchmarked against the affinity-matured FDA-approved TCR-T, plus TCR-scaffold bispecific engagers.

This is the most consequential thing posted this week. The authors argue that static affinity is the wrong optimization target for TCR sensitivity and show that force-induced catch bonds are what actually govern it — using the only FDA-approved TCR-T therapy, itself a product of high-affinity maturation, as the comparator. Catch-bond-engineered TCRs outperformed it against solid tumors without off-target toxicity or alloreactivity; sensitivity correlated with catch-bond strength and not with affinity; and the mechanism resolves structurally as force-induced interface reconfiguration creating a new hydrogen-bond network that *increases* peptide specificity. Histidine emerged as the most productive residue for locating engineering hotspots, which makes this actionable rather than merely conceptual. Critically for this readership, the authors report that bispecific T-cell engagers built on TCR scaffolds also form catch bonds with tumor antigens — implying the extracellular domains are intrinsically mechanosensory and that soluble-TCR engager arms (ImmTAC-class) should be optimized on force-dependent, not equilibrium, criteria.

DICE score: 5 — If it holds, this replaces the field's default binder-optimization objective for TCR-based and TCR-scaffold engager arms, and offers a concrete route past the affinity-maturation toxicity ceiling.

CAS Center for Excellence in Molecular Cell Science, Shanghai.

### 2. The Human Bindome: a proteome-scale atlas of designed binder candidates

Wenckstern J, Diaz-Rovira AM, Kuhn J, Ban A, Hamdani R, Pruano-Milla R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.30.741542](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.30.741542)

306,146 in silico designed binder candidates covering 8,296 human proteins (40.9% of the proteome), generated with a parallelized BindCraft pipeline.

De novo binder design has been gated less by method quality than by who can run it; this removes that gate at proteome scale. Every candidate ships with a defined sequence, a predicted binder–target complex, and in silico confidence metrics, with automated domain-level target selection doing the work that a specialist would otherwise do by hand. The finding that designed epitopes frequently overlap functional sites is what makes this more than a sequence dump — it positions the resource as genetically encodable perturbagens, and for multispecific work it means a starting binder against most human surface targets is now a lookup rather than a campaign. The caveat is unchanged and important: these are predictions, and the experimental hit rate at this scale is unmeasured.

DICE score: 4 — Turns de novo binder generation from a per-target project into a proteome-scale resource, though every candidate still requires experimental validation.

EPFL (Correia lab) with contributions from CeMM, IMP and Toronto.

### 3. Modular counterplays shape condensation and signaling of chimeric antigen receptor

Jiang Y, Ren Z, Luo J, Yang H, Liu X, Chen H, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.27.741008](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.27.741008)

CD28-CD3ζ CAR with an added CD3ε cytoplasmic module (E-CAR), engineered to resist PD-1 disruption of CAR–LCK signaling condensates.

The mechanistic claim is that PD-1 inhibits second-generation CARs by a route nobody had assigned to it: not the canonical SHP-phosphatase pathway, but competitive binding that dissolves the liquid-liquid phase-separated CAR–LCK condensate on which activation depends. LAG3 and CTLA4 do not do this. The engineering answer follows directly — splice in a CD3ε cytoplasmic module to add LCK-binding valency, and the condensate resists PD-1 incorporation, preserving synapse organization and proximal signaling under PD-L1 engagement, with retained antitumor activity against PD-L1-positive solid tumors. Treating signalosome condensate stability as a tunable design parameter, set by the valency of kinase-binding modules, is a new and quite general knob for engineered receptors and for the intracellular half of engager-adjacent constructs.

DICE score: 4 — Identifies condensate disruption as an unrecognized checkpoint mechanism and converts it into a concrete, modular receptor-engineering fix.

Institute of Biophysics, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

### 4. Engineering antigen-driven co-stimulation and T helper cell activity into TCR-T cells with CD8-41BB fusion receptors enhances anti-tumor activity

Dutta I, Oh J, Cam L, Luther A, Sharma P, Balwani I, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.27.739613](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.27.739613)

Chimeric CD8β–41BB fusion receptors knocked into TRAC alongside a WT1 or PRAME TCR by CRISPR-Cas9.

TCR-T cells lack the built-in co-stimulation that second-generation CARs get for free, and HLA class I-restricted CD4⁺ TCR-T cells contribute little helper activity. The fix here is to make the CD8 co-receptor itself the co-stimulatory device: wild-type CD8β restored some CD4⁺ activity but was insufficient, whereas screened CD8–41BB chimeras drove polarized Th1 cytokine secretion from CD4⁺ cells *and* boosted CD8⁺ CTL proliferation and cytotoxicity, without perturbing the WT1 TCR's HLA-A*02:01 restriction or peptide recognition motif. Combined with TGFBR2 disruption it fully regressed established ovarian tumors with durable rechallenge protection, and the whole cassette — TCR plus fusion receptor — installs at TRAC in one step at >80% efficiency. Validation on a second TCR (PRAME) supports the universality claim.

DICE score: 3 — A clean, manufacturable co-receptor-as-co-stimulator architecture with preserved TCR specificity, generalizable across TCR-T programs.

Intellia Therapeutics with Ospedale San Raffaele and OHSU.

### 5. Transplanting enzyme active site geometry into antibody CDRs for catalytic antibody design

Zhu Y

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.25.740676](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.25.740676)

Computational framework grafting enzyme catalytic residues, cofactors and water networks into Fv or VHH CDR loops under constrained all-atom diffusion.

A design framework rather than a result — no molecules were made — but the decomposition is worth noting. Scaffold selection (a developable Fv or VHH) is separated from catalytic motif selection (residues, ligand conformations, metals, cofactors, key waters lifted from an enzyme complex), with CDR loops rebuilt by constrained diffusion and filtered for folding, catalytic geometry, ligand positioning and developability. Explicitly avoiding enzyme–antibody fusion in favor of transferring only local geometry is the right instinct, and framing binding, turnover and product release as separate design objectives is a useful template. Treat it as a protocol proposal awaiting wet-lab validation.

DICE score: 2 — A sensible and well-structured computational protocol for motif transplantation into antibody scaffolds, with no experimental validation yet.

Shaanxi University of Technology.

## Weekly Themes

The organizing idea this week is that **the field's default binder-optimization objective may be wrong**. The catch-bond preprint is the sharp version — using the one FDA-approved TCR-T as its comparator, it argues that force-induced bond behavior, not equilibrium affinity, sets TCR sensitivity, and that catch-bond-engineered receptors beat affinity-matured ones without the off-target toxicity that affinity maturation reliably buys; the explicit finding that TCR-scaffold bispecific engagers also form catch bonds pulls ImmTAC-class arms directly into scope. The Blood Advances BCMA study makes the same point from the clinic in a different currency: linvoseltamab and teclistamab differ not in affinity but in epitope, and that choice alone decides which molecule survives patient-derived escape mutations. Both say the number a discovery campaign optimizes — K_D — is the wrong summary statistic. Against that, **quantitative frameworks for what actually drives engager output** had a strong week: a QSP model fit across 14 tumor lines put half-maximal T-cell activation at only ~2–5 productive trimers per T cell, an in silico trial of virally encoded BiTEs found effector killing rate and BiTE PK, not viral replication, to be the dominant sources of patient heterogeneity, and an epcoritamab exposure-response analysis anchored the approved step-up schedule — three independent arguments that for accessible targets, exposure shaping and trimer geometry dominate binding-arm potency. A third thread is **format pluralism at the effector-recruitment step**: JMB2403 abandons cytokine mutagenesis in favor of weakly agonistic nanobodies against IL2/15Rβ and the common γ chain gated by PD-1; the HIV fusion protein uses a receptor ectodomain rather than an antibody arm to target while delivering hyperIL-15 in cis; an ACE2-VHH fusion stacks paratope occupancy with receptor-decoy blockade for a 33-fold potency gain; and a gold-core "Fab-mimetic" bispecific dispenses with the immunoglobulin scaffold entirely. On the intracellular side, CAR signalosome condensate stability emerged as a tunable parameter — PD-1 inhibits second-generation CARs by dissolving CAR–LCK condensates, and adding a CD3ε module restores them — while CD8β–41BB fusion receptors turn the co-receptor itself into a co-stimulatory device for TCR-T. Finally, two results should unsettle comfortable assumptions: the Human Bindome makes a designed binder against 40.9% of the human proteome a lookup rather than a campaign (predictions only, hit rate unmeasured), and a Nature Communications paper shows CD19 — the field's foundational "lineage-exclusive" target — transfers by trogocytosis onto T cells and myeloid cells, is co-depleted by an approved anti-CD19 antibody in patients, and therefore carries an on-target off-tumor and fratricide liability that no CD19 engager was designed against.
