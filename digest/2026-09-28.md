## Top Papers

### 1. Specific targeting of MR1-antigen complexes using nanobodies

Hussain T, Redmond SJ, Awad W, Xu W, Soliman C, Wang H, et al.

*Science Immunology*

[10.1126/sciimmunol.aeb8726](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciimmunol.aeb8726)

MR1–5-OP-RU (microbial riboflavin metabolite) × CD3 nanobody-based TCR-mimic bispecific.

Every TCR-mimic program is currently paying the HLA tax: pick an allele, lose most of the patient population, then spend a year proving the paratope does not cross-react across the rest of the presented proteome. This work goes after a target class where that tax does not apply. MR1 is essentially non-polymorphic and presents small-molecule metabolites rather than peptides, so a binder raised against MR1–5-OP-RU is in principle a pan-population reagent against a chemically defined ligand instead of a nine-mer drawn from a combinatorial space. From yeast display the authors pull nanobodies with high affinity for the complex, show cross-species binding to mouse and human MR1–5-OP-RU, and demonstrate functional blockade of MAIT responses to 5-OP-RU and to bacterial challenge in vitro and in vivo. The part that makes this protein engineering rather than immunology is the crystallography: the lead nanobody co-binds MR1 and the metabolite in genuine TCR-mimic fashion. Reformatted into MR1–5-OP-RU × CD3 bispecifics, these drive broad T cell killing of bacterially infected cells and of tumor cells loaded with 5-OP-RU. The specificity argument is also structurally cleaner than the peptide case, since an off-target would have to be another MR1-presented small molecule rather than any of roughly 10⁴ self-peptides. What remains unproven is whether a tumor-relevant MR1 ligand exists at sufficient surface density without exogenous loading, which is now the gating question for the whole approach.

DICE score: 5 — Opens an HLA-unrestricted antigen axis for TCR-mimic engagers, with structures establishing that a nanobody can read a metabolite–MHC-like complex the way a TCR does.

Rossjohn, Godfrey, Koay and Gherardin groups, Monash University and University of Melbourne.

### 2. Phase 1 study of anito-cel, a d-domain BCMA CAR T cell for refractory or recurrent myeloma

Frigault MJ, Dhakal B, Jakubowiak AJ, Raje N, Banerjee K, Mu CJ, et al.

*New England Journal of Medicine*

[10.1056/NEJMoa2603527](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2603527)

BCMA-directed CAR using a synthetic non-immunoglobulin d-domain binder (ddBCMA), benchmarked head-to-head against the dual-VHH binder of cilta-cel.

The clinical numbers are extraordinary on their own — 100% response across 38 patients, 79% complete response, 30.2-month median progression-free survival at 38 months of follow-up, no grade 3+ CRS at the recommended phase 2 dose and only one grade 3 ICANS event — but the reason this belongs in an engager digest is the in vitro comparison buried in the results. The authors put the ddBCMA d-domain binder directly against a construct matching the published cilta-cel dual-VHH sequence and found that the d-domain has a faster off-rate, achieves similar cytotoxicity, produces less cytokine release, and shows no antigen-independent activation or off-target killing. That is the binder-kinetics argument the engager field makes constantly and almost never tests this cleanly: a faster off-rate buys serial killing and reduced cytokine output without costing potency, and a non-Ig scaffold avoids the tonic signaling that VHH aggregation propensity can produce. Read alongside last week's Maus lab result showing that cilta-cel's biparatopic VHH advantage largely disappeared under a different promoter, the field now has two independent lines of evidence that the dual-VHH format's reputation was built on confounded comparisons. The caveat is real — this is a single-arm phase 1, and the binder comparison is in vitro against a sequence-reconstructed comparator rather than the clinical product — but the direction is consistent.

DICE score: 4 — Pairs exceptional clinical outcomes with the cleanest available head-to-head evidence that a fast-off-rate synthetic scaffold beats a dual-VHH binder on cytokine output and tonic signaling at equal cytotoxicity.

Clinical-stage. Arcellx and Kite/Gilead, with Massachusetts General Hospital and University of Chicago.

### 3. Proteolytic activation of T cell engagers and bispecific proteins using modular coiled-coil peptides

Mondal S, Haikal C, Kwong GA, Blazeck J

*ACS Chemical Biology*

[10.1021/acschembio.6c00688](https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.6c00688)

PSMA or HER2 × CD3 masked BiTE: αCD3 Fab with leucine-zipper coiled-coil masks on both chains via MMP2-cleavable linkers, C-terminally fused to a targeting VHH.

Masking a CD3 arm has been tried with peptide masks, hinge extensions and anti-idiotypic domains, and the recurring failure mode is the same: masks that mask well do not unmask well, and masks that unmask cleanly leak. The contribution here is a masking element that is a genuine module — a leucine zipper fused N-terminally to each Fab chain, protease-cleavable at the junction, so masking works by forced dimerization rather than by steric occlusion of a single site. The numbers are the reason to care. OKT3- and UCHT1-based constructs lose ~400-fold and ~800-fold CD3 affinity when masked, and recover ~222-fold and ~457-fold on proteolysis, a dynamic range most masking chemistries do not reach — and reaching it symmetrically across two different clinical CD3 clones is the evidence that the module is clone-agnostic. Functionally the masked constructs fail to activate Jurkat cells while the unmasked ones do, and MMP2-cleavable versions kill PSMA⁺ and HER2⁺ cells in PBMC co-culture only in an MMP2-dependent manner. The honest limitation is that MMP2 is not tumor-exclusive and no in vivo therapeutic index is established here. But as a swappable part — zipper, cleavage site, effector arm and targeting VHH all independently exchangeable — this is more useful than a one-off masked molecule.

DICE score: 4 — A protease-gating module with a genuinely large and reversible affinity swing, demonstrated on two different clinical CD3 clones and two different tumor arms, which makes it a platform rather than a single construct.

Blazeck and Kwong labs, Georgia Institute of Technology / Emory (Georgia Immunoengineering Consortium).

### 4. First-line retlirafusp alfa plus chemotherapy for HER2-negative gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma: a randomized, double-blind, phase III study (RELIGHT)

Peng Z, Wang J, Zhang Y, Li H, Zhao Q, Zhu X, et al.

*Journal of Clinical Oncology*

[10.1200/JCO-25-02933](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02933)

PD-L1 × TGF-β bispecific antibody-trap fusion plus CAPOX, 731 patients, first line.

Dual-targeting bispecifics have a poor phase III record, and the PD-L1/TGF-β axis specifically has a graveyard — bintrafusp alfa failed across multiple indications and took much of the field's enthusiasm with it. RELIGHT is the first large randomized trial to make the format work, and the effect size is not marginal: median overall survival 16.8 versus 10.4 months in PD-L1 CPS ≥5 (HR 0.53) and 15.8 versus 11.2 months in the intention-to-treat set (HR 0.66), with grade 3+ treatment-related adverse events essentially unchanged versus chemotherapy alone at 62.6% versus 59.0%. For a protein engineer the interesting question is what distinguishes this molecule from its failed predecessor, and the trial does not answer it — the differences are in construct geometry, TGF-β trap valency and affinity balance between the two arms, none of which are dissected here. The stated limitation is also serious: the comparator was chemotherapy alone, because the protocol predates PD-1 inhibitor plus chemotherapy becoming standard in China, so the increment over the current standard is unknown. Still, a 5-6 month OS gain from a bispecific in first-line gastric cancer is a format-level datapoint the field needed after bintrafusp.

DICE score: 4 — First positive phase III for a PD-L1/TGF-β bispecific after a well-known failure of the same concept, which resets the viability argument for dual-pathway bispecifics even though the comparator limits interpretation.

Clinical-stage. Peking University Cancer Hospital and a Chinese multicenter consortium.

### 5. Development of Inno8, an orally administered factor VIIIa-mimetic antibody fragment for treatment of hemophilia A

Lund J, Bjelke JR, Granata D, Egebjerg T, Bardonnet PL, Wang Z, et al.

*Blood*

[10.1182/blood.2026034676](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2026034676)

FIXa × FX bispecific single-chain VHH-VHH fragment, pI-lowered and fatty-acid conjugated, formulated with SNAC for oral dosing.

Emicizumab established that a bispecific can substitute for a missing cofactor by co-localizing two proteases; Inno8 asks whether the same function can survive the gut. The engineering is a three-variable optimization that engager programs will recognize, with potency, route and exposure each tuned by a different handle. Structure-guided plus machine-learning-assisted engineering of the two VHH domains gets an average 90-fold greater in vitro potency than a sequence-identical emicizumab analogue, which is itself a strong statement about what iterative design can do to a cofactor-mimetic geometry. Lowering the isoelectric point — a developability knob usually turned to fix viscosity or clearance — is used here for an entirely different purpose and raises oral exposure ~10-fold in rats, while site-specific fatty acid conjugation supplies the systemic half-life a VHH-VHH construct otherwise lacks, reaching 115 hours in beagles. The tablet with SNAC and niacinamide achieves 0.215% oral bioavailability, which sounds dismal until you note it is sufficient for procoagulant activity and that first-in-human dosing is already complete (VOYAGER1). For engager design the transferable lesson is the pI result: a parameter the field treats as a purification and formulation constraint turns out to be the dominant lever on a completely different pharmacokinetic axis.

DICE score: 4 — First credible oral route for a bispecific antibody fragment, with the pI-versus-oral-bioavailability relationship quantified and a 90-fold potency gain over the marketed comparator's sequence.

Clinical-stage (first-in-human dosing complete). Novo Nordisk.

### 6. Computational design of optimized and preferentially paired human TCR constant regions for improved T cell function

Radman M, Shamul-Raybi A, Parker J, Fleishman SJ, Cohen CJ

*Science Advances*

[10.1126/sciadv.aeb9189](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeb9189)

Redesigned human TCR α/β constant domains (SET) for stability and preferential chain pairing, validated across six TCRs.

Chain mispairing is the structural problem shared by transgenic TCRs and by every multispecific format that has to assemble two different heavy-light pairs, and the standard fixes — murinization, engineered disulfides, knob-into-hole — are point solutions that trade one liability for another. Here PROSS and FuncLib are applied to the constant regions rather than the variable domains, which is the right target: the constant region is shared across all TCRs, so a solution found once transfers. After several screening rounds the SET variant shows markedly enhanced surface expression, higher functional avidity and reduced mispairing, with the functional consequences that matter — more cytokine secretion, greater activation, better cytotoxicity, and potent tumor control with improved survival in xenografts. Two details elevate this above a typical stability-engineering paper. First, the benefit reproduces across six different TCRs, which is the test that separates a scaffold improvement from a lucky variant. Second, SET functions in CD4 T cells, where transgenic TCRs are usually poorly expressed and largely written off, widening the effector compartment a TCR product can use. The argument applies directly to engager work: constant-domain redesign is an underused route to solving assembly without touching the paratope.

DICE score: 4 — Moves computational design from paratopes to the shared constant scaffold and demonstrates transferability across six TCRs, the property that makes a chain-pairing solution reusable rather than bespoke.

Fleishman lab (Weizmann Institute) with Cohen lab (Bar-Ilan University).

### 7. Near pan-alphavirus neutralization by an antibody-nanobody bispecific

Pletnev S, Gardner CL, Sutton M, Zhang B, Callahan V, Green J, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2731281](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2731281)

Alphavirus E1/E2 biparatopic: V2C3 nanobody fused via a 30-residue linker to the heavy chain of IgG SKT05.

This is a linker-and-geometry study wearing virology clothes, and it repays reading that way. The design question — can two binders against different epitopes on the same antigen be fused so the combination covers more target space than either alone, without the fusion degrading either arm — is exactly the question a biparatopic tumor arm poses. The authors built 23 SKT05-nanobody bispecifics by structure-based design, screened them against pseudotyped encephalitic alphaviruses, then took ten forward against live arthritogenic viruses, with four retaining potent neutralization across Mayaro, O'nyong'nyong, Ross River and Una. The winner, V2C3-30-SKT05H, neutralized 9 of 11 tested alphaviruses phylogenetically proximal to all 26 that infect humans, and reduced viral loads in chikungunya-infected mice. Two observations matter for format work. The 30-residue linker length was selected empirically from a designed series rather than assumed, and electron microscopy showed the bispecific inducing disorder in E1E2 spikes — a mechanism that emerges only from the fused geometry and not from either binder separately. That is the strongest available demonstration that in a biparatopic construct the linker is a functional element, not connective tissue.

DICE score: 4 — Shows that a systematically varied IgG-nanobody fusion series yields both breadth and a fusion-specific mechanism of action, with the linker treated as a designed variable rather than a default.

NIAID Vaccine Research Center and Emerging Virus Immunity Unit with Columbia University and USAMRIID.

### 8. TROP2-targeting chimeras (TRTACs) for tumor cell-selective membrane protein degradation and enhanced drug delivery

Chen L, Fu X, Dong W, Deng X, Chen S, Wang F, et al.

*Advanced Science*

[10.1002/advs.77898](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77898)

TROP2 × EGFR / HER2 / PD-L1 bispecific nanobody-nanobody fusions (~30 kDa) as lysosome-targeting degraders, plus payload-conjugated variants.

Extracellular targeted protein degradation has been constrained by a short list of lysosome-targeting receptors, most of which are broadly expressed and therefore give you degradation everywhere. Nominating TROP2 — already clinically validated as an ADC target and tumor-enriched — as the shuttle receptor converts the selectivity problem into a target-selection problem the field knows how to solve. The format is the interesting part for engager work: a fully genetically encoded bispecific VHH-VHH of about 30 kDa, the same size class as a tandem scFv engager, inheriting both its tumor penetration advantage and its short half-life. Modularity is demonstrated rather than claimed, with the same TROP2 arm driving degradation of EGFR, HER2 and PD-L1 preferentially in tumor cells over non-malignant cells. Appending a cytotoxic payload gives a construct that both degrades and delivers, with the HER2 version reaching 50% viability reduction at 0.2 nM on SKBR3 and in vivo activity without weight loss or organ histopathology. The obvious extension, unexplored here, is whether the same TROP2-anchored geometry tolerates a CD3 arm — whether one tumor antigen can serve as both degradation shuttle and synapse anchor.

DICE score: 3 — A clean demonstration that degrader selectivity can be bought by choosing a tumor-enriched shuttle receptor, in a modular small bispecific VHH format that transfers directly to engager design.

East China University of Science and Technology.

### 9. Degrading GPX4 via biomimetic nanoparticle-mediated in situ synthesis of deep-learning-designed binder-degron chimeras for prostate cancer therapy

Zhang SH, An Y, Zeng N, Liu MC, Sun JX, Gong X, et al.

*Advanced Science*

[10.1002/advs.77932](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77932)

De novo BindCraft-designed GPX4 binder fused to a C-terminal combinational degron, delivered as plasmid in a PSMA-targeted, CD47-camouflaged nanocarrier.

Two things are worth separating here. The first is that a deep-learning binder generator was used to hit an intracellular target where no antibody route exists, and the resulting binder was functional enough to serve as the targeting element of a degrader — another datapoint for de novo design working on a real target rather than a benchmark. The second, and the more interesting one for a protein engineer, is the delivery inversion: rather than manufacturing and delivering the protein, the authors encode the binder-degron chimera on a plasmid and have the tumor cell synthesize it in situ, with the nanocarrier's PSMA targeting and CD47 camouflage supplying the selectivity the protein itself does not have. That is the same move the mRNA-encoded CD19 engager made on the clinical side — let the delivery vehicle carry the specificity and the exposure profile, and let the protein be simple. The mechanism is credible, with CUL2-RING recruitment driving proteasomal GPX4 degradation and ferroptosis, but the causal chain from designed binder to tumor control runs through enough layers that attributing efficacy to binder quality specifically is not possible from the data shown.

DICE score: 3 — Useful proof that generative binder design plus genetically encoded delivery can reach an intracellular target, though the stacked platform makes the designed binder's own contribution hard to isolate.

Tongji Hospital, Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

### 10. Risk factors for CRS and ICANS with bispecific antibodies for aggressive lymphomas

Gill VA, Ollila TA, Ghosh U, Epperla N, Fitzgerald LA, Grover NS, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020595](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2026020595)

CD20 × CD3 engagers (glofitamab, mosunetuzumab, epcoritamab) — real-world toxicity across 255 patients at 14 institutions.

The single most useful number here is the one that is lower than expected: CRS rates for glofitamab and epcoritamab monotherapy were lower in practice than in their registrational trials. That matters for format arguments, because trial CRS rates are routinely cited as intrinsic properties of a molecule's CD3 affinity or valency when a substantial part of the signal may be population- and protocol-dependent. Across 255 patients CRS occurred in 31% (5% grade 3+) and ICANS in 8% (2% grade 3+), with no significant differences between the three agents — notable given that they differ in format (2:1 versus 1:1), Fc content and administration route, and an argument that target biology and dosing schedule dominate over architecture within a target class. The predictors are actionable: concurrent systemic chemotherapy associated with grade 2+ CRS, and any-grade CRS plus elevated ferritin independently associated with ICANS. All grade 3+ CRS patients had CRP ≥15 mg/L and ferritin >336 ng/mL where measured, giving candidate thresholds for outpatient administration triage. Retrospective and needing prospective validation, but the kind of numbers that shift practice before they are validated.

DICE score: 3 — The largest real-world CRS/ICANS comparison across three CD20 bispecifics, and its finding of no between-agent difference undercuts a common format-based explanation for toxicity.

Clinical-stage molecules. Fourteen-institution US consortium led from Brown University.

### 11. Reduced pathogen-specific T cell response with BCMA-targeted T cell-engaging therapy to multiple myeloma

Steiro I, Tryggestad SS, Hess ES, Hella H, Abdollahi P, Moore KLF, et al.

*EJHaem*

[10.1002/jha2.70388](https://doi.org/10.1002/jha2.70388)

BCMA × CD3 BiTE (pavurutamab) — effect on the pathogen-specific memory T cell compartment.

The infection burden of BCMA engagers has been attributed almost entirely to B cell and plasma cell aplasia plus hypogammaglobulinemia, which frames it as a downstream consequence of on-target depletion and therefore something to manage with immunoglobulin replacement. This preliminary study points at a second, additive mechanism on the effector side: T cells cultured with pavurutamab showed reduced pathogen-specific responses by ELISpot and flow cytometry while still killing myeloma cells, and the same reduction in pathogen-specific memory responses appeared in PBMCs from four patients responding to BCMA bispecific therapy. If this holds, polyclonal CD3 engagement is not a neutral recruitment event — it competes with or degrades the pre-existing memory repertoire, meaning the effector arm itself contributes to infection risk independent of the target arm. The n is small and the mechanism unresolved, so this is hypothesis-generating rather than settled. But it bears directly on the case for intermittent dosing and conditionally active formats, because both reduce cumulative CD3 occupancy rather than merely sparing the B cell compartment.

DICE score: 3 — Small but pointed evidence that CD3 engagement itself, not only target-cell depletion, erodes pathogen-specific memory — a mechanism that would change how engager infection risk is attributed.

Clinical-stage molecule. NTNU Trondheim with Oslo Myeloma Center.

### 12. Teclistamab for the treatment of autoantibody-mediated coagulation disorders

Thaler J, Gleixner K, Eischer L, Kammerer G, Rettl J, Sailer L, et al.

*Journal of Thrombosis and Haemostasis*

[10.1016/j.jtha.2026.09.028](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtha.2026.09.028)

BCMA × CD3 bispecific (teclistamab) repurposed against long-lived autoantibody-producing plasma cells.

The autoimmune expansion of T cell engagers has so far run almost entirely through CD19, on the logic that B cell depletion resets the autoreactive compartment. That logic has a known hole: long-lived plasma cells are CD19-negative or low, which is why some autoantibody-driven disease survives rituximab and, in the striking case reported alongside this series, survives CD19 CAR-T. Seven patients with acquired von Willebrand disease, acquired factor XI deficiency, or catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome all achieved complete remission on teclistamab at a median of 24 weeks, including two CAPS patients with no detectable paraprotein — meaning the target was autoimmune plasma cells rather than a clonal population. Tolerability is what makes this actionable: grade 1 CRS in three patients, with immunoglobulin replacement throughout. A companion report in the same issue documents durable remission of relapsed acquired hemophilia A after anti-CD19 CAR-T failure (10.1016/j.jtha.2026.09.027), which is the cleanest possible demonstration that BCMA and CD19 reach different compartments. For target selection in autoimmunity this argues the choice between the two is disease-mechanism dependent, not a matter of which platform is more mature.

DICE score: 3 — Establishes BCMA engagers as reaching the long-lived plasma cell compartment that CD19-directed therapy misses, with a case of success after CD19 CAR-T failure making the compartment argument concrete.

Clinical-stage molecule. Medical University of Vienna.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Collateral immune cell signaling compromises the efficacy of T-cell engaging therapies for cardiac fibrosis

Yang S, Parvathaneni A, Pai YL, Koenig A, Kadyrov F, Owen MC, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.15.751626](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.751626)

FAP × CD3 BiTE targeting activated cardiac fibroblasts after myocardial infarction.

This is the most instructive negative result the field has produced in some time, and its structure is the point. Genetic ablation of FAP⁺ fibroblasts after myocardial infarction is beneficial. A FAP BiTE eliminates the same cells efficiently. And yet the BiTE makes cardiac function, remodeling and scar size worse. The mechanism is not off-target killing and not incomplete depletion: the engager provokes a local cytokine response in the heart with prominent IFNγ and CD40L pathway activation, and IFNγ signaling to surviving fibroblasts drives differentiation of an activated fibroblast lineage that does not normally appear in the infarcted heart. Critically, target cell killing was independent of IFNγ and CD40L, and blocking either pathway unmasked the protective effect — so the cytotoxicity and the harm are separable, which makes this an engineerable problem rather than a dead end. For anyone building engagers against stromal or non-malignant targets, this reframes the design objective: it is not sufficient to kill the right cell, because the cytokine field generated at the synapse reprograms the bystanders. It also sits directly alongside last week's FAP-CAR-NK result, and together they make FAP a cautionary case study rather than a promising stromal target.

DICE score: 4 — Shows that an engager can succeed completely at its cytotoxic objective and still worsen outcome through collateral cytokine signaling to bystander cells, with the harmful pathways identified and separable from killing.

Lavine lab, Washington University School of Medicine, with Amgen.

### 2. De novo design of protease-activatable cytokine prodrugs

Shin H, Tello JG, Kim CJ, Chun JH, Peprah F, Parent BD, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.19.752834](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.19.752834)

De novo designed masking domains for IL-21 and other cytokines, including AND-gated split systems requiring both target colocalization and proteolytic cleavage.

Protease-activated masking has been attempted repeatedly with natural or semi-rational masks, and the recurring problems are incomplete masking, poor expression, and masks that are themselves immunogenic. Designing the mask de novo attacks all three at once, because a designed domain can be shaped to the specific surface it must occlude and optimized for stability independently of it. The demonstration goes further than single-input gating: the authors build split systems AND-gated on two conditions, requiring both target-dependent colocalization and proteolytic unmasking before active cytokine is generated. That is the logic engager programs have been chasing for years with masked CD3 arms and split formats, expressed here in a cleaner system — a cytokine has one activity to gate rather than two arms to coordinate. In syngeneic tumor models the IL-21 prodrugs reduced treatment-associated toxicity while maintaining antitumor efficacy, which is the correct comparison and the one most masking papers avoid. The abstract does not quantify the masking ratio or the in vivo therapeutic index, so the magnitude of the win is not yet assessable, but the design strategy transfers directly to CD3 arms.

DICE score: 4 — A de novo route to masking domains, with genuine two-input AND gating and a toxicity-versus-efficacy separation in vivo, addressing the failure modes that have limited every previous masking chemistry.

Baker lab, University of Washington, with Dougan labs at Dana-Farber and MGH.

### 3. A low-matrix organoid-T cell co-culture platform for functional evaluation of T cell engagers in patient-derived colorectal cancer organoids

Pinna CMA, Rakhimova K, Zhang J, Bills L, Tee L, Blanc C, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.21.751493](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.751493)

EGFR × CD3 and HER2 × CD3 engagers assayed against 21 patient-derived colorectal cancer organoid models with allogeneic T cells.

The assay problem is the quiet bottleneck in engager development: potency measured against a cell line in 2D tells you almost nothing about a solid tumor, and matrix-embedded organoid co-cultures that are more realistic do not scale. The engineering here is unglamorous and useful — drop matrix to 1%, run organoids in suspension with a 1:1 medium split, use 5-day mature organoids with 7-day activated T cells over 72 hours — and it yields a format that preserves organoid integrity and T cell motility while being screenable. The output is the part worth attention: across 21 CRC models, EGFR- and HER2-targeting engagers produced IC50 values spanning 0.0188 to 54.77 nM, nearly four orders of magnitude, with 75-85% killing in the most sensitive models and 12 of 21 models supporting evaluation at all. That spread is the number engager programs should be uncomfortable about, because it means a single-model potency figure is close to meaningless for ranking candidates, and because inter-patient heterogeneity of this magnitude implies patient selection matters as much as molecule design. Allogeneic T cells rather than autologous is the obvious limitation, and the platform measures killing rather than therapeutic window.

DICE score: 3 — A scalable patient-derived assay format for engagers whose main contribution is demonstrating a four-log spread in potency across 21 models, which reframes how single-model IC50 values should be read.

University of Birmingham with AstraZeneca.

### 4. Bispecific GD2xB7-H3 antibody improves tumor targeting and reduces toxicity while maintaining efficacy for neuroblastoma

Erbe AK, Feils AS, Reinthaler S, Hampton A, Rosenkrans Z, Medina Guevara Y, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.21.753180](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.753180)

GD2 × B7-H3 dual-tumor-antigen bispecific requiring simultaneous engagement of both antigens for tight binding.

Anti-GD2 therapy is the standard case study for a target whose normal-tissue expression sets the dose: GD2 is on nerves, and the resulting neuropathic pain is dose-limiting and severe enough to shape the entire treatment schedule. The design response here is avidity-based AND gating against two tumor antigens rather than affinity attenuation against one — tight binding requires both GD2 and B7-H3, and B7-H3 is largely absent from nerves. The reported result is what the design predicts: improved tumor specificity, comparable antitumor efficacy to the monospecific anti-GD2 antibody, and reduced nerve binding with reduced pain-associated toxicity. This is a well-posed test of dual-antigen gating because the off-tumor tissue is anatomically distinct and the toxicity endpoint is directly measurable rather than inferred from biodistribution — which is more than most masking and gating papers can say. What the abstract does not supply is the quantitative avidity window: how much GD2 and B7-H3 co-expression is needed, and how steeply binding falls off on single-positive cells, which is the number that determines whether this generalizes to other antigen pairs.

DICE score: 3 — A clean demonstration of dual-tumor-antigen avidity gating solving a well-characterized on-target off-tumor toxicity, in a setting where the toxicity endpoint can actually be measured.

Sondel lab, University of Wisconsin-Madison.

### 5. A dynamically regulated designed IL-2 for tumor-localized signaling

Moffett HF, Weitzner BD, Lissner MM, Davenport TM, Haworth KG, Hayes ET, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.18.752703](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.18.752703)

Computationally re-topologized IL-2 (dIL-2) preserving IL-2Rβγ interfaces and ablating IL-2Rα binding, expressed from a T cell activation-responsive promoter in CAR-T cells.

The not-alpha IL-2 concept is well established; what is new is doing it by redesigning the molecule's topology rather than by mutating the IL-2Rα interface, and then gating expression rather than gating activity. The redesign increases stability while preserving the IL-2Rβγ interfaces and ablating IL-2Rα binding, giving the expected CD8 and NK bias with Treg avoidance. Putting that molecule under an activation-responsive promoter inside a CAR-T cell is the part worth noting, because it converts a pharmacological problem into a circuit problem: the cytokine is produced where and when the effector cell sees antigen, a form of spatial restriction no dosing schedule can achieve. The reported outcome — CAR-T proliferation with preserved stemness and durable elimination in two solid tumor models — is the right endpoint, since stemness preservation is precisely what IL-2 usually costs. A companion preprint from the same group integrates dIL-2 with a shed-antigen-resistant mesothelin CAR, an exhaustion-resistant promoter and an EGFRopt safety switch in a single vector (10.64898/2026.09.18.752754). Read alongside the Baker prodrug work, this is the week's second answer to the same question, and the two are complementary: design the protein to be conditionally active, or design the cell to conditionally make it.

DICE score: 3 — Solid execution of topology-level cytokine redesign coupled to transcriptional gating, though the components are each precedented and the comparison is against wild-type IL-2 rather than an existing not-alpha variant.

## Weekly Themes

Conditional activity dominates this week, arrived at from four independent directions within seven days. The Georgia Tech masked BiTE gates a CD3 arm with cleavable coiled-coil zippers and reports a 400-800-fold masking swing; the Baker lab designs masking domains de novo for cytokines and adds genuine two-input AND gating on colocalization plus proteolysis; Outpace gates a re-topologized IL-2 transcriptionally inside the effector cell; and the Wisconsin GD2×B7-H3 bispecific gates by requiring two tumor antigens at once. Four different mechanisms — steric, designed-interface, transcriptional and avidity-based — converging on the same conclusion, that the therapeutic window is bought by restricting where activity exists rather than by reducing how much of it there is. Worth noting that only the GD2 paper actually measures the off-tumor toxicity it claims to spare, in a tissue where that measurement is possible; the rest report masking ratios and tumor efficacy, the same gap flagged in earlier weeks. The second thread is binder-format evidence finally arriving in a form that can settle arguments. The NEJM anito-cel study puts a synthetic d-domain against a reconstructed cilta-cel dual-VHH binder and finds the faster off-rate gives equal cytotoxicity with less cytokine release and no antigen-independent activation — which, read with last week's demonstration that cilta-cel's biparatopic advantage largely vanished under a different promoter, means two independent lines of evidence now say the dual-VHH format's reputation rested on confounded comparisons. Alongside it, the CRS/ICANS series across 255 lymphoma patients finds no toxicity difference between three CD20 bispecifics that differ in valency, Fc content and route, which points the same way: within a target class, schedule and target biology are explaining more than architecture. Third, computational design keeps moving off the paratope — SET redesigns TCR constant regions and shows the benefit transferring across six TCRs, Outpace redesigns IL-2's topology while preserving its receptor interfaces, BindCraft supplies a binder against an intracellular target no antibody could reach, and this week also brought the VDJdb 2026 update annotating TCR:epitope records with paratope embeddings and predicted structures (10.1093/nar/gkag904) plus an open, CDR H3-cluster-validated developability predictor (10.64898/2026.09.16.750421) that echoes last week's argument about random splits inflating reported performance. Against all this sits the FAP BiTE cardiac fibrosis preprint, which everyone building engagers against non-malignant targets should read: the molecule did exactly what it was designed to do, eliminated exactly the cells genetic ablation showed were worth eliminating, and made the outcome worse — because IFNγ and CD40L signaling at the synapse reprogrammed surviving fibroblasts into a lineage that does not otherwise exist in that tissue. Killing correctly is not the same as helping, and the cytokine field around a synapse is a design output nobody currently specifies. On targets, the MR1 nanobody work is the week's most consequential opening, a TCR-mimic axis that is HLA-unrestricted by construction and reads a metabolite rather than a peptide, with structures proving the recognition mode; whether a tumor presents an MR1 ligand densely enough without exogenous loading is now the gating question for a whole new class of engagers. And on assays, the Birmingham/AstraZeneca organoid platform reports engager IC50 values spanning nearly four orders of magnitude across 21 patient-derived colorectal models — a spread that should make anyone ranking candidates on single-model potency uncomfortable.
