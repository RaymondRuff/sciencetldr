## Top Papers

### 1. Validation and analysis of 12,000 AI-driven CAR-T designs in the Bits to Binders competition

Kosonocky CW, Abel AM, Feller AL, Cifuentes Rieffer AE, Woolley PR, Lála J, et al.

*Molecular Systems Biology*

[10.1038/s44320-026-00246-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44320-026-00246-1)

De novo 80-amino-acid minibinders against human CD20, deployed as CAR binding domains.

Every de novo binder paper reports affinity; almost none report whether the binder works as a functional module in a therapeutic construct, which is the only question that matters for an engager arm. Bits to Binders closes that gap at a scale no single lab could: 12,000 designs from teams in 42 countries, all 80-residue minibinders against CD20, all screened in the same pooled CAR-T proliferation assay, with 707 showing significant CD20-specific enrichment and top candidates validated individually for proliferation, expansion, cytokine production and lysis. The spread is the finding — team hit rates ran from 0.6% to 38.4% on an identical target with identical validation, which says method choice, not target difficulty, dominates outcomes. The authors then extract what separated the good campaigns: factors correlating with DNA synthesis success, expression and target-specific activation that nearly double hit rates when applied as a retrospective filter. For engager programs the transferable content is twofold — a prospective filter set for minibinder arms, and a public dataset of functionally labeled binder designs, which is exactly the label type the field has been short of. Caveat: one target, one easy epitope, and proliferation-based pooled screening rewards agonism rather than the tunable, avidity-driven engagement an engager arm actually needs.

DICE score: 4 — Converts de novo binder evaluation from affinity to higher-order cellular function at 12,000-design scale and releases the functionally labeled dataset plus a filter set that nearly doubles success rates.

Marcotte lab (UT Austin) with the BioML Society, Ellington lab and LEAH Laboratories.

### 2. LIF and let die: generation, mechanistical and structural characterization of bispecific antibodies triggering LIF receptor activation independent of the cytokine LIF

Harwardt J, Pudewell S, Keil K, Lipinski B, Unmuth L, Hue Tran TH, et al.

*Journal of Biological Chemistry*

[10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113634](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113634)

LIFR × gp130 bispecific single-domain antibody (sdAb) heterodimer acting as a cytokine-receptor agonist.

Receptor-clustering bispecifics are usually built to block or to kill; this one is built to signal, and the engineering question it answers is the same one that governs engager potency — which geometry of a two-arm molecule produces a productive receptor complex. Fourteen LIFR-binding sdAbs from camelid immunization and yeast surface display were each paired with a fixed gp130-targeting sdAb, and only one of the fourteen pairs reconstituted LIF-like proliferation on Ba/F3 reporters, with STAT3 phosphorylation confirmed in HeLa and TF-1 cells. Cryo-EM then explains why: the functional bispecific engages a distinct LIFR epitope and assembles a receptor architecture unlike the natural LIF complex, so agonism came from a non-native geometry rather than from mimicking the cytokine. Agonism was improved further by antibody engineering. For engager design the lesson is paratope-dependence quantified — 1 in 14 arms against the same receptor produced signalling, which is a sharper statement of the epitope-geometry problem than the usual format screen, and sdAb building blocks make the geometry enumerable. Limits: Ba/F3 and cell-line pharmacology only, no in vivo work, and IL-6-family receptors are unusually permissive about how they dimerize.

DICE score: 4 — Shows that an sdAb bispecific can agonize a heterodimeric cytokine receptor through a non-native receptor architecture, with cryo-EM establishing the geometry rather than inferring it from screening.

Zielonka group (Merck Healthcare KGaA, Darmstadt) with Ward lab (Scripps) and Scheller lab (Düsseldorf).

### 3. MK-4002, a B-cell maturation antigen-targeting T-cell engager, in relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma: a first-in-human, open-label, phase 1 study

Madan S, Costello C, Lipe B, Cowan AJ, Medvedova E, Hillengass J, et al.

*The Lancet Haematology*

[10.1016/S2352-3026(26)00228-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-3026(26)00228-0)

Trispecific BCMA-targeting T-cell engager (Harpoon TriTAC-class architecture), 97 patients, intravenous weekly or every 2 weeks.

The number to carry out of this paper is 30% — all-grade cytokine release syndrome across 97 heavily pretreated patients (median six prior lines), with only two grade 3 events and none worse. Set against the 70–80% CRS typical of BCMA bispecifics, including last week's GPRC5D dataset at 75%, that is the strongest clinical evidence yet that a trispecific architecture built around a serum-albumin-binding half-life domain and a small effector arm buys tolerability rather than just convenience. Activity is real but not better than the class: ORR 41% at 2.15–6 mg, 63% at 12 mg, 48% at 24 mg, with 12 mg chosen as preliminary RP2D — note the non-monotonic dose–response, which is what a bell-shaped engager exposure curve looks like in a clinic and an argument for exposure-guided rather than max-tolerated dosing. Median half-life was 68 h, so albumin binding delivered a weekly-to-biweekly schedule on an intravenous molecule without an Fc. The accompanying Lancet Haematology commentary makes the same architectural point. The honest caveat is that MTD was never reached in stepped-dose cohorts, dose escalation stopped for other reasons, and a 2020–2023 single-arm dose-finding study cannot be cross-compared with a contemporary bispecific trial on toxicity alone.

DICE score: 4 — First-in-human data for a trispecific BCMA engager showing 30% all-grade CRS against a class norm roughly twice that, with a non-monotonic dose–response that argues for exposure-guided dosing.

Clinical-stage. Harpoon Therapeutics, a subsidiary of Merck & Co.

### 4. Cell-delivered CD3×CD19 T-cell engager by STAb-T19 cells mediates leukemia clearance with limited systemic exposure

Sanz-Ortega L, Barba-Sarasúa E, Zagorac I, Tapia-Galisteo A, Rivas-Gómez L, López N, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-015573](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-015573)

CD19 × CD3 tandem engager secreted in situ by engineered T cells (STAb-T, secreting T-cell-redirecting bispecific antibodies).

The STAb-T concept — put the engager gene in the T cell and let the cell be the delivery vehicle — has always had an obvious pharmacology argument and thin evidence for it. This paper supplies the measurement: leukemia clearance with limited systemic engager exposure, which is the claim the whole approach rests on. If local secretion at the tumor is what drives killing, the therapeutic index problem that forces step-up dosing, CRS prophylaxis and hospitalization on systemically dosed engagers becomes a biodistribution problem instead, and the engager's own pharmacokinetics — the thing half-life extension exists to fix — largely stops mattering. The construct also recruits bystander untransduced T cells, which is why STAb-T reaches effector numbers CAR-T does not. For a protein engineer the design consequence runs the other way from the usual brief: a secreted engager wants short systemic persistence and high local potency, inverting the half-life-extension logic that dominates the field, and it puts expression level, secretion efficiency and stability in the extracellular milieu on the critical path instead of Fc or albumin engineering.

DICE score: 4 — Pharmacokinetic evidence that in situ engager secretion decouples efficacy from systemic exposure, which inverts the half-life-extension design brief for a whole class of molecules.

Álvarez-Vallina lab, Hospital 12 de Octubre / CNIO, Madrid.

### 5. Catching enhanced flexibility in antibody-antigen recognition: critical evaluation of physics-based and AI-driven methods

Xu H, Mi R, Bai G, Kang Y, Sun C, Wang Y, Chen C, Ma B

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2743404](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2743404)

HyHEL10 antibody lineage against lysozyme variants of differing conformational flexibility; MM/PBSA versus Chai-1 and related structure predictors.

Two results, and the mechanistic one is the surprise: the affinity-matured HyHEL10 antibody achieves high affinity by actively destabilizing its own CDR-H1 loop, capturing mobile antigen variants through what the authors call entropy-buffered conformational selection. That is the opposite of the rigidification story affinity maturation is usually told as, and if it generalizes it is a direct warning to anyone maturing an engager arm by optimizing a static predicted interface. The benchmarking result is the practical one: both MM/PBSA and AI structure predictors fail to rank affinities correctly on the more flexible systems — physics from sampling limits, AI from inaccurate structures — and supplying structural templates to Chai-1 recovers much of the correlation. Polarization effects also scale with flexibility, which is why fixed-charge scoring degrades. The recommended workflow (AI for fast screening, physics for evaluation, templates where available) is sensible but unvalidated prospectively, and the whole study rests on one well-characterized lineage against one antigen.

DICE score: 3 — Identifies loop destabilization as an affinity-maturation mechanism and quantifies where both physics-based and AI affinity ranking break down on flexible epitopes, with a template-based mitigation.

Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Ma lab) with Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

### 6. A dual-glycoprotein-targeting bispecific nanoantibody confers potent neutralization against Nipah virus

Wang S, Zhang X, Zhang H, Rao G, Yao Y, Tian G, et al.

*Virologica Sinica*

[10.1016/j.virs.2026.09.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.virs.2026.09.014)

Nipah RBP × F bispecific nanobody fusion (2A1-3-8–(AP)–G-3-1) with half-life extension.

The engineering content here is linker geometry, not virology. A new C-type nanobody against the RBP head domain was fused to an existing F-protein nanobody through a rigid (AP)n linker, and the result was improved and — the word the authors use — balanced neutralizing activity across both antigens, with in vivo prophylaxis confirmed for the bispecific and authentic-virus protection for the half-life-extended monovalent arm. Balanced is the operative claim: in any two-arm construct the arms compete for productive geometry, and a flexible glycine-serine linker lets the molecule satisfy one arm at the other's expense. A rigid alanine-proline spacer fixes inter-paratope distance and orientation, which is the same lever that sets whether a tandem engager can bridge a synapse at the right intermembrane spacing. Rigid-linker tandems are not new, but a side-by-side demonstration that rigidity rebalances dual engagement in a validated in vivo model is directly portable to tandem scFv and VHH engager formats. BSL-4 constraints mean the bispecific's in vivo data are pseudovirus-based.

DICE score: 3 — Demonstrates that a rigid (AP)n linker rebalances engagement across two arms of a nanobody tandem in vivo, a directly transferable geometry lever for tandem engager formats.

Wuhan Institute of Virology, CAS.

### 7. Translational and phase I evaluation of AZD0292, a novel, half-life extended anti-Pseudomonal Psl-PcrV bispecific monoclonal antibody

L E, Riff C, DiGiandomenico A, Reid F, Dsilva L, Kehl M, et al.

*The Journal of Infectious Diseases*

[10.1093/infdis/jiag500](https://doi.org/10.1093/infdis/jiag500)

Psl × PcrV bispecific IgG with an Fc N3Y substitution for FcRn-mediated half-life extension; 32 healthy adults, 100–2,000 mg.

This is a clean human read-out on an Fc half-life variant in a bispecific chassis, which is the part worth borrowing. AZD0292 is gremubamab with a single Fc N3Y substitution; the substitution delivered roughly 55% lower clearance in human FcRn transgenic mice and a geometric mean half-life of 24–47 days in healthy adults, with functional opsonophagocytic and anti-cytotoxicity activity preserved relative to the parent and no treatment-emergent anti-drug antibodies across 61–161 days of follow-up. The immunogenicity result matters more than the number: Fc mutations that raise FcRn affinity are a standing immunogenicity and safety concern, and zero ADA with no meaningful complement activation in 24 dosed subjects is a useful data point for anyone considering the same route on an Fc-bearing engager. Exposure was slightly less than dose-proportional across a 20-fold range, hinting at target-independent clearance at the top end. Cohorts are small, follow-up short for a 47-day half-life, and healthy-volunteer PK will not survive contact with an infected airway — the authors say as much.

DICE score: 3 — Human pharmacokinetic and immunogenicity data for a single-substitution Fc half-life variant in a bispecific, with 24–47 day half-life and no anti-drug antibodies.

Clinical-stage. AstraZeneca.

### 8. Reviving low-affinity glycan-targeting CAR with combinatorial design

Forcados C, Köksal H, Scherer E, Abrantes R, Dillard P, Duarte HO, et al.

*Cell Reports Medicine*

[10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.103108](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.103108)

CD75s (α2,6-sialylated N-glycan) CAR derived from the IgM antibody HH2, combined with CD19CAR.

Two things here transfer to engagers, and neither is about CAR-T. First, a glycan epitope is made into a usable targeting moiety: CD75s is a tumor-associated sialylation state rather than a protein antigen, and HH2CAR is cytotoxic against B-cell lymphoma lines while inducing little or no cytokine release — a dissociation between killing and cytokine output that is precisely the profile an engager field obsessed with CRS keeps asking for, and that deserves mechanistic follow-up on its own. Second, the salvage result: HH2CAR alone cannot control tumors in vivo, but combined with CD19CAR it becomes functional, and the pair also protects against the on-target/off-tumor liability of CD19 single targeting. The generalizable claim — that a binder too weak to work alone can be rescued by combinatorial avidity rather than discarded — reframes what counts as a failed arm in a multispecific campaign, where sub-threshold affinity is a design input rather than a disqualification. Caveats: glycan epitope density is hard to control, and IgM-derived paratopes bring their own developability problems in a secreted format.

DICE score: 3 — Shows a sub-threshold glycan-targeting binder can be rescued by combinatorial avidity, and reports killing substantially uncoupled from cytokine release.

Wälchli and Inderberg labs, Oslo University Hospital, with i3S Porto.

### 9. KPNA2-mediated MYC nuclear import enhances CAR-T cell memory and potentiates antitumor efficacy

Han S, Kong D, Yang C, Ye F, Yue J, Wang K, et al.

*Blood*

[10.1182/blood.2026034296](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2026034296)

KPNA2 co-expression in CAR-T cells to drive MYC nuclear translocation; not an engager molecule.

The transferable insight is a negative control that explains a long-standing failure: overexpressing MYC in CAR-T cells does essentially nothing, because the limiting step is nuclear import, not abundance. Supplying KPNA2 moves MYC into the nucleus and produces the memory phenotype, cytotoxicity and in vivo antitumor gains that MYC overexpression alone does not, with KPNA2 expression and MYC pathway activation correlating with potency in clinical CAR-T products and no evidence of transformation risk in the engineered cells. For engagers this is relevant in two ways. It identifies a transcriptional axis whose activation state predicts effector-cell fitness, which is a candidate pharmacodynamic biomarker for patients being dosed with a T-cell engager into an exhausted repertoire. And it is a reminder that for engagers — unlike CAR-T — the effector cell cannot be engineered, so the only available lever on this axis is the signalling quality of the synapse the molecule builds, which argues for costimulatory multispecific formats over pure CD3 crosslinking. The work is cell-intrinsic engineering with no engager data.

DICE score: 3 — Localizes the MYC bottleneck in T-cell memory formation to nuclear import and supplies an actionable axis whose activation state tracks effector-cell potency.

Zhejiang University School of Medicine (Huang and Hu labs).

### 10. Development and application of a mechanistic model for protein A resolution of a bispecific antibody for process characterization and optimization

Wadsworth S, Reilly J, Mattila J, Cowan C, Bak H

*Biotechnology Progress*

[10.1002/btpr.88560](https://doi.org/10.1002/btpr.88560)

Regeneron common-light-chain bispecific with a Protein-A-ablating dipeptide substitution in one heavy chain; ternary resolving Protein A chromatography.

Heterodimer purification is where elegant bispecific designs go to die, and the dipeptide Protein-A-ablation trick — one heavy chain engineered not to bind Protein A, so heterodimer and the two homodimers differ in avidity and resolve on one column — is one of the few format-agnostic answers. What has been missing is predictability. A general rate transport model with a colloidal particle adsorption isotherm, calibrated on ten laboratory runs, predicts bispecific yield within 3% and purity within 1% on average, and was then used to optimize the step and map its design space. That turns a ternary separation from an empirical campaign into something you can interrogate in silico before committing material, on commercially available GMP-compatible resins. The reason this is worth a protein engineer's attention rather than a process engineer's alone: if the separation's design space is predictable, the avidity difference engineered into the molecule becomes a quantitative design target, and the purification consequences of a chain-pairing choice can be evaluated during format selection instead of discovered at scale-up. One molecule, one platform, and the model is calibrated rather than first-principles.

DICE score: 3 — Makes avidity-based ternary Protein A resolution of a bispecific predictable to within 3% yield and 1% purity, moving heterodimer purification into the design-space-by-model regime.

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals.

### 11. Next-generation glutamine synthetase expression vector for therapeutic protein manufacturing using CHO cells

Johari YB, Wensley SJ, Ammar MA, Srivastava A, Ragurajan C, Patel J, et al.

*Biotechnology and Bioengineering*

[10.1002/bit.70405](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.70405)

Synthetic LHP-1 promoter plus bacterial origin of replication as a dual-function glutamine synthetase promoter; monoclonal and bispecific antibody formats in CHOK1SV GS-KO.

Multispecific programs are chronically material-limited in early development, and the fix here is upstream of any protein engineering. A synthetic constitutive promoter, LHP-1, raised titers up to 123% over CMV across both monoclonal and bispecific formats, and the authors then show — for the first time, as far as the record goes — that the plasmid's bacterial origin of replication can double as a minimal promoter for the glutamine synthetase selection marker. That removes methionine sulfoximine from the workflow entirely while adding up to 73% titer over an SV40-driven GS vector. The delivered numbers are what matter for a program plan: 5.7 g/L from bulk pools with no decline over 30 days, a 23% higher median clone titer, and 8.3 g/L from the best clone. Bispecific formats being explicitly included is the reason this belongs in an engager digest — pool-stage multigram titers change how many formats a program can afford to express and compare, which is usually the real constraint on format screening. Stability data stop at 30 days and the bispecific was not characterized for chain-pairing quality at these titers.

DICE score: 3 — Delivers MSX-free multigram-per-litre bispecific expression from bulk pools via a dual-function origin-as-promoter design, easing the material constraint on early format screening.

Lonza Biologics, Slough.

### 12. Next-generation immune cell engagers for B cell depletion in autoimmune diseases

Fu Y, Robert B, Bitoun S, Martineau P, Konig MF, Avouac J, et al.

*Nature Reviews Drug Discovery*

[10.1038/s41573-026-01534-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41573-026-01534-z)

Review of CD19- and BCMA-directed engager architectures recruiting T cells, NK cells, γδ T cells or myeloid cells for autoimmune indications.

Worth reading for the design section rather than the clinical summary. The autoimmune setting inverts several assumptions that oncology engager design is built on: the target-cell burden is small and tissue-distributed rather than bulky, the effector repertoire is dysfunctional in a disease-specific way, the tolerance for CRS is far lower than in relapsed myeloma, and depth of depletion in tissue — not response rate — is the endpoint. The architectural implications the authors collect are the useful part: CD8-biased T-cell engagement to avoid CD4-driven cytokine output, costimulatory multispecific formats to rescue engagement by exhausted effectors, and NK or γδ T-cell recruitment to sidestep the T-cell compartment altogether. Each is a live format decision with an oncology counterpart, and the autoimmune requirement set is a stiffer test of all three. As a review it contributes no new data, and the clinical evidence base it draws on — early CD19- and BCMA-directed reports — is still thin enough that the architectural prescriptions are arguments rather than conclusions.

DICE score: 2 — A review, but one that assembles the autoimmune requirement set into concrete format prescriptions (CD8-biased engagement, costimulatory multispecifics, NK and γδ recruitment) that oncology-trained designers will not have internalized.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. A signaling-optimized TIGIT chimeric switch receptor promotes metabolic fitness and antitumor efficacy of TCR/CAR T cells

Antunes Ferreira E, Legscha KJ, Schmits UR, Reinbrecht A, Theobald M, Echchannaoui H

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.02.755760](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.02.755760)

TIGIT ectodomain fused to a CD28 signalling tail, with a point mutation tuning the activation threshold.

The interesting part is the failure of the obvious design. The prototype switch receptor — inhibitory TIGIT ectodomain on a CD28 tail — boosted immediate cytolysis but drove premature proliferative arrest and terminal differentiation through excessive tonic signalling; a single point mutation in the CD28 cytoplasmic domain dialled the activation threshold down and produced superior mitochondrial bioenergetics under chronic antigen exposure, durable persistence and complete tumor clearance in xenografts. For engager design the principle is the one that keeps recurring this week: more costimulation is not better, and the design variable is signal strength rather than signal presence — directly relevant to anyone building a costimulatory arm into a trispecific, where tonic signalling cannot be tuned after dosing.

DICE score: 3 — Demonstrates that costimulatory switch receptors need threshold tuning rather than maximal signalling, with a single-residue lever and metabolic read-outs.

### 2. Programmable antigen-presenting scaffolds expand rare tumor-antigen-specific T cells from peripheral blood

DeVilla JN, Binenbaum Y, Cieri N, Zhang DK, Ji H, Martinolli D'Arcy V, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.05.756899](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.05.756899)

APC-mimetic scaffolds presenting defined peptide-HLA and costimulatory ligands on a fluid lipid bilayer.

A modular biomaterial that independently varies peptide-HLA, costimulatory ligand and cytokine support, enriching MART-1-specific T cells above 2% of CD8+ cells in 35 of 50 healthy donors from undetectable baselines, to a median 13.7% in five of eight treatment-naive melanoma patients, and recovering an HLA-A*24:01-restricted personal neoantigen specificity that was undetectable in blood. Relevance to engagers is as a discovery engine: TCR-mimic and ImmTAC-like programs are bottlenecked on obtaining the rare T cell whose receptor defines the paratope you want to mimic, and a fluid-bilayer scaffold whose ligand composition is independently tunable is also an experimental system for asking what synapse composition a redirecting molecule should reconstruct.

DICE score: 3 — A tunable platform that recovers rare and personal-neoantigen-specific T cells from blood, relieving the input bottleneck for TCR-mimic paratope discovery.

### 3. Complex enzyme active site scaffolding by iteratively detuned catalytic guidance

Qian T, Wang W, Gao S, Muraishi A, Zhao B, An DN, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.02.756307](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.02.756307)

Iteratively detuned catalytic guidance for de novo generation of multi-residue active sites.

Not an antibody paper, but the method addresses the general problem of designing a scaffold that holds several functional residues in a prescribed three-dimensional relationship — the same constraint satisfaction as placing a discontinuous paratope or positioning two arms of a multispecific at a fixed geometry. Iteratively relaxing then re-imposing catalytic constraints during generation is a scheduling trick that should transfer to any design task with multiple hard geometric requirements that conflict early in generation.

DICE score: 3 — A constraint-scheduling advance for multi-residue geometric design, plausibly portable to discontinuous paratopes and fixed inter-arm geometry.

Baker lab, University of Washington.

## Weekly Themes

Three independent results this week point at the same conclusion from different directions: for a redirecting molecule, the design variable is the quality and strength of the signal it builds, not the amount. MK-4002's 30% all-grade CRS — roughly half the class norm for BCMA bispecifics, and less than half last week's GPRC5D figure — comes with a non-monotonic dose–response that makes the bell-shaped engager exposure curve visible in the clinic rather than in a model, and argues for exposure-guided dosing over max-tolerated. The TIGIT switch-receptor preprint shows the same thing intracellularly, where a maximal CD28 tail drove terminal differentiation and a single threshold-lowering mutation rescued persistence. And the HH2 glycan CAR reports cytotoxicity substantially uncoupled from cytokine release, which is the profile the field keeps trying to engineer and here appears as a property of the epitope class. The accompanying Lancet Haematology commentary, titled plainly "Rethinking anti-BCMA T-cell engager design", makes the architectural case explicitly.

The week's second thread is geometry, measured rather than screened. The LIF-mimetic bispecific found that one of fourteen sdAb arms against the same receptor produced signalling, with cryo-EM showing the functional complex adopts a non-native architecture — a sharper statement of paratope-dependence than any format screen produces. The Nipah nanobody tandem shows a rigid (AP)n linker rebalancing engagement across two arms in vivo. The mAbs flexibility study finds that affinity maturation can work by destabilizing a CDR loop, and that both MM/PBSA and AI structure predictors mis-rank affinities on flexible epitopes until templates are supplied. Together they describe a field that is moving from enumerating formats to measuring why a particular geometry works, and the AI tooling is not yet reliable where the interface moves.

Third, the infrastructure layer had an unusually strong week, and all of it eases constraints that bind engager programs specifically. Bits to Binders provides 12,000 functionally labeled de novo binder designs plus a prospective filter set, which is the label type the field has been short of; the Lonza vector work delivers multigram bispecific titers from bulk pools without methionine sulfoximine, loosening the material constraint on format screening; and Regeneron's mechanistic Protein A model makes avidity-based heterodimer resolution predictable to within 3% yield, which lets purification consequences enter format selection rather than ambush scale-up. Finally, the STAb-T19 result is worth flagging as a quiet inversion: if a cell-delivered engager clears leukemia with limited systemic exposure, then for that whole class the half-life extension the rest of the field is optimizing becomes the wrong objective, and expression, secretion and extracellular stability take its place.
