## Top Papers

### 1. A novel CD123xVδ2 bispecific T cell engager induces potent and tumor selective Vγ9Vδ2-T cell activity against CD123 expressing acute myeloid leukemia and myelodysplastic syndrome

Ruben JM, Mousset CM, van de Berg D, Hofman K, de Jong M, Brouwer P, et al.

*Clinical Immunology*

[10.1016/j.clim.2026.110786](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clim.2026.110786)

CD123 × Vδ2 half-life-extended bispecific T cell engager (γδ effector arm in place of CD3).

The therapeutic-window problem for CD123 engagers is that CD123 is not tumor-restricted — plasmacytoid dendritic cells and other normal compartments carry it — so a CD3 arm converts any CD123-positive cell into a target. This engager swaps the effector arm for Vδ2, recruiting Vγ9Vδ2 T cells instead of the bulk αβ repertoire, and the selectivity that follows is the interesting part: Vγ9Vδ2 cells preferentially lysed malignant over CD123-positive non-malignant cells including pDCs, and the construct induced lower CRS-associated cytokines than a matched CD123 × CD3 comparator while retaining serial-killing capacity at low E:T ratios. Half-life extension gave antibody-like PK in non-human primates, addressing the short-exposure penalty that γδ engagers have historically carried. Caveats: Vγ9Vδ2 cells are a small, variably abundant subset, so efficacy depends on an effector pool the patient may not have, and the preferential-lysis mechanism is not fully resolved here.

DICE score: 4 — A matched head-to-head against a CD3 version shows that changing the effector arm, not the tumor arm, buys both tumor selectivity and lower cytokine output, now entering phase 1.

Clinical-stage (phase 1, ACTRN12624001214527); Lava Therapeutics / Amsterdam UMC (van der Vliet group).

### 2. A Tumor-Activatable DNA Nanoengager That Orchestrates NK Receptor Clustering and Immune Checkpoint Blockage to Enhance Antitumor Cytotoxicity

Shi WJ, Li CQ, Lai WR, Li XM, Cheng HJ, Wang Y, et al.

*Journal of the American Chemical Society*

[10.1021/jacs.6c15706](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c15706)

NKp46 × PD-L1 plus sialidase, displayed on a two-layer DNA origami barrel with an albumin-binding peptide and a pH-triggered i-motif shield.

This is a multispecific engager built on a nucleic acid chassis rather than an antibody scaffold, and the chassis buys things a protein format struggles with: defined stoichiometry and spatial placement of three functional modules, plus a physically removable protective layer. The inner barrel surface carries anti-NKp46, anti-PD-L1 and sialidase; the outer layer carries ABD035 for half-life and an i-motif switch that dissociates in acidic tumor microenvironment, exposing the functional face only on arrival. The result is simultaneous NK receptor clustering, checkpoint blockade and enzymatic stripping of surface sialic acid to break the Siglec axis — three immunosuppressive mechanisms addressed by one addressable structure. Caveats: DNA origami carries well-known nuclease-stability and manufacturability burdens that no antibody format has, pH-gated unmasking is a weak discriminator in heterogeneous tumors, and the receptor-clustering geometry is asserted from the design rather than measured structurally.

DICE score: 4 — Uses origami addressability to co-locate agonist, checkpoint and enzymatic modules with controlled spacing, demonstrating a conditional multispecific architecture that protein formats cannot easily reproduce.

### 3. Development and preclinical evaluation of tetraspecific antibodies in combination with ex vivo expanded NK cells infusion for improved control of esophageal cancer

Liu Y, Fan X, Zuo X, Zhao J, Wang Y, Wu B, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-015492](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-015492)

EGFR × MET × NK-receptor tetraspecific antibodies, paired with adoptive transfer of ex vivo expanded NK cells.

Antigen-pair selection here is driven by patient data rather than convenience: transcriptomic profiling of ESCC found EGFR uniformly high across samples while MET was specifically upregulated in recurrent disease, so the two tumor arms cover both the bulk and the relapse compartment. The tetraspecific format adds NK-receptor engagement on top of dual tumor targeting, and the constructs raised NK cytotoxicity by up to 30% over NK cells alone, including against paclitaxel-resistant lines, with 82.3% and 65.7% tumor volume reductions in early- and advanced-stage xenografts plus activity in a metastatic model. The honest finding worth more attention than the efficacy numbers is that the same constructs induced NK exhaustion markers alongside activation — a valency-driven overstimulation signal that tetravalent engager designs should be screened for. Caveats: efficacy depends on co-infused NK cells, so this is a combination product, and the engineering characterization stops at FPLC and SPR quality control without format comparison.

DICE score: 4 — Data-driven antigen pairing for a tetraspecific NK engager with in vivo validation, and a candid activation-versus-exhaustion readout that is directly relevant to multivalent format design.

### 4. DuoBody®-FAPαxDR4 is a novel DR4 agonist that induces potent antitumor activity through FAPα-dependent, DR4-transactivation-mediated apoptosis

Laoukili J, Jongerius I, de Goeij BECG, Paauwe M, Souteyrand E, van Schelven S, et al.

*Clinical Cancer Research*

[10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0600](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0600)

FAPα × DR4 Fc-silenced IgG1 bispecific (DuoBody controlled Fab-arm exchange).

Death receptor agonism has failed clinically for exactly the reason CD3 engagers fight: a receptor that works anywhere kills everywhere, and the liver pays first. The design answer here is to make DR4 clustering require a second cell type — the bispecific binds DR4 on tumor cells and FAPα on cancer-associated fibroblasts, so productive agonism happens only at the fibroblast–tumor interface by trans-crosslinking. The molecule localized to that interface, induced conditional caspase-8 activation and tumor-cell killing while sparing the fibroblasts themselves, and notably produced no cytotoxicity in human primary liver spheroids, the assay that has historically killed this target class. Activity held up in PDX models and in a cecal-implantation model of spontaneous multiorgan metastasis, supporting a first-in-human trial. Caveats: the strategy inherits FAPα's own expression in wound healing and some normal stroma, and stroma-dependent agonism will underperform in FAPα-low tumors.

DICE score: 4 — Converts a cell-intrinsic agonist liability into a two-cell geometric requirement, with the hepatotoxicity assay that sank earlier DR agonists coming back clean.

Clinical-stage (first-in-human initiated); Genmab with UMC Utrecht.

### 5. SIV reservoir depletion and clearance from neonatal macaques following transient CCR5 depletion

Deere JD, Merriam D, Machmach K, Chang WLW, Nekorchuk M, Méndez-Lagares G, et al.

*Nature Microbiology*

[10.1038/s41564-026-02511-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02511-3)

CCR5 × CD3 bispecific antibody used as a single-dose depleting agent in ART-suppressed infant macaques.

The engager-relevant point is the target logic: rather than trying to find and kill latently infected cells, which cannot be recognized without reactivation, the CD3 arm is pointed at CCR5 to wipe out the host compartment the reservoir lives in. A single dose in ART-suppressed neonatal macaques depleted CCR5-expressing cells, accelerated viraemia decline, and after ART cessation most treated animals stayed aviraemic for more than three months, with several remaining aviraemic to the end of follow-up and no replication-competent virus recoverable — against rapid rebound in every ART-only animal. For engager designers this is a demonstration that a redirected-cytotoxicity molecule can be used for deliberate, time-limited ablation of a defined cell population as a curative maneuver, with the therapeutic window managed by transience rather than selectivity. Caveats: profound depletion of CCR5+ cells is a drastic intervention whose tolerability in neonates will not transfer unexamined to adults, and the engineering content is target choice, not format innovation.

DICE score: 4 — Shows a CD3 bispecific achieving what had previously required cytotoxic transplant conditioning, reframing engagers as tools for transient compartment ablation rather than only tumor lysis.

### 6. Bispecific dual T cell agonist invikafusp alfa in solid tumors: the phase 1/2 START-001 trial

Sullivan RJ, Tschernia NP, Friedman CF, Herrera M, Sonpavde GP, He K, et al.

*Nature Medicine*

[10.1038/s41591-026-04711-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04711-0)

Vβ6/Vβ10 × costimulatory dual T cell agonist antibody — no tumor-targeting arm.

Invikafusp inverts the engager premise: instead of bridging a tumor antigen to CD3, it selectively agonizes a defined slice of the TCR repertoire (Vβ6/Vβ10) and lets endogenous antigen specificity do the targeting. Dose escalation through 0.16 mg/kg never reached MTD, CRS was predominantly grade 1–2 with only two reversible DLTs, and at the optimal biological doses the objective response rate was 14.8% (4/27) — including confirmed partial responses in microsatellite-stable colorectal cancer and PD-L1-negative NSCLC, two settings where checkpoint blockade reliably fails. Pharmacodynamics matched the mechanism: dose-dependent expansion of peripheral and intratumoral Vβ6/Vβ10 T cells with a memory-like phenotype, tracked by IFN-γ and soluble CD25. Caveats: responses are modest and from small cohorts, a repertoire-selective agonist has no tumor-side specificity at all, and the approach depends on pre-existing tumor-reactive clones within the selected Vβ subsets.

DICE score: 4 — First clinical validation that selective Vβ-subset agonism, with no tumor-targeting arm, produces responses in immunologically cold tumors, establishing an RP2D and a clean PD chain.

Clinical-stage (phase 1/2, NCT05592626); Marengo Therapeutics with MGH and NCI.

### 7. Lung neuroendocrine cancer induces immunosuppressive macrophages via serotonin to drive resistance to the T cell engager tarlatamab

Nomura K, Fukami T, Kato S, Tanaka S, Fukuda A, Koike Y, et al.

*Cancer Cell*

[10.1016/j.ccell.2026.09.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2026.09.016)

DLL3 × CD3 engager (tarlatamab) resistance; 5-HT–HTR7 axis in tumor-associated macrophages.

Engager resistance is usually discussed as antigen loss or T cell exhaustion; this paper identifies a third route that is specific to the tumor lineage. DLL3-expressing neuroendocrine cancers upregulate TPH1 and secrete serotonin, which acts on HTR7 on tumor-associated macrophages to amplify their immunosuppressive program, impairing CD8 T cell function and blunting tarlatamab. The causal chain is established genetically in both directions — ablating HTR7 in macrophages or TPH1 in the cancer cells restores CD8 function and improves TCE and PD-1 blockade efficacy — and the human relevance comes from SCLC and large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma cohorts showing the TAM phenotype tracks with resistance. For engager programs in neuroendocrine indications this nominates a pharmacologically tractable combination partner, since TPH1 inhibitors and HTR7 antagonists already exist. Caveats: a neurotransmitter axis tied to neuroendocrine biology may not generalize to other engager targets, and the clinical correlation is associative.

DICE score: 4 — Defines a lineage-specific, drug-targetable resistance mechanism for an approved T cell engager with reciprocal genetic validation.

### 8. Architecture of the Norrin signaling complex and structure-guided surrogate design

Hu Q, Li D, Long T, Sun Y, Junge HJ, Schmiege P, Li X

*Nature Structural & Molecular Biology*

[10.1038/s41594-026-01909-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-026-01909-1)

FZD × LRP5 bispecific binders as Norrin surrogates; de novo designed FZD binder used as a crystallization/cryo-EM "protein glue".

Two separate pieces of engager-transferable method here. First, a de novo designed FZD binder is used as a structural glue to stabilize an otherwise intractable four-component Norrin–FZD–TSPAN12–LRP5 assembly — designed minibinders as structural tools, not just therapeutics, which is directly applicable to the problem of visualizing engager-bridged synapse complexes. Second, the structure (two curved Norrin homodimers bridging two FZD–TSPAN12 heterodimers, with LRP5 engaging both) is used to build FZD × LRP5 bispecific surrogates, and oligomerizing the surrogate increased signalling potency. That valency-potency relationship is the same lever engager designers pull when choosing between bivalent and tetravalent tumor arms, measured here against a known receptor geometry. Caveats: Wnt-pathway agonism is a different pharmacology from cytotoxic synapse formation, and potency gains from oligomerization say nothing about the therapeutic window.

DICE score: 3 — Structure-guided bispecific surrogate design with a quantified valency–potency relationship, plus a reusable trick of using de novo binders to stabilize multi-component receptor complexes.

### 9. Engineering a Hyperstable Synthetic Miniprotein for B7-H3 Targeting

Windsor HK, Ulen NC, Beaudry C, Hackel BJ

*Protein Engineering, Design and Selection*

[10.1093/protein/gzag031](https://doi.org/10.1093/protein/gzag031)

B7-H3-binding 40-residue βαββ miniprotein (computationally designed scaffold, yeast-display evolved).

A clean building-block paper: starting from a computationally designed 40-amino-acid βαββ scaffold, constrained diversification at six β-sheet sites — structurally distinct from the original α-helical paratope — plus nine α-helix positions, sorted by yeast display against detergent-solubilized cell lysate, yielded binders at 8.4 and 11 nM and a lead, βm.1, at 1.2 ± 0.2 nM with apparent Tm ≥91 °C and reversible unfolding. The paratope-migration result matters beyond this target: it shows a designed miniprotein scaffold can support high-affinity binding from a secondary surface, which multiplies the usable paratope area per scaffold. At 40 residues with that thermostability, βm.1 is a plausible engager arm for a tumor antigen whose clinical formats so far have been scFv- and antibody-based. Caveats: no engager construct is built or tested here, and lysate-based selection leaves cell-surface specificity and off-target profiling open.

DICE score: 3 — Delivers a single-digit-nanomolar, hyperstable 40-residue B7-H3 binder and shows a designed scaffold can host a high-affinity paratope on a second structural face.

Hackel lab, University of Minnesota.

### 10. Engineered NKG2C+ CD8+ TKLR cells exhibit effective antitumor control in mice while mitigating cytokine release syndrome

Lupo KB, Panjwani MK, Halawi AG, Shahid S, Sottile R, Lawry C, et al.

*Journal of Clinical Investigation*

[10.1172/JCI193053](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI193053)

NKG2C-selected NK-like TCRαβ+ CD8 T cells (TKLR) carrying a CD19 CAR (1928z) or an NY-ESO-1 TCR.

The CRS-versus-persistence tradeoff has been framed as a cell-type choice: CAR-T brings persistence and CRS, CAR-NK brings neither. This paper looks for a cell population that breaks the tradeoff, identifying and expanding NKG2C+ NK-like TCRαβ+ CD8 T cells that, once transduced with a conventional second-generation CD19 CAR, outperformed both conventional CD8 CAR-T and CAR-NK cells for tumor clearance and persistence while producing significantly less CRS and lower PD-1, TIM-3 and LAG-3. The same cells engineered with an NY-ESO-1 TCR controlled tumors better than TCR-engineered conventional CD8 T cells, so the advantage travels with the chassis rather than the receptor. For engager work this is a reminder that the cytokine ceiling is partly a property of the effector subset — the same logic the γδ engager above exploits pharmacologically. Caveats: NKG2C+ frequency is donor-variable and largely CMV-driven, and CRS readouts in xenograft models are imperfect proxies.

DICE score: 3 — Shows the CRS-versus-persistence tradeoff is a property of the effector subset rather than a fixed constraint, in matched CAR and TCR comparisons.

Hsu lab, Memorial Sloan Kettering.

### 11. Local T-cell engager secretion reprograms endogenous antitumor immunity and enhances CAR-T cell persistence in solid tumors

Arroyo-Ródenas J, Chen A, Tonne J, Thompson J, Sangsuwannukul T, Rivas-Sánchez M, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-016460](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-016460)

Engager-secreting engineered T cells (STAb-T-style local secretion) combined with CAR-T in solid tumor models.

Secreted-engager cell therapy — engineering the effector cell to manufacture the engager in situ rather than dosing it systemically — is the central claim of this group's platform, and the reported result extends it in two directions that matter for solid tumors: recruitment of endogenous, non-engineered immune effectors, and improved persistence of the CAR-T compartment itself. Local secretion is the exposure-control argument for engagers in solid tissue, trading systemic Cmax for sustained intratumoral concentration, and coupling it to CAR-T persistence suggests the secreted engager is doing more than adding a second killing mechanism. Caveat: the abstract was not posted at the time of writing, so the specific antigen pair, engager format and the magnitude of the persistence effect could not be verified from the record — treat the mechanism as described by the title until the full text is available.

DICE score: 3 — Extends secreted-engager cell therapy to endogenous immune recruitment and CAR-T persistence in solid tumors, though format details were unverifiable at the time of writing.

Alvarez-Vallina lab (H12O-CNIO, Madrid) with the Vile lab, Mayo Clinic.

### 12. A novel anti-PD-1/VEGF bispecific fusion protein with enhanced VEGF binding capacity and developability for synergistic immunotherapy

Mao D, Xu J, Zhang D, Hang T, Zheng C, Xu M, et al.

*Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications*

[10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154769](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154769)

PD-1 × VEGF bispecific: anti-PD-1 IgG4 (Fc-silenced) with tandem VEGFR1 D2 domains fused C-terminally for tetravalent VEGF capture.

PD-1 × VEGF is the most contested bispecific class in oncology right now, and most entrants use an antibody arm for VEGF. PKVBP instead appends tandem VEGFR1 D2 receptor domains to the C-terminus of an Fc-silenced IgG4, giving four VEGF-binding sites from a receptor-trap module rather than a paratope — a ligand-trap-on-antibody architecture that reportedly beats bevacizumab on VEGF affinity and capacity. The paper is unusually disciplined about developability for an early format report: expression and purification optimization, confirmed assembly and domain integrity, favorable thermal stability, and long-term plus stress stability with minimal oxidation and retained target engagement. Caveats: the data are biophysical and in vitro only, with no in vivo efficacy or PK, and receptor-domain fusions carry ligand-trap liabilities (target-mediated clearance, VEGF accumulation) that this characterization does not address.

DICE score: 3 — A genuinely different architecture for a crowded target pair — tetravalent receptor-domain trap on a silenced IgG4 — reported with developability data rather than efficacy claims alone.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Targeting poorly differentiated thyroid cancer with bispecific antibodies and CAR T cells from the human anti-TSHR autoantibody repertoire

Fischer C, Eugster A, Besemer B, Buchi M, Bauer M, Schultheiss C, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.19.760040](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.19.760040)

TSHR × CD3 bispecifics (anti-TSHR Fab × anti-CD3 scFv) built from eight anti-TSHR clones, four of them human autoimmune-derived, plus matched CAR T cells.

The binder-sourcing idea is the contribution: autoimmune disease has already performed in vivo affinity maturation against a tissue-restricted self antigen, so the anti-TSHR autoantibody repertoire from thyroid autoimmunity supplies sequence-defined, fully human arms without a discovery campaign. Eight clones were reformatted as Fab × scFv CD3 engagers and the leads as CARs, then compared across graded and mixed antigen densities; TSHR was retained on a substantial fraction of poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma including metastases, the lead bispecifics suppressed xenograft growth, and patient-derived T cells collected during or after treatment retained full effector function. Two format observations are worth noting: scFv heavy/light-chain orientation was decisive for the CARs but not the engagers, and the bispecifics cleared mixed cultures well beyond the antigen-positive fraction — a bystander effect benchmarked against mosunetuzumab.

DICE score: 4 — Turns an autoimmune autoantibody repertoire into a binder source for engagers against a tissue-restricted target, with a format-sensitivity comparison and a quantified bystander effect.

Binder lab, University of Basel, with Peipp (Kiel).

### 2. Domain-resolved epitope mapping of B7-H3-targeting VHH-Fcs defines isoform-selective binders for antibody-drug conjugate development

Fang X, Chalopin B, Gokturk SS, Bhagwani AS, Sudareva M, Molkenbur R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.16.759583](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.16.759583)

Six B7-H3-targeting VHH-Fcs mapped by SPR, epitope binning, domain-resolved deep mutational scanning and internalization, benchmarked against ifinatamab, mirzotamab and vobramitamab.

B7-H3's extracellular region exists as a 2Ig isoform with one IgV-IgC pair and a predominant 4Ig isoform with two near-identical tandem pairs, which makes "anti-B7-H3" an underspecified claim — and this panel shows it matters. Most VHH-Fcs bound the membrane-proximal pair more tightly than the distal one and 4Ig 10- to 100-fold over 2Ig, while all three clinical antibodies showed no such discrimination, and yeast display of each domain pair independently pinned the selectivity to specific residues. The result engager designers should sit with is that affinity, epitope and internalization behaved as independent properties: two VHH-Fcs with near-identical footprints and comparable affinities differed several-fold in internalization, and the best internalizer still made a weaker ADC than ifinatamab-exatecan. For an engager arm, where internalization is a liability rather than the mechanism, this panel plus the mapping method is directly reusable — and it pairs neatly with the hyperstable B7-H3 miniprotein above.

DICE score: 4 — Residue-level domain mapping resolves isoform and domain-pair selectivity that clinical B7-H3 antibodies lack, and decouples affinity, epitope and internalization as independent design variables.

Janda lab, Princess Máxima Center for Pediatric Oncology.

### 3. Programmable Surface Receptor-Mediated Cell Targeting with Designer Antibody-Routed Nanoparticles (DARTs)

Roth GV, Gengaro IR, Ahmadian N, Gao K, Wang D, Xu S, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.18.757718](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.18.757718)

Enveloped virus-like particles displaying generic bioorthogonal antibody-binding interfaces, conjugated to off-the-shelf antibodies to set tropism.

The architectural move is decoupling: rather than genetically fusing a targeting domain into the particle surface and re-making the vehicle for every new tropism, the particle presents a universal antibody-binding interface and any off-the-shelf antibody can be clipped on without genetic or chemical manipulation of the antibody. That turns tropism into a screening variable — the authors use it to interrogate which surface antigens are transduction-competent across cell types, find combinations that synergistically enhance entry, and identify CD56 as a previously uncharacterized entry antigen for hard-to-transduce fetal murine neural populations. For engager and multispecific programs the transferable element is the universal-adapter pattern itself: one vehicle, swappable specificity, with antigen screening and format engineering separated.

DICE score: 3 — A plug-and-play adapter architecture that makes targeting specificity a swappable input rather than a re-engineered vehicle, demonstrated to discover new entry antigens.

Qi lab, Stanford.

### 4. Decision benchmarking reveals when protein foundation models add experimental value

Ding W, Lu C, Li Y, Gong H, Wang Z

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.13.758966](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758966)

95 predictors evaluated across 217 ProteinGym assays and 2.47 million variants, scored by decision quality at matched experimental cost.

This reframes model evaluation as a campaign-design question rather than a leaderboard: at matched budget, does using the model change what you should measure next? The headline numbers are sobering for anyone picking a model by benchmark rank — zero-shot accuracy explained less than a fifth of the variation in discovery outcomes after calibration, calibrated priors beat budget-matched additivity only in the 20-100 measurement window, and simple measured additivity overtook zero-shot models after 50-100 measurements for top-variant discovery and around 500 for global ranking. For an affinity-maturation or developability campaign on an engager arm, that is a direct scheduling rule: foundation models earn their place at the start of a campaign and lose it once real measurements accumulate, and evidence-based routing between strategies beat committing to either.

DICE score: 3 — Replaces fixed-dataset leaderboards with cost-matched decision quality and quantifies the measurement count at which foundation-model priors stop paying for themselves.

### 5. Stealth CAR-Tregs evade CD3 antibody therapy and skew immune responses toward regulation

Blein T, Ayas N, Charbonnier S, Gil A, Haddouche A, Hassan J, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.10.13.758862](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758862)

HLA-A2-targeted CAR-Tregs with TRAC deletion, combined with non-mitogenic CD3 antibody therapy.

A clean example of using a CD3-directed antibody as a selection pressure rather than an effector-recruiting drug. TRAC deletion removes surface TCR without impairing CAR-Treg antigen-specific activation, expansion, or their transcriptomic, phenotypic, epigenetic and functional profiles, so a non-mitogenic CD3 antibody depletes host alloreactive memory T cells while the TRAC-null CAR-Tregs survive in circulation and peripheral tissues — an engineered resistance channel that converts a depleting antibody into an in vivo enrichment step. The TRAC-sufficient CAR-T cells that escaped depletion carried an exhaustion signature, which sharpens the argument for making the therapeutic cell deliberately invisible to the antibody. The engager-adjacent reading is that CD3 as a target has uses beyond cytotoxic redirection, and that orthogonality between a drug and an engineered cell product can be designed in.

DICE score: 3 — Engineers CD3-antibody resistance into a CAR-Treg product so a depleting antibody selectively enriches it, with full functional equivalence after TRAC deletion.

Zuber lab, Université Paris Cité, with Chatenoud and Waldmann.

## Weekly Themes

The week's clearest pattern is engineering the effector side of the synapse rather than the tumor side. The CD123 × Vδ2 engager replaces the CD3 arm with a γδ-restricted one and gets both tumor selectivity and lower CRS cytokines against a matched CD123 × CD3 comparator; the NKG2C+ TKLR work finds a CD8 subset that breaks the persistence-versus-CRS tradeoff with an unchanged CAR; invikafusp goes further and removes the tumor arm entirely, agonizing a Vβ6/Vβ10 slice of the repertoire and letting endogenous specificity do the targeting — with responses in MSS colorectal cancer and PD-L1-negative NSCLC. Three different formats, one shared premise: the effector population you recruit sets the therapeutic window at least as much as the antigen you point at.

Geometry as a specificity mechanism is the second thread. DuoBody-FAPα×DR4 makes death-receptor agonism require two cell types, so clustering happens only at the fibroblast-tumor interface — and the liver spheroid assay that killed earlier DR agonists comes back clean. The DNA origami nanoengager uses an addressable chassis to place an NKp46 agonist, a PD-L1 blocker and a sialidase at controlled spacing behind a pH-removable shield. The Norrin structure quantifies the valency-potency relationship for a bispecific receptor surrogate and, as a bonus, shows de novo designed minibinders working as structural glue for intractable multi-component complexes. None of these buy selectivity from affinity; all of them buy it from arrangement.

On the binder-supply side, two papers argue that discovery campaigns are becoming optional. The TSHR preprint harvests fully human arms from an autoimmune repertoire that already matured against a tissue-restricted self antigen, and the B7-H3 VHH-Fc panel shows residue-level mapping resolving isoform and domain-pair selectivity that three clinical antibodies entirely lack — while demonstrating that affinity, epitope and internalization do not integrate into a predictable potency. Alongside the hyperstable 40-residue B7-H3 miniprotein, B7-H3 was the most-engineered antigen of the week from two independent directions. The decision-benchmarking preprint supplies the matching caution for the computational route: foundation-model priors pay off in the first 20-100 measurements and are overtaken by measured additivity shortly after, so model choice is a campaign-scheduling decision rather than a leaderboard one.

Finally, resistance and exposure control keep surfacing as format problems. The tarlatamab work identifies a lineage-specific serotonin-HTR7 axis that recruits immunosuppressive macrophages — pharmacologically tractable, and a reminder that engager resistance is not only antigen loss. The secreted-engager CAR-T paper trades systemic Cmax for sustained intratumoral concentration, and the CCR5 × CD3 reservoir result shows the opposite extreme working on purpose: a single dose used for deliberate, time-limited ablation of a whole cell compartment, with the window managed by transience rather than selectivity.
