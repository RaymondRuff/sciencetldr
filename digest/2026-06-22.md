## Top Papers

### 1. Engineering Fc heterodimerization via asymmetric β-strand reconfiguration for bispecific antibody assembly

Cano A, Morillo I, Goenaga A, Laugieri ME, de Blas A, Valdaliso-Díez E, et al.

*Journal of Biological Engineering*

[10.1186/s13036-026-00717-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13036-026-00717-x)

Format/manufacturability engineering — "StrandLock" obligate Fc heterodimerization for full-length IgG-like bispecifics.

Rather than the usual knobs-into-holes or charge-pair mutations, the authors redistribute a CH3 β-strand (residues N390–S400) — duplicating it on one heavy chain and deleting it from the partner — so that only the complementary chains can dimerize, suppressing homodimer formation. Combined with targeted point mutations, StrandLock yields bispecifics at ~99% purity via a single-step protein A purification comparable to a standard mAb, while preserving the CH3 fold, Fc effector functions, and native-like IgG pharmacokinetics. The strategy generalizes across multiple antibody pairs with only a modest thermal-stability penalty, offering a manufacturability-friendly alternative chain-pairing solution for the bispecific/engager backbone.

DICE score: 4 — A genuinely novel, structurally grounded heterodimerization mechanism that delivers near-homogeneous bispecifics through mAb-like single-step purification, directly addressing a core manufacturability bottleneck for IgG-like engagers.

University of the Basque Country / Ikerbasque.

### 2. B7 homolog 3-targeted CAR-T cells secreting EGFR T-cell engagers for improved control of glioblastoma progression

Zhang Z, Yang N, Zeng H, Chen Y, Lu H, Xu L, et al.

*Molecular Biomedicine*

[10.1186/s43556-026-00492-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43556-026-00492-7)

Armored cell therapy × engager: B7-H3 CAR-T cells engineered to secrete an EGFR × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (EGFR-BsTe).

The authors first show a resistance mechanism — sustained B7-H3 CAR-T pressure downregulates B7-H3 but upregulates EGFR on glioblastoma — then counter it by arming the CAR-T cells to secrete an EGFR-targeting BiTE, giving dual functionality: direct B7-H3-dependent killing plus recruitment of bystander untreated T cells against EGFR⁺ cells. Notably, EGFR-BsTe secretion also fed back to promote CAR-T proliferation and effector differentiation, and abrogated the EGFR-escape phenotype in a target-dependent manner. In orthotopic and antigen-heterogeneous xenografts the armored cells outperformed conventional B7-H3 CAR-T and extended survival, though efficacy fell when the CAR target was absent on much of the tumor.

DICE score: 3 — A well-executed local-delivery engager strategy that turns a defined antigen-escape mechanism into a combinatorial design, with in vivo survival benefit in heterogeneous GBM models.

Tong A group, Sichuan University.

### 3. TIGIT-targeted IL-12 fusion protein engages NK and CD8 T cells for potent tumor immunotherapy

Tang M, Huang Y, Bi J, Meng D, Zheng X, Peng H, et al.

*Cell Reports Medicine*

[10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102876](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102876)

Immunocytokine engager: anti-TIGIT scFv fused in tandem to IL-12 (αTIGIT-IL12, "T-12") to co-localize and activate intratumoral NK and CD8 T cells.

To tame the systemic toxicity that has blocked IL-12, the authors anchor the cytokine to TIGIT — upregulated on tumor-infiltrating NK and CD8 T cells — so payload delivery is restricted to the tumor compartment. T-12 preferentially activated intratumoral over peripheral effectors, raised the maximum tolerated dose roughly 100-fold versus wild-type IL-12, and drove NK- and CD8-dependent regression across multiple models, including checkpoint-insensitive and metastatic tumors. The work illustrates a receptor-tethering format that converts a too-toxic cytokine into a cell-engaging therapeutic with a markedly widened therapeutic window.

DICE score: 3 — A differentiated tumor-localizing immunocytokine format that simultaneously engages NK and CD8 effectors with a large tolerability gain, broadly applicable as an engager design principle.

University of Science and Technology of China.

### 4. Bispecific Antibody Ivonescimab Added to Chemotherapy in EGFR-Variant Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: The HARMONi-A Randomized Clinical Trial

HARMONi-A Investigators; Fang W, Zhao Y, Luo Y, Yang R, Huang Y, et al.

*JAMA*

[10.1001/jama.2026.7745](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.7745)

Clinical: PD-1 × VEGF tetravalent bispecific (ivonescimab) plus chemotherapy in EGFR-TKI-progressed NSCLC.

This final analysis of a 322-patient phase 3 trial reports that adding the PD-1 × VEGF bispecific ivonescimab to pemetrexed/carboplatin significantly improved overall survival (median 16.8 vs 14.1 months; HR 0.74) after EGFR-TKI failure, with 30-month survival of 29.1% vs 18.4% and a manageable, if elevated, grade ≥3 adverse-event rate. While not a redirecting T-cell engager, ivonescimab's avidity-driven dual checkpoint/anti-angiogenic blockade is a leading example of the bispecific format reaching positive late-phase outcomes, and the OS readout strengthens the clinical case for cooperative dual-target engagement.

DICE score: 3 — High-quality positive phase 3 overall-survival data for a dual-target bispecific in a hard-to-treat population, field-relevant clinical validation of cooperative bispecific engagement rather than a new engineering advance.

Akeso (ivonescimab); Chinese multicenter.

### 5. Patient-Centric First-in-Human Dose Selection: An Ex Vivo Minimal Anticipated Biological Effect Level Approach for T-Cell Engagers in Multiple Myeloma

Kraft TE, Marrer-Berger E, Belli S, Haegel H, Schneider A, Giusti AM, et al.

*Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics*

[10.1002/cpt.70366](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpt.70366)

Translational methodology: ex vivo MABEL framework for first-in-human starting-dose selection of the GPRC5D × CD3 T-cell bispecific forimtamig.

Standard in vitro MABEL calculations often force overly conservative starting doses and long escalations for T-cell engagers. Using fresh bone-marrow aspirates from myeloma patients, the authors anchor the FIH dose to patient-derived tumor-lysis EC values (ex vivo EC ~9 pM in the most sensitive patient), yielding a 6 µg starting dose that was accepted by regulators, produced only grade 1 CRS, and reached clinical activity already in the second cohort with durable responses by the third. The study offers a reusable, regulator-endorsed template for compressing TCE dose-escalation while preserving safety — directly relevant to the therapeutic-window problem that dominates engager development.

DICE score: 3 — A practical, regulator-accepted ex vivo dosing methodology that meaningfully de-risks and accelerates engager first-in-human trials, validated on a clinical-stage GPRC5D × CD3 bispecific.

Roche / Genentech (forimtamig).

### 6. Biparatopic targeting of IL-23 enables dual-epitope engagement and enhanced neutralization potency

Gauntlett R, Lodge J, Watson J, Yates J, Cordy J, Sinfield H, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2689777](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2689777)

Bispecific/biparatopic engineering: 1+1 IgG-like and 2+2 Fab-IgG biparatopic antibodies combining ustekinumab and guselkumab paratopes against IL-23.

The authors build biparatopic antibodies that simultaneously bind two non-overlapping IL-23 epitopes (the IL-23A and IL-12B subunits), using heterodimer-promoting CH3 mutations and redox half-antibody assembly, and characterize them with an orthogonal biophysical workflow including a bespoke SPR dual-engagement bridging assay and mass photometry. The constructs achieve picomolar, avidity-enhanced affinities and confirmed obligate biparatopic binding, with a two-parameter mechanistic model that predicts the potency shifts; functional neutralization, however, was only equivalent to an equimolar mix of the parents. The value is a generalizable engineering-and-modeling framework for soluble-cytokine biparatopics, a building-block discipline transferable to multispecific engager arms.

DICE score: 3 — A rigorous, transferable biparatopic engineering and characterization framework for soluble targets, tempered by neutralization gains that only match the parental antibody mixture.

University of Strathclyde.

### 7. First-in-human study of lomvastomig, a PD-1-TIM-3 bispecific antibody, in patients with advanced and/or metastatic solid tumors

Rohrberg KS, Melero I, F Sanmamed M, Muñoz-Couselo E, Cervantes A, Gambardella V, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2025-012729](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2025-012729)

Clinical: Fc-silenced IgG1 PD-1 × TIM-3 dual checkpoint bispecific, phase 1 dose-escalation and expansion.

Lomvastomig was tolerated up to 2,100 mg Q2W with no MTD reached, linear PK indicating target saturation, and >90% CD3⁺/CD8⁺ receptor occupancy at doses ≥70 mg. Objective responses were modest — 21% during escalation, 8% in checkpoint-experienced melanoma, and an encouraging 20% in checkpoint-naive esophageal squamous carcinoma — leaving the added value of TIM-3 co-blockade over PD-1 alone uncertain. As a clean first-in-human readout for an Fc-silenced dual checkpoint bispecific, it is a useful clinical reference point rather than a breakthrough.

DICE score: 2 — A well-conducted FIH safety/PK study of a dual checkpoint bispecific with limited efficacy signal and no new engineering contribution.

Roche / Genentech (lomvastomig).

### 8. Recent advances in bispecific natural killer cell engagers for cancer immunotherapy

Thalji M, Nieto Y

*Immunotherapy*

[10.1080/1750743X.2026.2688683](https://doi.org/10.1080/1750743X.2026.2688683)

Review: design and translation of bispecific and multispecific NK cell engagers (NKCEs).

The review surveys NKCE architectures that redirect NK cells by engaging activating receptors (CD16a, NKG2D, NKp46) against tumor antigens, and traces the move toward multispecific platforms that add dual tumor-antigen targeting, multi-receptor activation, and IL-15-based persistence support. It covers preclinical efficacy, early clinical translation, safety, resistance, and combination strategies with adoptive NK and checkpoint blockade. As a current synthesis of the NK-engager design space it is a helpful orientation but offers no new data.

DICE score: 2 — A timely, well-scoped review of NK cell engager engineering and clinical status, valuable as reference rather than primary contribution.

MD Anderson Cancer Center.

### 9. Real-world resource utilization, toxicity management, and efficacy outcomes of CD20 × CD3 bispecific antibodies in B-cell lymphoma: a study from the GELTAMO network

Bastos-Oreiro M, Iacoboni G, Navarro V, Martínez C, Manresa P, Magnano L, et al.

*HemaSphere*

[10.1002/hem3.70398](https://doi.org/10.1002/hem3.70398)

Clinical real-world evidence: multicenter outcomes, toxicity management, and resource use for CD20 × CD3 bispecific engagers in B-cell lymphoma.

Drawing on the Spanish GELTAMO network, this multicenter analysis reports real-world efficacy, CRS/toxicity management practices, and healthcare-resource utilization for approved CD20 × CD3 engagers (e.g., mosunetuzumab, epcoritamab, glofitamab) outside the controlled trial setting. The data extend the practical safety and effectiveness picture for marketed redirecting bispecifics in routine practice, informing the dosing, step-up, and prophylaxis context that engager engineering ultimately serves.

DICE score: 2 — Useful real-world benchmarking of marketed CD20 × CD3 engagers across a national network, contributing practice-level reference data rather than mechanism or format innovation.

GELTAMO network, Spain.

### 10. Real-world evidence on infection risk in multiple myeloma treated with BiTEs and CAR-T cells: a meta-analysis

Spataro F, Desantis V, Dicuonzo G, Molica S, Einsele H, Vacca A, et al.

*Experimental Hematology & Oncology*

[10.1186/s40164-026-00798-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40164-026-00798-w)

Clinical safety synthesis: pooled grade 3–4 infection rates for T-cell-redirecting therapies (BiTEs vs CAR-T) in relapsed/refractory myeloma.

Across 16 real-world studies and 2,097 patients, roughly one in four patients on T-cell-redirecting therapy developed severe infection (24.2%), with BiTEs at 26% (BCMA-directed 27% vs GPRC5D-directed 25%) and CAR-T lower at 19%. The authors appropriately caution that cross-platform and cross-target differences derive from indirect, non-randomized comparisons, but the data support folding frailty and infection history into platform/target selection. It quantifies a class-level liability of redirecting engagers relevant to safety-conscious target and format choices.

DICE score: 2 — A solid real-world safety meta-analysis benchmarking infection risk across engager platforms and targets in myeloma, informative reference data without new mechanism.

University of Bari (Solimando/Ria groups).

### 11. Bispecific CAR T and CAR NK cells in dual-threat immunotherapy: opportunities and challenges

Shahin Baher A, Khalid A, Ganjalikhani Hakemi A, Soltani M, Saltanatpour Z, Zamyatnin AA, et al.

*Molecular Biology Reports*

[10.1007/s11033-026-12144-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11033-026-12144-8)

Review: dual-targeting CAR architectures (tandem, dual-CAR, logic-gated, inhibitory CARs) and CAR-NK platforms against antigen escape.

The review dissects the mechanisms of antigen escape and heterogeneity that undercut single-antigen CAR therapies and bispecific antibodies alike, then critically evaluates dual-targeting CAR designs — carefully separating mechanistic rationale from clinically validated benefit and noting inconsistent superiority over optimized monospecific approaches. It positions CAR-NK as a complementary allogeneic-compatible platform with safety advantages but persistence limitations. The architectural taxonomy and logic-gating discussion are directly informative for multispecific engager design choices.

DICE score: 2 — A critical, design-oriented review of dual-targeting CAR and CAR-NK architectures that usefully frames antigen-escape logic for multispecific engineering, but contributes no new data.

Isfahan University of Medical Sciences.

### 1. Improved Calreticulin Nanobody by Framework Engineering

Mavar L, Pavlenok M, Paul A, Hall L, Larimer BM, Niederweis M

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.06.11.731675](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.11.731675)

Building-block engineering: framework optimization of an anti-calreticulin nanobody to improve expression, stability, and pharmacokinetics.

The authors re-engineer the framework regions of a calreticulin-targeting nanobody to overcome the poor recombinant production and unfavorable pharmacokinetics of current detection reagents, improving developability while retaining target binding. Although framed for cancer-biomarker detection, nanobody framework engineering of this kind is directly transferable to the VHH building blocks increasingly used as targeting and effector-recruiting arms of multispecific engagers.

University of Alabama at Birmingham.

## Weekly Themes

This week leaned toward the engineering scaffolding around engagers — chain pairing, local delivery, and dose/safety de-risking — rather than wholly new redirecting formats. The strongest engineering signal was StrandLock, an asymmetric β-strand reconfiguration that forces obligate Fc heterodimerization and yields ~99%-pure IgG-like bispecifics through a single mAb-like purification step (*Journal of Biological Engineering*), tackling the manufacturability bottleneck that constrains the engager backbone. Local-delivery and tumor-localization strategies recurred: B7-H3 CAR-T cells were armed to secrete an EGFR × CD3 BiTE to pre-empt EGFR-driven antigen escape in glioblastoma (*Molecular Biomedicine*), while an anti-TIGIT-tethered IL-12 fusion co-engaged intratumoral NK and CD8 T cells with a ~100-fold tolerability gain over native IL-12 (*Cell Reports Medicine*) — both exploiting receptor anchoring to widen the therapeutic window. Biophysics-heavy format work continued with a generalizable biparatopic IL-23 engineering and modeling framework (*mAbs*). On the translational axis, an ex vivo patient-derived MABEL approach offered a regulator-accepted route to faster, safer engager first-in-human dosing (forimtamig, GPRC5D × CD3; *Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics*), the HARMONi-A phase 3 delivered a positive overall-survival readout for the PD-1 × VEGF bispecific ivonescimab (*JAMA*), and a first-in-human study of the PD-1 × TIM-3 bispecific lomvastomig reported tolerability but modest activity (*JITC*). Real-world and review literature — CD20 × CD3 outcomes from GELTAMO, an infection-risk meta-analysis across BiTE and CAR-T platforms in myeloma, and surveys of NK-cell engagers and dual-targeting CAR/CAR-NK architectures — rounded out a week in which manufacturability, delivery, and safety engineering, not new binding architectures, set the tone. The most directly on-topic engager preprint (moderate-affinity TCRβ-targeting CSAN multivalent engagers from the Wagner lab) fell just outside the 7-day window and was held back per the date policy.
