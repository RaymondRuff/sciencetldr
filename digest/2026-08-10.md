## Top Papers

### 1. A TCR-mimic bispecific antibody reduces HIV-1 provirus and delays viral rebound in HLA-matched humanized mice

Yuan Z, Board NL, Zhao M, Zu G, Sengupta S, Li Q, et al.

*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*

[10.1073/pnas.2527880123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2527880123)

Pol peptide–HLA-A*02:01 × CD3 single-chain diabody (HI12), TCR-mimic paratope.

Every HIV bispecific to date has aimed its tumor-side arm at Env, and every one of them has run into the same two walls: sequence variation and surface Env downregulation on latently infected cells. HI12 sidesteps both by targeting an intracellular antigen — a conserved Pol-derived peptide displayed on HLA-A*02:01 — using a TCR-mimic binder built into a compact single-chain diabody rather than an IgG-like scaffold. In HLA-matched humanized mice on early ART, the molecule accelerated plasma virus decay and cut intact and total proviral DNA three- to eightfold in lymph-node and splenic CD4 T cells, and, critically, ART interruption showed a real delay in rebound rather than just a biomarker shift. The engineering lesson generalizes well beyond HIV: when the surface antigen space is exhausted or variable, moving the targeting arm onto the pHLA compartment restores a conserved, non-downregulatable epitope — at the cost of HLA restriction and the off-target burden that comes with peptide mimicry.

DICE score: 4 — Demonstrates in vivo reservoir clearance and delayed rebound with a pHLA-directed diabody, establishing TCR-mimic targeting as a workable answer to antigen escape in a format directly transferable to oncology engagers.

Siliciano lab, Johns Hopkins; preclinical.

### 2. Meditope-Enabled Chimeric Antigen Receptors Facilitate Plug-and-Play Control of T Cells

Kuo CF, Tong Z, Kuo YC, Park M, King J, Ly K, et al.

*Cancer Immunology Research*

[10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-1285](https://doi.org/10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-1285)

HER2-targeted Fab-based CAR bearing an engineered meditope-binding site; cyclic-peptide (meP) adaptors fused to fluorophores, IL-15, or full antibodies.

This is an adaptor-CAR platform where the orthogonal handle is built into the Fab framework itself rather than bolted on as a tag. The meditope is a small cyclic peptide that binds a grafted pocket in an engineered Fab, so a single meCAR T cell becomes a socket: meP-fluorophore for tracking, meP–IL-15 for selective in vivo expansion, and meP-antibody conjugates to redirect specificity at will — all without re-engineering the cell. Activity matched conventional scFv CARs, which matters because Fab-based CARs are usually the more manufacturable but less potent option. For engager designers the interesting read is the inverse: the same lock-and-key chemistry that makes a CAR retargetable is a general strategy for building modular multispecifics from one universal effector-arm chassis plus swappable targeting cassettes, converting antigen heterogeneity from a design problem into a dosing problem.

DICE score: 4 — A genuinely modular targeting architecture that decouples effector-cell engineering from antigen choice, with cytokine delivery and multi-antigen retargeting demonstrated on the same chassis.

Beckman Research Institute, City of Hope; preclinical.

### 3. Fc-optimized GITR antibody enhances a CD4 T cell–dendritic cell crosstalk to promote antitumor immunity

Avraham Y, Barth N, Yair Bar-On T, Toval B, Habshush Menachem A, Blanga J, et al.

*Nature Cancer*

[10.1038/s43018-026-01207-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43018-026-01207-1)

Agonistic human anti-GITR IgG with combined Fc protein and glycan engineering to tune FcγR engagement.

Anti-GITR is a textbook case of a target that failed in the clinic while the biology stayed sound, and this paper argues the failure was in the constant region rather than the paratope. The authors systematically varied both Fc protein sequence and glycan composition across human anti-GITR mAbs and characterized them in humanized mice, landing on a scaffold whose FcγR profile does more than deplete Tregs: it drives reciprocal engagement between CD4 T cells and dendritic cells, yielding CD4-mediated cytotoxicity plus improved CD8 activity. The generalizable point for multispecific work is that FcγR crosslinking geometry is an active design variable for agonist arms — the same lever that determines whether a CD3-silent Fc stays truly silent determines, on an agonist arm, which cell types get recruited into the synapse and what the downstream immune circuit looks like.

DICE score: 4 — Rescues a stalled agonist target class through Fc protein plus glycan engineering and identifies a previously unappreciated CD4–DC axis as the mechanism, making the constant region a first-class design surface.

### 4. Dual Targeting of EGFR and HER2 by a Bispecific Antibody-Drug Conjugate Demonstrates Broad Antitumor Activity in Multiple Solid Tumors

Chai X, Li F, Xie H, Yu H, Huang X, Lin Y, et al.

*Molecular Cancer Therapeutics*

[10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-26-0143](https://doi.org/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-26-0143)

EGFR × HER2 bispecific ADC (GenSci139) with a hydrophilic peptide-cleavable linker and topoisomerase I payload.

The design thesis is avidity-driven internalization: because EGFR and HER2 co-express across many carcinomas, a biparatopic-style dual binder should engage and internalize where either receptor alone is too sparse — precisely the HER2-low setting where DS-8201 runs out of room. GenSci139 showed superior binding and internalization versus the matched monospecific ADCs across a panel with varied EGFR/HER2 expression, potent bystander killing, and better efficacy than DS-8201 in both CDX and PDX models, while the proprietary hydrophilic linker preserved plasma stability at a DAR that would normally cost pharmacokinetics. The parental bispecific also blocked EGF-driven signaling on its own, so the molecule carries a signaling-blockade mechanism alongside payload delivery.

DICE score: 3 — A well-characterized dual-antigen ADC with head-to-head superiority over the standard-setting monospecific and a credible linker solution, though the format itself is an extension of established bispecific-ADC logic rather than a new architecture.

GenSci (Changchun GeneScience); preclinical.

### 5. Talquetamab in patients with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma: 3-year follow-up of the phase 1/2 MonumenTAL-1 study

Rasche L, Schinke C, Touzeau C, Minnema MC, van de Donk NWCJ, Rodriguez-Otero P, et al.

*Blood*

[10.1182/blood.2025031994](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2025031994)

GPRC5D × CD3 IgG-based bispecific, weekly 0.4 mg/kg versus biweekly 0.8 mg/kg.

Three-year data on the only approved non-BCMA myeloma engager, and the target-choice argument holds up: overall response 67–74% and ≥CR 33–42%, with 36-month OS of 49–61% in T-cell-redirection-naive cohorts, but the number that matters for target selection is grade 3/4 infection at 21–26% — materially below what BCMA-directed bispecifics produce, because GPRC5D is not expressed on normal plasma cells and does not strip humoral immunity the same way. The trade is on-target off-tumor toxicity in GPRC5D-expressing keratinized tissue: taste changes in 72–76% and skin and nail effects, plus ataxia/balance disorders in 5.3%. For engager design this is the cleanest available quantification of how antigen distribution, not format, sets the toxicity signature — and the biweekly 0.8 mg/kg schedule looks like the better exposure profile on both PFS and OS.

DICE score: 3 — Mature confirmatory data that quantifies the infection-versus-tissue-toxicity trade of moving off BCMA, directly informing target selection for next-generation myeloma engagers.

Approved agent (Janssen); clinical-stage follow-up.

### 6. Clinical Responses to Tarlatamab Among Patients with Pulmonary Carcinoid

Ross JS, Lee J, Ortiz E, Yildirim O, Aly RG, Solanki P, et al.

*Journal of Thoracic Oncology*

[10.1016/j.jtho.2026.104135](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtho.2026.104135)

DLL3 × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager in DLL3-high pulmonary carcinoid.

Eleven patients is a small series, but the effect size is not subtle: 73% response rate and 100% disease control in a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor where standard systemic therapy produces low single-digit-to-teens response rates, including a response in central-nervous-system-only disease. The relevant inference for target strategy is that DLL3 expression, not histologic aggressiveness, predicts engager benefit — tarlatamab was built and approved for small-cell lung cancer, and this shows the antigen travels to an indolent tumor of the same lineage. CRS occurred in 82% during cycle 1 with two grade 3 events, which is at the high end even for this molecule and worth noting for a population with better baseline performance status and longer expected survival.

DICE score: 3 — Extends an approved engager into a new, antigen-defined indication with a striking response rate, reframing DLL3 as a lineage marker rather than an SCLC-specific target.

Memorial Sloan Kettering; approved agent, off-label series.

### 7. Directed evolution of a stem-helix-targeting antibody enables MERS-CoV cross-neutralization through enhanced binding affinity

Zhou P, Yuan M, Zhang Y, Limbo O, Song G, Zhao F, et al.

*PLoS Pathogens*

[10.1371/journal.ppat.1014393](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1014393)

Human mAb CC65.1 against the conserved betacoronavirus S2 stem helix; affinity-matured variants with structurally resolved paratope mutations.

A clean demonstration of the binding-versus-neutralization gap and how to close it by engineering alone. CC65.1 bound the MERS-CoV spike but could not neutralize it, and the authors show the deficit was purely affinity: in vitro directed evolution against the MERS stem helix produced variants that acquired neutralizing activity without changing the epitope, and high-resolution structures pin the gain to specific paratope substitutions that stabilize epitope engagement. For anyone building the tumor-binding arm of an engager, this is a useful reference point on the affinity threshold question — the same epitope can be functionally dead or functionally potent depending on where you sit on the affinity curve, and structure-guided maturation moved that boundary across a divergent lineage rather than just within one.

DICE score: 3 — A well-executed affinity-maturation study with structural mechanism that converts binding into neutralization across viral lineages, generalizable to the affinity-tuning problem in engager arm design.

Scripps Research and collaborators.

### 8. Super-resolution imaging reveals altered actin architecture at CAR-T cell immune synapses

Dalby OPL, Kirtsios E, Radoykova HS, Zaza C, Joseph MD, Amrolia PJ, et al.

*Journal of Cell Biology*

[10.1083/jcb.202506119](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.202506119)

Single and dual CD19/CD22 CARs imaged by STED microscopy on supported lipid bilayers.

The first super-resolution look at actin remodeling in CAR-T synapses, and it says the synapse never finishes forming. Compared with TCR-driven synapses in untransduced cells, CAR-T synapses showed reduced actin-depleted regions, fewer actin foci, and microvilli-like protrusions that persisted rather than clearing — the signature of incomplete cytoskeletal contraction and arrested maturation. That is a mechanistic explanation for a set of clinical observations that have been hard to connect: unstable serial killing, odd signaling integration, and off-target activity. It applies directly to engagers, because a CD3-engager synapse is also built without the co-receptor and adhesion geometry of a native pMHC contact, and this gives a concrete structural readout for asking whether a given format, linker length, or membrane-proximal epitope produces a mature synapse or a stalled one.

DICE score: 3 — Supplies a quantitative structural assay and a plausible cytoskeletal mechanism for the long-standing question of why synthetic-receptor synapses underperform native ones.

UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health.

### 9. First-line treatment with epcoritamab in combination with bendamustine plus rituximab induces sustained remissions beyond 3 years in patients with follicular lymphoma

Vitolo U, Falchi L, Andersson PO, Nijland M, Offner F, Snauwaert S, et al.

*HemaSphere*

[10.1002/hem3.70443](https://doi.org/10.1002/hem3.70443)

CD3 × CD20 subcutaneous bispecific combined with bendamustine-rituximab, first-line follicular lymphoma (EPCORE NHL-2 arm 3).

At 41.3 months median follow-up, 96% overall response and 96% CR, with 87% of responders still in CR at three years and no high-grade CRS or ICANS — in treatment-naive patients, which is where engager toxicity has historically been hardest to justify. Two design details carry the result: subcutaneous administration flattens the Cmax that drives CRS, and step-up dosing plus the chemo-immunotherapy backbone means the engager meets a partially debulked tumor. The infection burden is the honest counterweight at 92%, mostly COVID-19, reflecting sustained B-cell aplasia from combined CD20 pressure. n=25, so this is a signal rather than a verdict, but it argues the subcutaneous CD20 engager format is tolerable enough to move to the front line.

DICE score: 3 — Durable three-year first-line data with an unusually clean CRS profile, supporting subcutaneous delivery and step-up dosing as the enabling design choices for earlier-line engager use.

Genmab/AbbVie; approved agent, phase 1b/2.

### 10. Bispecific Antibodies Are Associated With Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy

Gadoth A, Paran Y, Mina Y, Levy O, Shragai T, Cohen YC, et al.

*Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation*

[10.1212/NXI.0000000000200633](https://doi.org/10.1212/NXI.0000000000200633)

BCMA-directed (teclistamab, elranatamab) ± GPRC5D-directed bispecifics in myeloma; five PML cases.

A safety signal worth registering early: five myeloma patients developed JC virus PML at a median of 12 months on BCMA-directed engagers, one after only two prior lines, with diagnosis by CSF PCR in three and brain biopsy in two. The mechanistic thread is that these molecules impose plasma cell aplasia, hypogammaglobulinemia, and T-cell exhaustion simultaneously, and the T-cell arm of that triad is the one that matters for JCV control — notably, two of four tested patients retained detectable anti-JCV/BKV T-cell responses, so quantitative lymphopenia alone does not explain it. For target and schedule design this argues that chronic, continuous CD3 engagement carries a distinct opportunistic-infection profile from B-cell depletion alone, and supports interest in fixed-duration dosing and in targets that spare the normal plasma cell compartment.

DICE score: 3 — Identifies a rare but fatal opportunistic infection specific to sustained T-cell-redirecting therapy, with immunophenotyping that separates the responsible immune defect from simple hypogammaglobulinemia.

Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center; approved agents.

### 11. Bispecific ANXA2/CD147 CAR-T cell therapy for osteosarcoma

Tang HJ, Dai W, Xiao DT, Li HN, Xiong L, Yang MX, et al.

*Oncogene*

[10.1038/s41388-026-03926-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41388-026-03926-2)

Tandem ANXA2 + CD147 bispecific second-generation CAR.

A conventional dual-targeting CAR study, but the antigen pair is the contribution: single-cell RNA sequencing of osteosarcoma nominated ANXA2 and CD147 as co-expressed surface targets, validated across tissue by qRT-PCR, flow, and IHC, in a tumor type with essentially no validated CAR or engager targets. The bispecific construct outperformed both single-target CARs on cytotoxicity, GZMB and TNFα release, and tumor control across CDX, paw-pad, and PDX models, with reduced lymph-node metastasis and — the more interesting mechanistic observation — markedly increased M1 macrophage infiltration, suggesting the dual construct reshapes the myeloid compartment rather than just killing more efficiently.

DICE score: 2 — Solid but architecturally routine tandem dual-targeting, valuable mainly for nominating and validating a new co-expressed antigen pair in a target-poor sarcoma.

### 12. Improving T-cell engager efficacy in glioblastoma with multi-antigen targeting and novel delivery approaches

Tiwari A, Pawlowski KD, Balyasnikova IV

*Acta Neuropathologica*

[10.1007/s00401-026-03063-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03063-w)

Review of trispecific T-cell engager (TriTE) architectures and CNS delivery strategies for glioblastoma.

A focused review that is more useful than most because it treats format and delivery as one coupled problem rather than two. It walks antigen-selection principles for glioblastoma's heterogeneity, then the design consequences of adding a third arm — molecular architecture, stability, half-life extension, and safety optimization — and pairs each with the delivery routes that make it viable intracranially: gene-encoded expression, cellular carriers, and blood-brain barrier modulation. No new data, but it is the current best consolidated statement of where the trispecific-plus-delivery design space sits for solid tumors behind a barrier.

DICE score: 2 — Well-organized synthesis of TriTE design and CNS delivery considerations with no primary results.

Northwestern University (Balyasnikova lab).

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Systematic De-Risking of TCR-Mimic Therapeutics Through Proteome-Wide Off-Target Landscaping and a Generalizable Design Rule Framework

Schuster S, Hartl FA, Stehle J, Beier F, Al-Hasani H, Reinhart C, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.30.741432](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.30.741432)

MAGE-A4 peptide–HLA TCR-mimic antibodies (VR-4, VR-6) assembled into trivalent T-cell engagers.

The specificity problem in pHLA targeting has been treated as a screening burden; this proposes it as a design rule. ValidaTe is a hierarchical workflow — target prioritization, peptide-centric binder selection, proteome-wide off-target prediction, then therapeutic engineering — and its most transferable output is the WiFi ("Widened Fingerprint") principle: rather than chasing one maximally specific binder, combine two TCRm arms whose predicted off-target fingerprints are complementary in a trivalent engager, so that unintended cross-reactivities require simultaneous engagement and largely cancel while on-target avidity is preserved. That converts valency from a potency lever into a specificity lever, which is a genuinely different way to use the third arm. Given the fatal off-target toxicities that have dogged affinity-enhanced pHLA therapeutics, a computational-plus-experimental framework that makes the liability landscape explicit before lead selection is the right shape of solution.

DICE score: 4 — Reframes multivalency as a specificity-engineering tool via complementary off-target fingerprints, with a proteome-scale de-risking workflow demonstrated end-to-end on a clinically relevant cancer-testis antigen.

BioCopy AG with academic collaborators.

### 2. Seed-Guided De Novo Design Expands the Structural Diversity of Antitoxin Protein Binders

Britton D, Ghose DA, Halpin JC, Birnbaum F, Gundu K, Raval S, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.02.742339](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.02.742339)

De novo RelE binders generated by seeding RFdiffusion with PDB-derived surface-complementary fragments.

Current diffusion binder design collapses toward helical bundles against small convex epitopes, which is exactly the wrong bias for the extended, multi-site interfaces that real targets present. Seeding backbone generation with PDB fragments chosen for geometric complementarity produced substantially more diverse topologies and more target contacts than RFdiffusion alone; screening 1,402 designs yielded nanomolar-to-micromolar binders, one matching the native antitoxin peptide's neutralization. The result that matters for engager work is the selectivity finding: designs engaging the broader interface were less cross-reactive against RelE orthologs than the natural binder, indicating that larger designed interfaces buy specificity rather than costing it — directly relevant to discriminating closely related antigens or tumor-versus-normal epitope variants.

DICE score: 3 — A concrete fix for the topology-collapse failure mode of generative binder design, with experimental validation and an unexpected specificity benefit from extended interfaces.

Keating lab, MIT.

### 3. 2B4 co-engagement promotes serial degranulation and killing by human NK cells

Kröll L, Sandusky MM, Saretzki M, Claus M, Wingert S, Niemann JA, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.31.741999](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.31.741999)

Systematic ligand-combination mapping of NKp30, NKG2D, CD16, DNAM-1, and 2B4 on defined-ligand target cells.

For NK engager design the central question is which activating receptor to put on the effector arm and whether to add a second one, and this is the cleanest systematic answer to date. Using NIH3T3 targets expressing defined human ligands, the authors show B7H6/NKp30, MICA/NKG2D, and antibody-bound CD16 are all potent primary triggers, but that adding CD48 to engage 2B4 significantly increases both degranulation and cytokine secretion — and, importantly, enhances *serial* killing of multiple targets, which is the property that separates a durable engager from a one-shot one. DNAM-1 engagement only helped alongside NKp30. They also map receptor downregulation, some ligand-specific and some driven by general activation state, which sets practical limits on repeat dosing.

DICE score: 3 — A systematic receptor-combination map that identifies 2B4 co-engagement as a specific lever for serial killing, giving NK-engager arm selection an empirical basis instead of a guess.

Leibniz Research Centre for Working Environment and Human Factors (Watzl lab).

### 4. Humanized Anti-PD-1 Antibodies Generated Using The Conditional Kernel-Elastic Autoencoder

Shi Y, Li H, Liu P, Bunick CG, Tang S, Wang J, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.03.742579](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.03.742579)

Generative interpolation between nivolumab and pembrolizumab in a chemically informed latent space, with MD and experimental binding validation.

The setup is elegant: nivolumab and pembrolizumab both hit the PD-1 FG-loop hotspot but approach it 174° apart, so the latent space between them is a well-defined region to sample for new paratopes against a validated epitope. A conditional kernel-elastic autoencoder generates candidates, molecular dynamics maps their free-energy landscapes and binding conformations, and six designs were expressed and tested. The honest result is that three of six showed some PD-1 binding with none clearly beating the parents — a real but modest hit rate that the authors frame as a starting point for saturation mutagenesis. Useful as a template for interpolating between two known binders of a shared epitope, not yet as a source of leads.

DICE score: 2 — A well-posed generative-interpolation strategy with genuine wet-lab follow-through, but the affinities do not yet clear the bar set by the parental antibodies.

Batista lab, Yale.

### 5. Kukulu: Diffusion-Based Reconstruction of Antibody CDR Loops using a Structure-Aware Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture

Rabinowitz S, Nigam P, Santolla N, Ford CT

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.03.742168](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.03.742168)

JEPA context encoder plus conditional diffusion head for masked CDR reconstruction in antibody–antigen complexes.

A methods paper on CDR-H3 design, the loop that carries most binding specificity and resists design because sequence and backbone geometry are tightly coupled. The architecture combines a structure-aware joint-embedding predictive encoder over masked complexes with a diffusion head that jointly recovers loop coordinates, atom presence, and residue identity under geometry-aware losses, denoising only masked residues so framework context is preserved. The pipeline detail — Chothia-indexed CDR identification, Fv trimming, atom37 representation, optional sidechain rebuild and relaxation — is written for drop-in use in existing design workflows. As the authors state plainly, this is an implementation and evaluation-protocol overview rather than a benchmarked demonstration, so its value depends on results not yet reported.

DICE score: 2 — A reasonable architecture for the hardest loop in antibody design, but presented as methods and evaluation protocol without comparative benchmarks or experimental validation.

UNC Charlotte.

## Weekly Themes

The clear thread this week is that the field's engineering attention has moved off the tumor-binding arm and onto everything around it. Three of the top papers change what the *non-targeting* parts of a molecule do: Fc protein and glycan engineering turned a stalled GITR agonist into a molecule that recruits a CD4–dendritic cell circuit; the meditope platform makes the Fab framework itself an orthogonal socket so a single effector chassis can be retargeted after manufacture; and the ValidaTe preprint uses the third arm of a trivalent engager not for potency or a second antigen but to cancel off-target cross-reactivity by combining binders with complementary liability fingerprints. That last idea is the week's most transferable one — valency as a specificity lever rather than an avidity lever. Running alongside it is a strong showing for peptide–HLA targeting: the PNAS TCR-mimic diabody demonstrates in vivo reservoir clearance where Env-directed bispecifics stall on antigen variability, and the MAGE-A4 de-risking framework addresses the specificity liability that has historically made that compartment dangerous — read together, they suggest pHLA targeting is being systematically de-risked rather than abandoned. Clinically, the maturing datasets keep pointing at antigen distribution rather than format as the determinant of the safety profile: talquetamab's three-year data quantify how leaving BCMA trades infection risk for keratinized-tissue toxicity, the PML case series names the specific opportunistic hazard of sustained CD3 engagement, and tarlatamab's carcinoid responses show a well-chosen antigen carrying an approved engager into an entirely different tumor biology. The computational design papers were the weakest link this week — competent architectures with thin or absent experimental validation — with the exception of the seed-guided binder work, whose finding that larger designed interfaces improve rather than degrade selectivity deserves to be tested on tumor-versus-normal antigen pairs.
