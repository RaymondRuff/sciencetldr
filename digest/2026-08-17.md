### 1. Discovery and design of potent cell surface display elements

Fang Z, Saskin J, Lee SH, Zou C, Xin S, Huang X, et al.

*Nature Biotechnology*

[10.1038/s41587-026-03144-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03144-x)

De novo cell surface display (CSD) modules — signal and membrane-translocation elements — for mRNA-encoded antigen display.

Everyone building a synthetic surface protein — a CAR, an antigen for in vivo display, a membrane-anchored engager — inherits a signal peptide and transmembrane cassette by convention rather than by design, and the choice quietly sets how much protein reaches the surface. DeepSCan turns that into a measured, designable variable: the authors quantified surface expression across more than 570 chimeric antigens, derived translocation-strength labels for ~310 natural CSD elements, trained three generations of models on ~45 datasets, then computationally designed 3,700 candidates and experimentally tested ~120, recovering seven generative CSDs that match or exceed the best naturally occurring elements. The gains held across cell types and translated into an antigen-specific CAR-T cytotoxicity assay, which is the part that matters — it shows the module improves function, not just fluorescence. For engager work the transferable point is that expression-level engineering has its own design surface independent of the paratope, and it is now a generative problem rather than a screening one.

DICE score: 4 — Converts a universally used but never-optimized component into a learned, generatively designable module with de novo elements outperforming the natural repertoire and functional validation in a CAR-T assay.

Chen lab, Yale.

### 2. Local electrostatics governs tryptophan oxidation and enables rational stability engineering in antibodies

Lenka S, Mehta S, Stephens N, Seeger F, Liang WC, Watkins A, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2713231](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2713231)

CDR tryptophan oxidation liability in IgG1; distal charge-altering framework and CDR mutations, including an anti-CD33 antibody.

CDR tryptophan oxidation is one of the standard reasons a good binder dies in developability, and the standard fix — mutate the Trp — usually costs the binding it was supposed to protect. Training on oxidation profiles from 187 monoclonal antibodies, the authors show that local electrostatic potential is a strong independent driver alongside solvent accessibility: negatively charged microenvironments markedly raise susceptibility, and a two-parameter model reaches 79% accuracy against 84% for full ensembles, correctly classifying all 10 CDR Trp sites in a blind panel of eight clinical-stage IgG1s. Because electrostatics acts at a distance, they could then engineer the liability without touching the Trp at all — distal charge-reversing mutations cut oxidation roughly in half in four of five antibodies, and in an anti-CD33 antibody where the critical Trp oxidizes at 97% and Trp→Phe abolishes binding, a single point mutation gave 27% oxidation reduction at only a 1.4-fold affinity cost. Redox replica-exchange simulations back it with an 88 mV shift in Trp reduction potential. For multispecifics, where every added arm multiplies liability sites and paratope mutations are the least affordable currency, a non-paratope lever with this kind of blind-validation record is directly usable.

DICE score: 4 — Identifies a new, mechanistically grounded and long-range design handle for a common developability failure mode, and demonstrates it rescues a liability that direct substitution cannot fix.

Genentech.

### 3. In vivo genome-wide CRISPR screens of human T cells in solid tumours

Liu Q, Chen PA, Urs E, Zhang S, Arce MM, Wang CH, et al.

*Nature*

[10.1038/s41586-026-10906-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10906-9)

Genome-wide knockout screens in human T cells recovered from solid tumours; CAR and TCR effector systems.

The effector arm of a T cell engager only works if the T cells it recruits are functional in the tumour, and most of what limits them has not been visible to in vitro screens. The authors built an in vivo model that recovers human T cells from solid tumours efficiently enough to run genome-wide screens in a handful of mice, with recovered cells showing genuine dysfunction hallmarks relative to splenic T cells — so the screening context matches the problem. Two screens split the question: an abundance screen nominated the P2RY8–Gα13 GPCR axis as a brake on tumour infiltration, and an effector-function screen identified GNAS, whose product Gαs sits as a convergent node downstream of multiple GPCRs sensing distinct suppressive ligands. GNAS knockout made T cells resistant to several suppressive cues at once and improved efficacy across diverse solid tumour models in both CAR and TCR systems, with combined P2RY8–GNAS editing better still. Engagers cannot edit the T cells they recruit, but the identification of a single convergent node for GPCR-mediated suppression tells you what the tumour is doing to your effector cells and suggests a concrete combination target — or an additional arm.

DICE score: 4 — A screening platform that finally puts genome-scale discovery in the physiological setting that matters, and delivers a convergent, druggable suppression node validated across CAR and TCR systems.

Marson and Carnevale labs, UCSF/Gladstone.

### 4. CAR T Cells Targeting an Intracellular Leukemia Antigen Promiscuously Presented by Diverse HLA-II Alleles

Zheng EYF, Wang CH, Ochi T, Ohashi Y, Ihara F, Fukao S, et al.

*Blood Cancer Discovery*

[10.1158/2643-3230.BCD-25-0230](https://doi.org/10.1158/2643-3230.BCD-25-0230)

WT1<sub>330-348</sub> peptide–HLA class II; antibody-derived TCR-mimic binder in CAR format.

The standing objection to peptide–HLA targeting is HLA restriction: build a TCR-mimic against HLA-A*02:01 and you have addressed half the population at best, which is why the compartment stays underused despite solving the surface-antigen shortage. This work goes at that constraint directly by moving to HLA class II, where the open-ended peptide groove permits promiscuous presentation — an antibody raised solely against the WT1<sub>330-348</sub> peptide recognized it presented by 18 of 20 HLA-II alleles tested, and CAR T cells built from it killed leukemic cells in a WT1- and HLA-II-dependent manner in vitro and in vivo. That inverts the usual trade: instead of one allele's worth of patients per binder, a single paratope covers a wide and genetically diverse population while still reaching an intracellular oncoprotein. The obvious caution is that promiscuity of presentation cuts toward off-target risk as well, and HLA-II expression on the relevant malignant compartment is doing a lot of work here — but as a route around the central limitation of pHLA-directed engager arms, this is the most interesting proposal in a while.

DICE score: 4 — Attacks HLA restriction itself rather than working around it, establishing HLA-II-presented intracellular antigens as a binder target class that scales across a diverse patient population.

Princess Margaret Cancer Centre / University Health Network, Toronto.

### 5. An inherent T cell-activating mRNA delivery carrier for in vivo CAR T generation

Cao Q, Yao Y, Zheng W, Liu H, Jiang M, Xie D, et al.

*Nature Materials*

[10.1038/s41563-026-02675-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-026-02675-7)

FAP-targeted CAR mRNA delivered by p-toluenesulfonyl-arginine-modified oligoethylenimine polymer-lipid nanoparticles (ERTLNPs); no targeting ligand.

In vivo CAR generation has generally required an antibody-conjugated LNP to find T cells and separate stimulation to make them receptive, which is two engineering problems layered on a delivery problem. ERTLNPs collapse both: the arginine-modified polymer-lipid carrier preferentially transfects the spleen after systemic dosing and intrinsically activates T cells with no exogenous stimulation and no targeting ligand, engaging PI3K/AKT/mTOR to reprogram T cell metabolism toward expansion while restraining exhaustion. Delivering FAP-CAR mRNA generated functional CAR T cells in situ that cleared pathological fibroblasts in both cancer and fibrosis models with minimal off-target effect. The result is worth reading as a statement about what the carrier chemistry itself can be asked to do — here it is simultaneously the delivery vehicle, the costimulatory signal, and a metabolic conditioning agent. For engager designers watching the in vivo cell-engineering field, this narrows the manufacturing gap that has been the main argument for engagers over cell therapy, and the same logic suggests mRNA-encoded engagers delivered to a self-activating T cell compartment.

DICE score: 4 — Ligand-free in vivo CAR generation with the activating function built into the carrier chemistry rather than bolted on, demonstrated across two disease models.

Preclinical.

### 6. Dissecting PROTAB-mediated degradation of cell surface proteins

He J, Sun T, Zhang M, Sanoyca A, Tsai WK, Kee YS, Agard NJ, Li J

*The FEBS Journal*

[10.1111/febs.70670](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.70670)

HER2 × ZNRF3 bispecific IgG (PROTAB); cell surface E3 ligase tethering.

Antibody-based degraders are the most format-native new idea in bispecifics — instead of blocking or redirecting, the second arm recruits a surface E3 ligase and removes the target entirely — and until now the mechanism has been largely assumed. This dissection of a HER2 × ZNRF3 PROTAB establishes the actual sequence: rapid ternary complex formation, internalization, then predominantly lysosomal degradation reaching ~85% target depletion in 24 hours. Three findings change how you would design one. Ubiquitination enhances but is not strictly required for internalization, so the tether geometry may matter more than the ligase chemistry. ZNRF3 is not co-degraded but accumulates at the surface while the antibody is largely recycled, meaning the catalytic cycle is preserved and sub-stoichiometric dosing is plausible. And degradation does not reliably produce growth inhibition, which is the honest caveat: target depletion is a pharmacological readout, not a therapeutic one, and target-dependency screening has to precede format work.

DICE score: 3 — Supplies the missing mechanistic framework for bispecific surface degraders, including the recycling and non-co-degradation behaviour that determines dosing and ligase-arm design.

### 7. Oncolytic Virus-Delivered CEACAM6/CD3 BiTEs Combine with PD-1 Blockade to Achieve Synergistic Anticancer Efficacy

Zhang W, Dai S, Fan H, Yang N, Xu L, Liu Z, et al.

*Pharmacological Research*

[10.1016/j.phrs.2026.108392](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phrs.2026.108392)

CEACAM6 × CD3 BiTE encoded in an oncolytic herpes simplex virus (oHSV-anti-CEA6/CD3), plus PD-1 blockade.

CEACAM6 is a good tumour antigen with a bad therapeutic window — it is expressed on normal epithelium, so systemic CEACAM6 engagers run into on-target/off-tumour toxicity, compounded by the short serum half-life of a BiTE-format molecule. Encoding the engager in an oncolytic HSV addresses both liabilities with one move: the molecule is produced continuously inside the tumour, so exposure is spatially restricted and the half-life problem becomes a transgene-expression problem instead of a PK one. A single intratumoural injection drove regression in immunocompetent CT26 and 4T1 models with expanded CD8⁺CD44⁺ infiltration both locally and systemically, and combining with PD-1 blockade produced synergy and durable memory without added toxicity. The dual mechanism — oncolysis supplying innate stimulation alongside redirected T cell killing — is what distinguishes this from a plain combination, and it argues that for antigens with narrow windows the right engineering answer may be to change where the molecule is made rather than how it binds.

DICE score: 3 — A clean demonstration that gene-encoded, tumour-local BiTE production resolves both the toxicity and half-life constraints that block a difficult antigen, with checkpoint synergy on top.

Preclinical.

### 8. Calibration of CAR signaling promotes asymmetric cell division to direct CAR-T cell fate and antitumor potency

Shen X, Xu X, Jiang J, Zhang S, Chen J, Chen R, et al.

*Cell Reports*

[10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117848](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117848)

Second-generation CARs with wild-type versus 1XX CD3ζ ITAM mutations; CAR immune synapse (CARIS) inheritance.

Signal strength at the synthetic synapse has been treated as a potency dial; this reframes it as a fate switch. Antigen engagement induces asymmetric cell division in CAR-T cells through polarized inheritance of the CAR immune synapse, and the two daughters diverge — the CARIS-inheriting progeny becomes short-lived effector-like, the other retains memory-like features with better persistence and superior antitumour efficacy. Attenuating signalling via the 1XX ITAM configuration increases the rate of asymmetric division, mechanistically by promoting pre-mitotic MTOC–CARIS coupling through a PLCγ1–diacylglycerol polarity axis, and adoptive transfer links the asymmetry to therapeutic outcome. The read-across to engagers is direct and under-explored: CD3 arm affinity, epitope position, and linker geometry all set synapse signal strength, and this says those parameters are not only tuning the rate of killing but partitioning the recruited T cell pool between exhaustion and persistence. It also supplies a concrete assay — synapse polarity at first division — for comparing formats on something other than cytotoxicity.

DICE score: 3 — Establishes synapse-coupled asymmetric division as the mechanism connecting signalling strength to persistence, giving affinity- and geometry-tuning decisions a fate-level readout.

Includes Sadelain and Huse groups.

### 9. Mosunetuzumab With Response-Adapted Polatuzumab Vedotin and Obinutuzumab in Untreated Indolent B-Cell Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma

Lynch RC, Poh C, Shadman M, Till BG, Di M, Ujjani CS, et al.

*Journal of Clinical Oncology*

[10.1200/JCO-25-02906](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02906)

CD20 × CD3 IgG-based bispecific, eight cycles, with PET-adapted escalation to polatuzumab vedotin plus obinutuzumab.

The interesting design question in frontline lymphoma is no longer whether an engager works but whether it can carry the whole regimen, and this trial answers with a response-adapted structure: mosunetuzumab alone first, escalation only for patients not in complete response on PET. Single-agent mosunetuzumab produced 100% ORR and 71% CR in 42 previously untreated follicular and marginal zone lymphoma patients, and with the adapted second phase, end-of-treatment ORR and CR were 100% and 86%, with 2-year PFS of 89% and OS of 100% at 34 months median follow-up. Toxicity is the headline: CRS in 64% of patients but every event grade 1, with no tocilizumab required — a profile that makes chemotherapy-free frontline treatment genuinely defensible in a population with long expected survival. The four progressions are worth noting for target strategy, since two were CD20 loss and two histologic transformation, i.e. antigen escape is already the dominant failure mode at a point where efficacy is otherwise near-ceiling.

DICE score: 3 — Strong frontline chemotherapy-free data with a uniformly low-grade CRS profile, and a progression pattern that puts antigen escape rather than potency at the centre of next-generation CD20 engager design.

Fred Hutch / University of Washington; approved agent, investigator-initiated.

### 10. Glofitamab-Based Therapy for Relapsed or Refractory CNS Lymphoma: A Multicenter Retrospective Study

Xing R, Yang A, Huang J, Wang L, Guo W, Cui Q, et al.

*Blood Cancer Discovery*

[10.1158/2643-3230.BCD-26-0128](https://doi.org/10.1158/2643-3230.BCD-26-0128)

CD20 × CD3 2:1 bispecific (two CD20 binders, one CD3), primary and secondary CNS lymphoma.

The received view is that a ~195 kDa IgG-based bispecific does not meaningfully cross the blood-brain barrier, which has kept CNS lymphoma outside engager indications and motivated a good deal of intrathecal and BBB-modulation engineering. This 84-patient multicentre series argues the premise deserves re-examination: glofitamab monotherapy produced 88% ORR and 59% CR in primary CNS lymphoma and 88% ORR with 75% CR in secondary disease, rising to 100%/81% and 91%/65% with combination therapy, at a median of 2.5 prior lines. Median PFS was 19.5 months for PCNSL and 13.5 for SCNSL, with CRS in 40% — all grade 1-2 — and ICANS in 8%, which is unremarkable for the molecule and reassuring in a CNS population. Retrospective and unblinded, so treat the numbers as a ceiling; but a disrupted BBB in active lymphoma plus the 2:1 avidity format may be sufficient without delivery engineering, which if it holds changes the cost-benefit of every intracranial delivery strategy currently in development.

DICE score: 3 — Substantial activity in a compartment engagers were assumed not to reach, with direct implications for whether CNS delivery engineering is necessary or merely conventional.

Multicentre, China; approved agent, off-label.

### 11. Low-dose blinatumomab in multidrug-resistant rheumatoid arthritis — a case series

Bucci L, Hagen M, Böltz S, Tur C, Nöthling DM, Dashi T, et al.

*Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases*

[10.1016/j.ard.2026.07.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2026.07.011)

CD19 × CD3 tandem scFv (BiTE) at reduced dose, 15 patients with multidrug-resistant rheumatoid arthritis.

Autoimmunity has been the fastest-moving new indication for CD19-directed engagers, and the open design question is whether the oncology dosing philosophy — maximum depletion — is the right one when the goal is immune reset rather than tumour clearance. This series tests the opposite premise. At low dose, grade 1 CRS occurred in 3 of 15 patients with no ICANS and a single case of hypogammaglobulinaemia; by week 12, 9 of 15 reached CDAI low disease activity and 3 remission, with synovial B cell depletion confirmed in 4 of 5 biopsies and reduced FAPI-PET tracer uptake in involved joints showing the fibroblast compartment responding. The most useful observation is what happened afterwards: 14 of 15 flared, but 7 regained durable responsiveness to drugs they had previously failed — JAK inhibitors, abatacept, TNF inhibitors — suggesting a transient reset rather than a cure. Notably, lymph node B cells were not depleted, which is the likely mechanistic explanation for the flares and a concrete design brief for anyone building the next autoimmune engager: tissue penetration into secondary lymphoid organs, not blood depletion, is the limiting parameter.

DICE score: 3 — Shows a sub-oncology dose is sufficient for tissue-level effect and resensitisation, and localises the failure to lymph node penetration — an actionable specification for autoimmune engager design.

Erlangen (Schett group); named patient use.

### 12. First-line PD-1/VEGF bispecific antibody plus chemotherapy in triple-negative breast cancer: a phase 2 trial

Shao X, Tian C, Chen Z, Huang J, Wang Y, Sun T, et al.

*Nature Medicine*

[10.1038/s41591-026-04564-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04564-7)

PD-1 × VEGF bispecific (ivonescimab) with paclitaxel or nab-paclitaxel, first-line metastatic TNBC.

Not an immune cell engager, but the PD-1 × VEGF class is the clearest current test of whether co-targeting in one molecule beats two molecules, and TNBC is an unforgiving place to run it. In 36 previously untreated patients, ivonescimab plus taxane produced an 80% objective response rate with 5.7% complete responses at 22.1 months median follow-up, which is a strong signal in a subtype where first-line combinations have historically underdelivered. Toxicity was not trivial — 100% treatment-related adverse events, 58.3% grade ≥3, immune-related events in 41.7% with 11.1% grade ≥3 — though no treatment-related deaths. Single-arm and small, so the ORR should be read against historical controls with the usual caution. The design lesson generalizes past the specific target pair: the argument for these molecules is that VEGF binding concentrates the PD-1 arm in the tumour vasculature, an avidity-driven localization strategy rather than a pure dual-blockade one, and the same reasoning applies whenever the second arm can be chosen to steer biodistribution rather than to add a mechanism.

DICE score: 3 — A striking response rate for a dual-targeting bispecific in a hard first-line setting, illustrating the second-arm-as-localization design logic, tempered by single-arm design and substantial grade ≥3 toxicity.

Akeso (ivonescimab); phase 2, China.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Protein design to broadly reprogram engineered T cell function

Boyken SE, Merillat S, Langan RA, Moffett HF, Coventry B, Haeseleer F, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.13.742806](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.13.742806)

"OUTLAST Regulators" — designed proteins regulating NR4A transcription factors, Cbl-b/c-Cbl E3 ligases, and SOCS family proteins, delivered as compact genetic modules.

De novo protein design has mostly been pointed at binding: make a binder, make it tighter, make it selective. This points it at intracellular regulation instead, and the target choice is the argument — NR4A family transcription factors, Cbl-b and c-Cbl, and SOCS proteins are exactly the nodes where knockouts and overexpression have proved too blunt, because the pathways are dose-sensitive and multi-member. Designed proteins can regulate a whole family with tuned stoichiometry rather than removing one gene, and the authors report markedly improved CAR-T and TCR-T performance in vitro and in stringent solid tumour models in vivo. The implementation detail is what makes this credible rather than aspirational: compact modules compatible with standard viral vectors and existing manufacturing, not a new delivery problem. Engagers cannot carry an intracellular payload, so the direct application is cell therapy — but the demonstration that designed proteins can be aimed at signalling nodes rather than surfaces expands what the protein design toolkit is for, and the same regulators would be natural cargo for the in vivo mRNA CAR platforms now maturing.

DICE score: 4 — Extends de novo design from binders to tunable intracellular pathway regulators against nodes that resist genetic knockout, with solid tumour efficacy and a manufacturing-compatible format.

Outpace Bio with the Baker lab, University of Washington; preclinical.

### 2. L1CAMxCD3 bispecific antibodies exert potent anti-tumor effects in preclinical pancreatic cancer models with representation of the complex tumor microenvironment

Wandmacher AM, Brauer A, Kayser C, Stach C, Werner J, Beckinger S, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.10.743835](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.10.743835)

L1CAM × CD3 bispecifics in IgG-(L)-scFv format (scFv appended to the light chain).

PDAC is where T cell engagers reliably fail, and they usually fail in the model transition rather than in the assay — potent on 2D monoculture, inert once myofibroblasts and macrophages are present. The contribution here is less the antigen or the format than the validation ladder: both L1CAM × CD3 constructs held up in 3D spheroids and were impaired by neither pancreatic myofibroblasts nor macrophages, then induced PDAC cell apoptosis and T cell effector release in organotypic tissue slice cultures from patient tumours with the intact microenvironment. Activity was also shown with PBMCs from PDAC patients, not only healthy donors, which closes another common gap. L1CAM is a reasonable target choice — upregulated in primary and metastatic PDAC and tied to progression and therapy resistance — and the IgG-(L)-scFv geometry keeps Fc-mediated half-life while placing the CD3 arm at the light chain. Preclinical and unblinded, but the model rigour is above the norm for this class of paper.

DICE score: 3 — Nominates a credible PDAC antigen and, more usefully, demonstrates retained engager activity in patient-derived organotypic slices where the immunosuppressive stroma is actually present.

Kiel University (Sebens lab).

### 3. Design of high-affinity miniprotein antagonist targeting IL-4Rα for IL-4/IL-13 signal blockade

Yu Y, Wang N, Xu L, Wang H, Zhang Z, Yu B

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.09.743794](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.09.743794)

De novo miniprotein antagonists of human and mouse IL-4Rα, affinity-matured by partial diffusion; HSA-binding domain fusion for half-life extension.

The interesting number is not the 569 pM affinity but the path to it — 22.1 nM from initial design, improved roughly fortyfold by partial diffusion rather than by library screening, with the design accuracy confirmed by X-ray crystallography. A monomeric miniprotein then matched bivalent dupilumab in IL-4/IL-13 blockade assays, meaning a single small domain reproduced the function of a full antibody without needing avidity to get there. The developability profile is the part worth stealing for engager work: high expression, good solubility, thermal stability such that blockade survives heating to 95 °C, and HSA-binding fusion extending half-life from 2.7 to 60.6 hours. That combination — designed miniprotein binder, cross-reactive to mouse for preclinical work, thermostable, with a plug-in half-life module — is a plausible substitute for an scFv arm in a multispecific, where scFv aggregation and thermal instability drive most of the format-engineering effort. The work is on an allergy target, but the chassis is what generalizes.

DICE score: 3 — Demonstrates a designed monomeric miniprotein matching a bivalent approved antibody functionally, with crystallographic validation and a developability and half-life profile that recommends the format as an engager building block.

Shandong Second Medical University.

### 4. Oligomannose Fc Glycans Reprogram the Energetic and Conformational Basis of CD16a Recognition

Mani N, Polozova A, Chakraborty S

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.07.738819](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.07.738819)

All-atom molecular dynamics of IgG1 Fc–CD16a with paired biantennary, asymmetric unpaired, and paired mannose-5 glycoforms.

Afucosylation and galactosylation have absorbed nearly all the Fc glycoengineering attention; oligomannose species are common in therapeutic antibody manufacture, associated with accelerated clearance, and comparatively unexamined at the structural level. These simulations reproduce the experimental binding hierarchy and then explain it: paired M5 redistributes energetic contribution away from the productive Fc–CD16a interface, cuts both protein- and glycan-mediated contacts, and produces broader, less stable bound ensembles with weakened long-range dynamic coupling. The finding with actual design consequences is the asymmetry result — a single M5 glycan is enough to perturb recognition, and glycan identity on the receptor-proximal Fc arm is decisive, so the two arms of the Fc are not interchangeable with respect to effector function. That points at deliberate asymmetric Fc glycosylation as a tuning strategy, which is directly relevant to engagers built on asymmetric IgG scaffolds where controlling residual ADCC is a routine requirement. Computational only, with no experimental glycoform panel of its own.

DICE score: 2 — A well-constructed mechanistic account of an under-studied glycoform with a genuinely useful asymmetry insight, but entirely simulation-based and resting on previously published binding trends.

Northeastern University.

### 5. Focused framework sampling recovers binding-positive humanized anti-amyloid-β antibodies in a single sorting round

Kim Y, Kwon H, Song J, Lee Y, Park M, Lee CH

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.14.744767](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.14.744767)

Immune yeast Fab display (~3.5 × 10⁸ diversity) with structure-guided framework libraries sampling CDR-supporting and VH/VL packing positions.

Humanization is usually an iterative back-mutation exercise that costs weeks and often costs affinity, and the interesting claim here is that restricting the library to structurally motivated framework positions — those predicted to support CDRs or VH/VL packing — lets a single FACS round recover binding-positive humanized variants with parental CDRs intact. Two candidates against amyloid-β preparations were humanized this way, with increased predicted humanness and clean computational developability triage. The authors are appropriately careful about the limits: the Aβ preparations were not resolved into defined assembly states, so these are preparation-binding rather than aggregate-state-selective antibodies, and characterization is ELISA-level EC50 rather than kinetics. As a workflow rather than a result it is worth noting for multispecific programmes, where each arm needs independent humanization and the compounding time cost is real — but the evidence here is a single-target proof of principle without affinity or developability data at the depth a lead campaign would need.

DICE score: 2 — A sensible structure-guided shortcut through a routine bottleneck, demonstrated on one target with limited characterization and no comparison against conventional humanization.

Seoul National University.

## Weekly Themes

This was a thin week for core engager architecture and an unusually strong one for the layers underneath it, and the papers worth reading share a pattern: they take a parameter the field has treated as fixed and show it is designable. Cell surface display elements have been inherited by convention; DeepSCan makes them generatively optimizable and shows de novo modules beating the natural repertoire in a CAR-T assay. CDR tryptophan oxidation has been managed by mutating the tryptophan; the mAbs work establishes local electrostatics as a long-range handle that fixes the liability without touching the paratope, which is exactly the kind of non-paratope lever multispecifics need as each added arm multiplies developability risk. Fc glycans have been tuned as a bulk property; the CD16a simulations argue the two Fc arms are not interchangeable and that asymmetric glycosylation is a real tuning axis. Even the OUTLAST preprint fits — it points de novo design at intracellular regulatory nodes rather than surfaces, on the premise that pathway families are dose-sensitive in ways knockouts cannot address. The second thread is a sustained assault on the constraints that have bounded targeting. HLA restriction has been the standing objection to peptide–HLA arms; the WT1 CAR sidesteps it by moving to class II, where promiscuous presentation across 18 of 20 alleles turns one paratope into broad population coverage. Narrow therapeutic windows have ruled out antigens like CEACAM6; encoding the BiTE in an oncolytic virus changes where the molecule is made rather than how it binds, and resolves the half-life problem in the same move. The blood–brain barrier has been assumed impassable for a 195 kDa bispecific; 84 patients of glofitamab data in CNS lymphoma suggest that assumption is worth retesting before more delivery engineering is built on it. Running underneath both threads is a quieter mechanistic result that deserves more attention than its journal placement suggests: CAR signal strength governs asymmetric division and therefore how the responding T cell pool partitions between effector and memory fate. Engagers set synapse signal strength through CD3 affinity, epitope geometry, and linker length — parameters currently tuned for potency and CRS. If those same parameters are deciding persistence versus exhaustion, the field has been optimizing a fate switch while thinking it was adjusting a dial.
