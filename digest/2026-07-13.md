## Top Papers

### 1. iPSC-derived NK cell therapy induces durable responses in glioblastoma and overcomes resistance via a B7-H3-targeted tri-specific killer engager

Ning JF, Davis ZB, Zeb Q, Hinderlie P, Tuininga K, Wang L, Chaudhary S, Vue Y, Ma J, Zhu S, Zhang Y, Seeman ZJ, Malmberg KJ, Valamehr B, Largaespada DA, Zorko N, Felices M, Cichocki F, Miller JS, Chen CC

*Neuro-Oncology*

[10.1093/neuonc/noag154](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/noag154)

B7-H3 × CD16 × IL-15 tri-specific killer engager (TriKE) paired with off-the-shelf iPSC-derived NK cells (FT538).

Working from an engineered clinical iPSC-NK product (high-affinity non-cleavable CD16, membrane-bound IL-15/IL-15Rα, CD38 knockout), the authors used surface proteomics to identify B7-H3 as the antigen distinguishing FT538-resistant glioblastoma stem-like cells and then deployed a B7-H3-targeted TriKE to convert resistant tumors back to NK-sensitive. The engager restores potent killing in the ~23% of patient-derived GSC lines refractory to the NK cell alone, and the same effect held for adaptive NKG2C⁺ NK cells, showing the TriKE—not the cell product—is the resistance-breaking module. Intracranially the cells persisted ≥35 days without neurotoxicity, giving a clean solid-tumor rationale for an antigen-directed engager layered on an allogeneic effector.

DICE score: 4 — A resistance-mechanism-guided B7-H3 TriKE that rescues NK killing in an immunologically cold solid tumor and generalizes across NK effector types, a differentiated engager-plus-effector architecture with strong preclinical support.

University of Minnesota (Miller/Cichocki group) with Fate Therapeutics.

### 2. Engineering trispecific IL-2 receptor agonistic antibodies through geometry optimization for enhanced Treg targeting

Lykhopiy V, Pollenus E, Rangan L, Stakenborg M, Tezil T, Varheust M, Tanghe G, Lambrechts N, Malviya V, De Langhe E, Matteoli G, Blanchetot C, Van Rompaey L, Schlenner SM

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-75024-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75024-6)

Trispecific VHH-based antibody co-engaging all three IL-2R subunits (CD25/CD122/CD132) plus a second CD25-anchoring domain.

Rather than mutating IL-2 itself, the authors build a multispecific antibody that reconstitutes high-affinity trimeric IL-2R signaling only on cells expressing CD25, biasing activation toward Tregs over CD122/CD132-only effector and NK cells. Adding a second CD25-targeting VHH sharpened Treg selectivity, and—most instructive for the field—extensive optimization of antibody geometry (domain arrangement and spatial configuration) was the decisive lever for maximizing selective receptor engagement. The work is a clean demonstration that inter-domain geometry, not just affinity, governs the output of a multispecific receptor-clustering agonist.

DICE score: 4 — A novel trispecific receptor-agonist platform where geometry engineering, not affinity alone, drives cell-type selectivity, offering transferable design principles for any multispecific that must cluster defined receptor combinations.

KU Leuven with orionis/argenx-affiliated VHH engineering.

### 3. A covalent peptide engager approach to equip immunotherapeutic monoclonal and pan IgG serum antibodies with new tumour-targeting function

Krygier K, Hwang SS, Rullo AF

*British Journal of Pharmacology*

[10.1111/bph.70564](https://doi.org/10.1111/bph.70564)

Fc-binding SuFEx-electrophile "covalent peptide engager" that grafts a tumor-targeting arm onto intact IgG.

The covalent peptide engager (CPE) combines an Fc-binding peptide, a latent SuFEx electrophile, and a target-binding moiety to site-selectively and covalently rewire either a monoclonal antibody or bulk serum IgG into a new bispecific, sidestepping recombinant protein engineering entirely. A uPAR-directed CPE conferred antibody-dependent phagocytosis of uPAR⁺ tumor cells, and installing it on Herceptin produced dual HER2/uPAR targeting with avidity-driven selectivity for double-positive cells. Beyond the chemistry, it reframes multispecific construction as a plug-in covalent modification of endogenous humoral immunity rather than a de novo format-design problem.

DICE score: 4 — A first-in-class covalent chemistry to convert any IgG—including polyclonal serum—into a dual-targeting engager, a genuinely new and rapid design axis for building multispecifics, tempered by early in vitro-only functional data.

McMaster University (Rullo lab).

### 4. CD98hc-targeted antibody shuttles for central nervous system delivery with broad cross-species reactivity

Wu WH, Sivaneri N, Akin E, Spesia CJ, Huang YH, Bond LG, Doi DLQ, Stad J, Djouadi A, Xie Y, Lee HJ, Kwon NY, Greineder CF, Tessier PM

*Nature Biomedical Engineering*

[10.1038/s41551-026-01718-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-026-01718-3)

Active-learning-evolved anti-CD98hc blood-brain-barrier shuttle arm with matched mouse/cyno/human affinity.

Starting from a human antibody that bound human and cyno CD98hc but not the mouse orthologue, the team used an active-learning loop of iterative mutagenesis and library optimization to evolve variants that recognize all three species at similar affinities and shared epitopes while retaining efficient, long-lived brain delivery. Solving cross-species reactivity for a BBB transport arm removes a chronic translational bottleneck—reliance on surrogate antibodies or transgenic animals—for any bispecific intended to shuttle biologics into the CNS. The active-learning engineering workflow itself is directly portable to tuning the targeting arms of engagers and multispecifics.

DICE score: 4 — An AI-guided antibody-evolution strategy that yields a species-cross-reactive BBB shuttle arm, both a differentiated CNS-delivery module and a reusable engineering method for de-risking multispecific targeting domains.

University of Michigan (Tessier lab).

### 5. Pharmacologic activity, safety, and preliminary efficacy of GEN3009, a CD37-targeting DuoHexaBody, in relapsed or refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin's lymphoma

Awan FT, Gamse JT, Overdijk MB, Breij ECW, Jure-Kunkel M, Sanghavi K, Brady LK, Guan M, Verbrugge I, Chen J, Gong N, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2025-014311](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2025-014311)

Biparatopic IgG1 targeting two non-overlapping CD37 epitopes with an E430G hexamerization-enhancing Fc mutation (DuoHexaBody).

GEN3009 pairs dual-epitope CD37 binding with an Fc point mutation that promotes on-target IgG hexamer assembly, amplifying complement-dependent cytotoxicity alongside ADCC and ADCP. This first-in-human phase 1 established a 1200 mg recommended dose with an acceptable safety profile and modest antitumor activity across aggressive and indolent NHL, and—mechanistically notable—peak drops in serum CH50 correlated with clinical response, confirming CDC as the operative effector axis. It provides the first clinical proof-of-concept that hexamerization-potentiated Fc engineering translates into human antitumor activity.

DICE score: 3 — First clinical validation of the hexamerization-enhanced biparatopic Fc format with a CDC pharmacodynamic readout, an important format milestone despite only modest monotherapy efficacy.

Clinical-stage; Genmab (NCT04358458).

### 6. Engineering of tandem bispecific IL-7 receptor agonist antibody promoting selective T cell expansion

Park JK, Jung I, Lee S, Kim J, Shin S, Shin J, Noh S, Kwon D, Nam C, Kim H, Choi H, Yea K

*Protein Engineering, Design & Selection*

[10.1093/protein/gzag017](https://doi.org/10.1093/protein/gzag017)

Tetravalent tandem scFv-Fc bispecific (TB4) cis-engaging IL-7Rα and the common γ chain.

TB4 is a fully human tandem scFv-Fc built to cluster IL-7Rα with γc in cis; a systematically optimized linker length and domain orientation gave markedly higher avidity than a knobs-into-holes counterpart while limiting off-target γc engagement, and reduced receptor internalization sustained STAT5 signaling. Functionally it diverged from native IL-7, selectively expanding CD4⁺ memory (including TEMRA) subsets and imprinting a distinct antiviral/innate transcriptional program. The paper is a compact case study in how format valency, linker geometry, and cis-targeting reshape the biology of a cytokine-mimetic engager.

DICE score: 3 — A well-engineered tetravalent tandem format that turns IL-7 mimicry into a subset-selective immunomodulator, with clear format-versus-function lessons transferable to engager design.

DGIST, Republic of Korea (Yea lab).

### 7. Bispecific 10E8.4/iMab broadly neutralizing antibody in people with or without HIV-1: a partially randomized phase 1 trial

Theodore DA, Mayer BT, Rolle CP, DeJesus E, Ackerman ME, Seaman MS, Weiner JA, Mohri H, Huang Y, Gray B, Chang J, Gerber MW, Yu J, Luo Y, Padte NN, Yin MT, Barin B, et al.

*Nature Medicine*

[10.1038/s41591-026-04472-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04472-w)

Bispecific antibody joining an anti-envelope-MPER arm (10E8.4) to an anti-CD4 (ibalizumab) arm.

This first-in-human phase 1 tested a bispecific that bridges the HIV-1 envelope MPER and host CD4, delivered IV or SC across 54 participants with and without HIV. No treatment-related serious or grade ≥3 adverse events occurred and dose-proportional PK was observed, with a self-limited post-infusion rash in three of nine people living with HIV. As a clinical readout for a host-receptor-anchored bispecific bNAb, it informs tolerability and PK expectations for engager formats that co-engage a target antigen and a ubiquitous host receptor.

DICE score: 3 — Solid first-in-human safety and PK for a CD4-anchored bispecific bNAb, translationally relevant to host-receptor-tethered engager designs even though the format itself is established.

Clinical-stage; Columbia University and collaborators (NCT03875209).

### 8. Engineering bispecific antibodies for brain delivery of biotherapeutics in multiple species

*(Authorship not yet indexed at time of digest)*

*Nature Biomedical Engineering*

[10.1038/s41551-026-01717-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-026-01717-4)

Human IgG1-Fc bispecific engaging a brain-endothelial-enriched receptor for cross-species BBB transport.

Companion to the CD98hc shuttle work, this study engineers a bispecific whose human IgG1 Fc arm binds a brain endothelial cell-enriched receptor to enhance brain exposure of biotherapeutics across multiple species. Demonstrating a single BBB-transport format that behaves consistently from rodents through higher species addresses the same translational reproducibility gap that has slowed CNS-directed biologics. The transport chassis is directly applicable to delivering engagers and multispecifics into the brain compartment.

DICE score: 3 — A cross-species-validated BBB-transport bispecific that broadens the CNS-delivery toolkit for biologics and engagers, a useful platform advance alongside its companion paper.

### 9. Predicting antibody self-association with sequence-structure fusion models: the central role of CSI-BLI in early developability screening

Ahmed S, Devalle F, Leisen L, Pham T, Amofah B, Lee A, Hutchinson M, Chakiath C, DiChiara J, Farzandh S, Kreitz M, Hinton A, Mody N, Dippel A, Kaplan G, Pouryahya M

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2694124](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2694124)

ESM-2 language model fused with AlphaFold residue-graph structure to classify self-association from CSI-BLI data.

The authors tie a low-material self-interaction assay (CSI-BLI)—which they show tracks high-concentration viscosity and, in FcRn Tg32 mice, in vivo clearance—to a structure-aware classifier that fuses a fine-tuned protein language model with AlphaFold-derived residue graphs via disentangled multi-stream attention. The model predicts high- versus low-self-interacting clones across 1,499 IgGs and 841 VHHs (VHH F1 = 0.76), with interpretable charge/hydrophobicity/aggregation drivers. For engager assembly, where VHH and scFv building blocks routinely fail on viscosity and clearance, this is a directly usable early developability filter.

DICE score: 3 — A practical, interpretable sequence-plus-structure developability predictor validated against viscosity and in vivo clearance, addressing a recurring failure mode for the single-domain and fragment components of engagers.

AstraZeneca biologics engineering.

### 10. Progress in structure prediction and design of adaptive immune receptors

Cohen T, Hochner T, Schneidman-Duhovny D

*Current Opinion in Structural Biology*

[10.1016/j.sbi.2026.103326](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbi.2026.103326)

Review — deep-learning structure prediction and epitope-conditioned design for antibodies and TCRs.

The review surveys AIR-specific language models, structure prediction, and generative design (inverse folding, diffusion backbones, sequence-structure co-design) for antibodies and TCRs, while candidly cataloging the field's bottlenecks: sparse structural data, weak co-evolutionary signal, flexible CDR loops, and unreliable ranking of receptor-antigen complexes. It is a useful orientation to the computational tooling available for engineering and de-risking the binding arms of engagers, without introducing new methods itself.

DICE score: 2 — A timely, well-scoped synthesis of ML approaches for antibody/TCR structure and design, valuable as a reference for engager arm engineering but not an experimental advance.

Hebrew University of Jerusalem.

### 11. Comparison of epcoritamab, lenalidomide, and rituximab versus usual care in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma

Falchi L, Hutchings M, Tybor D, Yu J, Alshreef A, Wang A, Sail K, Ding Z, Mutebi A, Patah P, Kishore N, Rana A, Favaro E, Linton K

*Oncoimmunology*

[10.1080/2162402X.2026.2691549](https://doi.org/10.1080/2162402X.2026.2691549)

CD20 × CD3 bispecific engager (epcoritamab) added to lenalidomide/rituximab, benchmarked against usual care.

This comparative analysis positions an epcoritamab-based triplet against standard-of-care regimens in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma, extending the CD20 × CD3 engager into combination immunochemotherapy. The value here is clinical positioning and sequencing rather than any format innovation, reinforcing the accumulating evidence that T-cell-redirecting bispecifics can anchor combination regimens in indolent lymphoma.

DICE score: 2 — Useful comparative-effectiveness data for an established CD20 × CD3 engager in combination, incremental to the field's format and mechanism knowledge.

Clinical-stage; multicenter with AbbVie/Genmab.

### 12. Epcoritamab Step-Up Dosing Regimen Selection and Optimization Using Repeated Time-to-Event Modeling for Cytokine Release Syndrome Risk Mitigation

Li T, Tredennick A, Polhamus D, Putnins M, Liu S, Sanghavi K, Thalhauser CJ, Parikh A, Noorani B, Mohamed MF, Le Gallo C, Elliott B, Gupta M, Xu S

*Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics*

[10.1002/cpt.70362](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpt.70362)

Repeated time-to-event pharmacometric modeling of step-up dosing to minimize CRS for a CD3 bispecific.

Using repeated time-to-event modeling, the authors quantitatively link epcoritamab step-up dosing schedules to cytokine release syndrome hazard and optimize the priming regimen to mitigate risk. CRS management via priming/step-up dosing is a central, format-agnostic problem for every CD3-engaging bispecific, and the modeling framework offers a transferable, quantitative approach to schedule design rather than empirical dose ramps.

DICE score: 2 — A methodologically useful pharmacometric framework for CRS-minimizing step-up dosing that generalizes across CD3 engagers, though it optimizes an existing drug rather than a new format.

Clinical-stage; AbbVie/Genmab pharmacometrics.

## Notable Preprints

### 13. The membrane distal domain of CD16a allosterically regulates NK cell ADCC

Cid T, Fernandez-Quintero M, Fatima H, Robinson E, Christenson B, Loeffler J, Leaman DP, Lin R, Xu K, Matthias J, Henderson SC, Spencer K, Jardine J, Zwick MB, Ward AB, Mace EM, Murin CD

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.03.736431](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.03.736431)

Cryo-EM/MINFLUX dissection of an allosteric autoinhibitory checkpoint in CD16a, the Fc receptor exploited by NK-cell engagers.

The authors show that the membrane-distal D1 domain of CD16a (FcγRIIIa) acts as an allosteric switch: a nanobody binding an electronegative D1 patch competes with core-fucosylated IgG, locks a closed conformation, and suppresses CD3ζ phosphorylation and ADCC without touching the Fc-binding site, while structure-guided disruption of that epitope in NK-92 cells enhances killing kinetics. MINFLUX imaging reveals ~9 nm CD16a dimers, suggesting an FcεRI-like autoinhibitory dimerization shared among Fc receptors. Because NK-cell engagers and CD16-redirecting formats depend on productive CD16a triggering, this hands the field a concrete allosteric handle for engineering more potent NK effectors.

DICE score: 3 — A mechanistically deep, structurally resolved allosteric control point for CD16a that offers an actionable blueprint for boosting NK-engager and ADCC potency, at preprint stage.

San Diego Biomedical Research Institute with Scripps (Ward) and Columbia (Mace).

### 14. Breaking the Synthesis Barrier for AI-Designed DNA Libraries

Sussex S, Borevkovic E, Lohmann F, Chen N, Lüthi E, Reddy ST, Krause A

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.07.07.736931](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.07.736931)

Policy Gradients for Library Design (PGLD) — synthesis-aware optimization of stochastic DNA libraries from generative models.

PGLD parametrizes randomized DNA libraries with explicit synthesis-cost constraints and optimizes them by policy gradient against a design objective, bridging in-silico generative sequence design with billion-scale multiplexed screening without paying deterministic-synthesis costs. As a demonstration, the method designs a ~10⁶-sequence library exploring the mutational space of a broadly neutralizing influenza antibody for ~$700. For engager engineering, this is an enabling route to run large, controlled lab-in-the-loop campaigns over binding-arm sequence space at tractable cost.

DICE score: 2 — A clever cost-aware bridge between generative design and high-throughput screening, adjacent to engager arm optimization but demonstrated on general antibody libraries.

ETH Zurich (Krause and Reddy labs).

## Weekly Themes

This week's engineering signal centered on multispecific geometry and the arms around the engager rather than on new soluble T-cell-redirecting proteins. Two of the strongest papers make the same point from opposite directions—that spatial configuration, not affinity, is the decisive design variable: a trispecific IL-2R agonist whose Treg selectivity had to be unlocked by extensive antibody-geometry optimization, and a tandem tetravalent IL-7R agonist where linker length and cis-domain orientation reshaped which T-cell subsets expanded. Antigen-directed effector redirection stayed prominent, led by a B7-H3 tri-specific killer engager that identifies and reverses NK resistance in glioblastoma when layered onto an off-the-shelf iPSC-NK product. A recurring meta-theme was building or rewiring engagers by novel means: a first-in-class covalent peptide chemistry that converts intact monoclonal or serum IgG into dual-targeting antibodies, and a preprint mapping an allosteric autoinhibitory checkpoint on CD16a that hands NK-engager designers a concrete potency handle. Computation and delivery filled the adjacent tier—two companion Nature Biomedical Engineering papers on cross-species BBB-shuttle bispecifics (one evolved by active learning), an interpretable sequence-plus-structure developability predictor for the VHH/scFv parts that engagers are built from, a review of deep-learning design for antibodies and TCRs, and a cost-aware generative-library method for antibody sequence space. Clinically, the week was a format-milestone plus dosing story: first human proof-of-concept for a hexamerization-enhanced biparatopic CD37 antibody, a CD4-anchored bispecific bNAb safety/PK readout, and continued CD20 × CD3 combination and CRS step-up-dosing data—collectively reinforcing that manufacturability, delivery, and toxicity scheduling now shape the engager field as much as new binding formats do.
