## Top Papers

### 1. Novel bispecific T-cell engagers overcoming acquired EGFR resistance

Kühl L, Löffler AK, Seifert O, Michler D, Kuhlmann M, Russo G, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2674236](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2674236)

EGFR × CD3 bispecific T-cell engagers; 1+1 and 2+1 IgG-based formats built on resistance-bypassing EGFR binders.

The authors engineer novel antagonistic EGFR antibodies that bind epitopes overlapping but distinct from cetuximab while retaining high affinity for the major clinical ECD escape variants S492R and G465R, then reformat them as 1+1 and 2+1 EGFR × CD3 TCEs. In contrast to cetuximab-based TCEs, the new engagers drive efficient T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against wild-type and ECD-mutant EGFR-expressing tumor cells and show in vivo activity in a CT-26 syngeneic model. The combination of mutation-tolerant binders with stoichiometry comparisons gives a concrete engineering playbook for next-generation EGFR-directed TCEs in cetuximab-refractory disease.

DICE score: 4 — A genuine engineering advance solving a clinically validated EGFR-TCE resistance problem with mutation-tolerant binders and format comparison.

Kontermann group (University of Stuttgart) with YUMAB.

### 2. Tumor-targeted bispecific antibodies effectively inhibit oncogenic pathways while minimizing toxicity

Kschonsak YT, Liang WC, Chan J, Kan D, Nguyen T, Ruiz K, et al.

*Science Advances*

[10.1126/sciadv.adx3959](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adx3959)

Tumor-targeted "toxicity-sparing" bispecific antibodies; TROP2 × Frizzled, plus FGFR1 and EGFR co-targeting designs.

Using single-cell expression data to identify tumor-restricted co-receptors absent from cells that depend on the pathway being inhibited, the authors design bispecifics that selectively block oncogenic Wnt, FGFR1, and EGFR signaling on tumor cells while sparing normal tissues. The lead TROP2 × anti-Frizzled bispecific potently inhibits Wnt signaling in preclinical CRC and PDAC models with minimal effect on intestinal epithelium, and the framework extends to FGFR1 and EGFR pathways. This establishes a general, target-pair-engineering logic for decoupling oncogenic pathway inhibition from on-target normal-tissue toxicity — directly transferable to engager and ADC design.

DICE score: 4 — A generalizable framework that converts on-target toxicity from a screen-out to a design variable; immediately applicable to engager target-pair selection.

Genentech antibody engineering and computational sciences.

### 3. Engineering a β-Sheet Enables Bispecific Binding in Single VHH Domains

Torres S, Windsor HK, Beaudry C, Girton J, Cheung NT, Kuo TH, et al.

*ACS Synthetic Biology*

[10.1021/acssynbio.6c00034](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssynbio.6c00034)

Single-domain bispecific VHH architecture; second paratope engineered into the β-sheet, orthogonal to the native CDR paratope.

The authors install a second, independent binding paratope on the β-sheet face of a VHH nanobody, generating EGFR/PD-L1 and HER2/TfR dual binders within a single ~15 kDa domain (best variant: 1.7 nM PD-L1, 3.6 nM EGFR). The PD-L1-binding β-sheet paratope is transferable as a graft into two HER2-binding VHHs and even the trastuzumab scFv and IgG, preserving single-digit nM dual binding and demonstrating modularity across scaffolds. This is a meaningful new format-engineering primitive that could shrink and simplify multispecific engagers by collapsing two binding modules into one immunoglobulin domain.

DICE score: 4 — A novel and demonstrably modular bispecific format primitive that compresses multispecific architectures into single immunoglobulin domains.

Hackel lab (University of Minnesota).

### 4. Bispecific Macrophage Nano-Engager Couples Dual Checkpoint Blockade with Stimulator of Interferon Genes Activation to Potentiate Antitumor Immunity

Dang BT, Pham KY, Le HTT, Nguyen TTK, Vu ATT, Giri A, et al.

*ACS Nano*

[10.1021/acsnano.6c01144](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c01144)

Bispecific macrophage nano-engager (nanoBIME); anti-SIRPα × anti-PD-L1 with co-encapsulated STING agonist SR717 on a PLGA carrier.

BIS-PLGA-NP@SR717 conjugates anti-SIRPα and anti-PD-L1 antibodies onto a PLGA nanoparticle that also carries the STING agonist SR717, layering "don't eat me" blockade, PD-L1 blockade, and innate STING activation into a single particle. The platform enhances macrophage uptake and phagocytosis, repolarizes TAMs toward M1, matures DCs, and primes CD8 T cells, achieving deep tumor penetration and regression in CT26 with low systemic toxicity. The bispecific-on-nanoparticle architecture is a versatile, modular alternative to classical CD3 BiTEs for solid-tumor settings where T-cell engagers struggle.

DICE score: 3 — A genuinely multimodal macrophage-engager nanoplatform with strong preclinical synergy, though efficacy hinges on PLGA delivery rather than antibody engineering per se.

### 5. Anti-BCMA/GPRC5D CAR T in relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma patients with extraosseous extramedullary disease

Zhou D, Qi Y, Ma S, Sun Q, Gu W, Xia J, et al.

*Blood*

[10.1182/blood.2026033506](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2026033506)

Bispecific BCMA/GPRC5D CAR-T; phase 2 dual-antigen redirected cell therapy in EMD myeloma.

A single-arm phase 2 trial of anti-BCMA/GPRC5D bispecific CAR T cells in 37 RRMM patients with extraosseous extramedullary disease reports a 97% ORR with MRD-negativity in all responders and 43% sCR at a median follow-up of 10.1 months, all CRS grade 1–2. Median PFS was 5.8 months and OS was not reached, with manageable hematologic toxicity and minimal ICANS. The dual-antigen design directly addresses BCMA antigen escape in a difficult-to-treat MM population and informs analogous BCMA × GPRC5D × CD3 multispecific engager development.

DICE score: 3 — High-quality dual-target bispecific CAR clinical data in EMD MM with direct read-through to bispecific engager target combinations.

NCT05509530.

### 6. Real-World Evaluation of Talquetamab for the Treatment of Relapsed/Refractory Multiple Myeloma (RRMM): An International Myeloma Working Group Immunotherapy Registry Real-World Analysis

Janakiram M, Tan CR, Mian H, Huang CY, Popat R, Martínez-Lopez J, et al.

*American Journal of Hematology*

[10.1002/ajh.70376](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajh.70376)

GPRC5D × CD3 bispecific (talquetamab); international real-world efficacy and toxicity in heavily pretreated RRMM.

The IMWG registry analysis of 151 RRMM patients across five countries (55% would have been MonumenTAL-1 ineligible; 75% post-BCMA therapy) reports an ORR of 67.6%, median PFS of 7 months, and 12-month OS of 65%. Muco-cutaneous, nail, taste, and oral toxicities were common but rarely grade ≥3, and only platelet count <50 was independently associated with inferior PFS — neither penta-refractory status nor prior BCMA exposure was significant. This is the most comprehensive real-world readout to date for a GPRC5D engager and supports talquetamab's positioning post-BCMA.

DICE score: 3 — Largest, longest-follow-up real-world dataset for a GPRC5D × CD3 bispecific; pragmatically important for engager sequencing.

### 7. Clinical outcomes of CD3xCD20 bispecific antibodies in patients with central nervous system involvement by lymphoma

Chohan KL, Welkie RL, Godfrey J, Bair S, Falchi L, Kamdar M, et al.

*Blood Cancer Journal*

[10.1038/s41408-026-01519-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41408-026-01519-6)

CD3 × CD20 bispecific antibodies (epcoritamab, glofitamab, mosunetuzumab); multicenter cohort with CNS lymphoma involvement.

A multicenter retrospective cohort evaluates outcomes with CD3 × CD20 bispecifics in lymphoma patients with CNS involvement — a population historically excluded from registrational trials — addressing a critical knowledge gap on CNS engager efficacy and safety. The data quantify responses, durability, and neurotoxicity profile for engagers in primary and secondary CNS lymphoma. Findings are immediately actionable for engager labeling in CNS disease and inform CNS-penetrant engager design priorities.

DICE score: 3 — Practical CNS-disease engager data filling a major real-world evidence gap.

### 8. Multivalent nanobodies for potent and broad neutralization of Staphylococcus aureus toxins

Kim YJJ, Walton NR, Huang W, Lee M, Xiang Y, Sang Z, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-73120-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73120-1)

Multivalent VHH constructs; aerosolizable trimeric Nb and decameric Nb–IgG-Fc fusion targeting Hla, SEB, SEC, and TSST-1.

Cryo-EM and AlphaFold3 epitope mapping of clinical-vaccine-elicited nanobodies against S. aureus Hla and superantigens guides the engineering of stable, multivalent, multifunctional VHH constructs, including a trimeric aerosolizable nanobody and a decameric Nb–IgG-Fc fusion with pM potency against four major toxins. The constructs are protective in murine pneumonia and sepsis models, and the multivalency/Fc-fusion strategies are directly portable to multispecific engager design. The structure-guided, multivalent VHH-Fc framework is a useful template for higher-valency multispecific engagers requiring breadth across related epitopes.

DICE score: 3 — A clean, structure-guided multivalent VHH-Fc engineering exercise with portable lessons for multispecific engager design.

Shi lab (Mount Sinai).

### 9. A phase 1 study of PF-07260437, a B7-H4 × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager, in patients with advanced or metastatic breast, ovarian, and endometrial cancer

Yan F, Cruz-Correa M, Specht J, Wang EW, Lu J, Soliman H, et al.

*Investigational New Drugs*

[10.1007/s10637-026-01614-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10637-026-01614-2)

B7-H4 × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (PF-07260437); first-in-human subcutaneous dose escalation in advanced gynecologic and breast cancers.

This first-in-human phase 1 of PF-07260437, a subcutaneous B7-H4 × CD3 TCE administered every two weeks with or without priming dose, enrolled 30 patients across breast, ovarian, and endometrial cancers; the MTD was not reached, DLTs were limited to transaminase/bilirubin elevations and CRS, and exposures ≥300 µg reached the theoretical efficacious range. No objective responses were seen (DCR 33.3%), and the sponsor terminated the study based on overall safety, efficacy, and PK/PD. The negative-but-informative readout is important benchmarking data for B7-H4 as a TCE target and for subcutaneous-engager dosing.

DICE score: 3 — Informative first-in-human data on a new B7-H4 TCE; modest activity but useful target/format benchmarking and tolerability signal.

Pfizer; trial terminated (NCT05067972).

### 10. Patient-reported time toxicity with bispecific antibody therapies in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Major A, Ma E, Masaquel A, Tsang M, Di M, Kaur S, et al.

*The Oncologist*

[10.1093/oncolo/oyag202](https://doi.org/10.1093/oncolo/oyag202)

CD3 × CD20 bispecific antibodies (mosunetuzumab, glofitamab, epcoritamab); patient-reported real-world time toxicity in FL and DLBCL.

A 120-patient survey across FL and DLBCL quantifies healthcare visit counts and monthly time burden across three approved CD3 × CD20 bispecifics, finding significantly lower time toxicity for mosunetuzumab and glofitamab compared with epcoritamab (e.g., 31.2 vs 61.9 hours/month in FL) despite per-visit advantages for some SC regimens. ≥92% of patients preferred a hypothetical fixed-duration regimen over treat-to-progression. The data make a quantifiable case for fixed-duration engager regimens and a route/schedule lens on engager-format selection.

DICE score: 2 — Useful real-world time-burden quantification with implications for engager dosing-schedule design, but not novel molecular engineering.

## Weekly Themes

This week's signal is concentrated in classical protein-engineering and target-pair logic rather than de novo computational design. The strongest engineering papers are a pair of bispecific advances that solve very different problems with the same conceptual move — using a second binding arm to break an unavoidable tradeoff. Kühl et al. attach a CD3 redirection arm to mutation-tolerant EGFR binders to convert cetuximab-style escape mutations from a resistance liability into a covered space (mAbs), while Kschonsak et al. at Genentech pick TROP2-like tumor-restricted co-receptors specifically to spare normal Wnt-, FGFR1-, and EGFR-dependent tissues from on-target toxicity (Science Advances) — a generalizable "co-target as toxicity filter" framework directly applicable to engager design. Format engineering also had a striking entry: a single-VHH bispecific built on the β-sheet face rather than a second chain (ACS Synth Biol), with the second paratope shown to be transferable across VHH and IgG scaffolds, hinting at materially smaller multispecific architectures. The translational layer was dominated by GPRC5D and CD20 bispecifics — the largest real-world talquetamab dataset to date (AJH), CNS-lymphoma efficacy data for CD3 × CD20 bispecifics (BCJ), and patient-reported time toxicity comparing approved CD20 engagers (Oncologist) — alongside a Pfizer B7-H4 × CD3 phase 1 that came back informative-but-negative and was terminated. Innate-engager work continues to differentiate from CD3-based TCEs: a SIRPα × PD-L1 macrophage nano-engager combined with a STING agonist (ACS Nano), and a multivalent VHH–Fc platform against S. aureus toxins (Nat Commun) that previews higher-valency multispecific architectures. Overall: a strong week for engineering-driven engager innovation, a thin one for computational design and bona fide novel preprints in the strict 7-day window.
