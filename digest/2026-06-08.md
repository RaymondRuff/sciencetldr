## Top Papers

### 1. Discovery of TCR-like antibodies to the KRAS G12D neoantigen via in silico-in vitro workflow

Ahn S, Oh TS, Suh S, Jeon JY, Lah S, Ryu K, et al.

*Molecular Therapy*

[10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.05.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.05.032)

KRAS G12D/HLA-C*08:02 peptide-MHC × CD3 TCR-mimic antibody; computationally docked human VH/VL fragments with limited CDR design, then yeast-display affinity selection, reformatted as CAR and BiTE.

The authors couple an in silico stage — generating and docking multiple human antibody variable fragments to the KRAS G12D/HLA-C*08:02 target followed by constrained CDR sequence design — with an in vitro yeast-surface-display library and iterative selection to yield high-affinity, allele-specific TCR-like antibodies against an intracellular oncogenic neoantigen. Phage-display specificity profiling showed no detectable human off-target reactivity and in silico analysis predicted low immunogenicity, and the binders mediated selective cytotoxicity when reformatted as CARs or CD3 bispecific engagers. By compressing the notoriously hard problem of pMHC-directed binder discovery into a practical design-to-function pipeline, this is a portable computational route to engager binding domains for "undruggable" intracellular targets.

DICE score: 4 — A differentiated in silico-to-display pipeline that produces specific, low-immunogenicity TCR-mimic binders against a validated intracellular neoantigen and demonstrates direct reformatting into CAR and BiTE engagers.

TheraZyne / KAIST.

### 2. HIV tri-specific killer engagers directly enhance NK cell function and safely expand NK cells in vivo

Cromarty RT, Clain JA, Soigner Y, Dasgupta T, Dixon K, Nguyen K, et al.

*Cell Reports Medicine*

[10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102847](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102847)

HIV Env × IL-15 × CD16 trispecific killer engager (TriKE); anti-Env arm built either from VRC01/07 antibody domains or the CD4 ectodomain 1, fused to an IL-15 cross-linker and an NK-recruiting anti-CD16 domain.

The team adapts the TriKE architecture — anti-CD16 NK-engagement plus an IL-15 self-sustaining module plus a targeting arm — to HIV by swapping in either VRC01/07 broadly neutralizing antibody domains or the CD4 ectodomain, driving NK-cell-mediated ADCC against HIV-infected cells with CD38⁺CD57⁺ NK cells as the principal effectors. Crucially, a dose-escalation study in rhesus macaques showed the CD4 TriKE was safe and expanded both peripheral and lymphoid NK cells in vivo, providing rare non-human-primate pharmacodynamic and safety data for a multispecific killer engager. The work extends the trispecific NK-engager platform beyond oncology and validates CD4-domain and bNAb-domain targeting arms as interchangeable engager modules.

DICE score: 4 — A differentiated trispecific killer-engager format applied to a new disease area with interchangeable bNAb/CD4 targeting arms and supportive in vivo NHP safety and NK-expansion data.

Miller group, University of Minnesota / Emory NPRC.

### 3. Engineering a TACI×CD3 Bispecific Antibody to Redirect T Cells Against Multiple Myeloma

Zhang M, Leong WF, Huo J, Ngoh EZX, Loh H, Chen Q, et al.

*Blood Advances*

[10.1182/bloodadvances.2025018210](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2025018210)

TACI × CD3 T-cell-redirecting bispecific antibody; a new TNFR-superfamily target antigen chosen to backstop BCMA-directed engagers.

Recognizing that BCMA downregulation or loss is a recurrent escape route from BCMA-directed immunotherapy, the authors engineer a bispecific antibody against transmembrane activator and CAML interactor (TACI), another TNFR-superfamily member on myeloma cells, paired with a CD3 arm. The TACI × CD3 bsAb drives robust T-cell activation, proliferation, and cytotoxicity, kills primary patient myeloma cells, and — most importantly — eliminates BCMA-low/negative cells that are refractory to BCMA × CD3 engagers. As a novel target-antigen pair explicitly engineered to address a validated clinical resistance mechanism, it offers a complementary or combinatorial engager for relapsed/refractory myeloma.

DICE score: 4 — A novel target-antigen pair for T-cell redirection that demonstrably overcomes BCMA-loss resistance against primary myeloma cells, a clearly differentiated rationale even at the preclinical stage.

A*STAR, Singapore.

### 4. TP53 deficiency in AML induces resistance to T-cell engagers through an immunosuppressive secretome

Winter L, Pawlowsky L, Muth A, Neumann AS, White K, Kazerani M, et al.

*Leukemia*

[10.1038/s41375-026-02991-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41375-026-02991-6)

CD3 × CD33 BiTE (AMG 330) as a probe; mechanistic dissection of TP53-deficient AML resistance to T-cell engagers.

Using TP53-deleted primary AML cells and TP53-knockdown lines co-cultured with AMG 330 (CD3 × CD33)-stimulated T cells, the authors show that TP53 loss blunts engager-mediated cytotoxicity, T-cell proliferation, and proinflammatory cytokine secretion. Transwell and proteomic analyses pin the effect on a secreted, cell-extrinsic program — with TGF-β1 identified as a key mediator — that pushes co-cultured T cells toward a senescent cell-cycle transcriptional state. The study reframes a major AML engager-resistance pathway as a druggable secretome rather than an antigen problem, pointing toward combination strategies (e.g., TGF-β blockade) to restore engager activity.

DICE score: 3 — A clean mechanistic insight identifying an immunosuppressive secretome (TGF-β1) as a cell-extrinsic driver of T-cell-engager resistance in TP53-mutant AML, with clear combination-therapy implications.

LMU Munich / MD Anderson; uses Amgen's AMG 330.

### 5. Characterization of T Cell Engaging Bispecific Antibodies and Combination Therapies with a High-Throughput Multiparametric Automated Assay and Data Analysis Platform

Dela Cruz Chuh J, Anifa S, Torres E, Mirenda M, Munaretto A, di Somma M, et al.

*Molecular Cancer Therapeutics*

[10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-25-1360](https://doi.org/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-25-1360)

Methodology: automated, high-throughput kinetic cytotoxicity and cytokine assay for screening CD3 bispecifics and costimulatory-agonist combinations.

The authors build an automated, high-throughput T-cell-dependent cytotoxicity platform that captures the kinetics of killing and cytokine production across multiple target antigens, CD3 epitopes, target-expression levels, and effector-to-target ratios — dimensions that conventional end-point assays collapse. Bundled data-analysis and visualization tools integrate the multiparametric readouts to classify engagers into distinct functional subsets and extend to screening costimulatory-receptor agonist antibodies that pair with TCEs. As a lead-discrimination engine, it directly addresses the bottleneck of differentiating closely related engager and combination candidates during early protein-engineering optimization.

DICE score: 3 — A practically useful, multiparametric high-throughput characterization platform that improves lead selection and combination screening for CD3 engagers, a meaningful methodological contribution.

Genentech (with Evotec).

### 6. The double-edged nature of antibody bivalency: mathematical and experimental analysis of cell surface antigen occupancy and opsonization

Heirene L, Lodge J, Fedorova M, Gauntlett R, Cordy J, Rattray Z, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2681843](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2681843)

Modeling/engineering: a calibrated mathematical model of bivalent vs monovalent antigen engagement relating avidity to occupancy and opsonization.

Calibrating a binding model against matched bivalent and monovalent anti-PD-1 panels spanning a range of affinities and target densities, the authors map the regimes in which avidity boosts antigen occupancy (favoring antagonism) versus those in which it limits the number of antibodies bound per cell (constraining opsonization and Fc-mediated effector recruitment). They also identify and correct an affinity-dependent discrepancy between measured and predicted cell binding attributable to assay limitations, yielding refined on-cell binding estimates. The framework gives engager and antibody designers a quantitative basis for choosing valency and dose according to whether occupancy or effector-cell recruitment is the intended mechanism.

DICE score: 3 — A useful quantitative framework linking valency and avidity to occupancy versus opsonization that can directly guide structural and dosing decisions for multivalent engager design.

University of Oxford / GSK.

### 7. Designing T-cell Engagers: Trade-offs Between Activity and Safety

Zhang Y, Wei M, Shen Y, Zhang Z, He Q, Yang B, et al.

*Protein & Cell*

[10.1093/procel/pwag038](https://doi.org/10.1093/procel/pwag038)

Review: a design-strategy framework organizing TCE engineering around the activity-versus-safety trade-off.

This review systematically organizes T-cell-engager design into T-cell-interface strategies (anti-CD3 affinity tuning, costimulatory signal incorporation, blockade of immunosuppressive pathways) and tumor-side strategies (antigen selection, binding valency, and tumor-microenvironment-responsive conditional activation), emphasizing that activity and toxicity are too often optimized in isolation. It critically compares the strengths, limitations, and interdependencies of these levers and argues for integrated, mechanism-informed design to widen the therapeutic window, especially in solid tumors. As a structured synthesis of the format-engineering toolbox, it is a useful orientation for engineers balancing potency and safety.

DICE score: 2 — A well-organized but non-empirical review of engager design trade-offs; valuable as a framework rather than a new advance.

Zhejiang University.

### 8. Landscape of T-cell bispecific antibodies in cancer therapy: therapeutic strategies, challenges and future prospection

Jiao G, Peng H, Yang Y, Liu Y, Liu X, Hu Y, et al.

*Molecular Cancer*

[10.1186/s12943-026-02685-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12943-026-02685-8)

Review: comprehensive survey of T-cell bispecific antibody design, mechanisms, formats, and clinical performance.

The review compares current T-cell bispecific platforms by therapeutic strategy, structural format, safety profile, and clinical outcome, situating their transformative hematologic efficacy against persistent barriers of CRS/ICANS toxicity and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. It catalogs emerging mitigation strategies — novel formats, rational checkpoint-inhibitor and immunomodulator combinations, AI-assisted de novo antibody design, and ADC pairings — as routes to a safer next generation of T-cell-redirecting therapies. It is a thorough reference for the field's current state and open problems, without presenting new data.

DICE score: 2 — A comprehensive but incremental landscape review of T-cell bispecifics, useful as a reference rather than a primary contribution.

University of Macau.

### 9. One-step cleavage and purification: a bifunctional IdeS-Protein A fusion protein for on-column generation of antibody fragments

Zhang T, Liao H, Teng CB, Wang C

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2684388](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2684388)

Manufacturability tool: a bifunctional IdeS-Protein A fusion enabling simultaneous IgG cleavage and Fc/enzyme removal for fragment generation.

The authors fuse the IgG-specific protease IdeS to Fc-binding Protein A so that, in solution, an antibody is cleaved and the enzyme plus liberated Fc are affinity-captured in a single step; immobilizing the fusion on resin further yields an "on-column cleavage-purification" platform that delivers high-purity F(ab')₂ in the flow-through while Fc, uncleaved IgG, and enzyme stay bound. By collapsing the conventional multi-step enzymatic-digestion-and-purification workflow into one operation, it streamlines small-scale production of Fab/F(ab')₂ and VHH-based building blocks relevant to engager assembly. The contribution is a generic process tool rather than a new molecule or format.

DICE score: 2 — A neat, automatable process improvement for antibody-fragment generation with modest, indirect relevance to engager building-block production.

BioNTech (Zhuhai) / Northeast Forestry University.

### 10. Overcoming immunotherapy resistance in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer: Insights into mechanisms and therapeutic opportunities

Guan R, Zhang C, Zhang J, Du S, Wang H, Zhang Y, et al.

*Critical Reviews in Oncology/Hematology*

[10.1016/j.critrevonc.2026.105411](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.critrevonc.2026.105411)

Review: resistance mechanisms and emerging modalities (DLL3/B7-H3 ADCs and T-cell engagers) in extensive-stage SCLC.

The review maps the molecular networks driving immune resistance in extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer and positions MHC-independent modalities — antibody-drug conjugates and T-cell engagers against DLL3 and B7-H3 — as the agents reshaping a historically "cold," immunotherapy-refractory disease. It outlines a precision-oncology roadmap incorporating biomarkers, liquid-biopsy stratification, and toxicity management for these emerging therapies. For engager developers it is a useful disease-context survey of the DLL3/B7-H3 target landscape, though it offers no new engineering or clinical data.

DICE score: 2 — A field-relevant but non-novel review contextualizing DLL3/B7-H3 engagers within SCLC resistance biology.

## Weekly Themes

The dominant signal this week was computational and modular engineering of engager binding domains rather than new clinical molecules. The standout is a TheraZyne/KAIST in silico-to-display pipeline that generates allele-specific TCR-like antibodies against the intracellular KRAS G12D/HLA-C*08:02 neoantigen and reformats them directly into CARs and CD3 bispecifics (*Molecular Therapy*) — a portable route into the hardest binder-discovery problem in the field. Modularity recurred as a theme: a trispecific killer engager swapped VRC01/07 bNAb domains or the CD4 ectodomain into the TriKE scaffold to redirect NK cells against HIV-infected cells, with supportive non-human-primate safety and NK-expansion data (*Cell Reports Medicine*), and a TACI × CD3 bispecific introduced a fresh TNFR-superfamily target antigen explicitly to backstop BCMA loss in myeloma (*Blood Advances*). Quantitative and high-throughput design tooling was unusually well represented — a calibrated mathematical model dissecting when bivalency/avidity helps versus hurts occupancy and opsonization (*mAbs*), and an automated multiparametric kinetic platform for discriminating engager and costimulation-combination leads (*Molecular Cancer Therapeutics*) — both aimed squarely at the lead-optimization bottleneck. On the mechanistic side, TP53-deficient AML was shown to resist T-cell engagers via an immunosuppressive, TGF-β1-driven secretome rather than antigen loss (*Leukemia*), reframing a major resistance pathway as druggable. Two TCE reviews and a fragment-purification process tool round out the week. De novo/AI engager design clearly continues to accelerate in the preprint literature, but bioRxiv's inaccessibility again prevented in-window verification, so genuinely fresh preprints could not be surfaced.
