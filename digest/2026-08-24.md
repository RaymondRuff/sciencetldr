## Top Papers

### 1. A blinded, prospective benchmark of in silico antibody discovery anchored to experimental affinity and developability

Erasmus MF, Bedinger D, Hopkins E, Ferguson G, Strickler J, Graff CP, et al.

*Nature Biotechnology*

[10.1038/s41587-026-03238-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03238-6)

Not a molecule — a CASP-style prospective benchmark of 511 AI-designed or AI-predicted antibodies from 29 organizations across affinity maturation, HCDR3 affinity ranking, and out-of-library CDR design.

Every claim about computational antibody design published in the last three years has been retrospective, self-scored, or both, and AIntibody is the first attempt to settle the question the way structure prediction was settled: designs submitted blind, before the answers existed, then run through diverse experimental affinity and developability assays. The results are unflattering in a way that is far more useful than another success story. In silico affinity maturation genuinely works — several groups produced developable antibodies below 100 pM — but the wins did not transfer across tasks, and on the second task, ranking high-affinity clones within HCDR3 clusters from a selection output, all but one method performed *worse than picking clones at random*. Out-of-library CDR design was highly variable and many submissions failed to beat a standard selection campaign. For multispecific work the implication is direct and slightly deflating: the tractable regime is local optimization of an arm you already have, not de novo generation of the arm, and affinity prediction — the thing you most want when co-tuning a CD3 arm against a tumor arm across a 50-fold affinity bias — remains the weakest link. Cross-task generalization, which any real engager campaign requires, is not yet demonstrated by anyone.

DICE score: 5 — The field's first blinded prospective benchmark with experimental anchoring, spanning 29 organizations, and it reverses several widely repeated claims about what AI antibody design can currently deliver.

Specifica/IQVIA-coordinated consortium including Scripps, Xencor, BMS, Takeda, Novo Nordisk, Johns Hopkins, and Tencent AI for Life Sciences.

### 2. Design and preclinical characterization of an affinity-tuned BCMA×CD3 bispecific T-cell engager for broad B-cell depletion

Thomas J, Xu G, Lock H, Song Q, Yin Y, Luo W, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2718587](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2718587)

BCMA × CD3 humanized IgG4-based 1+1 bispecific with Fc-silencing mutations and a deliberately de-tuned CD3 arm.

The engineering thesis is explicit and worth reading as a design argument rather than a molecule announcement: build in a >50-fold affinity bias toward the tumor antigen over CD3, and target-density dependence rather than T cell avidity becomes the thing that gates activation. Gamgertamig pairs high-affinity BCMA binding with a reduced-affinity CD3 arm on an Fc-silenced IgG4 1+1 scaffold, and the preclinical package is unusually complete for a format paper — sub-nanomolar EC50 cytotoxicity across B cell and myeloma lines spanning a wide range of target densities, depletion across naïve, memory, and transitional B cell subsets in primary PBMCs, and consistent activity in samples from healthy donors, autoimmune patients, and myeloma patients rather than cell lines alone. In rhesus, circulating B cells and bone marrow plasma cells were durably depleted with a serum half-life above five days. The notable positioning is the indication: this is a BCMA×CD3 engager built for autoimmune immune reset as much as for malignancy, where the tolerance for cytokine release is far lower and the affinity-bias strategy is the whole safety argument. Whether a 50-fold bias is enough in humans is exactly what the clinic will answer.

DICE score: 4 — A cleanly articulated affinity-biasing design with cross-species, cross-donor, and primary-patient validation, aimed at the autoimmune window where the CD3-detuning approach matters most.

Ouro Medicines and Keymed Biosciences; clinical-stage candidate.

### 3. AI-enabled discovery and biochemical optimization of minibinders targeting cancer cell-surface proteins

Broske B, McEnroe BA, Frechen SC, Kempchen TN, Fandrey CI, Tan E, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-76760-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76760-5)

De novo minibinders against PD-L1, CD276 (B7-H3), and VTCN1 (B7-H4); mammalian cell-surface display screening, then redesign of non-interface surfaces for CAR compatibility.

This is the paper that names the failure mode nobody publishes. Thousands of designs screened by mammalian surface display gave several high-affinity PD-L1 minibinders and far fewer against CD276 and VTCN1 — target dependence is severe, and Chai-1 ipTM scores with ESM embeddings correlated with success well enough to be useful triage, including for interface mutations. But when the successful binders were dropped into CAR constructs, some trafficked poorly to the cell surface and functioned badly, despite binding perfectly well as soluble reagents. The fix was not at the paratope: a genetic-algorithm diversification that held the binding interface fixed while varying non-binding surfaces revealed an isoelectric-point window that restored CAR expression and target-selective killing. For anyone building engagers from designed minibinders instead of scFvs, that is the transferable lesson — affinity and specificity are necessary and demonstrably insufficient, the rest of the molecular surface sets whether the domain behaves in the format you actually want to ship it in, and the pI window is a concrete, testable design constraint rather than a vague developability warning.

DICE score: 4 — Establishes that de novo binder success is strongly target-dependent and, more usefully, isolates non-interface biochemistry as the decisive variable for functional deployment, with a redesign strategy that fixes it.

Hölzel and Hagelueken groups, University of Bonn.

### 4. Mimic antibodies: leveraging ligand mimicry for epitope-targeted antibody discovery

Abanades B, Lopez-Morales J, Tanasijevic I, Wagner C, Speck J, Fenn S, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2719289](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2719289)

Antibodies that recapitulate a cognate ligand's binding mode; ligand-guided mining of immune repertoires, applied to IL-18 / IL-18RA.

Selecting for a *predefined epitope* rather than for binding is the perennial unsolved problem in discovery, and it is the specific problem multispecific design keeps running into — the tumor arm must hit a particular face at a particular geometry for the synapse to form productively, and panning gives you whatever is immunodominant instead. The authors first show, through systematic PDB analysis, that antibodies converge on their target's natural ligand binding mode far more often than expected, via several distinct structural routes (single-loop, multi-loop, and scattered interaction mimicry) — interfaces constrain solutions strongly enough that convergence is the norm, not a curiosity. They then turn that into a screening filter: mine a repertoire for antibodies whose predicted interface mimics the ligand's interaction motif. On a 20,000-sequence repertoire against IL-18RA, 31 candidates yielded 11 binders to the intended D3 domain — a 35% hit rate for epitope-directed selection — with eight reaching sub-nanomolar affinity, exceeding the cognate ligand itself. The method needs a known ligand interaction, which limits it to receptor-family targets, but for those it converts epitope targeting from a post-hoc screening question into a prospective selection criterion.

DICE score: 4 — Turns a structural observation about interface convergence into a working prospective filter for epitope-directed discovery, with a hit rate and affinity range that make it immediately usable on receptor targets.

Roche Innovation Center Munich and Prescient Design, with EPFL.

### 5. Membrane-Bound Multimodal Plasmonic Transducers for Noninvasive, In Situ Monitoring and Control of CAR T Cells Against Heterogeneous Solid Tumors

Kim M, Zamat A, Cadena M, Thiveaud C, Sen R, Lee J, et al.

*Advanced Materials*

[10.1002/adma.74686](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.74686)

Membrane-anchored plasmonic gold transducers on HER2 CAR-T cells; photoacoustic imaging plus localized thermal triggering of thermogenetic circuits that secrete T cell engagers.

Strip away the nanomaterials and the engager-relevant claim is a delivery architecture: put the engager gene behind a heat-inducible circuit inside a CAR-T cell, then use an external laser to decide where and when it is transcribed. The transducers — plasmonically coupled gold nanospheres in an anisotropic framework, ~90% absorption efficiency, stable across repeated lasing — sit on the T cell membrane without compromising function, and serve two roles at once. Longitudinal photoacoustic imaging of trafficking in heterogeneous HER2 breast tumors stratified responders from non-responders prospectively, which is itself a useful capability. The consequential result is the second: thermal actuation of intratumoral CAR-T cells engineered with thermogenetic circuits drove local secretion of T cell engagers that redirected cytotoxicity onto antigen-negative tumor cells, converting antigen escape from a terminal failure into something addressable in situ. Systemic engager dosing cannot do this — the whole point is that the molecule is made only inside the tumor, only when the operator says so. The obvious caveats are depth of light penetration and clinical practicality of the laser, but the control layer is a genuinely different answer to the therapeutic-window problem than affinity tuning or masking.

DICE score: 4 — Externally triggered, spatially confined in situ engager secretion from CAR-T cells is a novel mechanism for both antigen escape and engager exposure control, demonstrated end to end with imaging-based response prediction.

Kwong and Emelianov labs, Georgia Tech/Emory.

### 6. A Synthetic Platform for Antibody Junctional Diversification Beyond Natural Constraints

Liu W, Yang J, Zhang Y, Xue X

*Advanced Science*

[10.1002/advs.77339](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77339)

ARESEC: programmable V(D)J recombination in non-lymphoid HEK293T cells via engineered recombination signal sequences, tuned RAG1/2, and TdT, coupled to mammalian display and FACS.

Vertebrate junctional diversity is confined to CDR3 and to lymphocytes, an accident of RAG transposon domestication that every engineered antibody library inherits as a constraint. ARESEC breaks both limits: engineered RSS elements direct RAG-mediated recombination across CDR1, CDR2, *and* CDR3 in ordinary HEK293T cells, and TdT co-expression adds 41.5%–86.3% additional junctional diversity across the three CDRs. Because the platform produces native IgG and couples surface display to FACS, selection happens on the real molecule rather than on a scFv or Fab surrogate — which matters when the endpoint is a full-length bispecific. Starting from clinical-grade scaffolds rather than naïve germline, focused multi-CDR diversification of nivolumab and durvalumab recovered affinity-improved variants, including an anti-PD-L1 with 2.01-fold enhanced binding, from libraries of modest complexity. The affinity gains are small and the demonstration is on two well-behaved targets, so this reads as platform-establishing rather than result-defining. But a mammalian system that diversifies all three CDRs in native IgG context is a real alternative to yeast Fab display for the arms of a multispecific, where format-transfer losses between display and final molecule are a recurring tax.

DICE score: 4 — Escapes the CDR3-only and lymphocyte-only constraints of natural junctional diversification in a mammalian, native-IgG display context, though the demonstrated affinity improvements are modest.

Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

### 7. Virus-like particles enable targeted gene engineering and pooled CRISPR screening in primary human myeloid cells

Jung H, Devant P, Ching C, Ota M, Dann E, Zhu R, et al.

*Nature Biotechnology*

[10.1038/s41587-026-03258-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03258-2)

VLP-delivered CRISPR ribonucleoproteins in primary human monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells; SLICeVLP pairs VPX-lentiviral sgRNA delivery with VLP-delivered Cas9 for pooled and Perturb-seq screens.

Myeloid cells have been the compartment functional genomics could not reach — they sense and destroy the delivery vehicles used everywhere else, so knockout, base editing, and epigenetic silencing in primary human macrophages have all been low-yield and viability-costly. This toolkit delivers all three modalities efficiently while preserving viability and, importantly, innate immune responsiveness, so the cells still behave like myeloid cells after editing; adding AAV donor delivery gets site-specific integration of large sequences by HDR. The screening result validates the platform: pooled loss-of-function and Perturb-seq screens in human macrophages nominated regulators of TNF and CD80 and converged on TNFAIP3, whose ablation locked cells into a proinflammatory state resistant to suppressive repolarization and enhanced CAR macrophage cytotoxicity. The engager relevance is indirect but real. Myeloid engagers targeting CD64, CD89, or SIRPα are an active format direction, and the effector biology of the cell being recruited has been essentially unscreenable. This makes it screenable, and hands over one node — resistance to microenvironmental repolarization — that a myeloid-engaging molecule would want to phenocopy pharmacologically.

DICE score: 4 — Opens genome-scale functional screening in the primary human myeloid compartment for the first time at usable efficiency, and delivers a validated repolarization-resistance node relevant to myeloid-directed engagers.

Marson, Carnevale, Eyquem, and Doudna labs, Gladstone/UCSF/Berkeley.

### 8. Neutrophil-integrated syncytial CAR macrophage for cancer immunotherapy

Tian T, Zhao S, Tian T, Li W, Zhang Z, Shi T, et al.

*Nature Immunology*

[10.1038/s41590-026-02615-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41590-026-02615-2)

S-CAR-M: CAR macrophages fused with neutrophils to create a syncytial effector inheriting chemokine-driven migration, NET release, and ROS production.

Cell fusion as a format-engineering move is unusual enough to be worth noting on its own — rather than adding transgenes to fix a CAR macrophage's deficits, the authors fuse in a different cell type and inherit its machinery wholesale. The syncytial cells accumulate in tumors far better than conventional CAR-Ms because they carry neutrophil chemokine responsiveness, and the inherited NET and ROS capacity does something mechanistically interesting: it drives phosphatidylserine exposure on tumor cells, opening a second, antigen-independent phagocytic route through PtdSer–MerTK alongside the scFv–antigen route. A single dose reduced tumor burden, limited metastasis, and prevented recurrence across syngeneic and xenograft models, with antigen spreading limiting escape by antigen-low cells. The dual-route killing is the part worth transplanting conceptually — engagers are strictly antigen-dependent by construction, and antigen-low escape has no analogous fallback, so a format that couples specific engagement to a nonspecific, locally induced second mechanism is an argument for arming engagers rather than only affinity-tuning them. Whether a fused-cell product is manufacturable at scale is a separate and considerable question.

DICE score: 3 — A genuinely novel effector-cell architecture with a well-characterized dual killing mechanism and antigen-spreading benefit, but preclinical only and with substantial unresolved manufacturability questions.

Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

### 9. Bispecific chimeric autoantibody receptor T cells eliminate acetylcholine receptor-specific B cells in myasthenia gravis models

von Wardenburg N, Spagni G, Reincke SM, Wernick S, Kornau HC, Zinnow V, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-76750-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76750-7)

AChRα1 and β1 extracellular domains as dual CAAR ectodomains, co-transduced into T cells to capture polyclonal anti-AChR autoantibody-producing B cells.

The design problem in chimeric autoantibody receptors is coverage: the pathogenic B cell pool in myasthenia gravis recognizes multiple epitopes across the nicotinic AChR subunits, so a single-subunit CAAR selects for escape by construction. Co-transducing CAARs bearing the α1 and β1 extracellular domains is the direct answer — use the autoantigen itself as a bispecific bait pair rather than engineering a paratope at all. The cells secreted effector cytokines only on engagement, lysed target cells efficiently, and in xenograft models depleted pathogenic B cell lines while lowering circulating autoantibody and autoantibody at the neuromuscular junction. What generalizes to engager work is the targeting logic rather than the cell platform: where the pathogenic population is defined by what it *binds* rather than by a surface marker, the antigen is a better targeting moiety than any antibody, and multivalent antigen presentation is how you cover a polyclonal repertoire. That argues for antigen-armed engager formats — a soluble bispecific presenting two autoantigen domains against CD3 — in exactly the autoimmune indications the BCMA×CD3 engagers above are now moving into, with the advantage of not requiring a cell product.

DICE score: 3 — A well-executed solution to the epitope-coverage problem in CAAR design using dual autoantigen ectodomains, with clean in vivo autoantibody reduction, but confined to xenograft models and a single indication.

Charité–Universitätsmedizin Berlin and DZNE, with Yale.

### 10. Glofitamab in combination with immunochemotherapy in patients with relapsed/refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma: Phase 1b dose-escalation study

Townsend W, Ghosh N, Hutchings M, Dickinson M, Tani M, Santoro A, et al.

*British Journal of Haematology*

[10.1111/bjh.70741](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjh.70741)

CD20 × CD3 2:1 bispecific plus obinutuzumab/rituximab-CHOP; dose escalation from 70 µg to 30 mg with step-up dosing in the higher cohorts.

The question this study actually answers is whether a CD20×CD3 engager can be layered onto full-dose immunochemotherapy in patients whose CD20 is already occupied by a competing anti-CD20 antibody, and at what dose. Thirty-one patients with follicular, mantle cell, or marginal zone lymphoma, all with prior obinutuzumab or rituximab exposure, received glofitamab from cycle 2 day 8 with G/R-CHOP. Complete metabolic response was 83.9% and median progression-free survival was not reached at 44.2 months of follow-up, which is a long enough tail to be meaningful rather than a snapshot. The safety picture is the part requiring judgment: grade 3/4 events in 80.6%, serious events in 74.2%, and four grade 5 events mostly from infection — heavy, though not obviously beyond what R-CHOP in this population produces. CRS occurred in 51.6% but was grade 1/2 in the majority, and the step-up cohorts support taking 2.5/10/30 mg forward into untreated DLBCL. For engineering readers the useful inference is that target-arm competition with a co-administered parental antibody did not abolish activity at these doses — a real design consideration when a program's engager and its antibody backbone share an antigen.

DICE score: 3 — Mature efficacy data with 44 months of follow-up establishing a workable dose and schedule for engager-plus-immunochemotherapy combination, and evidence that co-administered anti-CD20 competition is tolerable, though the toxicity burden is substantial.

Roche/Genentech-sponsored; NCT03467373.

### 11. A bispecific antibody targeting conserved hemagglutinin epitopes confers broad protection against H7 avian influenza and accelerates viral clearance via localized respiratory delivery

Wu H, Yang F, Wang P, Fu J, Zhang J, Guo J, et al.

*Emerging Microbes & Infections*

[10.1080/22221751.2026.2713326](https://doi.org/10.1080/22221751.2026.2713326)

BsAb-H7: chimeric bispecific engaging two non-overlapping conserved epitopes on the H7 hemagglutinin head, delivered intranasally.

The epitope-selection method here is the part worth extracting, and it is directly portable to tumor antigens. Rather than picking epitopes by immunogenicity, the authors mapped the H7 HA head with 30 murine mAbs, then intersected that map with longitudinal evolutionary analysis of over 2,500 isolates to find residues that are *functionally constrained* — G70, G132, N167, M173, near-invariant across decades because the virus cannot afford to change them. Engaging two such non-overlapping sites with one bispecific gave neutralization breadth across H7N9, H7N7, and H7N3 and prophylactic and therapeutic protection in lethal heterologous challenge. The second finding is a route-of-administration result with wider relevance: intranasal delivery substantially outperformed systemic dosing, accelerating clearance and resolving pulmonary immunopathology even in delayed-treatment windows. Selecting epitopes by escape-constraint rather than by affinity or accessibility is exactly the discipline that antigen-loss escape in engager therapy demands, and the local-delivery result is a reminder that where a bispecific is administered can matter as much as how it binds.

DICE score: 3 — A rigorous escape-constraint-driven epitope selection strategy for biparatopic bispecific design, with a clear local-versus-systemic delivery result, though the target is infectious rather than oncologic.

Zhejiang University.

### 12. A Highly Stringent Split Intein-Mediated DHFR Selectable Marker Enables Efficient Development of High-Producing CHO Cells for Therapeutic Proteins

Lee EJ, Yoon C, Heo NY, Ryu SH, Lee GM, Kim YG

*ACS Synthetic Biology*

[10.1021/acssynbio.6c00133](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssynbio.6c00133)

SiMPl-DHFR: computationally guided split-site design plus split intein-mediated protein ligation, reconstituting DHFR only when all transgenes are present.

Multispecific manufacturing has a selection problem that monoclonal manufacturing does not: a bispecific needs four coordinated genes, and conventional DHFR selection is stringent enough for one or two but degrades badly as the number rises, which is why methotrexate amplification and long screening campaigns remain the norm. Splitting DHFR and requiring intein-mediated ligation to reconstitute it converts selection into an AND gate — survival requires every piece. The numbers are strong: 98.5% producer cells in stable pools for an Fc-fusion glycoprotein and 22.5-fold higher specific productivity than wild-type DHFR selection, with dhfr-nonamplified SiMPl pools outperforming *every* amplified wild-type pool. The result that matters for this readership is the last one — efficient generation of stable bispecific-producing CHO pools requiring coordinated expression of four genes, alongside two-gene mAb pools. Cell line development timelines are a real and rarely discussed constraint on how many multispecific formats a group can actually test, and a selection architecture that scales with transgene count instead of collapsing under it addresses that directly.

DICE score: 3 — A genuinely extensible selection architecture that removes gene-number scaling as a bottleneck in multispecific cell line development, with large productivity gains and no need for amplification.

KRIBB and KAIST.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. Tumor-tropic E. coli engineered as living T and NK cell engagers

Yang S, Bader AC, Sendker S, Hu A, Chen DC, Nath H, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.18.745642](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.18.745642)

LIME: non-pathogenic tumor-colonizing *E. coli* surface-displaying tandem scFvs against a tumor antigen and an activating receptor on T or NK cells.

Every hard problem in solid-tumor engager therapy is a distribution problem — the molecule cannot reach the tumor in sufficient concentration without also reaching everything else, and half-life extension trades one failure mode for another. LIME dispenses with pharmacokinetics entirely by making the engager a surface feature of a bacterium that goes to tumors on its own, which turns exposure into a colonization problem instead of a dosing problem. The platform is modular across both arms (T or NK activating receptors, swappable tumor antigen), bridged effector and tumor cells, and induced bona fide transcriptional programs of T cell activation, metabolism, and proliferation rather than just cytotoxicity readouts. The headline comparison is the one the field will argue about: LIME reportedly outperformed tarlatamab in small cell lung cancer models, and induced durable immunity in lymphoma, with combination benefit from RAS inhibition and PD-L1 blockade in pancreatic cancer including humoral responses. Multi-lineage immune modulation stayed tumor-confined with no organ toxicity reported. That safety claim carries the entire proposition and will need far more than mouse data, but as a restatement of what an engager *is* — a locally manufactured, self-localizing surface rather than a systemically dosed protein — this is the most interesting thing posted this week.

DICE score: 5 — Reframes engager delivery as bacterial colonization rather than pharmacokinetics, with a modular T/NK-swappable architecture, patient-derived organoid data, and a direct efficacy comparison against an approved DLL3 engager.

Romee, Wu, Ritz, and Aguirre labs, Dana-Farber Cancer Institute.

### 2. Protein design to broadly reprogram engineered T cell function

Boyken SE, Merillat S, Langan RA, Moffett HF, Coventry B, Haeseleer F, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.13.742806](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.13.742806)

OUTLAST Regulators: designed proteins that modulate NR4A family exhaustion transcription factors, the E3 ligases Cbl-b and c-Cbl, and SOCS family proteins, delivered as compact genetic modules.

The premise is that the processes limiting T cell function in solid tumors are family-level and dose-sensitive, so knocking out one NR4A or one Cbl paralog is the wrong instrument — you need something that engages a whole protein family with tunable strength, and that is a protein design problem rather than a genome editing one. The designs cover three distinct regulatory node types (pro-exhaustion transcription factors, ubiquitin ligases that dampen TCR signaling, and cytokine signaling suppressors), and improved CAR-T and TCR-T performance both in vitro and in stringent in vivo solid tumor models. The practical detail that makes this more than a design demonstration is packaging: compact modules compatible with standard viral vectors and existing cell therapy manufacturing, which is the difference between a Nature-worthy result and something a cell therapy group can actually adopt next quarter. Engagers cannot carry a genetic payload into the T cells they recruit, so this is not directly transferable — but it maps which intracellular nodes are worth targeting and establishes that the intracellular regulatory layer is now a designable surface, which is where soluble engager-adjacent modalities eventually have to go.

DICE score: 4 — Extends de novo protein design from binding surfaces to intracellular regulatory families with tunable, non-binary control, validated across CAR-T and TCR-T in demanding solid tumor models and packaged for real manufacturing workflows.

Outpace Bio with the Baker lab, University of Washington.

### 3. Nanoscale 3D profiling of the T cell membrane reveals CD2 enrichment at microvilli tips, positioning adhesion near TCR zones in the immunological synapse

Kim S, Lai E, Akhbariyoon H, Klimas A, DiBernardo E, Zhao Y, Cai E

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.17.744600](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.744600)

NanoMAP: expansion-microscopy platform resolving TCR, CD2, and LFA-1 organization on T cell microvilli and within the immunological synapse at 35–60 nm.

Engager design is, at bottom, geometry — linker length, domain arrangement, and epitope membrane-proximity all exist to force an intermembrane spacing that mimics the native synapse. This work supplies the structural ground truth those decisions have been guessing at. At 35–60 nm resolution the authors show effector CD8 T cells carry 5.1-fold higher microvillar density than naïve cells, that CD2 prefers microvilli tips more strongly than TCR or LFA-1 does, and that CD2 associates spatially with TCR clusters while LFA-1 does not. At the synapse, TCR and CD2 co-occupy close membrane contacts while LFA-1 is excluded to distal regions, and on termination of activation TCR clusters selectively disengage from microvilli while the adhesion receptors persist. Two consequences for engager work: the tight CD2–TCR spatial coupling supports CD2 as a costimulatory arm placed within, not adjacent to, the close-contact zone — relevant to the growing set of trispecifics adding a costimulatory arm — and the effector-versus-naïve microvillar density difference implies that the same engager samples very different scanning surfaces depending on which T cells it recruits.

DICE score: 3 — Provides nanoscale structural ground truth for the synapse geometry that engager linker and epitope design implicitly assumes, with a directly actionable finding on CD2–TCR spatial coupling.

Cai lab, Carnegie Mellon University.

### 4. Proximity directs microbial transglutaminase site selectivity in native antibody modification

Nishioka R, Murozono K, Kawaguchi Y, Kimura M, Sakuraba S, Hashii N, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.18.745451](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.18.745451)

pG(Fab)-EzMTG: an engineered transglutaminase zymogen fused to a Fab-binding protein G domain, where fusion orientation dictates which native IgG1 lysine is labeled.

The finding is almost a controlled experiment in geometry. The same catalytic domain and the same targeting domain, joined in the opposite orientation, moves the labeled residue from Lys65 to Lys225 in the hinge — proximity, not chemistry, chooses the site. The practical payoff is that Lys225 labeling generalizes across different IgG1 antibodies while the prior Lys65 approach did not, removing the per-antibody re-engineering that has kept enzymatic site-specific conjugation from being a platform method; an MMAE conjugate at Lys225 retained antigen-specific cytotoxicity. For multispecific work this is a conjugation-handle result rather than an architecture result, but a hinge-proximal, sequence-generic, native-antibody labeling site is useful for exactly the things engager programs bolt on late — imaging labels for biodistribution, half-life modules, or a payload on an IgG-like bispecific scaffold without re-cloning cysteine variants. The generalizable principle is broader: in proximity-directed enzymatic modification, fusion architecture is a design variable with as much leverage as the catalytic site itself.

DICE score: 3 — Establishes fusion-protein orientation as the controlling variable for site selectivity in enzymatic antibody modification and delivers a cross-antibody-generic hinge labeling site, though demonstrated on IgG1 with a single payload.

Kamiya group, Kyushu University.

### 5. Integration of clinical and T-cell immune profiling to predict early response to CD3xBCMA bispecific antibody immunotherapy in Multiple Myeloma

Deredec N, Aziez L, Boussaid I, Decroocq J, Guedon A, Michot M, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.08.17.743749](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.743749)

Longitudinal high-dimensional profiling of circulating T cells and clinical parameters during BCMA × CD3 bispecific therapy in relapsed/refractory myeloma.

Response to BCMA×CD3 engagers is heterogeneous and the field has mostly looked at baseline features to explain it; this study looks at the first two weeks instead, and finds the dynamics are more informative than the starting state. All patients showed early depletion of circulating T cells with concurrent activation within fourteen days — margination, not failure — and a mixed-data factorial analysis separated responders on a combination of lower baseline soluble BCMA, fewer bone lesions, higher baseline lymphocyte count, and, during the first days of dosing, a sharper CXCL10 rise paired with a steeper drop in circulating T cells. The soluble BCMA finding has a direct engineering reading: shed antigen is a competing sink for the tumor arm, and its concentration predicting response is an argument for either shed-antigen-discriminating epitopes or affinity tuning that tolerates the sink. The T cell disappearance-plus-CXCL10 signature as a *positive* marker is the more counterintuitive result, and if it holds it is a pharmacodynamic readout available within days rather than cycles. Modest cohort, single center, and no independent validation.

DICE score: 3 — A useful early pharmacodynamic stratification signature for BCMA×CD3 therapy with a mechanistically interpretable soluble-antigen finding, limited by cohort size and absence of external validation.

Institut Cochin, Université Paris Cité.

## Weekly Themes

The dominant story this week is a hard, evidence-based recalibration of what computational design can and cannot do, arriving from two independent directions on the same day. AIntibody is the more consequential: the first blinded, prospective, experimentally anchored benchmark of AI antibody design across 29 organizations finds that in silico affinity maturation genuinely works, that affinity *ranking* within a selection output is worse than random for all but one method, and that nothing generalizes across tasks. The Bonn minibinder paper delivers the complementary lesson from the wet side — de novo binder success is severely target-dependent, and even successful binders can fail in the format you need them in for reasons that have nothing to do with the interface, with isoelectric point emerging as the concrete culprit and non-interface redesign as the fix. Read together, these argue that the tractable regime for engager arms is local optimization of an existing binder plus deliberate engineering of everything *around* the paratope, and that anyone promising de novo generation of a co-tuned CD3 arm is ahead of the demonstrated evidence. The mimic-antibody work is the constructive counterpoint, showing that interface convergence is common enough to turn ligand mimicry into a prospective epitope-directed selection filter with a 35% hit rate — a computational method that works precisely because it exploits a structural regularity rather than trying to predict affinity.

The second thread is a shift in where the engager gets made. Gamgertamig is the week's orthodox entry and a good one — a 50-fold affinity bias toward BCMA over CD3, aimed at the autoimmune reset window where the tolerance for cytokine release is lowest — but three other papers attack exposure rather than affinity. Georgia Tech puts the engager gene behind a heat-inducible circuit inside a CAR-T cell and triggers local secretion with an external laser, converting antigen escape into something addressable in situ. The Dana-Farber LIME preprint goes further and abolishes systemic pharmacokinetics altogether, displaying tandem scFvs on tumor-tropic *E. coli* so that colonization replaces dosing, with a reported efficacy edge over tarlatamab in SCLC models. Even the H7 influenza bispecific finds that intranasal delivery substantially beats systemic administration for the same molecule. The premise these share is that the therapeutic window is a spatial property as much as a binding one, and that affinity tuning — the field's default lever for a decade — is one instrument among several. Underneath all of it, the infrastructure papers were unusually strong: a mammalian platform that diversifies all three CDRs in native IgG context, a split-intein selection architecture that makes four-gene bispecific CHO pools routine, and nanoscale synapse profiling that finally supplies the geometric ground truth that linker-length and epitope-proximity decisions have been assuming. Together they lower the cost of *testing* format ideas, which given how poorly the field currently predicts which formats will work may matter more this year than any single new architecture.
