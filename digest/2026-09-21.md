### 1. mRNA-encoding CD19-targeting T cell engager for refractory immune thrombocytopenia

Wang M, Liu T, Dai Q, Teng J, Wu Y, Jia J, et al.

*Cell*

[10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.039](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.039)

CD19 × CD3 T cell engager delivered as lipid nanoparticle-formulated mRNA (ABO2203) rather than as recombinant protein.

The engineering claim here is not the molecule but the pharmacokinetics of how it arrives. A CD3-engaging protein dosed intravenously produces a concentration spike, and that spike is what drives the cytokine burst that forces step-up dosing, hospitalization, and tocilizumab standby onto every engager program. Encoding the same engager as LNP-mRNA converts a bolus into hepatic secretion over hours to days, and in transgenic mice the authors report complete B cell depletion with attenuated cytokine release relative to the equivalent TCE protein — the exposure profile doing the work that affinity de-tuning and masking strategies are usually recruited for. The first-in-human data, three patients with refractory secondary immune thrombocytopenia, are small but unusually clean: rapid and complete peripheral B cell depletion with sustained depletion in bone marrow, durable platelet recovery and improved serology through six months, and only grade 1-2 adverse events with no cytokine release syndrome at all. Bone marrow depletion is the part worth dwelling on, because it is where CD19 engagers have historically underperformed CAR-T and where the autoimmune indications actually need reach. B cell reconstitution returned transitional and naive cells, consistent with a reset rather than a permanent ablation. If this holds at larger n, the delivery route becomes a design parameter co-equal with format, and the Fc-versus-no-Fc half-life argument that has shaped engager architecture for a decade gets reframed.

DICE score: 5 — Establishes mRNA-LNP delivery as a genuine engager modality with first-in-human evidence that the exposure profile, not the binding domains, is what governs cytokine release.

Clinical-stage (first-in-human). Ruijin Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine.

### 2. Strategy for modular assembly of tetravalent, multispecific antibodies

Mallette E, Blazer LL, Hokanson CA, Chen C, Perez JG, Pavlenco A, et al.

*Protein Science*

[10.1002/pro.70797](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.70797)

PD-L1 (three distinct epitopes) and CD30 binders combined into bispecific, tetravalent, triparatopic IgG-like architectures from a common-light-chain Fab-phage library.

The chain-pairing problem is the tax every multispecific program pays, and the common light chain is the oldest answer to it — but the usual objection is that forcing one light chain across many heavy chains narrows the accessible paratope space enough to cost you binders. This work attacks that objection directly and with structures. From a single-light-chain Fab-phage library the authors pull three antibodies hitting non-overlapping sites on PD-L1 plus one against CD30, and show by biophysical and cellular characterization that these match clinically relevant benchmarks rather than merely binding. They then assemble the paratopes combinatorially into tetravalent triparatopic molecules that retain parental functionality, which is the harder test — valency and geometry changes routinely destroy the activity of components that were fine in isolation. The structural analysis of each complex is the durable contribution: it shows the same light chain adopting diverse paratopes against an array of distinct heavy chains, which is the evidence that the format constraint is not the binder-diversity bottleneck it is assumed to be. For engager work this is a plug-and-play route to tuning valency on the tumor arm while leaving the effector arm fixed, without renegotiating the assembly problem each time.

DICE score: 4 — Turns common-light-chain multispecific assembly into a genuinely modular exercise and supplies the structural evidence that the shared light chain does not constrain paratope diversity.

Sidhu group, The Anvil Institute of Systems Biologics, Toronto, with Aarvik Therapeutics.

### 3. γδ T cells modulate anti-tumor immunity in small cell lung cancer

Ng J, You Y, Zhang TZ, Hess JB, Best SA, Caneborg A, et al.

*Cancer Cell*

[10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.015)

DLL3 × CD3 BiTE (tarlatamab) redirecting Vδ2 γδ T cells rather than conventional CD8 T cells; zoledronate as a sensitizing agent.

Engager design has been built almost entirely around the αβ CD8 compartment, and small cell lung cancer is precisely where that assumption breaks: epigenetic silencing of MHC-I leaves conventional T cells poorly primed and checkpoint blockade largely ineffective. Using single-cell profiling of patient biospecimens the authors find γδ T cell infiltration that, despite PD-1 expression, retains a cytotoxic transcriptional program, and high γδ infiltration predicted improved anti-PD-L1 response across two practice-changing trial cohorts. The engineering-relevant result is that tarlatamab redirects these γδ cells to kill SCLC effectively in preclinical models — the CD3 arm works on a compartment whose killing does not depend on the MHC-I that the tumor has switched off. That reframes what a CD3 binder is recruiting, and raises the question of whether effector-arm design should deliberately bias toward or away from γδ engagement rather than treating it as incidental. The zoledronate result adds a cheap, approved sensitizer that raises γδ-mediated killing, which is the kind of combination a clinical program can actually run. An accompanying commentary in the same issue (10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.016) frames the MHC-I bypass argument.

DICE score: 4 — Shows that an approved CD3 engager's efficacy in an MHC-I-silenced tumor may run substantially through γδ T cells, which changes how the effector arm should be characterized and combined.

Clinical-stage molecule (tarlatamab). Sutherland lab, Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research, Melbourne.

### 4. Gene regulatory elements determine efficacy of BCMA-targeted CAR-T cell products

Blumenberg V, June KB, Birocchi F, Escobar G, Graham C, Fan Y, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-016839](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-016839)

BCMA CARs comparing a biparatopic dual-VHH binder against an scFv binder, each expressed from EF1α+WPRE or MND promoters.

This is a binder-attribution correction with uncomfortable read-across for anyone justifying a format choice from clinical performance. Cilta-cel outperforms ide-cel in trials and real-world comparisons, and the field has largely credited cilta-cel's biparatopic dual-VHH BCMA binder. The two products also differ in the vector regulatory elements driving CAR expression, and nobody had separated the variables. Holding vector copy number or surface expression matched, the authors find MND-driven constructs transduce better and express more strongly but carry higher tonic signalling by NFAT and CD69 readout, while EF1α-driven cells give superior cytokine secretion, tumor control, and survival in MM.1S xenografts — independently of the binder. Crucially the dual-VHH advantage disappeared under MND, and the ide-cel-style scFv did better under EF1α than under MND. The lesson generalizes past CAR-T: a binder comparison run in a cellular context where expression level and tonic signalling differ is not a binder comparison, and engager teams benchmarking VHH tandems against scFv formats in cell-based assays are exposed to the same confound. It also reinforces that expression strength and functional quality are not the same axis.

DICE score: 4 — Demonstrates that a headline format advantage attributed to a biparatopic VHH binder is actually conferred by the promoter, invalidating a widely cited piece of binder-format evidence.

Maus lab, Cellular Immunotherapy Program, Mass General Brigham Cancer Institute.

### 5. Predicting non-specific binding of VHHs using machine learning models with cluster-aware validation

Stanev V, Devalle F, Boroumand M, Pouryahya M, Sermadiras I, Caldwell J, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2732787](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2732787)

VHH single-domain antibodies; structure-descriptor ML model predicting baculovirus particle binding as a polyreactivity proxy.

The useful contribution is methodological honesty rather than the model itself. Developability ML in industry is trained on pipeline data, which is dense with closely related sequences from the same campaigns, and a random train/test split will place near-siblings on both sides and report a performance number that evaporates on a genuinely new project. The authors build a custom cluster-aware cross-validation procedure that evaluates the model on unseen data in several distinct contexts, then use it to train a VHH polyreactivity predictor against the baculovirus particle assay from computed-structure descriptors, surfacing several structural risk factors that track with higher polyreactivity. For engager work the target is well chosen: VHH domains are the building block of choice for compact multispecifics precisely because they tolerate tandem arrangement, and polyreactivity in a single-domain arm translates into the off-tumor sink and rapid clearance that sink an otherwise good molecule. Anyone maintaining an internal developability model should read the validation section as a diagnosis of their own benchmark numbers.

DICE score: 3 — A transferable correction to how developability models are validated on pipeline data, applied to the polyreactivity risk that most affects single-domain engager arms.

AstraZeneca Biopharmaceutical Development, Gaithersburg.

### 6. Combining AI structure prediction and integrative modeling for nanobody-antigen complexes

Sánchez-Marín M, Giulini M, Bonvin AMJJ

*Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling*

[10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01301](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01301)

Nanobody-antigen complex prediction; ensemble docking pipeline over AlphaFold2 and ImmuneBuilder nanobody models in HADDOCK.

Nanobody-antigen complexes are the case where end-to-end AI structure prediction is weakest, for reasons that are structural rather than incidental: there are no coevolutionary signals across an engineered binder-target pair, and the long CDR3 loop that does most of the binding samples a conformational space too broad for a single predicted model to represent. The authors benchmark current ML methods for nanobody structure prediction, then test HADDOCK workflows that dock ensembles of those predicted models under varying information scenarios. The pipeline beats the AlphaFold baseline across all generated models provided some epitope information is available — which is the realistic setting, since epitope binning or mutagenesis data usually exists by the time structural modeling is worth doing. The practical value for engager design is in epitope selection and geometry: predicting where a VHH sits on its antigen, and at what angle relative to the membrane, is what determines whether the synapse distance works, and this is a reproducible route to that answer without a crystal structure.

DICE score: 3 — A benchmarked, information-driven docking recipe that outperforms end-to-end AI prediction on the nanobody complexes most relevant to compact multispecific design.

Bonvin lab, Utrecht University.

### 7. Sequence optimization targeting mRNA stability enhances monoclonal antibody titers in CHO cells

Schneckener S, Haag K, Leweke S, Wolf T, Mayer-Bartschmid A

*New Biotechnology*

[10.1016/j.nbt.2026.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbt.2026.09.004)

IgG1 light chain and trastuzumab coding sequences; nucleotide-transformer-guided codon optimization in CHO with targeted integration.

Codon optimization has been driven by translational metrics — codon adaptation, GC content, avoidance of rare codons — with mRNA stability treated as something you get or you don't. This work makes it a tunable design parameter. A combinatorial library of synonymous IgG1 light-chain variants was integrated as single copies at a defined genomic locus under identical regulatory elements, which is the control that makes the readout interpretable, and steady-state mRNA abundance from paired gDNA/mRNA deep sequencing served as the stability proxy. A nucleotide-transformer-embedding model trained on that data was folded into a genetic algorithm for multi-objective optimization alongside the conventional translational criteria. Optimizing trastuzumab sequences to maximize versus minimize predicted abundance produced a 41% mRNA difference, 59% higher titer and 85% higher cell-specific productivity, with comparable viable cell densities — and against two commercial optimization services, 70% higher titer and 98% higher specific productivity. Multispecifics are chronically expression-limited and chain-ratio-sensitive, and a per-chain lever on steady-state mRNA that costs nothing but a different synthesis order is directly applicable to engager manufacturability.

DICE score: 3 — Converts mRNA stability from an uncontrolled variable into an explicit, model-guided codon-optimization objective with large and cleanly controlled titer gains.

### 8. Comparison of Silevimig, a novel bispecific anti-rabies monoclonal antibody, with human rabies immunoglobulin for post-exposure prophylaxis: a phase III, randomized, double-blind, non-inferiority trial

Wang X, He J, Zha Y, Jiang Y, Tan S, Li Q, et al.

*Clinical Infectious Diseases*

[10.1093/cid/ciag551](https://doi.org/10.1093/cid/ciag551)

Bispecific antibody engaging rabies virus glycoprotein antigenic sites I and III simultaneously.

Not an immune cell engager, but a clean phase III readout on the biparatopic premise that engager programs invoke constantly and rarely test at this scale. Silevimig binds two non-overlapping antigenic sites on the rabies glycoprotein, and the question is whether dual-epitope engagement of a single antigen can substitute for polyclonal human rabies immunoglobulin, which covers many epitopes by construction. Across 1,200 participants with WHO category III exposure randomized 3:1, Silevimig met non-inferiority on day 7 adjusted geometric mean concentration of neutralizing antibodies (0.40 versus 0.36 IU/mL, ratio 1.11, 95% CI 1.01-1.21), gave slightly higher seroresponse rates at days 14 and 90, and produced no rabies cases in either arm over one year. It also did not interfere with vaccine-induced immunity, which is the specific failure mode that has constrained passive immunization. The transferable point for engager designers is that two well-chosen epitopes on one antigen reproduced the protective breadth of a polyclonal product with the manufacturability and lot consistency of a recombinant — the argument biparatopic tumor arms are built on, here with a survival-relevant endpoint behind it.

DICE score: 3 — Phase III evidence that biparatopic single-antigen engagement can match a polyclonal immunoglobulin, validating at clinical scale a design premise engager programs usually assert.

Clinical-stage (NCT05846568).

### 9. Linear antibody epitope prediction using AlphaFold2

DeRoo J, Terry JS, Zhao N, Stasevich TJ, Snow C, Geiss BJ

*eLife*

[10.7554/eLife.98369](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.98369)

PAbFold: scFv-grafted CDRs docked against overlapping antigen-derived peptides using localColabFold.

Epitope mapping is the step that gates everything downstream in engager design — synapse geometry, competition with the physiological ligand, cross-reactivity risk — and the empirical routes to it are slow and expensive enough that programs often proceed without it. PAbFold predicts the structure of antibody heavy/light chains complexed with short overlapping peptides tiled across the antigen, with CDRs grafted onto several scFv framework regions, and flags the peptides that dock as epitopes. It correctly recovered known epitopes for well-characterized HA and Myc antibodies, then identified the epitope of a novel anti-SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid antibody with no public structural data, confirmed by peptide competition ELISA. The authors are usefully explicit about the limits: results are sensitive to peptide length, AlphaFold2 network version, and MSA database choice, and the method addresses linear epitopes only — which excludes most conformational epitopes on the folded tumor antigens engagers usually target. Still, sequence-only epitope triage in hours, with open code, is a real change in the cost of asking the question.

DICE score: 3 — Makes linear epitope mapping a sequence-only computation with experimental validation and candid methodological caveats, and ships as open tooling.

Colorado State University. Code at github.com/jbderoo/PAbFold.

### 10. Engineered α9-nAChR-targeting antibodies attenuate nicotine-enhanced triple-negative breast cancer progression and exhibit favorable short-term tolerability in cynomolgus macaques

Lee CH, Yang YY, Chuang KH, Lin YH, Cheng TC, Hung MC, et al.

*Biomaterials*

[10.1016/j.biomaterials.2026.124638](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biomaterials.2026.124638)

α9-nAChR × mPEG bispecific antibody (scFv-derived) plus an α9-nAChR scFv-Fc fusion, paired with PEGylated liposomal doxorubicin.

Two things here are worth an engager reader's attention. The first is target-class: α9-nAChR is a multi-pass ion channel, and generating a specific binder required engineering an antigen from its extracellular loops (α9CLP) for immunization and phage display — the same problem any engager program faces when the attractive tumor antigen is a GPCR or channel rather than a clean single-pass receptor. The second is the anti-mPEG arm, which is a chemically defined universal handle: rather than conjugating drug to antibody, the bispecific bridges an untouched targeting arm to any PEGylated payload, so one binder can retarget an existing approved nanomedicine. That modularity is the same logic as a pretargeting engager, with the effector being a liposome rather than a T cell. The molecules inhibited PDX growth, blunted nicotine-driven acceleration, and improved docetaxel efficacy. The cynomolgus tolerability data matter disproportionately because α9-nAChR is a nicotinic receptor and the obvious risk is neuromuscular: no myasthenia-like signs were seen on short-term dosing, though the duration is short and the safety question is not settled.

DICE score: 3 — A workable route to binders against a multi-pass channel antigen, combined with a universal anti-PEG arm that lets one bispecific retarget existing PEGylated nanomedicines.

National Defense Medical University, Taipei, with iReal Biotechnology.

### 11. Cord blood-derived fibroblast activation protein-targeted chimeric antigen receptor natural killer cells persist in vivo

Hiroshima T, Kimura T, Maekawa M, Noguchi M, Matsui T, Ikeda S, et al.

*Cancer Science*

[10.1111/cas.70539](https://doi.org/10.1111/cas.70539)

FAP-targeted CAR on cord blood-derived NK cells, tested against patient-derived lung cancer CAFs.

The value of this paper is in the dissociation it reports, and the authors do not paper over it. FAP-CAR NK cells expanded from CD3-depleted cord blood mononuclear cells produced cytokines and killed both FAP-transduced HT1080 cells and primary CAFs from human lung cancer tissue in vitro, and in an intrathoracic xenograft co-injecting A549 cells with luciferase-labeled CAFs they expanded robustly and persisted for five weeks — persistence being the usual complaint against NK effectors. But the CAFs were not eliminated. That gap between antigen-specific in vitro killing plus in vivo persistence on one hand, and failure to clear the target population on the other, is exactly the pattern stromal-targeting engagers should expect: FAP is a legitimate antigen, the effector arrives and survives, and the stromal compartment still does not clear. Whether the limiting factor is CAF accessibility, antigen density, or local suppression is the question the field needs answered before committing to FAP as an engager tumor arm, and a negative result reported this cleanly is more useful than another in vitro killing curve.

DICE score: 3 — A clean negative on stromal FAP targeting: effectors persist and kill in vitro yet fail to clear CAFs in vivo, which is the failure mode FAP-directed engagers should be designed against.

University of Osaka Graduate School of Medicine.

### 12. Teclistamab-associated adverse events: a disproportionality analysis of the FDA adverse event reporting system

He X, Chen G, Zhan Z, Li Z

*Hematology*

[10.1080/16078454.2026.2730041](https://doi.org/10.1080/16078454.2026.2730041)

BCMA × CD3 bispecific antibody; post-marketing pharmacovigilance across 2,631 FAERS reports, 2022-2025.

Real-world safety accumulation on the first approved BCMA engager, using four disproportionality methods plus time-to-onset, Weibull, Kaplan-Meier and semi-quantitative clinical prioritization. Of 2,631 reports, 85.2% involved serious and 21.7% fatal outcomes, and the dominant system organ class was infections and infestations rather than the CRS and ICANS that dominate trial reporting and dosing protocol design. Seventy significant preferred-term signals emerged, 52 already documented and 18 novel, with sepsis the sole high-priority signal by their prioritization scheme. Disproportionality analysis cannot establish causation and is confounded by reporting behavior, so the numbers are hypothesis-generating. But the direction is consistent and matters for design: sustained BCMA engagement drives profound and lasting hypogammaglobulinemia, and the resulting infection burden — not acute cytokine toxicity — is increasingly where the mortality sits. Intermittent dosing, response-adapted de-escalation, and conditionally active formats all attack this, and the case for them rests on evidence like this.

DICE score: 2 — Routine pharmacovigilance methodology, but it quantifies the shift in the BCMA engager risk profile from acute cytokine toxicity toward infection-driven mortality.

## Notable Preprints

### 1. A foundation model learns the sequence and functional grammar of fully human heavy-chain-only antibodies

Nona Biosciences AI4S Team, Miao H

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.10.750553](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.10.750553)

HCAbLM: antibody language model pretrained on 31.8 million fully human heavy-chain-only antibody sequences from 73 immunized HCAb mice.

The premise is that generic antibody language models, trained on conventional paired repertoires, encode priors that do not hold for specialized formats. The authors first establish this model-independently: source-aware comparison of fully human HCAbs against conventional human VH domains shows a reproducible distributional shift concentrated in CDR1/2 and CDR3 architecture, plus a restricted framework region, rather than diffuse framework remodeling. That localized difference is the justification for repertoire-specific pretraining, and HCAbLM duly learns a region-selective sequence-compatibility prior distinct from conventional models. The transfer result is the one that would change a workflow: frozen representations predicted experimentally measured SEC purity, HIC behavior and thermal stability under grouped internal validation and retrospective cross-project evaluation — developability endpoints, not just sequence likelihood. For anyone building VHH-based tandem engagers, a format-matched prior over the exact building block is worth more than a general-purpose model, and the framing of repertoire composition as a design variable for foundation models is the generalizable idea.

DICE score: 4 — First HCAb-specific antibody foundation model, with the distributional case for why format-matched pretraining is necessary and developability transfer to back it up.

Nona Biosciences (Shanghai). Industrial repertoire, not publicly available.

### 2. Targeting IL-2 to inflamed tissues via oxidation-specific epitopes enables third-generation bispecific IL-2 therapeutics

Vazquez T, Cuevas-Martinez M, Billiald N, Porsch F, Saddouk F-Z, Sion M, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.10.750022](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.10.750022)

IL-2 × oxidation-specific epitope (OSE) bivalent scFv fusion — a bispecific Treg engager for inflamed tissue.

IL-2 mutein engineering has spent a decade trading potency for Treg selectivity by weakening IL-2Rβ binding, and the clinical disappointment of that class suggests the trade was bad. This group keeps native IL-2 signaling intact and buys selectivity spatially instead, fusing IL-2 to a single-chain antibody against oxidation-specific epitopes — damage markers abundant at inflamed sites. The targeting moiety is not inert: anti-OSE antibodies have independent anti-inflammatory activity, so the molecule is genuinely bifunctional rather than a cytokine with a homing tag. In vivo it showed increased Treg-over-Teff specificity, which the authors attribute to conformation-dependent modulation of receptor engagement rather than affinity tuning — a mechanism worth scrutiny, since it implies the targeting arm is allosterically shaping the cytokine's receptor preference. Delivery to inflamed tissue was demonstrated in psoriasis and colitis models, with therapeutic benefit extending to atherosclerosis. The design lesson transfers directly to engagers: localization to a disease-state marker rather than a cell-lineage antigen is an underused axis for building in selectivity without detuning the active arm.

DICE score: 4 — Achieves cytokine selectivity through spatial targeting to a damage marker instead of receptor detuning, with an unexpected conformational contribution to receptor bias.

Klatzmann lab, Sorbonne Université/INSERM, with Binder (Vienna) and Mallat (Cambridge).

### 3. PAIR-Scan: single-step identification of highly potent TCR-neoantigen pairs through library-on-library screening

Messemaker M, Richterich CA, Wenner J, Ma Y, Moravec Z, Voogd R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.07.749948](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.07.749948)

HLA-agnostic library-on-library screen pairing tens of thousands of candidate TCR-neoantigen combinations in one step.

The bottleneck in pHLA-directed therapeutics is not building the molecule but knowing which peptide-TCR pair is worth building against, and the conventional route — deconvolute TCRs, synthesize candidate peptides, test pairwise — scales quadratically and is HLA-restricted at every step. PAIR-Scan screens both libraries against each other simultaneously and HLA-agnostically, resolving functionally active pairs from tens of thousands of candidates in a single experiment, and it identifies the minimal recognized peptide directly rather than inferring it. The result most relevant to engineering is that PAIR-Scan correctly ranks TCRs against the same antigen by relative tumor-killing efficiency — functional ordering, not just binding detection, which is what separates a screen you can design from one you can only mine. For TCR-mimic bispecifics and ImmTAC-style molecules this supplies validated target pairs at the front of the pipeline, and the authors explicitly pitch the throughput as a way to generate training data for predictive TCR-reactivity models, which is where the compounding value probably sits.

DICE score: 4 — Collapses TCR-neoantigen pair discovery into one HLA-agnostic screen that ranks pairs by killing efficiency, addressing the target-selection bottleneck upstream of every pHLA-directed engager.

Schumacher lab, Netherlands Cancer Institute, with Dana-Farber.

### 4. Closed, automated CAR-T cell manufacturing: from research to point of care production

Boudousquie C, Migliorini D, Esensten J, Irving M, Maillard J, Vuillefroy de Silly R, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.08.748871](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.08.748871)

Closed, automated manufacturing platform spanning process development and GMP production of CAR-T cells at point of care.

Decentralized manufacturing is the main structural argument against cell therapy and for off-the-shelf engagers, so the engineering that erodes it is worth tracking even from the other side of the comparison. The contribution here is that one closed system covers both flexible process development and GMP production, which is normally a handoff that forces requalification and is where academic point-of-care efforts stall. The economics matter for engager programs mostly as a moving baseline: as the cost and access gap narrows, the case for an off-the-shelf engager rests more on its clinical profile and less on logistics.

DICE score: 2 — Solid process engineering on the development-to-GMP transition rather than a new capability, but it moves the manufacturing baseline that engager-versus-cell-therapy positioning is argued against.

Lausanne (CHUV/Ludwig).

### 5. Patient-derived ascites reveals functional advantages of γδCAR T-cells within the ovarian cancer-like tumor microenvironment

Hahn P, Ntarntani K, Micallef Nilsson I, Gailiesaite E, Foord E, Uhlin M, et al.

*bioRxiv* (preprint)

[10.64898/2026.09.08.749386](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.08.749386)

γδ T cell-derived CAR-T cells assayed in patient-derived ovarian cancer ascites as a suppressive microenvironment surrogate.

A second γδ signal this week, from the cell-therapy side and pointing the same direction as the Cancer Cell SCLC work: γδ effectors hold up in conditions where αβ cells degrade. Using patient ascites rather than defined suppressive media is the methodological choice that makes this worth noting — it exposes the effectors to the actual soluble milieu of the solid tumor compartment, including whatever combination of suppressive factors is present, instead of one at a time. If γδ effectors are genuinely more robust in that environment, the implication for engagers is that an effector arm biased toward γδ recruitment could be an advantage in solid tumors rather than an off-target curiosity.

DICE score: 2 — Preliminary and single-indication, but the ascites-based assay and the γδ robustness result converge with this week's engager-side evidence for γδ effectors in solid tumors.

Karolinska Institutet.

## Weekly Themes

Two threads dominate this week, and both are about variables the field has been treating as fixed. The first is delivery: the *Cell* first-in-human study of an mRNA-encoded CD19 engager achieves complete B cell depletion with no cytokine release syndrome at all, which strongly implies that the CRS burden shaping every engager dosing protocol is a property of the bolus rather than of CD3 engagement, and the OSE-targeted IL-2 preprint makes the same move in a different direction — buy selectivity from spatial localization instead of detuning the active arm. When the exposure profile is the design surface, affinity detuning and masking become one option among several rather than the default. The second thread is a set of overdue corrections to how we attribute results to design choices. The Maus lab shows that cilta-cel's celebrated biparatopic VHH advantage disappears under a different promoter, meaning a widely cited piece of binder-format evidence was really about vector regulatory elements; the AstraZeneca VHH developability work shows that pipeline-data models validated by random splits report numbers that will not survive a new project; and the FAP-CAR-NK result shows effectors that persist for five weeks and kill in vitro yet fail to clear their targets in vivo. Each is a reminder that the confound sits in the assay context, not the molecule. Alongside that, γδ T cells surfaced twice and independently — tarlatamab redirecting Vδ2 cells in MHC-I-silenced small cell lung cancer, and γδCAR T-cells holding function in patient ascites — which raises a real question about whether the effector compartment a CD3 arm actually recruits has been correctly characterized in solid tumors. Tooling was incremental but coherent: format-specific antibody language models, nanobody complex docking that beats end-to-end prediction when epitope information exists, sequence-only linear epitope triage, and mRNA-stability-aware codon optimization worth 59-70% in titer. On the clinical side, the teclistamab FAERS analysis and a companion *Blood Advances* report corroborating a progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy signal for BCMA bispecifics (10.1182/bloodadvances.2026022192) both point at the same conclusion: for sustained-engagement BCMA products the dominant risk has migrated from acute cytokine toxicity to infection under prolonged B cell and plasma cell aplasia, which is an argument for intermittent dosing and conditionally active formats rather than for better CRS management.
