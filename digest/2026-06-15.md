## Top Papers

### 1. Combination therapy with a novel CD2-targeted costimulatory bispecific antibody overcomes limitations of CD3 T cell engager treatment for solid tumors

Danquah W, Pichery M, Eden T, Boyance A, Pasquet L, Roure V, et al.

*mAbs*

[10.1080/19420862.2026.2684378](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2684378)

HER2 × CD2 non-blocking costimulatory bispecific antibody, co-dosed with an EpCAM × CD3 T-cell engager to deliver tumor-localized costimulation.

Built on a unique non-blocking anti-CD2 antibody, the authors construct a HER2 × CD2 costimulatory bispecific that, dosed independently from an EpCAM × CD3 engager, restores full anti-tumor cytotoxicity at a sub-efficacious low TCE dose — converting 1-of-9 to 8-of-9 complete remissions in a humanized xenograft. Critically, CD2 costimulation engages CD28-negative CD8 T cells (a dominant cytotoxic subset in solid tumors and elderly patients) and compensates for tumor CD58 loss, while inducing markedly lower cytokine release than an analogous HER2 × CD28 bispecific. This positions CD2 as a differentiated, potentially safer costimulatory axis to widen the therapeutic window of solid-tumor TCEs.

DICE score: 4 — A differentiated costimulatory format that exploits a novel CD2 axis to rescue low-dose TCE efficacy, recruit CD28-negative effectors, and reduce cytokine release versus CD28 bispecifics, with strong in vivo proof of concept.

### 2. Functional screening of TCR-like antibodies using STAR-T cell library for cancer immunotherapy

Li Y, Huang D, Liu C, Zhou Z, Song Y, Yang D, et al.

*EMBO Molecular Medicine*

[10.1038/s44321-026-00455-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44321-026-00455-z)

Function-based binder discovery platform; nanobodies against CD22 and the intracellular p53 neoantigen, directly reformatted into CARs and CD3 bispecifics.

Rather than affinity-based selection, the authors exploit Synthetic T-cell receptor and Antigen Receptor (STAR)-T cell libraries in which antigen engagement triggers synchronous receptor endocytosis and T-cell activation, combined into an "Endocytosis–Activation" functional readout that links binder discovery directly to T-cell function. The screen rapidly yielded nanobodies against both a surface antigen (CD22) and an intracellular neoantigen (p53), which retained potent cytotoxicity when reformatted into CARs and bispecific antibodies. As a portable route to engager binding domains — especially for hard pMHC/neoantigen targets — the platform addresses one of the field's core bottlenecks.

DICE score: 4 — A novel function-coupled discovery platform yielding reformattable, neoantigen-specific nanobody building blocks for CARs and bispecific engagers, validated in vitro and in vivo.

Lin Xin / Fu Yang-Xin groups, Tsinghua University.

### 3. DNA hypomethylating agents preserve T cell stemness and potentiate the efficacy of CD3-bispecific antibodies

Aleynick M, Wang Q, Wu JE, Lemus S, Chaimowitz M, Mir H, et al.

*Cancer Immunology Research*

[10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-1420](https://doi.org/10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-1420)

PSMA × CD3 bispecific combined with the DNA hypomethylating agent decitabine; single-cell epigenetic dissection of engager-driven T-cell states.

Recognizing that prolonged TCR stimulation by CD3-bispecifics drives chromatin rewiring and T-cell dysfunction, the authors show that adding decitabine produces synergistic tumor growth inhibition across preclinical models, including humanized PSMA × CD3 prostate-cancer systems. Single-cell profiling maps the epigenetic trajectory of engager-treated T cells and demonstrates that the combination preserves a TCF-1⁺ stem-like population and delays the dysfunctional state at both chromatin and protein levels, maintaining naive-like methylation at stemness loci. The work provides both a mechanistic resource and a directly testable clinical combination to extend engager durability.

DICE score: 4 — A mechanistically deep, clinically actionable combination strategy that preserves T-cell stemness during CD3-bispecific therapy, with single-cell epigenetic evidence and clear translational rationale.

Regeneron.

### 4. Targeting PD-L1 and disrupting Siglec-9 cooperatively potentiate macrophage phagocytosis of breast cancer via a trispecific macrophage engager (TriME)

Muneekaew S, Panya A, Saiprayong K, Chupradit K, Nuchphongsai T, Sasithong P, et al.

*Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy*

[10.1016/j.biopha.2026.119609](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopha.2026.119609)

CD16 × IL-15 × PD-L1 trispecific macrophage engager, combined with Siglec-9-null iPSC-derived macrophages.

Adapting the trispecific killer-engager concept to the myeloid compartment, the authors build a trispecific macrophage engager (TriME) that simultaneously engages CD16 (macrophage activation), IL-15 (effector survival), and PD-L1 (tumor recognition), driving dose-dependent phagocytosis of PD-L1⁺ triple-negative breast cancer cells and NK92 activation via the IL-15 arm. Pairing TriME with CRISPR-generated Siglec-9-null iPSC-derived macrophages amplified phagocytosis beyond checkpoint disruption alone, accompanied by increased pro-phagocytic Syk phosphorylation. The study extends the multispecific immune-engager architecture to a new effector cell type, though data remain largely in vitro.

DICE score: 3 — A novel trispecific engager format redirecting macrophage phagocytosis with an integrated checkpoint-disruption strategy; a notable architectural advance limited by early, mostly in vitro evidence.

Wattanapanitch group, Mahidol University.

### 5. Treatment-related adverse events of CD3-based T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies in patients with cancer: a meta-analysis of clinical trials

Zhu Y, Liu K, Rosen ST, Liu W, Zhu H

*The Lancet Oncology*

[10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00086-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00086-0)

Clinical safety synthesis: pooled toxicity profiles of CD3-based T-cell-engaging bispecifics across hematologic and solid-tumor indications.

This meta-analysis of 104 trials enrolling 10,353 patients quantifies the treatment-related adverse-event burden of CD3 engagers, showing near-universal all-grade toxicity (≈97.5–97.9%) with grade ≥3 events in 70.3% of hematologic versus 45.3% of solid-tumor patients, and cytokine release syndrome as the most frequent all-grade event in both settings (~43–46%). Treatment-related mortality was low (1.0%), driven mainly by infection and organ failure, and toxicity patterns varied substantially by cancer type and antibody class. As the most comprehensive safety reference to date, it provides a quantitative backdrop for the safety-engineering and dosing strategies that dominate current engager design.

DICE score: 3 — A large, rigorous clinical-safety synthesis that establishes class-wide toxicity benchmarks directly relevant to engager therapeutic-window engineering, though it contributes reference data rather than new mechanism or format.

Multi-institution (incl. City of Hope; Central South University).

### 6. Blinatumomab bypasses CD28 blockade to sustain T-cell cytotoxicity and improve survival in a xenograft B-ALL model

Leite GSF, Sullivan J, Filioglou D, Kounelis M, Batatinha H, Simpson RJ, et al.

*Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer*

[10.1136/jitc-2026-014890](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-014890)

Mechanistic/translational: CD19 × CD3 BiTE (blinatumomab) cytotoxicity under CD28-costimulation blockade (abatacept) in B-ALL models.

The authors test whether blinatumomab retains antileukemic activity when CD28 costimulation is blocked by abatacept — a GvHD-mitigation strategy in the post-transplant setting — and find that BiTE-driven cytotoxicity, degranulation, IFN-γ production, and activation-marker induction are fully preserved across effector-to-target ratios and even after prolonged CTLA-4-Ig exposure. In humanized NSG-IL15 mice, the blinatumomab–abatacept combination retained potent leukemia control while extending median survival by 35 days by limiting lethal GvHD. The result mechanistically uncouples graft-versus-leukemia from graft-versus-host activity and shows CD3-engager cytotoxicity is CD28-independent.

DICE score: 3 — A clean mechanistic insight that CD3-engager killing is CD28-independent, with a translational combination rationale to separate GvL from GvHD post-transplant.

University of Arizona.

### 7. C1q-targeting bispecific antibodies bypass Fc to trigger complement activation and receptor clustering agonism

Dijkstra DJ, Blanter MI, van der Meijden E, Groenland RJ, Rodriguez C, Bleijlevens B, et al.

*Molecular Immunology*

[10.1016/j.molimm.2026.06.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molimm.2026.06.006)

Bispecific antibody engineering: Fab-arm engagement of the complement initiator C1q to drive Fc-independent complement activation and receptor clustering.

Across bispecifics combining four C1q-binding clones with anti-CD20, CD37, HER2, EGFR, GITR, and DR5 arms, the authors show that direct Fab-C1q binding can enhance C1q recruitment, C3 deposition, and complement-dependent cytotoxicity beyond Fc-mediated activation — and, using LALAPG and F(ab')₂ variants, that this is Fc-independent. The same C1q-tethering strategy strongly clustered GITR and DR5 to trigger outside-in agonist signaling. The work offers a modular route to engineer potent complement and clustering effector functions into bispecific formats whose natural Fc poorly activates complement.

DICE score: 3 — A differentiated, modular bispecific mechanism that engineers Fc-independent complement activation and receptor-clustering agonism, broadly applicable across target arms.

Genmab / Leiden University Medical Center.

### 8. Cadonilimab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment in PD-L1-negative advanced non-small cell lung cancer: a phase II clinical trial

Wang L, Rao C, Wang Q, Huang K, Liu L, Zhao J, et al.

*Nature Communications*

[10.1038/s41467-026-74241-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74241-3)

Clinical: PD-1 × CTLA-4 bispecific antibody (cadonilimab) plus chemotherapy in PD-L1-negative NSCLC.

This single-arm phase II trial reports that first-line cadonilimab plus chemotherapy met its primary endpoint in PD-L1-negative advanced NSCLC, with a 12-month PFS rate of 42.1%, objective response rate of 66.0%, and median PFS of 9.7 months, alongside a manageable safety profile (grade ≥3 TRAEs in 52%). A cfDNA-methylation molecular-response signal predicted clinical response roughly five cycles before radiographic assessment and stratified PFS. While not a redirecting engager, it provides positive clinical data for a dual-checkpoint bispecific in a hard-to-treat, biomarker-negative population.

DICE score: 2 — Useful positive clinical data for an approved-class checkpoint bispecific in a difficult NSCLC subgroup, with a notable methylation-based response biomarker, but no engager engineering advance.

Akeso (cadonilimab); Chinese multicenter.

### 9. Odronextamab: a bispecific antibody for follicular lymphoma

Garcia-Sancho AM, Cabero-Martínez A, Gutierrez NC

*Expert Opinion on Investigational Drugs*

[10.1080/13543784.2026.2689652](https://doi.org/10.1080/13543784.2026.2689652)

Review: clinical positioning of the CD20 × CD3 bispecific engager odronextamab in follicular lymphoma.

The review summarizes odronextamab's mechanism, pharmacokinetics, and ELM-1/ELM-2 efficacy and safety data supporting EMA approval, and contrasts it with mosunetuzumab and epcoritamab on treatment duration, administration route, dosing, and CRS prophylaxis. It surveys the OLYMPIA phase III program extending the agent toward frontline and combination use. As a focused clinical reference for a marketed CD20 × CD3 engager, it is useful orientation but presents no new data.

DICE score: 2 — A focused but non-empirical clinical review of a marketed CD20 × CD3 engager, valuable as a reference rather than a new contribution.

University of Salamanca.

### 10. Monospecific and Bispecific Chimeric Antigen Receptor (CAR) T-Cell Therapy in Multiple Myeloma: A Systematic Review, Meta-analysis and Meta-regression

Lee ARYB, Wong HJ, Lim CC, De Mel S, Ooi M, Chng WJ, et al.

*Transplantation and Cellular Therapy*

[10.1016/j.jtct.2026.06.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtct.2026.06.001)

Clinical synthesis: comparative efficacy of monospecific versus bispecific/dual-target CAR-T constructs in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma.

Pooling 44 cohorts and 1,833 patients, the meta-analysis finds bispecific and dual-target CAR-T strategies especially promising — BCMA/CD19 reached an 88–92% ORR and BCMA/CD38 88% ORR — and shows GPRC5D-directed antigen switching after BCMA failure achieves an 82% ORR, while extramedullary disease and high prior treatment burden remain poor prognostic factors. The construct-level comparisons of dual-targeting and antigen-switch strategies inform multispecific design choices, though the data derive largely from early-phase single-arm studies.

DICE score: 2 — A useful comparative synthesis quantifying bispecific/dual-target and antigen-switch CAR-T outcomes in myeloma, informative for multispecific design but not a primary engineering advance.

National University of Singapore.

### 11. Engineering and characterization of a novel PD-L1/VEGF bispecific antibody with enhanced VEGF-neutralizing capacity

Chen Q, Hang T, Mao D, Zhou A, Xu J, Liu T, et al.

*Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications*

[10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154136)

PD-L1 × VEGF bispecific (PLVBP); a high-affinity anti-PD-L1 arm fused to four VEGFR1-D2 VEGF-binding domains for avidity-driven VEGF capture.

The authors engineer PLVBP by appending four VEGFR1 domain-II modules to a novel anti-PD-L1 antibody, achieving sub-nanomolar binding to both targets, >98% purity from a three-step process, and VEGF-neutralizing potency exceeding bevacizumab in HUVEC proliferation/migration assays while retaining PD-L1 engagement. The tetravalent VEGF-trap design illustrates an avidity-based format choice for dual checkpoint/anti-angiogenic blockade, though the work is early-stage characterization without in vivo efficacy.

DICE score: 2 — A competent but incremental format-engineering study of an avidity-enhanced PD-L1 × VEGF bispecific in a well-trodden target class, with characterization-level data only.

## Weekly Themes

This week's signal centered on engineering around the costimulation and durability problems of T-cell engagers rather than on new redirecting formats per se. The standout is a HER2 × CD2 non-blocking costimulatory bispecific that, dosed alongside an EpCAM × CD3 engager, rescues low-dose TCE efficacy, recruits CD28-negative CD8 effectors prevalent in solid tumors, and releases less cytokine than a CD28 counterpart (*mAbs*) — proposing CD2 as a safer costimulatory axis. Durability was tackled epigenetically by Regeneron, who showed decitabine preserves a TCF-1⁺ stem-like state and delays dysfunction during PSMA × CD3 therapy, with a single-cell atlas and a directly testable clinical combination (*Cancer Immunology Research*). Binder discovery and modular effector engineering recurred: a function-coupled STAR-T cell library yielded reformattable neoantigen-specific nanobodies (including against intracellular p53) for CARs and bispecifics (*EMBO Molecular Medicine*), a trispecific macrophage engager (CD16 × IL-15 × PD-L1) plus Siglec-9 knockout extended the multispecific killer-engager architecture into the myeloid compartment (*Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy*), and Fab-arm C1q tethering was used to engineer Fc-independent complement activation and receptor-clustering agonism into bispecifics (*Molecular Immunology*). On the clinical/mechanistic side, a 104-trial *Lancet Oncology* meta-analysis benchmarked CD3-engager toxicity class-wide, blinatumomab was shown to retain CD28-independent cytotoxicity under abatacept to uncouple GvL from GvHD, and reviews/meta-analyses of odronextamab, cadonilimab, and bispecific/dual-target myeloma CAR-T rounded out the translational picture. De novo and AI-assisted engager design continues to accelerate in the preprint literature, but bioRxiv's persistent inaccessibility again prevented in-window verification, so no fresh preprints could be surfaced.
